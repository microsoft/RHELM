### 3.2 Data Visualization Challenges and Technical Barriers

Persistent Wi-Fi outages disrupted the meeting, limiting access to shared drives and remote databases. The team’s plans to collaboratively review digital charts and network graphs were hindered, as visualizations could not be displayed in real time. Dr. Ellison’s inability to access a laptop further complicated collective analysis, as he was restricted to verbal contributions and unable to examine visual material alongside his colleagues.

Jamal attempted to mitigate connectivity issues by offering a mobile hotspot, but the connection proved unstable, providing only intermittent access. As a result, essential collaborative tasks were postponed, and the group had to rely on verbal descriptions rather than direct, data-driven discussion.

These technical setbacks had a tangible impact. The lack of digital access echoed findings in the academic literature stating that technological and infrastructural failures within research groups impede group cognition and productivity (see [1]). Participation was inequitable; Dr. Ellison could not engage fully with the digital aspects of the meeting, limiting the depth and immediacy of his feedback ([2]). The temporary workaround of manually recording data and updating documents at a later time increased the likelihood of transcription errors and interrupted communication flows. Meeting outcomes were affected, as only partial progress could be made on planned objectives.

### 3.3 Collaborative Note-Taking and Workload Distribution

With only one device available, Jamal served as both note-taker and technical monitor for the duration of the meeting. This resulted in a lopsided distribution of cognitive and logistical responsibilities—a dynamic previously shown in team research to reduce overall engagement and inclusiveness ([2]). To ensure transparency, Jamal later synthesized the notes and shared the meeting summary with all participants via email, providing an opportunity for post-session review and comment.

The group discussed the importance of distributing note-taking and administrative tasks more equitably in the future and suggested formalizing a rotation system.

## 5. Consultative Follow-Up and Proposed Solutions

### 5.1 Technical and Infrastructural Improvements

**Immediate Steps:**  
- Meet in locations with consistently reliable Wi-Fi, such as the library seminar room or department’s main conference space ([3]).
- Request loaner devices from IT to ensure all members can access digital materials during sessions.
- Send all essential documents—including drafts, data visualizations, and agenda items—via email ahead of meetings so participants can prepare and provide feedback even if unforeseen issues occur.

**Long-Term Strategies:**  
- Submit a formal request for Wi-Fi upgrades in the Sociology Department lounge.
- Advocate for the establishment of a shared device pool within the department, drawing on best practices for resource management in academic settings ([1]).
- Adopt collaborative tools with offline capabilities, such as Google Docs’ offline mode or synchronized OneDrive folders, to protect against workflow interruptions ([4]).

### 5.2 Promoting Equitable Participation

- Adopt a rotating note-taker arrangement, ensuring responsibilities are shared and engagement is sustained across team members, aligning with equitable team structure guidelines ([2]).
- Guarantee universal access to meeting materials—both in digital and print form—before each session. Follow up by inviting asynchronous feedback for members who experience access constraints ([4]).

### 5.3 Enhancing Workflow and Continuity

- Introduce a hybrid approach to meetings, allowing remote participation when needed. This provides additional flexibility and helps mitigate disruptions caused by local technical issues ([4]).
- Continue to monitor which spaces provide stable digital infrastructure, and prioritize them for future sessions.
- Explore splitting group work between synchronous discussions and asynchronous document review, so progress continues outside of scheduled meetings.

### 5.4 Scheduling Next Steps

- **Option 1:** Secure a library seminar room for the next meeting, ensuring advance confirmation of available Wi-Fi and required devices.
- **Option 2:** Conduct a hybrid session—combining on-campus meeting with Zoom access for any participants with ongoing device or connection barriers.
- **Option 3:** Hold a short (20–30 minute) virtual check-in upon restoration of connectivity to collectively review and discuss the outstanding data visualizations.
