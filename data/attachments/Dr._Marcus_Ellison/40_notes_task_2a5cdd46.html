Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture

Overview

This itinerary outlines the full day of professional engagements for Dr. Marcus Ellison, Associate Professor of Sociology at Columbia University, at the Northeast Urban Studies Symposium. The schedule is designed not only to detail the logistics of the day but to highlight themes central to Dr. Ellison’s work: inclusivity, community engagement, mentorship, and the pursuit of open, meaningful scholarly dialogue. Across the day’s activities, emphasis is placed on fostering connections—among scholars, community members, and emerging researchers—and ensuring that each element of the day reflects the symposium’s mission to advance knowledge and community collaboration in urban studies.

Activity Highlights and Role-Specific Emphases

Early Preparation (8:00–8:50 AM)
The day begins deliberately and quietly. Arriving early, Dr. Ellison reviews slides, checks that all handouts are ready, and ensures technical needs are met. Time is set aside to check in with staff about special arrangements—especially dietary requirements for the later refreshments and accessibility in presentation spaces. This period sets the tone for a day centered on careful planning and inclusivity.

Keynote Presentation & Q&A (9:00–10:20 AM)
The main session, "Urban Belonging and the New Commons," positions Dr. Ellison at the intersection of scholarship and facilitation. The presentation draws on recent sociological studies, highlighting concrete strategies to nurture more inclusive, welcoming urban spaces. Intentional acknowledgment of mentee contributions illustrates a commitment to mentoring the next generation. In the Q&A, the use of live digital polling creates space for a variety of voices, and Dr. Ellison models respectful, engaged listening—thanking participants and capturing salient questions for follow-up.

Photography Exhibit Opening (10:30–11:15 AM)
Partnering with Maya Chen, Dr. Ellison opens "Urban Stories in Focus"—a photography exhibit that brings visual ethnography centerstage in discussions of urban identity. By guiding attendees through the exhibit, Dr. Ellison highlights the value of visual storytelling and its power to reveal overlooked aspects of urban life. These moments prompt broader conversations about representation, context, and the ethics of visual research.

Refreshments & Community Building (11:30 AM–12:15 PM)
Far from being a routine break, this period is dedicated to community building. With Maya Chen, Dr. Ellison ensures the refreshments comply with a range of dietary needs and reflect cultural diversity. He actively initiates table-mixer conversations, helping attendees connect across disciplines, institutions, and backgrounds, and gathers informal feedback on how hospitality practices can foster belonging.

Mentoring (12:30–1:30 PM)
Mentorship is central to Dr. Ellison’s work, and this session is carefully structured to provide both established and new mentees space for dialogue. Discussion topics typically include project feedback, publication strategies, and opportunities for collaboration. New mentees are welcomed and introduced, and time is reserved for sharing concrete resources. Dr. Ellison documents mentee goals, emphasizing accountability and ongoing support beyond the symposium.

Networking (1:40–2:20 PM)
In this session, Dr. Ellison purposefully connects mentees with senior colleagues and visiting researchers from different institutions, with particular attention to amplifying underrepresented voices. He facilitates introductions, encourages connections over shared interests, and—only with permission—organizes a commemorative group photo. Practical tools like QR codes and LinkedIn links are shared to make the networking both inclusive and effective.

Post-Event Discussion Circle (2:30–3:15 PM)
The day closes with a reflective group dialogue, bringing together attendees to synthesize what they have learned and to identify priorities for ongoing collaboration. Dr. Ellison moderates using facilitative tools—such as a talking piece and a set of agreed ground rules—to ensure everyone’s contributions are valued. Discussion notes and key action points are distributed after the session to maintain momentum for future work.

Special Notes and Reminders

  • Photography & Documentation: Ensure camera equipment is fully charged and permissions are obtained from participants for photos and recordings.
  • Handouts: Prepare and bring extra copies covering session content, mentoring information, and inclusivity resources.
  • AV Setup: Double-check all audio-visual equipment ahead of the main presentation and the discussion circle to avoid disruptions.
  • Accessibility & Inclusion: Stay proactive—regularly check for accessible seating, clear signage, and available language interpretation. Use inclusive language in all public comments.
  • Active Listening: Throughout the day, especially with mentees and in group discussions, make careful note of key ideas and actionable feedback.
  • Consent and Digital Engagement: Always obtain participants’ consent before photos or recording. Share event hashtags (#NEUrban24, #UrbanBelonging) to promote engagement.
