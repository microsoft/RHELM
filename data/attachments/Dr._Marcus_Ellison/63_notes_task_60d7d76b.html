Dinner Discussion Highlights

On the evening of November 16, Marcus and I (Maya) met for dinner to deepen our ongoing work in university outreach. Our conversation was lively and reflective, grounded in our shared goal of making engagement more responsive to both student voices and community needs. Over the course of the meal, we exchanged ideas on how to strengthen student agency, build more authentic partnerships between departments, and ensure our outreach aligns with both the university’s strategic priorities and the broader interests of the society it serves.

We drew inspiration from current research and best practices, discussing possibilities such as hosting storytelling evenings, organizing interdisciplinary panels, and launching a “Sociology Spotlight” series on social media to highlight classroom work and research. Throughout our dialogue, we both kept returning to key values: equity, cultural responsiveness, and mutual learning between the university and its larger community. We recognized the importance of ongoing reflection and feedback, aiming to keep our outreach adaptive, inclusive, and relevant. Our conversation reaffirmed our commitment to integrating these principles into every stage of our planning and execution, ensuring our efforts genuinely reflect the vision of engaged scholarship that our university upholds.

Transition and Rationale

Each planned initiative is designed not only to meet concrete outreach goals, but also to foster environments where learning flows both ways—between students and community partners. By structuring events and programming to encourage active participation, we hope to ensure these actions remain dynamic and relevant. Interdisciplinary collaboration remains central in our outreach, as it helps us to draw on diverse expertise and perspectives, enriching the university’s overall public impact.

Additional Ideas and Opportunities

During our dinner, several new possibilities for extending outreach naturally surfaced.

  • Guest Speaker Series: We see real value in hosting local activists and alumni, especially those working in the nonprofit and advocacy sectors. Their real-world perspectives can inspire students, spark career interests, and offer mentorship opportunities.
  • Interdepartmental Outreach Micro-Grants: Seeding small projects that bring together, for example, Sociology and Environmental Studies, could encourage faculty and students to co-create innovative outreach models. We intend to formally propose a pilot round for the spring semester.
  • Virtual Community Forum: To increase accessibility, we plan to pilot an open-access Q&A session where any community member can present pressing questions or topics for the university to address.
  • “Roots & Routes” Podcast: Inspired by lived experiences in our campus neighborhood, we would like to involve students in collecting and sharing oral histories, exploring the dynamics of migration, belonging, and community identity.
  • Research-Practice Partnerships: We are making initial contacts with local schools to design participatory action research projects for undergraduates. These would bridge classroom learning with tangible social impact and mutual benefit.

Continuous Reflection & Feedback

Reflective Questions

To keep ourselves accountable, we pose these guiding questions at every stage:

  • Are our activities accessible to all students and community members, regardless of background?
  • In what concrete ways do we foreground issues of race, class, gender, and ability both in planning and delivery?
  • Are our feedback processes straightforward, inclusive, and genuinely responsive?
  • Have we embedded reflexivity, fostering honest self-assessment from initial design through post-event review?
  • Do our initiatives strengthen the university's commitment to public scholarship and engagement?

Feedback Mechanisms

To ensure our programming remains grounded and relevant, we have implemented the following mechanisms:

  • Anonymous post-event surveys available in both digital and paper formats
  • Structured debriefs after events, inviting both student facilitators and community partners to reflect on their experiences
  • Ongoing open email channel dedicated to suggestions, concerns, and informal feedback
  • Biweekly outreach team meetings focused on reviewing recent feedback and refining upcoming programming

Incorporating Community Input

We aim to actively involve community partners in both the design and evaluation of outreach. We invite community liaisons into event planning, adapt timing and format in direct response to survey and focus group input, and consistently share back a summary of what was learned—including substantive changes we’ve made as a result.

Alignment with Departmental and University Goals

All initiatives outlined in our schedule are firmly rooted in the university’s mission of fostering engaged citizenship, interdisciplinary learning, and a deep sense of social responsibility. By centering inclusive programming and providing students with leadership opportunities, our outreach directly advances the university’s strategic objectives: broadening public impact, promoting diversity, equity, and inclusion, and reinforcing a culture of continuous reflection and improvement.

We are guided by foundational scholarship and established frameworks for community engagement [1-5]. The process Marcus and I have developed reflects specifically the principle that engagement with communities must be reciprocal and mutually beneficial, shaped by ongoing, honest reflection. In this way, the spirit of outreach is woven into the fabric of our teaching, research, and service.
