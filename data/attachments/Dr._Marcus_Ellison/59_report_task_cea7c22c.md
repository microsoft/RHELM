## Discussion Summary

### Photographic Techniques

The group conducted a detailed critique of the latest photo submissions. Maya’s series stood out for its use of natural light and careful composition, prompting discussion on how lighting can elevate narrative intent. Priya’s portraits sparked debate about framing and implied context, especially through the lens of Barthes’ punctum and studium. David shared an experimental set using low light, inviting feedback on balancing technical skill with expressive risk-taking.

Dr. Ellison highlighted the importance of sociocultural context, urging mentees to interrogate the subjects and underlying stories within their frames. Although connectivity issues limited Luis’s full participation, his submitted images were reviewed by the group, with feedback to be compiled and shared with him asynchronously.

### Collaborative Zine Project

Progress updates revealed that most mentees were on track with their assigned curation and editorial tasks. Maya and Priya had begun assembling initial page layouts, focusing on visual flow and thematic links. David took active notes on the evolving editorial workflow.

A scheduled discussion around the final zine theme was only partially completed due to technical interruptions. To ensure that all perspectives are captured, the group agreed to continue this discussion asynchronously via the collaborative document. Dr. Ellison suggested extending the zine draft deadline by one week to compensate for lost collaboration time and to guarantee full participation.

### Peer Feedback Structures

The group recognized the need for more consistent and equitable feedback mechanisms, particularly in light of ongoing connectivity concerns. Maya proposed establishing a shared Google Doc for delivering structured, written peer critiques, accessible to all mentees regardless of connection problems. Dr. Grant introduced a feedback rubric to standardize assessments, with an emphasis on clarity, specificity, and respect.

The group agreed to pilot this approach, allowing for both synchronous and asynchronous feedback and creating more flexibility in participation.

### Effects of Technical Issues

The session was compressed, and critiques were necessarily briefer than intended to accommodate repeated technical delays. Absent or interrupted mentees were invited to submit comments and photo analyses after the session, ensuring their input remains an integral part of the process.

As a result, the group reemphasized the importance of developing contingency plans—such as backup communication channels, flexible deadlines, and alternative modes of contribution—in order to sustain momentum and foster inclusion.

### Implications for the Zine Project

Delays and reduced input risk undermining both thematic cohesion and group engagement. To address this, the group agreed to:

- Prioritize troubleshooting and offer support to mentees facing persistent technical challenges.
- Emphasize regular, supplemental engagement (e.g., through written feedback, smaller group check-ins) to maintain progress and ensure equity.

All participants supported these measures, recognizing the collective responsibility to uphold artistic and academic standards while adapting to the realities of remote collaboration.

## Reflective Summary (from Dr. Ellison)

Despite encountering significant technical disruptions, I was impressed by the resilience and adaptability demonstrated by the group. Those able to remain connected stayed focused and contributed thoughtfully, with effective adjustments to session structure as challenges surfaced. Janelle’s complete absence and Luis’s intermittent contributions highlighted the fragility of virtual-only engagement, underscoring our ongoing need for contingency planning.

Key lessons from this session include:

- The necessity of providing multiple avenues for input—including asynchronous written reflections—for those affected by technical problems.
- The value of establishing clear troubleshooting procedures and timely communication when issues arise.
- The importance of sustaining group momentum and cohesion through immediate follow-up, transparent action tracking, and consistent documentation.

Moving forward, I recommend the group continue exploring flexible feedback methods, maintain a vigilant approach to technical troubleshooting, and uphold accountability through clearly assigned action items. As we approach the zine project’s completion, we must ensure every participant’s voice is included, and equity is prioritized, despite logistical obstacles. A hybrid approach—combining live discussion, asynchronous critique, and structured feedback tools—will best support our collective goals. I am confident that, with these adjustments, we will restore momentum and maintain both the integrity and the creative potential of our collaborative work.
