## Overview

## 4. Weekly Reflections: Wellness and Sociological Insights

### Week of July 1–7

**Mood & Stress**

Starting each day with a jog has a remarkable effect on my mood, even on the mornings when the pollen count is high and my allergies are flaring. After a run, I usually feel clearer, lighter, and more resilient heading into my teaching and meetings. The yoga sessions with Maya add another layer of calm and emotional balance, helping me recover more quickly from inevitable daily stressors.

**Managing Stress**

Exercise remains my anchor for stress management. On days packed with academic commitments or back-to-back meetings, I rely on morning movement to stay centered. Yoga has taught me to bring elements of breathwork and mindful awareness with me into the rest of my day—like pausing for a few deep breaths before stepping into difficult conversations with colleagues or when navigating crowded campus spaces.

**Social Connections**

This week, I joined two impromptu group faculty walks after our runs—these short, unplanned strolls became an easy space for informal connection and, surprisingly, some of our best professional brainstorming. I also appreciated post-yoga conversations with Maya, who brings a thoughtful perspective on how diverse cultural narratives shape our understanding of health, embodiment, and the use of public urban spaces.

**Sociological Observations**

My routines continue to confirm the value of accessible green spaces in sustaining urban wellness. Central Park, in all its summer energy, is not just my exercise venue—it’s a gathering point for people from every background, and observing this variety always sharpens my thinking about inequalities in health and opportunity. Managing allergies this week also reminded me how environmental determinants like high pollen and air quality hit different groups unevenly, often reflecting and reinforcing broader social disparities.

As I track my health, I’m more attuned to the push and pull between my academic responsibilities and my self-care. Balancing research, teaching, and personal routines sometimes feels like a sociological case study in role conflict—but it’s also a reminder that these negotiations are central to both personal health and the broader social dynamics I study.

**Integration**

Documenting these metrics and reflections puts my own experience into context, illustrating how everyday wellness is woven through with social, cultural, and environmental factors. I hope these ongoing notes will inform future research, both for myself and for broader discussions about wellness practices among urban professionals.
