## Breakfast as Connection: A Personal and Cultural Ritual

For me, breakfast is never just about food. It’s the gentle beginning to a new day, a slow return to the world, and—perhaps most importantly—a chance to share space and intention with someone I care about. On this particular morning, as Marcus and I planned our meal, I found myself reflecting on the deeper meaning of these early hours together.

Research consistently highlights what I’ve come to feel intuitively: gathering for a morning meal builds real emotional well-being, strengthens relationships, and helps create a sense of belonging (see Fiese et al., 2012; Grignon, 2001). Sitting down with Marcus at breakfast isn’t just routine; it’s a small, nourishing ceremony—one that gives both of us a moment to be present, linger over conversation, and ground ourselves before the day accelerates.

With that spirit in mind, I assembled a meal inspired by tradition and self-care, blending influences from Jewish-American and Scandinavian cuisines (the smoked salmon), staples from childhood (eggs and hearty bread), and bursts of color and nutrition from seasonal produce. I made a conscious effort to choose ingredients that are responsibly sourced and ethically produced whenever possible, hoping that our choices, however small, reflect our values.

## How I Prepared Our Breakfast

### 1. Getting Ready: The Mise en Place

First, I washed everything: asparagus, tomatoes, berries, lemon, and the fresh dill. The scent of lemon always wakes me up—something simple but satisfying. Bread and avocado sliced, cherry tomatoes halved. I cracked the eggs into a bowl, everything lined up and ready to go. Meanwhile, the coffee was starting to brew—just grinding those beans releases ten hidden mornings into the kitchen air.

### 2. Cooking the Eggs

Marcus loves poached eggs, while I sometimes lean toward soft scrambled. Today felt like a poaching day:  
- A pot of water, a splash of vinegar, quiet rising bubbles.  
- I swirled the water gently and slid in each egg, trying not to break the yolk—a small act of patience.  
- Three minutes later, each egg had a tender set white and a perfectly runny center.

(There’s something universally comforting about eggs for breakfast; the ritual feels ancient, familiar.)

### 3. Sautéing the Vegetables
  
A drizzle of olive oil warmed in the skillet, then in went the asparagus spears. They sizzled and brightened over a few minutes, then I tossed in the cherry tomatoes, letting them soften and release a little of their juice. Seasoned with a pinch of sea salt and coarse-cracked black pepper, the colors alone felt like another kind of sunrise.

### 4. Assembling the Plates
  
I toasted the rye so it was just crisp at the edges, then layered on slices of creamy avocado, topped each with a gently poached egg, laid a few stems of asparagus and some warm, bright tomatoes beside the smoked salmon, and finished with clouds of fresh dill. Lemon squeezed across it all, more olive oil, a little pepper to finish.

### 5. Sharing the Meal
  
Fresh berries went into small bowls—some tart, some sweet. Pot of coffee at the table, steam swirling above our mugs. We took our time eating, talking about the day ahead and whatever else wanted to surface. I was reminded once again how much brighter even an ordinary day feels with a shared meal to anchor it.

## Reflections: What This Breakfast Means to Me

Sharing breakfast with Marcus, I feel grounded. Social science backs this up, but it’s the lived experience that matters most to me. Regularly beginning the day together not only keeps our lives in rhythm—it’s also a subtle act of care, an invitation to pause and check in before we both scatter into our daily routines.

Fiese et al. (2012) remind me that sharing meals can improve the ways we communicate and regulate our emotions, even influencing our long-term dietary habits for the better. Grignon (2001) digs deeper, exploring how rituals—like “breaking bread” in the morning—can restore a sense of normalcy in the constantly shifting tempo of modern life. Warde (2016) has written about how food rituals, no matter how simple, weave meaning through the fabric of ordinary days.

Intentional breakfasts—even unpolished ones—help me feel connected: to Marcus, to our city, to the seasons unfolding in the markets. The act of choosing food with care, preparing it alongside someone, and sharing it at the table shapes our sense of hope and continuity.

Today’s meal was simple, beautiful, and enough. I finished breakfast feeling not just nourished, but re-centered and a little more optimistic about whatever the day would bring.
