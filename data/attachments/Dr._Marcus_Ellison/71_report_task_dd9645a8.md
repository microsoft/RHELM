## 1. Overview of Data Management Activities

This log documents the comprehensive process of research data backup and archiving for the Harlem Social Spaces Photographic Study conducted on November 28, 2024. The research data for this session includes digital photographic images from fieldwork across several Harlem neighborhoods, along with detailed field notes, interview summaries, and contextual observations.

### Data Backup Procedures

After completing fieldwork, I transferred all newly collected data—including photo batches and digital notes—from the field devices (a DSLR camera and an encrypted mobile device) to my secure, password-protected university-issued laptop. Before initiating backup, I performed a SHA-256 hash check on each original file to ensure complete and uncorrupted transfers.

To safeguard against data loss, I created three redundant backups:
- **Primary Backup:** Uploaded all files to my dedicated, encrypted folder on Columbia University’s secure cloud storage platform.
- **Secondary Backup:** Stored an encrypted copy on an external hard drive, which I keep in my faculty office lockbox.
- **Tertiary Backup:** Copied all files to Columbia’s secure institutional network drive, with access restricted to authorized departmental staff.

This multilayered approach aims to minimize any risk of data loss and ensures that, even in the event of technical failure or device loss, the research data remains protected and recoverable.

### Data Integrity Protocols

To maintain data authenticity and traceability, I validated all transferred files with checksum verifications. Metadata—such as original timestamps and, where available, GPS tags—was preserved throughout every step of transfer and backup. I regularly maintain detailed logs to record file versions, transfer events, and access history for audit purposes.

### Privacy, Security, and Ethical Considerations

Data privacy and participant confidentiality remain central priorities, particularly given the sensitivity of studying urban, marginalized communities. Whenever images, audio, or notes included identifiable information (such as faces or specific location markers), I flagged them for special handling according to Columbia University’s IRB and department requirements. All sensitive files are encrypted during storage and transfer. Only authorized research team members have access to them, and all cloud and network permissions are reviewed regularly to prevent unauthorized usage.

To comply with IRB protocols for human subjects research, I make sure to remove personally identifying metadata from any materials prepared for public-facing outputs. Images or notes that might increase participant risk—such as those capturing community activism or vulnerable individuals—are coded for heightened protection, and their access is even more tightly controlled.

Throughout the project, I remain actively engaged with Columbia’s evolving policies, IRB guidance, and ongoing discussions around ethical research in urban and marginalized settings. Where ethical dilemmas arise in the field, I log and review them in consultation with colleagues to ensure that the study upholds both research integrity and a commitment to community trust and safety.

## 3. Challenges, Solutions, and Reflections

### Technical Issues

- **Wi-Fi Instability:**  
  I encountered unreliable Wi-Fi in the field, which prevented immediate uploads to the cloud. To avoid any risk of data loss, I saved all files to locally encrypted storage until I was able to securely transfer everything from my office network later in the day.

- **Device Battery Failure:**  
  The DSLR camera battery unexpectedly failed in the afternoon, so I reverted to using my encrypted mobile device for photography and note-taking. As soon as I was back in the office, I transferred these files according to protocol to maintain the established chain of custody.

### Logistical and Ethical Considerations

- **Crowded Urban Environments:**  
  Navigating busy streets, I recorded images that captured sensitive activities such as community protests. I flagged these files for further ethical review, recognizing the increased risk their publication might have for participants.

- **Managing Identifiable Information:**  
  Images featuring recognizable individuals, particularly minors or local leaders, were classified as high sensitivity. I ensured these materials were stored only in the most secure, restricted-access directories and reviewed their usage carefully with the IRB guidance in mind.

- **Verbal Consent Documentation:**  
  During several spontaneous street interviews, I was not able to obtain signed consent forms. In these cases, I documented detailed records of verbal consent, emphasizing participants’ right to withdraw and adhering to best practices recommended by Columbia for dynamic field settings.

### Process Improvements and Training

- **Enhanced Field Protocols:**  
  Given the technical challenges, I have now implemented a standard of always carrying redundant, encrypted field devices to mitigate the risks of network outages or equipment failure.

- **Team Training:**  
  Ongoing training ensures all research assistants are proficient in the latest data transfer, encryption, and anonymization protocols, helping minimize risks to participants and maintain research standards.

- **Ethical Oversight:**  
  I now schedule immediate post-field reviews to flag high-sensitivity materials for special handling. I am also working to institutionalize scheduled compliance audits, staying abreast of any updates from IRB or community advisory feedback.
