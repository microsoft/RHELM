## Exhibit Overview

### Purpose

The upcoming photographic exhibit is dedicated to shedding light on the daily experiences and unyielding resilience within Harlem’s communities. By focusing on the intersecting themes of social inequality and collective strength, the collection aims to foster public dialogue about the enduring challenges faced in urban environments. The exhibit also celebrates the resourcefulness and solidarity that residents demonstrate in meeting adversity head-on.

### Theme

- **Primary Focus:** Social inequality and community resilience within Harlem  
- **Complementary Themes:**  
  - Urban renewal and the impact of displacement  
  - Preservation and celebration of cultural heritage during times of hardship  
  - Grassroots activism and the power of collective action

Harlem has long been a focal point for discussions about inequality and renewal. This exhibit uses photography not just to document these realities but to explore how individuals and groups navigate change, find hope, and build shared futures.

### Social Relevance

This exhibit brings attention to disparities in housing, education, and health that shape daily life in Harlem. By documenting real stories, including those of community organizations and residents working together, the project counters simplistic or stereotypical portrayals often found in mainstream media. Emphasizing both challenges and communal solutions, the photographs are intended to spark empathy, inspire meaningful conversations, and encourage movement toward more just and inclusive urban policies. The exhibit aims to serve as both a mirror and a catalyst—reflecting lived realities while supporting advocacy and informed action.

### Intended Audience

The exhibit is designed for a broad, yet deeply engaged audience, including:
- Harlem residents, community organizations, and local leaders
- Activists and policymakers focused on urban development and social equity
- Scholars and students in urban studies, sociology, and visual arts
- Members of the wider New York City public who are interested in the multifaceted story of Harlem

### Community Engagement Aims

A central goal of the project is to ensure that the community is at the heart of both development and presentation. Engagement initiatives include:
- Organizing participatory events and storytelling sessions to invite community voices into the exhibit narrative
- Building partnerships with local schools, youth centers, and advocacy organizations
- Facilitating open forums and roundtables that encourage dialogue among residents, academics, and local officials
- Designing the exhibit as a springboard for educational programs and public debates centered on urban justice

## Image Selection Process

### Selection Criteria

To ensure a collection that is both representative and respectful, the curatorial team applied the following standards:
- **Social Relevance:** All images were chosen for their clear depiction of social inequality (such as disparities in housing, education, or income) or examples of community-led resilience.
- **Authentic Representation:** Selected photographs capture genuine moments and candid scenes, avoiding staged or artificial setups. Community collaboration was prioritized, especially in images depicting sensitive contexts.
- **Diversity and Inclusion:** The collection intentionally reflects Harlem’s diversity, spanning different ages, genders, and ethnic backgrounds. Both public and more intimate moments are included to show the breadth of community life.
- **Ethical Responsibility:** Written consent was obtained from all individuals clearly identifiable in the photographs, particularly minors and participants in vulnerable circumstances. Special care was taken to avoid images that might stigmatize or expose at-risk individuals.
- **Technical Quality:** Only images meeting the necessary standards for print—minimum resolution and clarity—were selected. Any photograph with unrecoverable technical issues or damage was excluded.

### Data Recovery and Digital Safeguards

Given the importance of safeguarding both content and context, the following measures were taken:
- All images were recovered from storage devices using certified data-recovery software, with each step carefully documented to ensure integrity.
- Metadata—including dates, locations, and other contextual information—was preserved to maintain provenance.
- A transparent log of all recovery, verification, and curation procedures is maintained for archival purposes.

### Ethical Curation

Recognizing the sensitivity of some subject matter, image curation involved:
- Consultation with a review board comprising local leaders, scholars with expertise in Harlem’s history, and experienced exhibition staff.
- Strict adherence to community guidelines for images relating to trauma, disadvantage, or personal privacy. Face blurring and anonymization methods were used where needed.
- Every phase of selection included opportunities for community feedback, ensuring that the exhibition represents Harlem’s reality as determined by those who live it.

### Final Selection Procedures

- The curatorial team conducted an initial pre-selection based on relevance and quality.
- Selected images were then presented in focus groups with community members for discussion and feedback.
- A panel that included academic and historical experts verified the authenticity and context of the final set.
- The final vote on the exhibition lineup rested with a joint group of community representatives and partner institutions.

## Technical Notes

### Camera Equipment

All images were captured using professional-grade cameras (Canon EOS R6, Nikon D750) and a combination of high-quality lenses, including 24-70mm f/2.8 and 50mm f/1.4 models. Shooting in RAW format enabled maximum flexibility in color correction and post-processing, ensuring both detail and authenticity.

### Digital Recovery and Verification

To address issues with damaged storage media:
- Stellar Photo Recovery v11 software was used to restore images from SD cards.
- Throughout recovery, all original metadata (timestamps, geotags) was retained.
- Post-recovery, checksums and meticulous visual reviews verified file integrity.
- Any files with irreparable corruption were set aside, maintaining only the highest-quality images in the final selection.

### Archival and Data Security

- Each photograph is archived securely in institutional cloud storage and duplicated on encrypted offline drives, with backups kept at two separate secure locations.
- Every image is catalogued with a unique identifier and extensive metadata, streamlining future curation and ensuring easy retrieval for research or exhibition purposes.

### Print Preparation Standards

- All prints will be produced at a minimum of 300 dpi to guarantee visual clarity at their intended display sizes.
- Archival-quality, acid-free photographic paper has been selected for longevity.
- The entire workflow, from editing to printing, uses color calibration processes to deliver consistent, accurate representation across all exhibit pieces.

## Next Steps

### Exhibit Timeline

- **November 2024:** Install the exhibit at the Harlem Community Center, with a public opening event inviting residents, project contributors, and stakeholders.
- **December 2024 – March 2025:** Host a traveling version of the exhibit at local schools, libraries, and partner institutions to broaden access and deepen engagement.
- **Ongoing:** Document exhibit outcomes, evaluate impact, and continue programming around urban justice themes.

### Collaborative Partnerships

The exhibit’s success depends on robust local and academic partnerships, including:
- Academic support from centers such as Columbia University’s Center for Justice and the City College of New York’s Department of Urban Studies
- Formalized collaboration with Harlem-based organizations, including youth centers, neighborhood associations, and cultural societies
- An advisory group that brings together local advocates, historians, educators, and exhibit staff to guide ongoing curation and programming

### Educational and Outreach Initiatives

To deepen community involvement and educational value, planned initiatives include:
- Guided exhibit tours and public panel discussions featuring photographers, community members, and scholars who participated in the project
- Curriculum modules for both K-12 and university classrooms, focusing on documentary photography’s role in social analysis and urban history
- Inviting additional oral histories, creative works, and artifacts from local residents to enrich and diversify the exhibit’s storytelling

### Impact Evaluation

The project is committed to ongoing assessment and reflection, using:
- Qualitative feedback gathered through attendee surveys, focus groups, and interviews with participants
- Quantitative measures of outreach, such as event attendance, classroom engagement, and active collaborations
- Pre- and post-exhibit focus groups to gauge shifts in public understanding and community discourse
- Publishing a transparent, accessible impact report to document successes and identify areas for further growth
