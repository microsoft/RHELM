1. Agenda Overview

The mid-project review meeting centered on a comprehensive evaluation of progress to date, focusing on methodological rigor, ethical integrity, and collaborative planning for the remaining phases. The team prioritized open discussion, critical self-reflection, and forward-looking strategy to address emerging challenges and opportunities.

Agenda Highlights: - Clarification of meeting objectives and reaffirmation of research priorities - Presentation and evaluation of preliminary findings and research design - Update on data acquisition and discussion of dataset quality and credibility - Strategies for visualizing data ethically and accessibly - Open team dialogue with a focus on ethical considerations and continuous improvement - Assignment of action items and responsibilities - Planning for upcoming milestones and follow-up meeting logistics

2. Summary of Participant Contributions

2.1 Dr. Marcus Ellison — Facilitation & Ethical Oversight

Dr. Ellison opened the session by reiterating the team's commitment to ethical research practices and transparency, emphasizing both the immediate and broader societal implications of the study. He encouraged candid conversation around complex ethical issues, particularly in relation to sensitive aspects of the data and participant confidentiality. Drawing on current departmental policies and established research ethics frameworks, Dr. Ellison emphasized vigilance in matters of informed consent, responsible representation, and dissemination of findings. He facilitated a productive atmosphere in which all voices were heard and ensured that any unresolved issues were clearly identified for further consultation prior to the next project milestone.

2.2 Dr. Aisha Grant — Preliminary Findings & Methodological Review

Dr. Grant provided a comprehensive overview of the initial analyses, beginning with a detailed explanation of the study’s mixed-methods approach. The project combines a stratified survey—designed to capture a diverse range of perspectives, with particular attention to oversampling marginalized communities to examine social inequalities—with a series of semi-structured interviews. She highlighted a key pattern identified in the preliminary data: a clear correlation between housing instability and experiences of systemic discrimination. In presenting these findings, Dr. Grant examined potential sources of bias, noting disparities in response rates among demographic subgroups, which could affect data reliability. She underscored the broader implications of these early results, particularly as evidence of ongoing social stratification, and recommended focused qualitative follow-up with specific subgroups. Plans for continuous validity checks were outlined, including inter-coder reliability assessment and cross-validation using external benchmarks, to enhance the robustness of future findings.

2.3 Dr. Priya Nair — Data Acquisition & Dataset Evaluation

Dr. Nair reported on the recent acquisition of a new eviction dataset through collaboration with a national housing non-profit. She assessed the dataset’s reliability by reviewing its documented collection methods and comparing it with municipal records to verify consistency. Variables of particular analytical value were identified, such as duration of tenancy, stated cause of eviction, and key demographic indicators. Dr. Nair noted the dataset’s strengths for both cross-sectional insights and potential longitudinal examination, pending sustained data access in upcoming phases. She also flagged several documented gaps in coverage—specifically within certain metropolitan areas—and proposed data imputation approaches to address missing information while ensuring findings remain transparent and accurately contextualized. To enable integration of additional municipal data, Dr. Nair recommended extending the project timeline to secure necessary institutional permissions. She emphasized that finalizing a data-sharing agreement by the end of the month would expand possibilities for longitudinal analysis significantly.

2.4 Jamal Rivers — Data Visualization & Ethical Communication

Jamal Rivers presented draft visualizations, including accessible infographics that illustrate disparities across demographic groups and an interactive map prototype displaying eviction trends. He spoke to the underlying principles guiding these materials: clarity, accessibility, and ethical responsibility. Jamal highlighted several design decisions made to avoid reinforcing stereotypes or misrepresenting groups through visual emphasis. He underscored best practices for accessibility, including color contrast optimization and ensuring compatibility with screen readers to maximize stakeholder engagement and usability. Jamal requested input from the team on proposed revisions, especially with the goal of enhancing communication with both community partners and policy audiences. He also suggested developing a set of formal guidelines for creating and presenting sensitive sociological visualizations—a proposal the team agreed to review in the next meeting.

4. Next Steps & Preparations for Follow-Up Meeting

The team will continue analyzing both quantitative and qualitative data, placing special emphasis on validating results through transparent and replicable processes. Integration of the new eviction dataset is a priority, contingent on the successful completion of data imputation and the receipt of necessary permissions. All data visualizations will be revised according to accessibility and ethical standards established during the meeting, to ensure effective and responsible communication of findings.

An external panel of experts will be convened to review the preliminary results, with their feedback informing the project’s final phase, including targeted qualitative follow-up and refinement of policy recommendations. In preparation for the May follow-up meeting, the team will circulate a detailed progress report and an updated ethics memo, reaffirming their shared commitment to methodological transparency, reflexivity, and accountability to the studied communities.

Moving forward, the team is unified in its dedication to: - Sustaining methodological transparency and critical reflexivity at every stage - Upholding data and visualization ethics, focusing on avoiding harm and ensuring clarity - Maintaining a collaborative, open environment for constructive dialogue - Documenting all processes thoroughly to allow for auditability and to foster public trust in the research
