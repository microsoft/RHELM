## 4. Key Discussion Points

### A. Muralist Selection and Confirmation

The search for a lead muralist for the winter exhibit is nearing its conclusion, with final confirmation expected by October 29, 2024. Shortlisted candidates were selected based on their prior experience in community-driven public art projects, particularly emphasizing representation from Black and Afro-Latinx artists rooted in the Harlem area. Artists who demonstrate a deep commitment to inclusive, participatory processes and have established ties to Harlem’s creative communities emerged as strong contenders.

To ensure equity, the selection committee is weighing each candidate’s record on collaborative practice, fair working agreements, and support for emerging local talent. Promoting authentic community voices and advancing opportunities for historically underrepresented artists remains a central criterion in the selection process.

### B. Youth Workshop Development and Scheduling

The workshops connected to the winter exhibit are designed to foster meaningful creative engagement among Harlem youth, particularly those aged 10 to 18. The planning team outlined a framework for three to four weekend workshops, strategically distributed across accessible neighborhood venues.

Logistical planning prioritized equitable access, targeting youth from a wide range of schools and community centers, with a focus on public housing residents and students from lower-income backgrounds. Scheduling will be responsive to school calendars and community religious observances to maximize participation.

To strengthen community connections and foster inclusivity, the team will partner closely with local organizations, faith groups, and schools. Facilitators leading these workshops will receive dedicated training on equity, cultural responsiveness, and best practices for cultivating inclusive creative spaces.

### C. Social Equity and Community Engagement

Central to both exhibit planning and workshop scheduling is a commitment to social equity and genuine community engagement. All programming is intended to promote participatory, community-led narratives reflecting Harlem’s diversity. The team explicitly set goals to honor Harlem’s cultural heritage and ensure all youth feel seen and celebrated within the exhibit.

Discussions also addressed the need for a culturally responsive curriculum to prevent any potential for exclusion or misrepresentation. Regular evaluation mechanisms, including feedback from youth and caregivers, will help the planning team identify and swiftly resolve any barriers to access or participation.

## 7. Ethical Commitments and Anticipated Community Impact

### Ethical Foundations

The Harlem Winter Exhibit and associated programming are grounded in a strong commitment to cultural responsiveness and equity. The planning team will implement regular participant and caregiver feedback channels to address any emerging issues or barriers swiftly and transparently. Each workshop’s content will undergo review to ensure it affirms diverse identities, combats stereotypes, and provides space for positive self-expression.

### Anticipated Outcomes

By prioritizing accessible, high-quality arts education, the initiative aims to reach Harlem youth who have historically been underrepresented in arts programming. The program is designed to build a sense of pride and community ownership, amplify the voices of Harlem’s established and emerging artists, and foster cross-generational connections through public art. The overarching goal is to create a lasting, positive impact on participants, with the hope of strengthening ongoing community engagement around the arts.

---

## 8. Stakeholder Engagement and Follow-Up

To maintain transparency and maximize community buy-in, the planning team will distribute the finalized workshop schedule and venue details to local schools, community centers, and faith-based organizations by mid-November. A community forum including parents, educators, and neighborhood leaders is planned for late November to gather further feedback and ensure programming meets local needs.

In addition, a comprehensive “Codes of Practice” document summarizing equity and inclusivity guidelines will be prepared and shared with all facilitators and participating organizations. After the exhibit, a public forum will be held to review program successes, collect community recommendations, and inform continued improvements to Harlem Arts Foundation events.
