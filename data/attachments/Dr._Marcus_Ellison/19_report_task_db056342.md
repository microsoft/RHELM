## Key Discussion Points

### 1. Article Revision and Integration

Dr. Ellison and Dr. Grant emphasized the importance of combining their methodological strengths to create a robust, multidimensional analysis. Dr. Ellison provided a quantitative foundation by analyzing urban housing data—including displacement rates and affordability indices—while Dr. Grant contributed qualitative depth through detailed interviews with residents affected by housing inequalities in Harlem and the Bronx.

By triangulating empirical data with firsthand accounts, they enhanced the paper’s credibility, bringing greater insight to the complexities of urban housing inequality. This approach allowed the results to reflect both macro-level trends and the lived experiences shaping local narratives.

They systematically restructured the mixed-methods results section. Statistical findings and resident narratives are now clearly differentiated, ensuring readers can distinguish between empirical trends and personal stories, while also highlighting points where the two perspectives converge or diverge—such as quantitative indicators of affordability levels versus community members’ perceptions of stability and security.

The policy implications section underwent a significant reframing. Rather than providing generalized critiques, the revised section focuses on specific, actionable recommendations that are both substantiated by evidence and grounded in community-identified needs. This shift is designed to make the article more impactful and relevant to practitioners and policymakers.

### 2. Integration of Quantitative and Qualitative Approaches

Dr. Ellison underscored the necessity of presenting quantitative data—such as census analyses and regression results—in an accessible manner that maintains analytical depth yet invites a broader readership. In parallel, Dr. Grant advocated for amplifying marginalized voices by weaving more resident interview excerpts through the manuscript, offering nuance and reinforcing the authenticity of the research.

The revised article now contains strategically placed sidebars that align quantitative indicators (for example, patterns in eviction rates) with residents’ personal narratives. This interwoven format ensures ethical considerations and social context are not sidelined, but instead, inform the analysis at each stage.

Rather than treating qualitative and quantitative findings as separate silos, the authors adopted a framework that deliberately interlaces empirical data and narrative insights throughout each analytical phase. This structure offers readers a richer, more cohesive understanding of urban housing challenges from multiple vantage points.

### 3. Editorial and Methodological Decisions

Revision decisions were finalized through a collaborative assessment process, with careful attention to clarity, analytical rigor, and ethical sensitivity. Each change was evaluated using core principles: honoring the voices of underrepresented participants, maintaining accuracy in the representation of numerical findings, and upholding recognized scholarly standards.

In anticipation of peer-review, methodological transparency was strengthened. Additional explanations now clarify sampling strategies and methodological limitations. The manuscript also explicitly outlines ethical safeguards, such as procedures for obtaining informed consent and rigorously anonymizing interview data in accordance with Institutional Review Board (IRB) policies.

## Strategic Planning for Community Engagement

### Harlem Youth Mentorship Workshop

Drs. Ellison and Grant reaffirmed a shared commitment to advancing community impact through a mentorship workshop aimed at Harlem youth. The program’s central goal is to bridge academic research with actionable knowledge, empowering young people who are directly influenced by urban housing policy.

The mentorship workshop will draw upon key findings from their co-authored article to develop curriculum modules, interactive case studies, and policy simulation exercises. The partnership intends to give participants hands-on experience with urban studies methodologies, facilitating both skill development and critical reflection.

#### Community Engagement Strategies

- **Partnership Development:** The team will reach out to local schools, tenant associations, and trusted community leaders to establish broad and inclusive collaboration.
- **Participatory Action Research (PAR):** Workshop participants will have opportunities to design and conduct mini-studies within their own neighborhoods. These projects will be supported by mentorship from Columbia University students and faculty, fostering a collaborative learning environment.
- **Accessible Outreach:** All communications and materials will be available in multiple languages. The workshop will offer both virtual and in-person sessions to maximize accessibility and accommodate diverse needs.

#### Anticipated Outcomes

- Young Harlem residents will gain agency and skills to advocate for themselves in local urban governance and neighborhood matters.
- The mentorship initiative will serve as a model for translating sociological research into practical benefits, reinforcing university-community reciprocity.
- The post-workshop phase will include the collection of qualitative feedback, which will guide future curriculum improvements and inform broader engagement strategies.

## Reflections on Ethics and Social Justice

Both collaborators reiterated their commitment to social equity and ethical responsibility throughout both academic and community work. The research emphasizes prioritizing the perspectives of marginalized groups and maintaining high ethical standards—especially regarding data privacy and informed participation.

All research activities and the upcoming mentorship initiative have been carefully reviewed to ensure robust IRB compliance. The consent and anonymization protocols are fully integrated, with flexible safeguards to adapt as the mentorship program evolves, particularly in handling youth-generated data and sustaining participant confidentiality.

They agreed to ongoing reflection and assessment, including post-publication reviews and continual dialogue with community partners, to address any unintended consequences and to strengthen their approach in future projects.
