## Activity Highlights and Reflections

### Morning Yoga & Mindfulness (7:15–8:15 AM)
Beginning my day among friends, I found that practicing restorative yoga together not only relaxed my body but also created a safe space for honest dialogue. Our discussion about mindfulness and social theory felt especially relevant—each shared silence and gesture reinforced how we shape, and are shaped by, the groups we’re in. Creating space for rotating leadership helped us tune in more deeply to each other’s emotional states and built a sense of mutual trust that lingered throughout the day.

### Academic Collaboration (9:30–10:45 AM)
Meeting Priya at the café was both productive and energizing. We not only mapped out our research tasks with clarity, but also pushed each other to refine our arguments for a wider audience. The discipline of scheduling concrete deadlines and divvying up the work left me feeling focused and optimistic about our project’s trajectory.

### Urban Fieldwork & Mentoring (1:30–3:30 PM)
The photography walk reminded me why I love teaching in the field. Observing how each mentee noticed different patterns—graffiti marking territorial boundaries, the shape of informal gatherings, new cafés nudging out long-time businesses—deepened everyone’s understanding of urban change. Guiding my mentees as they took the lead on parts of our discussion felt especially meaningful. Empowering them boosted their analytical confidence, and I found myself learning just as much from their observations as they did from my suggestions.

### Editing, Synthesis, and Reflection (5:30–7:00 PM)
Integrating field notes with personal reflection is where my research often comes alive. Today, I noticed how ideas from our yoga session and the photography walk began to intersect: the interplay of presence, observation, and the boundaries we draw (physically, socially, intellectually). Sorting my thoughts by theme revealed new connections to pursue in both upcoming scholarship and classroom discussion.

### Personal Connection & Analogies (8:00–8:30 PM)
Talking with Samuel always reminds me that the best insights often come from outside the discipline. His stories from jazz rehearsal—navigating the tension between structure and improvisation—sparked new ways to think about collaboration, creativity, and teaching. Translating those musical lessons into the context of sociological group work, I left our call with inspiration for my next seminar.
