## 3. New Interactive Workshop Segment: “Community Narratives—Interactive Reflection”

We collectively decided to develop an interactive segment for the workshop, titled “Community Narratives—Interactive Reflection.” This segment aims to bridge the gap between theory and practice, empowering mentees to share and explore community issues from their own perspectives.

**Structure of the Segment:**
- **Timing:** After the main presentation and before the closing summary
- **Activity:** Mentees break into small facilitated groups, where they—
  - Share personal or observed community stories
  - Respond to guided questions linking these stories to sociological ideas discussed earlier
  - Provide and receive live feedback among peers and facilitators
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Apply sociological frameworks to real experiences in Harlem
  - Nurture reflective practice and deeper community understanding
  - Foster a sense of agency in addressing community needs
- **Rationale:** 
  - This participatory model draws from constructivist pedagogy, which has demonstrated positive impacts on learner engagement and knowledge retention, especially when content is locally relevant.
  - By centering community voices, we move the workshop toward a collaborative, learner-focused model that aligns with principles of equity and empowerment.
  - This directly answers the mentees’ call for more interactive and personally meaningful engagement.

## 4. Strategies for Meaningful Community Engagement

Building effective mentorship in Harlem requires rooted, collaborative engagement. Drawing insights from Community-Based Participatory Research (CBPR) principles (Minkler & Wallerstein, 2019), we agreed on several strategies to ensure the mentorship program is grounded in equity, transparency, and mutual respect:

- **Active Community Partnerships:** Intentionally involve local leaders, residents, organizations, and an advisory panel in the co-design and delivery of the program, ensuring cultural resonance and community ownership.
- **Consistent Feedback Loops:** Establish ongoing channels for gathering both formal (surveys, interviews) and informal (open conversations, check-ins) feedback, enabling real-time program adjustment.
- **Linguistic and Cultural Accessibility:** Adapt all materials to reflect local language, lived experience, and cultural nuance. Whenever possible, use community members’ voices, stories, and images.
- **Transparency and Shared Ownership:** Maintain open communication about program goals and continuously invite input from all stakeholders, sharing decision-making authority to avoid top-down approaches.

These practices foster an environment of trust and shared accountability, ultimately deepening the impact of mentorship.

## 5. Insights from Collaboration with Celeste Broussard

Dr. Aisha Grant shared her collaborative experiences with Celeste Broussard, a respected community advocate and educator in Harlem. Their ongoing project has yielded several important lessons:

- **Building Trust Through Long-Term Engagement:** Establishing credibility and fostering participation takes consistent, multi-year effort. Sustained presence enables us to develop trust and support ongoing collaboration.
- **Community Advisory Board:** Bringing together a diverse advisory board—including youth, elders, and educators—ensures that the program truly reflects the unique perspectives of Harlem residents and avoids a one-size-fits-all approach.
- **Intergenerational Storytelling:** Actively integrating stories from multiple generations preserves local history, honors collective experience, and passes on lessons in civic agency and resilience.
- **Practical Outcomes:**  
  - Co-developing resources and co-facilitating sessions increases legitimacy and resonance in the eyes of participants.
  - Mentees and mentors respond positively to Harlem-specific narratives and visuals, often describing these elements as transformative for their engagement.
  - This collaborative and community-driven model has strong potential for replication in other urban neighborhoods facing similar challenges.
