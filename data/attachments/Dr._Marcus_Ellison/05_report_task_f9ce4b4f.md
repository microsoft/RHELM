# Incident Report and Analysis: Technical Disruptions during Dr. Marcus Ellison’s Keynote Address – ASA Annual Meeting, February 18, 2024

## Executive Summary

This report documents and analyzes the technical disruptions that occurred during Dr. Marcus Ellison’s keynote address at the American Sociological Association (ASA) annual meeting on February 18, 2024. The incident is contextualized within the broader challenges facing academic dissemination, particularly the reliance on technological infrastructure to support the clear and effective exchange of complex research on social inequality. These disruptions not only impeded the transmission of Dr. Ellison’s findings but also exposed persistent vulnerabilities in conference planning, resource accessibility, and contingency preparedness. The impact extended to audience engagement and equity concerns, underscoring the need for improved protocols and institutional learning to ensure reliable scholarly communication for all presenters.

## Description of Technical Issues

### Audiovisual System Failure
Upon Dr. Ellison’s arrival and setup, the ballroom’s AV system failed to connect with his presentation devices. The projector remained unresponsive to all input, while the control panel displayed persistent error messages. Audio components, including microphones and speakers, experienced intermittent outages, further complicating communication.

### USB Incompatibility
Attempts to load presentation slides from a USB flash drive encountered critical compatibility issues. The system did not recognize the drive’s exFAT format, preventing access to necessary files. The AV technician tested various ports and adapters, but the problem persisted across all attempts.

### Outdated Equipment and Software
The backup laptop provided by the venue operated on an outdated version of PowerPoint (2013) and lacked support for embedded audio, video, and certain formatting required by Dr. Ellison’s presentation. The MacBook Pro, which Dr. Ellison had specifically requested and confirmed in advance, was unavailable due to scheduling conflicts elsewhere at the conference.

### Systemic and Organizational Factors
Communication lapses between conference organizers and AV support led to confusion about contingency measures for technical failure. Asset management protocols fell short in ensuring equitable distribution of up-to-date technology among keynote speakers, highlighting wider issues of access and resource planning.

## Impact on the Presentation

### Adaptation and Loss of Narrative Continuity
Without access to visual materials, Dr. Ellison was compelled to deliver his address from memory and annotated notes. The absence of projected data disrupted the strategic pacing and multifaceted structure of the presentation, making transitions between theoretical perspectives and empirical findings less seamless. This constraint diminished narrative cohesion, particularly given the session’s reliance on integrating quantitative and qualitative evidence.

### Disrupted Data Communication
Critical graphs and charts demonstrating patterns of intersectional inequality could not be displayed, limiting the audience’s ability to follow statistical analysis and key arguments. Notably, qualitative elements—such as audio interviews and fieldwork photographs—embedded in the slides were omitted, reducing the empirical richness and emotional resonance of the session. The necessity of addressing technical malfunctions mid-session shortened Dr. Ellison’s planned content, forcing the exclusion of select analytic sections.

## Audience Response and Interaction

### Tension and Methodological Challenges in Q&A
During the question-and-answer segment, the absence of visual aids led to repeated inquiries about research methodology and analytic rigor. Without the customary direct access to supporting data visuals, audience members voiced concerns about interpretation and transparency. This dynamic underscored the essential role of multi-modal communication in academic presentations and prompted attendees to recommend improvements for technology management and backup protocols.

### Broader Reflection on Equity and Conference Preparedness
Beyond individual challenges, the incident sparked a broader, spontaneous dialogue among participants about systemic inequities in technological access at scholarly gatherings. Attendees questioned conference preparedness for those from institutions with fewer resources or those who need specific accessibility accommodations. The event illuminated the disproportionate burden technical failures place on underrepresented presenters and emphasized the imperative for intentional planning and structural inclusion.

## Recommendations for Future Presentations

### Ensuring Equity and Accessibility
Technological support at academic conferences is foundational to equitable scholarly exchange. All presenters should be guaranteed access to up-to-date, compatible equipment, regardless of institutional affiliation or individual resource levels.

### Comprehensive Planning and Clear Communication
Routine pre-event technical rehearsals are essential and should always involve presenters directly. Surfaces for requesting technical support and reporting on delivery must be clear, documented, and accountable. Prompt, accessible troubleshooting channels are necessary to address on-the-spot contingencies effectively.

### Robust Contingency Measures
A reserve pool of fully updated laptops for both operating systems, together with a full suite of adapters, should be maintained for all major sessions. Preset protocols to submit backup copies of presentations both on physical media and via secure cloud-based systems are critical risk mitigations. Offering backup access for attendees, such as QR codes linking to presentation files in the event of AV failure, enhances accessibility.

### Institutional Learning and Best Practices
Post-event technical debriefs and feedback loops should routinely inform procedural updates. Embedding principles of universal design and accessibility through every phase of conference organization strengthens resilience against future disruptions.

### Policy Development
Formal, transparent guidelines for equipment reservations must be established, with explicit attention to equitable allocation, particularly for presenters from underserved backgrounds or with accessibility needs. Major disruptions should be documented and incorporated as case studies in ongoing program evaluation and conference staff training to foster continuous organizational improvement.

## Contextualization in Academic Discourse

Technical difficulties during academic presentations, while sometimes inevitable, carry significant risks for research communication and professional inclusion. Relevant literature highlights that such breakdowns can disproportionately affect presenters deploying marginalized perspectives or innovative, media-rich methodologies. These incidents reveal systemic gaps in infrastructural support and reinforce the urgency of equity-centered planning, detailed advance preparation, and adaptive capacity in scholarly convenings.
