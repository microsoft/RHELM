## Discussion Summary

### 1. Group Critique of Luis Martinez’s Street Portraits

Luis Martinez presented his portfolio featuring ten recent street portraits captured across upper Manhattan. The group engaged in an in-depth critique, focusing on both the artistic qualities and the broader social context conveyed by the images.

**Key Points from the Critique:**

- **Artistic Technique**:  
    The portraits were praised for their strong composition and skillful use of natural light, which added depth and subtlety to each image. Participants noted Luis’s ability to capture candid, intimate moments while maintaining respect for his subjects’ dignity and privacy.

- **Ethical Considerations**:  
    The group discussed Luis’s approach to informed consent, acknowledging his practice of securing both written and verbal agreements from participants, as documented in his field notes. This proactive ethical stance was commended as a model for visual research.

- **Social Inequality Narratives**:  
    Priya Shah highlighted how the selected subjects and scenes brought attention to often-overlooked groups, encouraging more nuanced public conversation about urban marginalization. Jinsoo Kim observed that the images subtly but powerfully traced the contours of systemic issues, including housing insecurity, over-policing, and economic exclusion. Rebecca Tran reflected on the emotional tone of the portraits, considering their potential to influence public perceptions and empathy toward communities featured in the work.

- **Areas for Further Development**:  
    Feedback centered on strengthening the sociological impact of the project. Suggestions included incorporating more detailed, context-rich captions; conducting follow-up interviews with portrait subjects to invite their voices into the narrative; and involving community perspectives to deepen both rigor and authenticity. The group encouraged a participatory approach that amplifies lived experiences and co-creates meaning with community partners.

The critique drew on established methods for group feedback in visual arts, prioritizing constructive discussion and evidence-based suggestions [3].

**Agreed Action Items:**

- Luis will pilot a revised captioning approach that integrates direct input from interviewees and community dialogue.
- All mentees will share up-to-date literature on participatory visual research practices and ethics to inform next steps.

## Issues and Resolutions

### 1. Google Drive Sync Challenges

Mentees reported intermittent issues when syncing collaborative documents to Google Drive, leading to version confusion and lost edits, particularly when uploading large image files.

**Root Causes Identified:**

- Persistent network instability, magnified during simultaneous batch uploads.
- Permissions inconsistently set due to manual updates, limiting access and creating confusion.

**Immediate Solutions:**

- Marcus Ellison standardized folder permissions to "Editor" for all mentees, ensuring equal access and streamlined collaboration.
- Raw photo files are now housed in clearly labeled, date-specific subfolders to improve organization and retrieval.

**Ongoing Preventive Steps:**

- A weekly backup schedule is now in place, overseen by the program coordinator, with regular snapshots archived to institutional cloud storage.
- File uploads are limited to designated off-peak hours to alleviate network strain.
- All participants will attend an annual refresher on university digital security and collaborative tool best practices [1].

---

### 2. Recovery and Management of Notion Field Notes

During preparations, mentees discovered that field notes stored in Notion had been inadvertently overwritten, threatening the integrity of key qualitative data.

**Analysis:**

- Overwriting resulted from concurrent edits without real-time awareness of who else was updating the files.
- No systematic backup or export routine was in place.

**Corrective Measures:**

- Jinsoo Kim and Rebecca Tran used Notion’s “Version History” to restore earlier note versions, recovering at-risk data sets.
- Critical notes were exported as PDFs and archived in the secure university cloud environment.

**Long-Term Safeguards:**

- Real-time edit tracking and user presence indicators are now enabled in Notion to prevent accidental overlaps.
- Monthly exports and version checks will be managed by Rebecca Tran to ensure a reliable academic record and robust data protection.

All interventions are consistent with the university’s IT policies on academic data security and privacy [2].
