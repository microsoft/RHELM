## Analysis of Thematic Patterns and Sociological Relevance

A close reading of the feedback identifies four interrelated priority areas in the workshop design and delivery: content, facilitation, engagement, and inclusivity.

### Workshop Content

Both Tariq and Janelle emphasize the necessity of content that is both relevant and accessible. Tariq observes that redundant case studies dilute learning impact, recommending a stronger focus on locally rooted examples and participant narratives. Janelle raises concerns over complex language, suggesting that plain-language summaries and visual supports are needed to make material more approachable for participants with varying literacy backgrounds. These insights are consistent with place-based and inclusive pedagogical approaches, which hold that contextual familiarity and clarity are foundational for meaningful participant engagement [1,5].

### Facilitation

Feedback points to the need for a balance between facilitator direction and participatory learning. While clear instruction is valued, both contributors note that facilitation sometimes becomes too instructor-centered, limiting dialogic exchange. Incorporating techniques such as think-pair-share, group dialogue, and critical inquiry can help participants become active co-creators in the learning process. Training facilitators in participatory methods, as advocated in Freire’s pedagogy, is essential to shift the culture toward genuine dialogic engagement [2].

Janelle further notes inconsistencies in time management, advocating for standardization and visible agendas. Clear session structure, with pacing cues and time equity strategies, will improve session flow and participant comfort [6].

### Engagement

Both participants recognize that workshop engagement is highest at the outset, but wanes toward the end. Tariq attributes the decline to insufficient activity variety and unclear transitions between segments, while Janelle notes that some activities are not directly relevant to participants’ lived experiences. To address these concerns, it is important to design a diverse range of interactive tasks—such as role plays, group mapping, and storytelling—that are directly co-created with participants. Embedding participant voices in the design process, and utilizing methods from participatory action research, strengthens relevance and sustains energy throughout the workshop [3,7].

### Inclusivity

Equity stands out as a central concern, reflected in both group dynamics and physical accessibility. Tariq observes that some voices, particularly those from underrepresented backgrounds, are not consistently heard, while Janelle highlights physical barriers and inconsistent use of assistive technology. Creating diverse groupings, appointing inclusivity observers, ensuring structured turn-taking, and routinely auditing space and technology all advance equity. These practices align with established principles in Universal Design for Learning (UDL) and ADA-compliant education [3,4,8].

## Recommendations and Action Plan

Drawing on the feedback and educational literature, the following recommendations are proposed to strengthen the mentorship workshops:

- **Curriculum Enhancement:**  
  Update learning materials to reduce redundancy and incorporate region-specific, accessible scenarios. Materials should be co-developed with input from participants to increase relevance and relatability. Glossaries, visual aids, and plain language summaries should be embedded throughout all content [1,5].

- **Facilitator Development:**  
  Conduct targeted facilitator training focused on participatory, dialogic methods. Establish standardized session timings with clearly visible agendas and incorporate real-time pacing cues [2,6]. Facilitation guidelines should prioritize equitable voice participation and flexible session flow.

- **Activity Redesign:**  
  Build a broader range of interactive activities—role-plays, asset mapping, storytelling—co-designed alongside participants to reflect the diversity of their experiences. Offer multimodal engagement options to cater to different learning preferences, ensuring activities stay relevant and stimulating [3,7].

- **Strengthen Inclusivity:**  
  Regularly conduct accessibility audits of physical spaces and technology, standardize the use of assistive devices, and introduce structured protocols for equitable participation (such as designated inclusivity observers and diverse groupings). Continuous participant feedback mechanisms should inform ongoing improvement [3,4,8].

## Emergent Themes

### Content and Context

Locally relevant and clearly presented material is essential for fostering engagement and making workshops meaningful. Adapting case studies to the realities of the participant community, and ensuring language accessibility, enable a more personal connection to the content. These strategies are supported by situated learning theory, which emphasizes learning as a social process situated within a particular context.

### Facilitation Approach

Empowering participants as co-creators in the learning process leads to deeper engagement and ownership. Facilitators who use dialogic and participatory methods not only transmit information but help build a collaborative learning culture, in line with Freirean and Lave & Wenger's pedagogical theories.

### Sustaining Engagement

Long-term engagement requires activities that reflect participants’ goals, backgrounds, and challenges. Dynamically adapting tasks, gathering iterative feedback, and co-creating workshop elements ensures that energy and focus remain high throughout the program.

### Equity and Accessibility

Addressing both visible and invisible barriers—ranging from group interaction dynamics to physical and technological accessibility—is non-negotiable for delivering equitable mentorship programs. Embedding universal design and equity checks into every aspect of workshop delivery helps guarantee that all participants can contribute meaningfully.

## Engagement Metrics and Evaluation

While this round of feedback is qualitative, both contributors' observations point to strong initial engagement, followed by a decline attributed to pacing and activity relevance. Future evaluations should incorporate quantitative measures such as pre- and post-workshop surveys and demographic analyses to more fully assess engagement, learning outcomes, and equity impacts. Collecting such data will enable ongoing adjustment and more robust accountability.

## Next Steps in Workshop Development

- Organize co-design sessions with participant representatives to review and shape future workshop content and activities.
- Pilot the revised workshop structure and use real-time feedback mechanisms, such as digital polls and feedback cards, to inform continuous improvement.
- Document facilitation adaptations and outcomes systematically to support future evaluation and reporting needs.
- Continue proactive audits of physical and technological accessibility for every session, ensuring alignment with UDL and ADA guidelines.

## Conclusion

The feedback provided by Tariq and Janelle, contextualized within educational and sociological research, highlights the ongoing need to ground mentorship workshops in participants’ real-world experiences, facilitate genuine collaboration, and build accessible, equitable learning environments. Implementing these recommendations will drive meaningful improvements in program efficacy and inclusivity, contributing significantly to the success and community impact of the upcoming exhibit.
