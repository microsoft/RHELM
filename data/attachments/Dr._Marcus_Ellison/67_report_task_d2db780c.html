2. Discussion Summary

2.1 Review of Mentee Photo Submissions

Marcus Ellison opened the session with an in-depth review of recent photo submissions by Janelle Brooks and Tariq Mensah. Each portfolio was evaluated for its alignment with the exhibit’s main theme, “Youth, Agency, and Inequality," as well as for technical proficiency and narrative strength.

Janelle’s photography was commended for its compelling intergenerational storytelling, effectively illustrating personal and familial dimensions of agency. Her work received positive feedback on its narrative clarity and emotional resonance, particularly in the way it bridges personal identity with broader social themes.

Tariq’s submissions offered a more structural view, shedding light on urban inequality through nuanced compositional choices and motifs. His photos were recognized for capturing subtle aspects of social division in public spaces, prompting conversation about representation and the importance of situating subjects ethically within these contexts.

A key aspect of this discussion involved reaffirming best practices around informed consent, especially in settings involving minors or sensitive subject matter. The group agreed to maintain rigorous ethical standards, ensuring all participants depicted in the images had provided explicit consent and that confidentiality was protected where necessary. This ongoing attention to ethics underpins the project's academic credibility and its commitment to responsible storytelling.

2.2 Exhibit Zine Progress Update

Tariq provided a comprehensive update on the progress of the exhibit zine, noting that content sequencing and drafting of artists’ statements have reached preliminary completion. The editorial schedule remains on track for a draft release by December 8, 2024.

Participants identified the need for broader narrative diversity in the final version, emphasizing outreach to different community contributors. Copyright permissions, particularly for external or archival photographs, were discussed as an immediate priority. It was agreed that any outstanding permissions must be resolved before inclusion in the draft, and alternative images would be sourced if clearance cannot be secured.

The curation process continues to prioritize equitable representation, transparency in editorial decisions, and opportunities for mentee input. These measures reflect the mentorship’s participatory approach and reinforce best practices for collaborative editorial work.

2.3 Evaluation of Janelle’s Youth-Led Q&A Proposal

Janelle introduced her proposal to host a youth-led Q&A session as part of the exhibit launch. Her plan is to foreground lived youth experiences and foster genuine engagement with issues of social inequality.

The group assessed the proposal’s relevance to project objectives, highlighting that such a forum would offer a uniquely authentic platform for young voices. This initiative aligns well with the values of empowerment and participatory education embedded in the program.

Discussion focused on the projected impact of the Q&A, both as a means of amplifying youth perspectives and as an opportunity for dialogue between youth participants and community stakeholders. This format is expected to support critical engagement and collective reflection on structural injustice.

Implementation details were carefully considered, including selection of youth moderators, ensuring accessibility for all participants, and establishing clear guidelines for respectful and safe discourse. Marcus emphasized that while mentors would provide logistical and risk management guidance, primary leadership and decision-making would remain with youth participants. The group viewed this as a promising step toward deepening agency and leadership among the mentees.

2.4 Ethics, Engagement, and Collaboration

A substantial portion of the meeting was dedicated to discussing the continuing importance of ethical decision-making in all aspects of the project. The team reaffirmed procedures to safeguard subject identities and maintain rigorous, ongoing consent. There was a collective commitment to balancing mentorship guidance with the autonomy of mentees, acknowledging that mentee leadership is central to the project’s methodology.

Editorial decisions will remain collaborative, drawing on transparent processes such as group voting and inclusive agenda-setting for future meetings. All agreed to the “first voice” principle, whereby young creators are prioritized in authorship and curation decisions. This approach is consistent with recognized university mentorship ethics and supports the development of authentic youth expertise within academic settings.

4. Next Steps and Future Considerations

  • Schedule a follow-up session in early December (target date: December 11, 2024) to review the Q&A plans and conduct a comprehensive walkthrough of the exhibit zine prior to committee submission.
  • Continue expanding leadership roles for mentees in planning and facilitating project events, with mentor support focused on risk management and ensuring smooth processes.
  • Start developing a retrospective evaluation tool, co-designed by mentees and mentor, which will gather feedback on the learning outcomes of both the exhibit zine and the youth-led Q&A. This tool should address both participant learning and the broader impact on audience awareness regarding social inequality.
  • Explore opportunities for institutional publication of the exhibit zine, which would recognize the students’ work formally, enhance its professional visibility, and deepen its impact within and beyond the university.

6. Ethical Principles and Collaborative Practices

  • The group reiterated its ongoing commitment to ethical mentorship, maintaining as priorities the continuous review of privacy, accuracy of representation, and clarity in expectations for mentee roles and contributions.
  • Decision-making and documentation processes are designed to formally recognize mentees as co-authors and co-curators, consistent with participatory mentorship models endorsed by leading academic institutions.
  • Editorial responsibilities and curatorial authority are distributed collaboratively, with crucial decisions—such as image selection and event planning—made through open, consensus-based discussions in regular meetings.

7. Reflective Observations and Recommendations for Program Development

This session underscored the significant impact of structured, youth-led engagement on both participant growth and project quality. The mentees’ involvement in critical decision-making not only strengthens their sense of ownership but enhances the authenticity and relevance of the collective creative output.

Consistent open discussion of ethical issues, transparent editorial processes, and active efforts to diversify contributions contributed to an environment that fosters inclusion, empowerment, and skill development. Looking ahead, future mentorship cycles should consider additional scaffolding for mentee leadership—especially in event facilitation—and implement more robust feedback mechanisms. Enhanced peer and audience evaluations will further validate youth expertise and offer rich insights for ongoing program improvement.
