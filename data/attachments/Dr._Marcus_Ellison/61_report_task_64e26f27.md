## 3. Summary of Contributions

### Dr. Marcus Ellison

Dr. Ellison opened the meeting with a concise overview of current research on youth digital access and peer engagement, referencing recent Pew Research Center findings [3]. He advocated for an Asset-Based Community Development (ABCD) approach, focusing on empowering youth as leaders and co-creators rather than passive recipients. His proposal included incorporating “mini-action research cycles,” enabling participants to investigate issues relevant to their lives and propose actionable solutions. He underlined the value of mixed-methods evaluation frameworks for both formative (process) and summative (outcome) assessment, ensuring the program remains both evidence-based and responsive to participant feedback.

### Dr. Aisha Grant

Dr. Grant emphasized the significance of intersectionality in youth engagement, drawing from Crenshaw’s foundational work [2]. She recommended integrating social and emotional learning (SEL) outcomes into the workshops, which would help participants build resilience and self-efficacy. Dr. Grant also suggested session topics such as civic engagement, restorative justice, and mental health, citing best practices from initiatives like the Summer Youth Employment Program (SYEP) and My Brother’s Keeper. She highlighted the critical need to address technology access as an equity issue and recommended that all data collection and evaluation activities undergo IRB review to uphold ethical and safeguarding standards.

### Celeste Broussard

Bringing in frontline experience, Celeste shared feedback collected from past program cohorts. Youth overwhelmingly preferred hybrid options, combining in-person and virtual participation. Engagement was consistently stronger in sessions scheduled after 3:00 pm, pointing to after-school slots as optimal. She noted that not all sites could guarantee stable internet connectivity, and some youth lacked personal devices. Language and cultural diversity was flagged as high-priority; at least two sessions need bilingual facilitation. Celeste proposed inviting Harlem-based guest speakers to serve as relatable role models and recommended sessions on college access and essential life skills, reflecting ongoing community requests.

**Connectivity-Related Gaps:**  
Audio issues between 12:44–12:50 interrupted detailed discussion on outreach messaging and strategies for shared accountability. These topics were noted for urgent follow-up to ensure clear, consistent communication and robust engagement with both youth and parents. Additionally, nuanced perspectives on trauma-informed facilitation were truncated, requiring further input before implementation.

## 6. Analysis of Connectivity Disruptions

All participants experienced a notable 6-minute period of connectivity loss, marked by audio lags and repeated requests to revisit agenda items. These difficulties caused a reduction in effective discussion time, with particular impact on the workshop’s outreach messaging strategies and accountability measures. Follow-up is necessary to bridge any resulting miscommunications and ensure all perspectives—especially youth and parent voices—are included in final decisions.

**Mitigation Strategies Moving Forward:**
- Distribute concise summaries or preparatory notes ahead of each meeting, allowing focus to remain on critical decisions irrespective of technical difficulties.
- Use shared, collaborative documents in real time for transparent agenda tracking and live note-taking.
- Offer a backup dial-in option for all participants, ensuring continued access even amidst bandwidth failures.
- Assign a dedicated technology coordinator for all sessions to address issues promptly.
- Obtain participant consent to record meetings for accurate reference and continuity.

These provisions should strengthen the group’s ability to adapt to technological unpredictability and maintain the integrity of deliberations.

## 7. Next Steps, Research Priorities, and Ethical Safeguards

**Immediate Follow-Up:**
- Schedule a supplementary video call within the next week to resume unfinished discussion and build consensus on outreach and communication protocols.
- Deploy digital feedback forms to both youth and families to supplement secondhand reports and capture firsthand input on workshop content and delivery preferences.
- Conduct a comprehensive audit of technology resources at participating sites, reporting specific Wi-Fi and device accessibility gaps.

**Ongoing Research Needs:**
- Analyze past participation data from Harlem Youth Collective to pinpoint trends in engagement by modality (virtual, in-person, hybrid).
- Review leading literature and case studies on trauma-informed facilitation in urban youth programs, giving particular attention to models successfully implemented in similar contexts.
- Investigate frameworks from other university-community partnerships that have prioritized youth equity and empowerment.

**Ethical and Safeguarding Considerations:**
- Ensure informed consent is gathered systematically, with particular care for youth under 18, ensuring comprehension in all necessary languages.
- Maintain IRB oversight across all aspects of data collection—including surveys, interviews, recordings, and session observations—to protect privacy and participant dignity.
- Provide access to counseling and support resources during sessions that address sensitive or potentially triggering subjects.
- Implement regular monitoring to spot and address any unintended consequences, such as digital fatigue, retraumatization, or persistent inequities in access.

**Expected Community Impact:**
Workshops are designed to nurture agency, leadership, and self-advocacy in Harlem youth, offering structured opportunities for skill-building and connection to local mentors. By sustaining engagement over several months and responding to participant feedback, the program aims not only to impart skills but deepen youth involvement in shaping their own community futures. Sessions on life skills and college access are anticipated to provide practical, actionable information, helping open pathways to higher education and employment.

**Information Gaps Still to Be Addressed:**
- Final confirmation of guest facilitators and speaker availability.
- Specification of technical infrastructure required for secure, reliable hybrid participation.
- Direct feedback from youth and parents on preferred session topics and optimal scheduling (pending distribution and collection of post-meeting surveys).
