Detailed Revision Actions

Abstract

The abstract was revised to state the central research questions more clearly, spotlighting the importance of intersectionality in urban inequality. It now references specific ethical procedures and the role of community partnerships in the study design. These refinements highlight the manuscript’s integration of theoretical frameworks and ethical practices, as requested by reviewers, and set the tone for the rigorous and transparent approach taken throughout the study.

Introduction

The introduction now offers a broader review of foundational theories—including urban ecology, critical race theory, and lived experience research—to frame the intersection of structural, spatial, and experiential factors in urban inequality. Citations have been updated to include recent contributions in urban sociology and critical geography, ensuring the study is grounded in the latest scholarship. This revision provides a firm conceptual starting point and effectively situates the research within ongoing debates about social inequality in urban contexts.

Literature Review

Recent literature from 2020–2024 is now integrated, with emphasis on social stratification, residential segregation, and patterns of urban marginality. The revised section synthesizes how policy and economic structures impact lived experience, weaving structural analysis with narrative and ethnographic perspectives. Responding to prior critiques, the review covers global South research and a diversity of methodological approaches, balancing perspectives from both established and emerging contexts. This synthesis offers a well-rounded understanding of the international and intersectional dimensions of urban inequality.

Methods

The methods section has been substantially expanded. It now details a multi-stage sampling strategy designed to accurately reflect socioeconomic diversity across neighborhoods. Operational definitions are provided for key quantitative measures—including indices of neighborhood disadvantage and residential mobility. A new subsection outlines qualitative interview protocols, recruitment practices, and informed consent procedures, underscoring the research team’s commitment to ethical engagement with vulnerable populations. These additions improve methodological transparency and set a clear standard for reproducibility and participant care.

Results

Statistical models have been refined to more robustly address confounding variables, with results validated through alternative model specifications. Quantitative findings are supplemented with expanded narrative accounts that offer depth and context to the voices of marginalized participants. Data visualizations have been improved to make spatial and demographic disparities more accessible, using clear heatmaps and detailed bar charts. Together, these changes offer a richer portrait of unequal urban experiences and the mechanisms driving them.

Discussion

The discussion now more thoroughly connects the empirical results to frameworks such as spatial justice and intersectionality. There is an expanded analysis of policy implications, positioning the study’s findings within debates on urban governance and equity-oriented intervention. The updated section draws explicit links between research insights and actionable strategies for reducing urban inequality, referencing current policy literature and practices observed in different municipal contexts. This approach highlights the project’s practical significance and its broader contributions to policy and community action.

Limitations and Generalizability

The revised limitations section offers a candid assessment of potential constraints, such as sample representativeness, sources of bias, and the extent to which findings can be generalized to other urban contexts. Guidance is offered for future research directions, with a focus on under-explored forms of marginalization and comparative analyses across cities. These additions promote transparency and position the study as a valuable step in an ongoing, iterative inquiry into urban inequality.

Ethics Statement

The new ethics statement confirms institutional review board (IRB) approval and details the anonymization protocols adopted throughout the research process. The section outlines culturally sensitive consent procedures and describes ongoing efforts to share results and benefits with community partners. By foregrounding these ethical commitments, the manuscript demonstrates its alignment with best practices for research with marginalized communities and affirms its credibility.
