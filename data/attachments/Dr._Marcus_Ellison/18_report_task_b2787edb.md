## Comprehensive Discussion Notes

### Theoretical Framework and Ethical Considerations

The group began by reaffirming the central focus of the ASA proposal on **urban displacement**, approaching it as a phenomenon shaped by intersecting structures of **social inequality**, policy, housing instability, and broader processes like gentrification and economic vulnerability. Drawing from core sociological scholarship, the conversation engaged with intersectionality (Crenshaw) to explore how displacement affects individuals along multiple, overlapping dimensions of identity. Theoretical grounding also included Galtung’s notion of structural violence—highlighting how displacement is produced by embedded institutional forces—and Wacquant’s analysis of urban marginality, emphasizing the durable consequences for communities on the margins of metropolitan life.

Ethical considerations were a major point of emphasis in this meeting. The team agreed on the following guiding principles:

- **Protections for Vulnerable Populations:** All research activities must prioritize the rights and dignity of individuals and communities who have experienced displacement, recognizing the risks inherent in participation and the power differentials present in research relationships.
- **Confidentiality and Anonymity:** Procedures will be established to maintain strict confidentiality across both qualitative and quantitative data, with particular attention to anonymization and secure data storage.
- **IRB Compliance and Informed Consent:** All prospective fieldwork will adhere fully to institutional IRB protocols, including clear and compassionate communication of rights, risks, and benefits with participants. Extra care will be taken in obtaining informed consent from those with lived experiences of displacement, and in ensuring transparency throughout the research process.
- **Researcher Positionality:** The importance of self-reflection regarding the researchers’ own backgrounds, positions of privilege, and potential biases was emphasized. The team will incorporate mechanisms for ongoing reflexivity and open dialogue about how perspectives shape both research design and field interactions.

The discussion also touched upon the importance of considering unintentional harm or retraumatization, especially when working with communities that have experienced multiple forms of marginalization. The team resolved to include a dedicated section on research ethics and participant protections in the proposal.

### Task Assignments and Milestones

To advance proposal development, clear deliverables and timelines were established:

- **Literature Review (Dr. Marcus Ellison):** Marcus will conduct a thorough review of existing research on urban displacement, emphasizing the interplay between housing policies, gentrification, and patterns of inequality. This review will also map the evolution of the field and identify theoretical and empirical gaps that the new research will address. **Deadline: May 3, 2024.**

- **Mixed-Methods Data Plan (Dr. Priya Nair):** Priya will articulate a data collection strategy that integrates both qualitative (e.g., semi-structured interviews, ethnographic observations) and quantitative (e.g., survey instruments, demographic datasets) methods. Her plan will detail sampling strategies, interview guides, survey design, and measures to ensure representativeness and depth. **Deadline: May 10, 2024.**

- **Methodological Framework (Jamal Rivers):** Jamal will develop a methodological framework that synthesizes the literature review and data plan, providing a rationale for the mixed-methods approach. This will include strategies for triangulation, analytical techniques (such as statistical modeling and GIS mapping), and a plan for integrating qualitative and quantitative findings to ensure a holistic analysis. **Deadline: May 17, 2024.**

The team agreed that each deliverable builds on the previous ones, emphasizing the need for ongoing communication and coordination.

### Shared Resource: Urban Displacement Article

Dr. Priya Nair shared the article, ["Urban Displacement and Affordable Housing: A Comparative Analysis"](https://example.com/urban-displacement-study)[1], prior to the meeting to inform the proposal’s contextual and methodological design. The group recognized several strengths of this resource:

- Offers robust comparative case studies that provide templates for positioning new research within the broader literature.
- Presents effective strategies for integrating quantitative data (such as demographic shifts and housing market changes) with qualitative testimonies from affected residents.
- Highlights recent trends in urban policy, which are crucial for situating the project both empirically and theoretically.

Each team member agreed to annotate key findings, extract relevant frameworks, and incorporate them into their deliverables, ensuring a well-integrated and evidence-driven proposal.
