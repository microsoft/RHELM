# Student Feedback Summary: Budgeting Workshop for Financial Literacy  
Nashville, TN • April 2024

## Executive Summary

This report provides a detailed summary of student feedback from a recent budgeting workshop focused on financial literacy for middle and high school students in Nashville, Tennessee. The analysis highlights high levels of satisfaction and engagement among participants, particularly in response to interactive, hands-on activities and content that connects directly to real-life financial situations. Student input has informed targeted recommendations to improve future sessions, such as expanding practical examples, differentiating instruction for diverse learning needs, and enhancing technological integration. These findings both reinforce the effectiveness of current workshop design and identify opportunities to amplify its impact on students’ long-term financial wellbeing.

## Workshop Overview

### Objectives and Audience

The workshop was developed to help students build key budgeting skills and financial knowledge, in alignment with Tennessee’s state educational standards for financial literacy. The primary goal was to empower students to take control of personal finances and foster habits of responsibility and forward planning.

**Core objectives included:**
- Introducing fundamental budgeting concepts—including income, expenses, and savings.
- Illustrating how budgeting skills apply to everyday decisions, from managing allowance to spending earnings from part-time jobs.
- Encouraging analytical thinking with scenario-based activities, promoting problem-solving and reflection.
- Supporting students in setting realistic financial goals and laying the groundwork for financial independence.

The workshop served a diverse group of middle and high school students, representing a broad spectrum of socioeconomic backgrounds and prior financial literacy experience.

### Structure and Format

To maximize learning and engagement, the workshop featured:
- Interactive presentations explaining essential principles.
- Group activities such as peer budgeting scenarios and role-play exercises.
- Q&A sessions to address common questions and personal challenges.
- Real-life simulations, including mock expense tracking and budgeting games designed to reinforce practical skills.

Local educators partnered in delivering the workshop as part of a district-wide initiative to strengthen financial literacy curriculum and support youth development.

## Emerging Themes and Areas for Growth

- **Authentic Relevance:** Students valued content tailored to tangible scenarios, such as saving for personal goals and managing school-related expenses. Practicality enhanced engagement.
- **Active Learning:** Activities such as budgeting games were widely praised, helping students internalize concepts and apply them in simulated decision-making.
- **Expanded Content Needs:** Many participants expressed interest in topics like budgeting for college, early career planning, and handling unexpected costs, indicating areas for curricular enrichment.
- **Supporting Diverse Learners:** Feedback from some students indicated that budgeting concepts—especially around balancing saving and spending—can be challenging, reinforcing the need for differentiated instructional strategies.
- **Community Ripple Effect:** The workshop often spurred family conversations and changes in household spending, extending its impact beyond the classroom.
- **Suggested Improvements:**
  - Introduce more real-world stories and testimonials to make concepts relatable.
  - Incorporate digital budgeting tools and apps to connect instruction to daily student life.
  - Offer small-group or one-on-one support for students needing extra guidance.

## Recommendations for Improvement and Future Planning

In light of student feedback, the following strategies are recommended for future workshops:

- **Enhance Interactivity:** Increase the frequency of hands-on simulations, role playing, and group challenges to foster deeper engagement.
- **Differentiate Instruction:** Develop tiered lesson materials and provide peer mentoring or additional visual supports to help students at different learning levels.
- **Broaden Scenario Coverage:** Add modules on budgeting for post-secondary education, moving out, emergencies, and typical youth milestones.
- **Strengthen Life Connections:** Invite guest speakers, facilitate interviews with family financial planners, and link students to community resources.
- **Leverage Technology:** Integrate digital platforms and budgeting apps for authentic practice and to reflect the technological realities of students’ daily experience.
- **Monitor Long-Term Outcome:** Collect feedback several months after workshops to assess sustained knowledge and behavior change, and adjust the curriculum as needed.

**Scaling and Partnership Opportunities:**
- Expand workshops across additional Nashville schools and surrounding districts.
- Create facilitator guides aligned with Tennessee state standards for financial literacy.
- Offer targeted professional development for educators, equipping them with tools for teaching financial concepts effectively.
- Continually revise curriculum based on national frameworks, such as the Jump$tart Coalition and Council for Economic Education guidelines.

## Broader Impact and Advocacy

Financial literacy workshops such as this one have demonstrated potential to spark meaningful conversations among students, their families, and the larger community. By equipping young people with essential skills for managing money, these initiatives help bridge gaps in educational access and support lifelong independence.

Aligning feedback-driven improvements with state and national policy objectives ensures that program offerings remain responsive, equitable, and evidence-based, reinforcing the value of experiential approaches in financial education.
