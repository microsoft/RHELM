## Purpose of the Meeting

David R. Ellis and Alicia Torres convened to map out the next phase of developing a comprehensive, evidence-based online financial literacy course. The discussion focused on ensuring high educational standards, effective pedagogy for different learner groups, and strategic outreach and evaluation to broaden the course's impact.

### Central Objectives

- **Curriculum Design:** Establish a robust framework grounded in nationally recognized financial literacy standards, customizing content for children, adults, and policymakers.
- **Outreach Strategy:** Develop approaches to engage diverse learner populations and stakeholders, facilitating widespread adoption and advocacy.
- **Evaluation & Implementation:** Define clear metrics and methodologies for course deployment and continuous improvement, emphasizing community involvement and data-driven decision-making.

Participants worked together to clarify these objectives, setting the stage for an inclusive and impactful financial education initiative.

---

## Key Discussion Points

### 1. Evidence-Based Frameworks and Curriculum Alignment

Both participants agreed on anchoring the course content to established financial literacy standards from the Jump$tart Coalition, Council for Economic Education (CEE), and National Financial Educators Council (NFEC). By harmonizing curriculum modules with these benchmarks, the team can ensure the program’s relevance, credibility, and transferability. Recent academic literature supports using interactive modules and scenario-based learning to enhance online financial education, which will guide the development process.

### 2. Pedagogical Strategies for Target Audiences

**Children:**  
To promote engagement among young learners, the curriculum will incorporate gamified learning experiences, animated instructional videos, and ongoing formative assessments. These tools are shown to improve retention and make abstract financial concepts accessible.

**Adults:**  
Content for adult learners will center on practical scenarios, such as household budgeting, debt management, and financial decision-making simulations. Modules will be designed for flexibility and ease of use—featuring short micro-learning units that learners can fit into their schedules.

**Policymakers:**  
Specialized modules will address macro-level financial issues, policy analysis, and advocacy training. These are intended to equip policymakers with the tools needed to drive systemic improvements in financial education at the institutional and legislative levels.

### 3. Building Stakeholder Partnerships

The team highlighted the importance of connecting with local schools, community organizations, and advocacy groups during the course rollout. By fostering these collaborations early, they can collect valuable feedback, accelerate adoption, and ensure the course responds to real community needs. Periodic live webinars and Q&A sessions with invited financial experts will further enrich the learning experience and build trust among participants.

### 4. Outreach and Impact Evaluation

A multi-channel outreach plan is being developed, covering social media, email campaigns, and direct engagement at grassroots events. To measure the program’s effectiveness, pre- and post-course assessments, focus groups, and ongoing user feedback will be integrated. These evaluation methods will guide regular course refinement and document impact for stakeholders and funders.

### 5. Policy and Advocacy Integration

Recognizing the evolving landscape of financial education mandates, David and Alicia explored strategies to make course resources adaptable for use in legislative advocacy and institution-wide adoption. They discussed plans to create policy briefs and resource toolkits tailored for leaders and policymakers.

### 6. Consensus and Transparency

Throughout their discussion, the team committed to a transparent decision-making process. All major decisions will be documented, reviewed regularly, and justified to keep development aligned with advocacy goals and stakeholder expectations.

## Next Steps

Moving forward, David and Alicia will continue refining the course framework and curriculum, ensuring each module aligns fully with national standards and reflects feedback from user testing. Outreach efforts will begin with tailored presentations to local schools, advocacy partners, and community centers, aiming to build momentum for initial rollouts.

Regular evaluation will be a priority, with scheduled feedback sessions using focus groups and course analytics to guide updates and improve functionality. The team is also preparing policy briefs and advocacy toolkits to support legislative initiatives and facilitate community workshops.

To foster meaningful user engagement, an integrated feedback platform will be implemented within the online course portal. This will enable swift responses to user input and ongoing content enhancements, following evaluation protocols recommended by the NFEC.
