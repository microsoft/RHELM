## Workshop Objectives

The main goal of this workshop is to empower Nashville-area educators to deliver high-quality financial literacy instruction that fully aligns with Tennessee academic standards. We aim to:

- Ensure teachers understand and implement current requirements for K-12 financial literacy education in Tennessee.
- Integrate proven teaching practices—including active learning, inquiry-based learning, and culturally responsive approaches—into classroom instruction.
- Enhance teacher skills in curriculum development, lesson planning, and formative assessment specific to financial literacy.
- Encourage collaboration through professional learning communities (PLCs), facilitating ongoing sharing of effective strategies and resources.
- Equip teachers to reach and engage students from diverse backgrounds, drawing on Nashville’s rich community resources and cultural context.

## Key Learning Outcomes

By the end of the workshop, participants will be able to:

1. Clearly articulate Tennessee’s financial literacy standards and identify the core instructional components required at each grade level.
2. Plan and deliver lessons utilizing evidence-based methods—such as inquiry projects and student-led activities—to teach financial literacy concepts effectively.
3. Design lesson plans integrating formative assessment tools that promote student ownership and engagement in financial topics.
4. Apply culturally relevant teaching strategies to connect with Nashville’s diverse student population and foster inclusion.
5. Establish routines for ongoing self-reflection, peer feedback, and student input as part of continuous professional growth.
6. Collaborate within professional learning communities to share resources and refine instructional practices over time.
7. Critically evaluate instructional materials for financial literacy, selecting those best suited to local contexts and student needs.

## Workshop Flow and Integration

The agenda is structured to begin with foundational knowledge, then move into application and peer collaboration. Early sessions focus on standards and instructional research, ensuring all participants start from a common understanding. As the workshop progresses, the focus shifts to lesson design, assessment, and culturally responsive teaching—allowing teachers to tailor strategies to their own classrooms.

Hands-on activities and small-group planning encourage active participation and build a network of support among attendees. Regular opportunities for reflection and feedback are integrated, laying the groundwork for ongoing professional development and continuous improvement beyond the workshop day.

---

## Notes and Next Steps

At the conclusion of the workshop, we propose the following action items:

- **Schedule follow-up meetings** to review classroom implementation and share experiences.
- **Form a Nashville-area professional learning community** dedicated to financial literacy, enabling regular exchange of ideas and resources.
- **Collect participant feedback and evaluation forms** to assess workshop effectiveness and identify areas for future improvement.
- **Adapt ongoing training and support** to reflect changes in Tennessee financial literacy standards and local education policies.
- **Create a shared digital repository** for lesson plans, instructional materials, and best practice guides.
- **Seek consensus among teachers** for collaborative curriculum development and participation in future action research projects.
- **Monitor measurable outcomes**—such as student engagement data, classroom application examples, and teacher self-assessment reports—to guide refinement of financial literacy instruction.

## Evidence Base and Standards Alignment

This workshop is fully aligned with Tennessee’s Academic Standards for Financial Literacy and reflects current state mandates for comprehensive personal finance education at both middle and high school levels. The structure and activities are informed by best practices in teacher professional development—particularly collaborative lesson design, active learning, and ongoing evaluation.

Special attention is paid to culturally responsive teaching, ensuring strategies resonate with Nashville’s diverse student body and promote equity in financial education. All content is designed based on principles outlined in recent guidance from the Tennessee Department of Education and leading research in K-12 financial literacy instruction. Documentation and resources have been sourced from official state standards as listed below.
