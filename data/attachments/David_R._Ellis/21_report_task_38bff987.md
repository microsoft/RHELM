# Budgeting With Heart: Evidence-Based Tips and Creative Activities for Nashville Families

## Introduction

Managing family finances is more than just crunching numbers—it’s about building confidence, teamwork, and lifelong skills together. Here in Nashville, families and educators are coming together to strengthen financial literacy, using practical strategies backed by research and creative hands-on activities. Drawing inspiration from the Eastwood Middle School Financial Literacy workshop (March 2024), this report shares actionable tips, collaborative approaches, and engaging activities designed to make budgeting a meaningful and positive experience for every member of the family.

## Collaborative Budgeting: Practical Steps for Families

### 1. Holding Family Budget Meetings to Build Consensus

Welcoming everyone into conversations about money ensures family goals are shared and understood. Regular budget meetings help children and teenagers develop financial habits while encouraging openness and problem-solving. By rotating leadership roles, everyone—from parents to kids—gets a chance to shape decisions and learn from each other.

**How to Begin:**
- Set aside a consistent time each month for budget discussions.
- Start every meeting by identifying collective goals—whether it’s saving for a special trip or building an emergency fund.
- Alternate who leads the meeting, giving each family member an opportunity to practice facilitation and build confidence.

### 2. Planning Budgets Together: Parents and Kids as Partners

Involving children in budget planning not only teaches real-world math but also empowers them to make thoughtful choices. Research supports that collaborative budgeting leads to better understanding, accountability, and long-term financial mindfulness.

**Practical Steps:**
- Gather together and list all sources of income—like allowances, part-time jobs, and any chore earnings—as well as anticipated expenses such as school supplies or outings.
- Use a visual tool, like a whiteboard or a shared spreadsheet, for clarity and transparency.
- Invite kids to identify areas for potential savings and to suggest positive spending habits. This encourages ownership and creativity.

### 3. Creative Activity: The “Needs vs. Wants” Jar

During the Eastwood Middle School workshop, the “Needs vs. Wants” Jar became a favorite activity for recognizing the difference between essentials and extras. Adapting this activity at home helps spark conversations and thoughtful reflections about purchases, making budgeting more engaging.

**How the Activity Works:**
- Place two jars or boxes labeled “Needs” and “Wants” in a visible area of the home.
- Each time someone in the family makes a purchase, they write a quick note or add a receipt to the appropriate jar.
- At weekly family meetings, gather around the jars, discuss each item, ask questions, and celebrate wise decisions together.
- Through these discussions, families learn to identify patterns in spending and brainstorm ways to improve as a team.

### 4. Keeping Progress Visible: The Family Budget Checklist

Tracking achievements and milestones together motivates everyone to stay engaged. A simple, visual checklist easily keeps families accountable and encourages celebration when goals are met.

**Suggested Checklist Items:**
- Set three financial goals together (e.g., saving for vacation, reducing snack spending, or starting an emergency fund)
- Track all expenses for two weeks
- Hold at least one family budget meeting each month
- Complete the “Needs vs. Wants” Jar activity for four consecutive weeks
- Highlight and celebrate a financial success, such as reaching a savings target

By making progress tangible and visible, families reinforce positive habits and adjust their plans openly when challenges arise.

## Bringing Budgeting Into Everyday Family Life

Talking openly and regularly about money makes it less intimidating and more approachable for everyone. Here are some ways Nashville families can weave financial conversations into daily routines:

- Make time each week for relaxed, judgment-free money talks
- Encourage both adults and children to track expenses, whether on paper or using easy-to-use apps
- Set up a shared savings challenge, matching contributions toward a family goal such as a fun outing, donation, or new purchase
- Review budgets together when life throws in surprises—like an unexpected bill or a new opportunity—and work as a team to adjust
- Explore local resources, such as financial literacy workshops at the Nashville Public Library or nearby community centers, to learn alongside other families
