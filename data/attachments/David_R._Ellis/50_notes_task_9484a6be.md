## My Annotated Grocery List  
*(with Budget Tips & Substitution Suggestions)*

**Fruits & Vegetables**  
- **Cherry tomatoes:** We pick a lot from our plants, but when buying, grape tomatoes are often a cheaper swap.
- **Edible marigold petals/leaves:** If you haven’t grown these, nasturtiums or calendula flowers, or even a sprinkle of fresh herbs, add the same pop of color.
- **Baby greens/spinach/arugula:** Bags or boxes from the bulk section are better value. Some weeks, I pick leaves right from the garden.
- **Cucumbers, carrots, bell peppers, apples:** I try to buy whatever’s in season or on special—makes a real difference to the budget.
- **Snap peas, green beans:** In the off-season, I don’t hesitate to reach for frozen—they’re less expensive and still nutritious.
- **Potatoes, lemons, bananas:** Pantry mainstays, and I use bottled lemon juice in a pinch.
- **Seasonal berries and melons:** The farmer’s market sometimes gives a good deal, but frozen fruit works well too.
- **Orchard fruit or a frozen fruit mix:** I like to use up whatever’s left from our picnic basket.

**Staples & Grains**  
- **Quinoa:** I’ve found it cheapest in bulk bins.
- **Brown rice or bulgur:** Whatever’s on sale makes it into our cart.
- **Whole wheat bread or buns, oatmeal, wraps/pita, pasta:** Store brands save a few dollars each week.
- **Muffin and pancake mix:** When we have time, making these from scratch costs less and tastes better.

**Dairy & Dairy Alternatives**  
- **Eggs:** For vegan friends or days, chickpea flour works surprisingly well for scrambles and pancakes.
- **Greek yogurt, mozzarella cheese:** I always buy larger tubs or blocks—cheaper and less packaging.
- **Milk or oat/soy/almond milk:** Store-brand options are great. We keep both dairy and plant-based around.

**Proteins**  
- **Skinless chicken (breasts or thighs):** I look for family packs when they’re discounted. We eat lots of bean-based meals too.
- **Canned/dried lentils & chickpeas:** Dried are budget winners if soaked and cooked at home.
- **Tofu:** The Asian supermarket nearby stocks fresh tofu for less.
- **Canned beans:** Handy for quick salads and our make-your-own taco night.

**Pantry & Miscellaneous**  
- **Hummus:** Making it at home with canned chickpeas takes minutes and costs pennies.
- **Mrs. Patel’s spiced pickles:** Or your favorite pickled veggies.
- **Olive oil, vinegar, spices:** I buy big basic bottles and refill smaller jars.
- **Nuts/seeds for snacks:** Sunflower seeds are cheap and the kids love them.
- **Nut butter:** Peanut butter remains a staple, but we occasionally mix in almond or sunflower butter.
- **Baking staples:** Flour and baking powder are always on hand for muffins and pancakes.

**Specialty**  
- **Edible marigolds, calendula, nasturtiums:** Whatever’s in the garden or the local nursery adds that special touch.

**How We Keep Our Budget (and Sanity) Intact:**  
- I always plan for leftovers and stash extras in the freezer (saved us at least three dinner panics last month).
- Leftover grains or veggies become wraps, stir-fries, or a quick lunch bowl.
- Having a set shopping list helps avoid impulse purchases; the kids and I are less likely to wander the snack aisle.
- We keep an eye out for coupons and "imperfect" produce bins; slightly wonky carrots taste just as good and cost less.

## Our Food Philosophy: Health, Flexibility, and Family Fun

**Balanced Nutrition**  
Every meal is built around a colorful mix of vegetables, whole grains, protein, and healthy fats. I turn to the [USDA MyPlate guidelines][1] whenever I’m unsure about proportions—most days, it just feels right to see those colors on our plates. We make sure there are plenty of snacks that keep everyone energized, combining fiber and protein (Greek yogurt parfaits are always a hit).

**Customizable and Allergy-Friendly**  
Our family doesn’t have any food allergies, but I always keep plant-based swaps ready in case we’re hosting friends or our tastes shift midweek. On nights when we skip meat, beans, lentils, or tofu are an easy stand-in. We also keep gluten-free bread and oat milk around for guests.

**Smart Prep and Time Savers**  
Sunday afternoons, I set aside some time to batch-cook our grains for the week and pre-chop a mound of veggies so weekday prep is quick. Roasting extra veggies during dinner saves time for lunches. Anything leftover goes into packed lunches or makes for easy snacks. Lunches and snacks are often portioned the night before, especially on busy school days—it really helps mornings go smoothly.

**Togetherness in the Kitchen (and Beyond!)**  
One of the best parts of the week is getting our kids involved. They help pick marigold petals or basil leaves and love assembling their own picnic snacks. Shopping together sparks conversations about where our food comes from and even leads to impromptu math lessons as we weigh tomatoes or look for sales. Sharing stories—like how Mrs. Patel’s pickles are made with old family traditions—gives our food even more meaning.

**Wellness and Sustainability**  
We aim to eat with the seasons and use as much local or homegrown produce as we can. I’ve slowly switched to buying more in bulk to cut down on packaging, and we use our stash of canvas bags for weekly shopping trips. The more we lean into plant-based meals, the more I see positive impacts on our health and our grocery budget. These choices align with what’s recommended by [Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health][2]: plant-forward meals, less processed food, and lots of variety.
