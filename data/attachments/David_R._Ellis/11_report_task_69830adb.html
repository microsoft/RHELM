Workshop Agenda: Evidence-Based Strategies for Adult Educator Development

The workshop was designed to deepen educators’ understanding of financial literacy instruction and foster collaborative action planning. Each session built on evidence-based practice and addressed real-world challenges in adult learning settings.

Time Session & Facilitator Session Description
09:00–09:15 Welcome & Overview (J. Martinez) Opening remarks covering objectives, expected outcomes, and a brief explanation of delays in final documentation.
09:15–10:00 Foundations of Financial Literacy Pedagogy (Panel) Exploration of key concepts in financial literacy, adult education theory (andragogy), and proven instructional methods.
10:00–10:45 Challenges & Solutions in Teaching Financial Literacy (Group Activity) Interactive identification of common instructional hurdles using case studies and peer-led strategies for overcoming them.
10:45–11:00 Break Refreshments and informal networking.
11:00–11:45 Policy Landscape & Standards (M. Lee) Overview of national, state, and district financial literacy policies; discussion of required standards and curricular alignment.
11:45–12:30 Curriculum Enhancement & Resource Sharing (S. Clark) Collaborative review to identify curriculum gaps and exchange supplemental resources, with focused brainstorming on adaptation for diverse learner populations.
12:30–13:00 Action Planning & Collaborative Review Process (D. Kim) Assignment of specific tasks and responsibilities, timeline setting, and launch of a structured document review cycle.
13:00–13:30 Open Discussion & Pending Notes (All Attendees) Group reflection, Q&A, suggestions for next steps. Reminder to continue contributing while notes are completed.

Collaborative Documentation Structure

To maximize accessibility and facilitate collaborative work, the workshop notes are structured with:

  • Clear Section Headers: Distinct sections for each agenda item, facilitating quick navigation for users in Google Docs or Word.
  • Tables & Checklists: Visual organization of agenda topics, key findings, and action items, promoting clarity and team accountability.
  • Editable Format: Document is designed for real-time collaborative editing, with comment features active to capture feedback, track changes, and resolve issues efficiently.
  • Iterative Review: Multi-stage consultation involving educators, curriculum leaders, and policy experts, ensuring all perspectives are integrated before notes are finalized.

Best Practice Models and Resources

The workshop framework reflects leading practices from established organizations in financial literacy education and professional development. Notably, the structure and agenda align with models advocated by:

  • Jump$tart Coalition for Personal Financial Literacy: Their educator workshop structure emphasizes research-based instruction and actionable resource sharing.
  • National Endowment for Financial Education (NEFE): Facilitator guides promote both pedagogical rigor and practical application in adult education.
  • Council for Economic Education (CEE): Workshops feature robust agendas, alignment with policy, and comprehensive support for educators’ growth.
  • District-Level Professional Development: These local initiatives provide frameworks for structured action planning and sustained collaborative documentation.
