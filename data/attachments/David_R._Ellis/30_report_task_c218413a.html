Eastwood Middle School Financial Literacy Workshop

Feedback Review & Improvement Checklist

Executive Summary

The Financial Literacy Workshop at Eastwood Middle School aimed to strengthen students’ practical financial skills, focusing particularly on money management, budgeting, and responsible decision-making. Over several sessions, the program brought together students, teachers, and parents through a diverse format including lectures, interactive games, and real-world simulations. In addition to formal post-workshop surveys, feedback was captured from informal conversations and written comments, helping to assess the workshop’s impact on financial understanding, engagement, and ongoing discussion within families.

Key Findings:

  • Students demonstrated substantial growth in financial literacy, with average post-workshop survey scores increasing by 28% (from 52/100 to 80/100).
  • Engagement was highest in interactive components, especially budgeting games and group activities.
  • Teachers expressed interest in integrating financial concepts more directly into core curriculum and in having clearer assessment methods.
  • Parents appreciated real-world applications and reported greater family conversation around financial topics after the workshop.
  • Overall, the structure and activities reflected current best practices in middle grades financial education, prioritizing interactive, relevant, and evidence-based instruction.

This report provides a comprehensive review of the feedback, proposes targeted improvements for future workshops, and recommends specific strategies for deeper stakeholder engagement.

Stakeholder Engagement: Next Steps

Ongoing collaboration is essential for the continued success and relevance of financial literacy instruction. The strategies below are designed to maintain dialogue, adapt programming, and foster strong connections among students, faculty, and parents.

Faculty

  • Host a post-workshop debrief for teachers, presenting outcomes, collecting ideas for integration, and outlining changes.
  • Form an advisory panel of educators to guide future development, ensuring modules remain aligned with academic standards.

Parents

  • Distribute a family financial literacy guide with practical tips and discussion starters.
  • Invite parents to attend a pilot financial literacy session tailored to household needs and concerns.
  • Provide ongoing updates through newsletters, encouraging feedback and highlighting planned improvements.

Students & Student Leaders

  • Recruit student ambassadors to co-design workshop sessions and champion financial literacy across campus.
  • Hold regular student feedback forums, allowing young participants to share suggestions and keep learning experiences engaging and relevant.

Building Consensus & Continuous Improvement

  • Use structured faculty and parent meetings, as well as electronic polls, to prioritize enhancements.
  • Communicate clearly about planned changes, the evidence supporting them, and project progress.
  • Schedule periodic reviews before and after key implementation dates to evaluate improvements collaboratively.
