## Workshop Objectives

Drawing on established financial literacy frameworks, including those from Jump$tart Coalition, NEFE, and the FDIC Money Smart program, this workshop is designed to build practical skills for adults and youth in our community. Our goals are clear and focused:

- **Increase Financial Awareness:** Workshop participants will develop an understanding of core personal finance principles, tailored for both children and adults. Key topics include saving, budgeting, spending, and distinguishing needs from wants.
- **Demystify Budgeting:** We will use relatable, hands-on examples to teach realistic budgeting strategies, making these concepts engaging and accessible across ages.
- **Promote Family Financial Dialogue:** By encouraging open conversations about money between parents and children, we aim to strengthen communication around financial decisions and family values.
- **Develop Decision-Making Skills:** Participants will practice evaluating choices based on needs and wants, becoming more confident in making informed financial decisions.
- **Encourage Lifelong Learning:** We will connect families with resources for ongoing financial education beyond the workshop, reinforcing the value of continuous learning.

These objectives reflect best practices according to the Jump$tart Coalition’s National Standards for K–12 Personal Finance Education and are supported by NEFE and FDIC guidelines.[1][2][3]

## Proposed Activities

### Overview

Our activities are intentionally hands-on, interactive, and designed for families to learn together. We aim to make financial literacy accessible, engaging, and relevant to everyday life, recognizing the diverse backgrounds and ages of our attendees.

- **Icebreaker: “Money Memory” Game**  
  To build a welcoming atmosphere, we’ll begin with a sharing activity where participants recall and discuss memorable family experiences or lessons related to money, such as saving up for something special or learning about spending.

- **Parent-Student Budgeting Challenge**  
  Each family will receive scenario cards, like planning for a school trip or budgeting for a family celebration. Working together, they will:
    - Identify items that are essential versus optional (needs vs. wants)
    - Allocate a mock budget using play money or tokens provided
    - Discuss potential tradeoffs and strategies for saving
    - Present how they made their budgeting decisions to the group

  This activity puts financial decision-making into a familiar, family context and encourages teamwork as well as communication skills.

- **Interactive Learning Stations**  
  Three stations will allow participants to explore financial concepts in depth:
    - **Saving for a Goal:** Children will decorate personal savings jars, reinforcing the idea of setting goals and building the habit of saving.
    - **Smart Spending:** Families will compare prices and make spending decisions, practicing critical thinking about purchases.
    - **Financial Values Wall:** Everyone will contribute their ideas for responsible money management, adding notes to a collaborative display.

- **Group Reflection & Sharing**  
  At the close of the session, facilitators will guide participants in reflecting on what they learned, inviting them to share insights and intentions for how they’ll apply new habits at home.

By integrating practical activities, collaborative experiences, and meaningful reflection, the workshop follows best practices recommended by FDIC and NEFE for effective, inclusive financial education.[3][2]

## Featuring Jasper’s Drawing: Motivation Strategy

One highlight of our workshop will be featuring Jasper’s drawing, “Saving for a Dream.” This artwork will serve as a visual focal point and motivational tool, tying creative expression to the concept of financial goal-setting.

- **Display Logistics:**  
  Jasper’s drawing will be placed near the entrance and at the parent-student challenge station, framed and accompanied by a placard sharing Jasper’s story and the inspiration behind the piece.

- **Activity Integration:**  
  During the main budgeting challenge, facilitators will reference the drawing to initiate conversations about dreams and the importance of saving toward meaningful goals. Families will be encouraged to write down their own “dream savings goals” on sticky notes and affix these around the display, creating a collaborative “Dreams Wall.”

- **Promotional & Session Communication:**  
  Inclusion of Jasper’s drawing will be highlighted in pre-session materials and community flyers. At the workshop’s beginning, Jasper will be introduced to the group, and participants will be invited to reflect on what goals are most important to them.

Using visual storytelling not only makes the topic more relatable, especially for younger attendees, but also draws on NEFE’s evidence that motivational visuals enhance learning and engagement in financial literacy settings.[4]
