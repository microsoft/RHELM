Financial Literacy Workshop: Stakeholder Feedback Summary

Date: April 20, 2024

1. Overview

This summary consolidates feedback from key participants following the recent Financial Literacy Workshop, with the intention of informing the Education Policy Advisory Group’s ongoing review and program refinement efforts. Input from Alicia Torres and Ms. Ramirez, two engaged stakeholders, has been analyzed to identify actionable opportunities for improvement. Responses from David, the workshop facilitator, outline planned modifications rooted in current financial literacy education standards.

By directly tying feedback to evidence-based enhancements, this summary reflects a commitment to transparency and accountable, consensus-driven progress. All recommended changes align with recognized best practices for financial literacy instruction and are designed to foster overall program effectiveness and reach.

3. Workshop Facilitator’s Response and Planned Enhancements

David, the workshop facilitator, appreciates the thoughtful feedback from both Alicia Torres and Ms. Ramirez. Drawing from current education research and policy guidelines, several targeted changes are being implemented:

Content Development
Hands-on budgeting simulations will be central to future workshops, giving participants realistic scenarios to apply new skills. The curriculum will be differentiated to better support learners of varying financial backgrounds and competencies. These updates reflect guidance from the U.S. Department of Education and the OECD, both of which advocate experiential learning and tailored instruction to ensure engagement and retention [1][2].

Instructional Approach
Sessions will feature case studies directly reflecting the backgrounds and experiences of participants, making learning outcomes more relatable and actionable. The Jump$tart Coalition highlights that relatable examples significantly boost understanding and motivation in financial education [3]. This approach will help connect abstract concepts to everyday financial decisions.

Assessment and Support
A structured assessment process, including pre- and post-workshop evaluations, will be introduced. This enables both facilitators and participants to measure progress and understand the impact of the workshop. Protocols will follow the evaluation methods recommended by NEFE [4]. Additionally, take-home resource packets—in both print and digital formats—will be offered to support continued learning, consistent with best practices for program sustainability [5].

Rationale
These planned improvements are founded on government and academic recommendations supporting active learning, contextual relevance, and ongoing assessment. Multiple studies confirm that differentiated instruction and authentic assessment help students retain skills and apply them beyond the classroom. Stakeholder feedback remains essential, ensuring each program iteration is responsive to participant needs.
