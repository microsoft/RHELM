# My Weekly Routine Adaptation Plan for Back Recovery

## 1. Overview

Recovering from a back injury while juggling my roles as a financial literacy advocate, consultant, and family member has required me to rethink my daily routines. I've learned, both from physical therapy and behavioral health research, that real progress comes from gradual habit-building—just like in financial planning. Tiny, routine changes add up to greater well-being, and practicing consistency (as I do with my finances) has kept me moving forward without backsliding. I put together this weekly plan for myself, drawing on structured tools I'm familiar with, to help keep my recovery sustainable, minimize my risk of re-injury, and coordinate with my loved ones and healthcare team. I’m sharing it here in the hope that it can be useful to others—whether you're a client, colleague, or family member supporting someone through a similar journey.

### Why I Needed to Adapt My Routine

- **Holistic Recovery:** I couldn't just focus on my back. Every day, I had to pay attention to my physical recovery, but also how I sat, worked, relaxed, and managed stress. Making these adjustments in different areas of my life has helped me feel more in control and set the stage for lasting health improvements[1][2].
- **Habit-Based Progression:** Relying on inspiration just wasn’t enough. Borrowing from my financial planning background, I set up small, trackable habits that I could repeat and measure. Sticking to these habits—even when I was tired—helped me avoid setbacks and built my confidence week by week[3][4].
- **Integrating Family and Work:** My recovery couldn't happen in a vacuum. Every routine had to fit around my work calls, client deadlines, and time with my family. Open communication and teamwork, both at home and with my professional contacts, have been crucial to making this work in reality[5].

### Key Adaptations I Rely On

- Ergonomic seating and lumbar cushions (I bring mine everywhere!)
- Taking timed breaks: I set a 30–45 minute alarm, then get up and move
- No lifting, twisting, or bending—even when I feel “up for it”
- Hydration is part of my schedule, not just when I’m thirsty
- Built-in time for virtual health consultations and debriefs

## 3. My Exercise and Self-Care Checklist

Having a concrete checklist I can mark off each day keeps me honest about my habits and lets me spot patterns when things don’t go right. Here’s what I stick to, at home and when working:

- **Daily Movement**
   - Seated and standing stretches (pelvic tilts and gentle spinal rotations, as approved by my PT)
   - Light walks (usually 5–15 minutes)—I avoid slippery or uneven surfaces
   - Change posture every 30–45 minutes (phone reminders help)
- **Hydration**
   - I aim for at least 2 liters of water each day and track this in my planner
- **Rest**
   - One to two breaks for full rest per day (sometimes using heat or ice), 20–30 minutes each
   - I try to get 7–8 hours of sleep, lights out at a consistent bedtime
- **Workstation Ergonomics**
   - Always use my adjustable chair with lumbar support
   - Keep feet flat, knees at hip height, wrists above the keyboard
   - Sit up straight and avoid crossing my legs (still working on this one!)
- **Stress Reduction**
   - Mindfulness or breathing exercises daily, about 10 minutes each
   - Journaling any pain or mood spikes so I can discuss triggers with my PT
- **Self-Care**
   - Use heat or ice packs after high-activity periods, as my doctor recommends
   - Log pain and energy every night, rating out of 10—this helps me track trends
- **Adjusting Professional Tasks**
   - Use my headset for long calls
   - Tackle complex projects in short bursts, broken up by breaks

*I usually print this list and check off tasks daily, which makes it easy to share updates with my PT and family.*

## 4. How Monica and Jasper Support My Recovery

One of the biggest lessons I've learned is that recovering alone isn’t realistic—or necessary. Monica and Jasper have truly been teammates through this.

- **Dividing Up Chores:** Monica and Jasper now manage most cooking, shopping, and outdoor chores. I chip in by supervising or taking on tasks that don’t require bending or lifting.
- **Communication:** We have quick family check-ins each afternoon to talk about chores, changes to routines, or if I need more help on a tough day. It’s not always a formal meeting—sometimes it’s just a few minutes after dinner.
- **Encouragement:** Nothing keeps me more accountable than Monica’s regular reminders to stretch or Jasper’s excitement to update my water tracker. Celebrating little victories together makes the process less frustrating.
- **Shared Calendars:** We mark upcoming PT appointments, important reminders, and who’s handling what on our shared calendar. Jasper loves to help organize (and sometimes color-code) household tasks.
- **Problem-Solving as a Team:** When a routine isn’t working, we openly ask, “How can we make this better for everyone?” This approach keeps us solution-focused and avoids blame. Everyone’s voice, even Jasper’s, counts.

## 5. Weekly Review & Adjustments—My Financial Planning Approach

As a financial consultant, I know the value of regular check-ins and making small course corrections. I’ve applied this to my health just as I’d advise clients to do for their finances.

### My Weekly Goal-Setting

- I write down clear, measurable goals (e.g., “Walk 15 minutes every day without pain” or “Use ergonomic chair for all work calls this week”).
- I highlight one or two priority habits each week—last week, it was standing up every 45 minutes.
- I set a hydration target and use a daily log.

### Questions I Reflect On Each Week

- What routines worked best for me, and how did they help?
- What got in the way, and was there a way around it?
- Did I accept enough help from Monica and Jasper, or try to tough it out solo?
- Any surprises—good or bad—that came up this week?
- What small adjustment can I try next for a smoother week ahead?

#### Using Financial Tools for Health

Just as I use spreadsheets and goal trackers for client plans, I’ve set up a simple tracking app for my health routines. Seeing “returns” on my efforts—better energy, mood, or productivity—keeps me motivated and gives me data to share with my care team[6].
