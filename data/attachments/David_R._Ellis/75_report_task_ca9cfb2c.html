Prepared by: David Ellis, Monica Ellis
Date: October 1, 2024
Location: Nashville, Tennessee

Executive Summary

We met on October 1 to thoroughly review our household budget here in Nashville, aiming to reflect the newest changes in our family's finances. With the start of the new school year, some expenses—especially related to education—have increased, while others, such as seasonal utilities, also needed adjustment as autumn sets in. Following guidance from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the National Endowment for Financial Education, we focused our discussion on practical strategies: regular budget reviews, open dialogue between us and our children, and making sure every major spending decision is made together.

During this session, we decided to increase what we set aside for the kids’ educational needs and add more to our emergency savings. We also recalibrated our utility budget, anticipating higher costs as the weather cools. To help us stay on track with our long-term goals—like building our emergency fund and saving for retirement—we agreed to cut back on discretionary spending. Transparency and teamwork continue to be the foundation of our financial approach, which makes us confident in our progress and in passing good habits on to our children.

Adjustments, New Items, and Family Observations

Key Adjustments

  • Education: We added $140 more to this category, thanks to extra fall supplies and activity fees that weren’t part of our summer budget. The kids’ new extracurriculars and school field trips account for most of this jump.
  • Emergency Fund: We increased our monthly contribution by $100. We're actively building toward the recommended three-month reserve, aiming to strengthen our safety net if job security or health issues ever arise. Knowing we’re closing in on that goal gives us real peace of mind.
  • Utilities: With the weather turning cooler, our electricity bill in October is estimated to go up by $30. We compared our utility bills from last year and decided this increase is reasonable, but we’ll keep tracking it into the colder months.
  • Discretionary Spending: As a family, we voted to reduce entertainment, dining out, and clothing purchases. Our motivation is simple—we want to build our savings and focus on what matters most, such as future travel and home improvements.

Family Trends and Reflections

  • Education costs always spike when a new semester starts, and this year is no exception. We’re already planning ahead for additional fees in November, especially for clubs and tech supplies.
  • We expect our heating bills to keep climbing as we get deeper into autumn, so we’re preparing by setting aside extra funds in our utilities budget.
  • Medical spending has stabilized now that the kids’ routine checkups are finished; we anticipate little variation before year-end unless something unexpected happens.
  • With higher savings contributions, we feel more secure—especially after reading CFPB’s emergency preparedness recommendations.
  • Reducing dining out has been easier than we thought, especially since Monica enjoys cooking and the kids love to help. Maintaining and repairing the house ourselves also allowed us to save more this month.

New Additions to Our Budget

  • We’ve finally started a “Future Home Repair” savings fund after researching common issues older Nashville homes face; just knowing we’re setting money aside for roof, HVAC, or plumbing makes us less anxious.
  • We subscribed to a monthly children’s book box, bundled into our education spending. Seeing the kids’ excitement when their books arrive reminds us how much lifelong learning means to our family.

Action Steps and Recommendations

What We’re Doing in October

  • We’re organizing an insurance policy review later this month to check for potential upgrades or savings, hoping to streamline our coverage and reduce overlapping policies.
  • Monica plans to step up our grocery-saving strategies by leveraging coupons and cashback apps. She’s already joined a local coupon swap group to help stretch our food budget further.
  • David will export discretionary spending from our banking app for better categorization, aiming for clearer reports and fewer surprises.
  • We’ll host a financial literacy chat with the kids next weekend, using simple budgeting games and example stories to get them involved.

Tasks for Next Month’s Review

  • We’ll compare our emergency fund total against the recommended three-months’ worth of family expenses using CFPB guidelines. That benchmark motivates us to stay consistent.
  • David will review our options for alternative utility providers and bundled service discounts, exploring whether we can lower bills without sacrificing quality.
  • Before quarter-end, we’ll reassess our retirement account contributions and available plans. Our goal is to make sure we're not missing out on employer matches or investment opportunities.

These checklists and shared responsibilities help us remain accountable and keep the conversation ongoing, in line with NEFE’s approach to collaborative household budgeting.

Budget Structure and Reporting Standards

We build every monthly budget from a foundation of established best practices:

  • Income, fixed expenses, variable expenses, and savings are clearly separated, so we can spot trends and track impacts. We follow CFPB’s recommended structure for spending and saving plans (see CFPB worksheet).
  • Every line’s variance—up or down—is reviewed side-by-side to understand how family decisions play out month to month, which aligns with NEFE’s household budgeting strategies (NEFE guidelines).
  • We’re meticulous with our tables and records, which makes delegating tasks and improving our habits much easier.

Reflection: Family Financial Habits and Lessons

Open communication and joint decision-making have truly changed the way we manage money. When we sit down together—whether it’s at the kitchen table with laptops or during a quick closet cleanout with the kids—we talk about every major category and make adjustments as a family. That consensus-building helps us prioritize what’s most important and stay focused on our essential goals, such as saving and education.

Tracking our spending in detail has revealed habits we wouldn’t have noticed otherwise; for example, it became clear last month that frequent takeout lunches were eating into our savings. Changing that pattern was easier knowing we had a team approach. We regularly discuss what works and what doesn’t, which keeps everyone engaged—even our youngest, who loves sorting receipts and guessing totals.

Adjusting our budget proactively, rather than in response to a crisis, gives us a sense of control. It has also made us more adaptable, so we’re prepared when an unexpected bill shows up or when school expenses rise. These habits foster discipline, teach our children valuable life skills, and make us confident that we’re laying a strong financial foundation for our future.
