## Physician Recommendations and Today's Wellness Guidance

### Summary of Telehealth Physician Recommendations (March 11)

**Activity Targets**
- Sustain at least 15–30 minutes of moderate physical activity per day, focusing on joint-friendly options—walking, stretching routines, or using resistance bands.
- Balance out these sessions with breaks. Listen to my body and pause if discomfort creeps above a 3 out of 10.

**Pain Management**
- Keep an eye on any new aches or changes in discomfort, especially as I add new exercises.
- Maintain activity levels that keep pain mild (ideally no higher than 2 or 3).

**Progression**
- If pain stays steady and low (at or below 2) throughout the week, gradually add more time or intensity—about 10% more each week is reasonable.

**Pacing and Rest**
- Spread out activity with mini rest stops, especially during new routines. This has helped with fatigue in the past.

**Symptom Tracking**
- Carefully log any new pain points, their location, and whether they're sharp, dull, or persistent. Note if fatigue is a bigger issue or if there's anything different about my recovery afterwards.

**Hydration and Nutrition**
- Stay on top of water intake, before and after exercise. Eat a nutritious meal or snack within two hours after finishing, aiming for a good balance of protein, carbs, and some healthy fat.

**Next Steps**
- Bring this log (and Fitbit data) to next month's appointment so the care team can offer feedback and adjust my plan as needed.

### Actionable Guidance for Ongoing Wellness

**Stay Consistent**
- Daily tracking keeps me honest and helps catch patterns quickly. It's better to exercise a little each day than to overdo it sporadically.

**Listen to My Body**
- No pushing through anything more than mild discomfort. On tougher days, it’s okay to shorten or modify my routine.

**Maximize My Fitbit Data**
- Step count and heart rate are great objective markers. Target at least 7,500 steps and scan for heart rate spikes that don’t match activity (so I can bring it up with my doctor).

**Mix It Up**
- Variety helps keep motivation up and prevents any one muscle group from getting overworked. I'm aiming to blend walks, strength exercises, and relaxing activities like yoga or stretching.

**Support System**
- Exercising with Monica or involving a friend always improves my mood and holds me accountable. Even a short text about what I accomplished feels good.

**Share Openly at Appointments**
- Up-to-date logs mean I don’t have to rely on memory, and my doctor gets a clearer sense of what’s helping or where new issues pop up.

## Best Practices for Daily Logging and Communication

- **Clear Table Layout:** Using the same format daily makes it easy both to update and to spot trends over time.
- **Flexible Entries:** Extra open rows allow me to add spontaneous activities or unusual events without reformatting.
- **Editable Fields:** Duration, intensity, pain levels, and other specifics are easy to tweak before sharing.
- **Professional Communication:** Keeping the language factual but personal ensures all parties understand what happened and how I felt physically.
- **Integrating Tech Data:** Pulling Fitbit data into the log means a more objective view of my progress and helps my doctor adjust recommendations.
- **Easy to Share:** I save this as a digital file (spreadsheet and PDF), and will upload it to my online health portal before appointments for the best continuity of care.
