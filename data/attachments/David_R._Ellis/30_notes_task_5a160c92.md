## Reasons Behind Each Purchase

### Marigold Seeds

I’ve seen firsthand how marigolds work wonders as companion plants, attracting pollinators and keeping pests in check—especially around our veggie beds. With packets under $3 from trusted brands like Burpee or Home Depot, they fit our budget without sacrificing quality. I always look for non-GMO, untreated options and recyclable packaging, keeping both garden health and environmental impact front of mind. Knowing these flowers also help fill our beds with color made them impossible to pass up. [1][2]

### Trowel

My old trowel had finally worn out, so I made replacement a top priority. After reading reviews and considering our sustainability goals, I went with a model that combines recycled steel and an FSC-certified handle—part of Fiskars’ eco-friendly line. This investment costs a few dollars more upfront, but the improved durability means I won’t be replacing it soon. I find it comfortable during long planting sessions, and it feels good knowing I’m choosing responsibly made tools. [3]

### Compost

Quality compost forms the backbone of our organic approach, so I’m always careful in this department. David picked up a bag for Monica, making sure to get one that’s OMRI-listed (Organic Material Review Institute) and made from locally sourced organic matter. Bulk pricing during the spring sale made this a particularly smart buy. The compost not only improves our soil but also aligns with our effort to minimize transportation emissions and support local producers. [4][5]

### Gloves

We go through gardening gloves quickly, and disposables just create too much waste. This time, I searched out reusable gloves made with sustainable bamboo fiber and recycled fabrics. The multi-pack offered was perfect for our family, covering all sizes and letting us share a set, which cuts down on both trash and expenses. They’re comfortable, washable, and durable enough for tougher garden chores. [6][7]

## Additional Notes & Next Steps

- **Overdue Replacements:**  
  I finally replaced my old trowel, which was long overdue. The new one has already made planting feel less like hard labor.
  
- **Glove Sizing:**  
  The glove multipack claims to fit a range of sizes, but I’ll double-check everyone in the household is covered. If not, I’ll pick up an extra pair or two.
  
- **Pending Items:**  
  We’re holding off on buying organic pest controls until we see if the marigolds keep insect pressures down. If issues persist, I’ll add traps or natural sprays next. I've also noticed we're running low on seedling trays and soaker hoses—if we extend our garden or want to streamline watering, these will be at the top of the list.

- **Budget Tracking:**  
  Every choice this week had an eye on value. By prioritizing durability (like the trowel and gloves) and seeking out deals (multipacks, seasonal sales), I’ve managed to stretch the garden fund. It’s true, eco-friendly versions often cost a bit more at checkout, but the longer lifespan and reduced need for replacements make these wise investments.
  
- **Shopping Strategies Going Forward:**  
  I’ll keep focusing on local products when I can, to save on both cost and environmental impact. Certifications like OMRI and FSC remain my benchmarks for soil amendments and tools. Shopping during sales or as part of a group maximizes savings—coordinating with family has helped avoid any unnecessary double-buys.
