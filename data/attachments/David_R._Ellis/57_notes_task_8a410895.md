## Exercise Safety: Movements to Avoid

**Dr. Patel has strongly advised me to avoid the following:**
- Free weights (such as dumbbells, barbells, kettlebells, or any weighted machines)
- Any high-impact activities (including running, jumping, plyometric exercises, or box jumps)
- Explosive or rapid power movements (like power cleans or snatches)
- Any movement that leads to pain, dizziness, or a sense of instability
- Contact sports or similar high-risk activities (unless I receive medical clearance to restart them)

**Why these restrictions?**  
Current best-practice guidelines emphasize the use of bodyweight and resistance band exercises for adults who require low-impact, joint-protective routines—often due to injury recovery, recent inactivity, or specific medical circumstances[1][2]. This approach minimizes risks while still promoting full-body strength and stability.

## Weekly Exercise Plan

The following weekly schedule balances all major muscle groups and incorporates regular core and mobility work. The format is flexible—if work or life gets in the way, I’ll adapt, but will aim for at least three active resistance/mobility sessions per week. Each session is designed for morning or evening, depending on when I can consistently fit it into my day.

### Weekly Resistance Band & Bodyweight Routine

| Day         | Main Circuit Exercises                                                              | Mobility & Core Routine                        | Focus/Notes                                   |
|-------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **Monday**  | Bodyweight Squats, Resistance Band Rows, Knee/Wall Push-ups, Band Lateral Walks, Glute Bridges | Full circuit as outlined below                 | Lower body/upper body split; focus on form    |
| **Tuesday** | Step-ups (low surface), Band Chest Press, Seated Band Pull-Aparts, Wall Slides, Bird Dogs       | Full circuit                                   | Full-body activation, gentle pace             |
| **Wednesday**| Stationary Lunges (support as needed), Band Deadlift, Incline Push-ups, Band External Rotations, Plank | Full circuit                                   | Lower/upper body split; monitor knee comfort  |
| **Thursday**| *Active Recovery Only*                                                             | Mobility, gentle stretching, deep breathing    | Rest/light day: focus on flexibility          |
| **Friday**  | Squat/Band Combo, Band Lat Pull, Modified Dips (Chair), Pallof Press (Band), Side Plank     | Full circuit                                   | Full-body, extra core focus                   |
| **Saturday**| Calf Raises, Band Upright Row, Wall Sit, Cat-Cow, Clamshells (with Band)                  | Full circuit                                   | Gentle repetition, stability enhancement      |
| **Sunday**  | Rest or Light Walk/Yoga                                                            | Gentle stretch and breathing                   | Full rest or mindful movement, as desired     |

- **Each main circuit:** 2–3 sets/exercise, 8–12 reps (as tolerated)
- **Rest:** 30–60 seconds between sets, or as needed
- **Abbreviations:** BW = Bodyweight, Band = Resistance Band

**If I feel fatigued or experience any pain or discomfort, I will pause and adjust my routine as needed.**

## Exercise Rationale and Motivational Strategies

### Why I’m Following This Approach

- **Low-impact resistance and bodyweight exercises** help me build strength and muscular stamina without putting excessive stress on my joints or risk of injury—something especially important as I work through recovery and return to activity[1][2].
- **Daily mobility and core exercises** improve posture, reduce common aches (like back or neck tension), and support sustained energy and resilience both at work and outside it[3].
- **Progressive, varied scheduling** keeps me engaged and prevents my routine from feeling monotonous, which is crucial for sticking with the program despite my busy calendar.
- **Tracking small wins and daily reflections** builds self-efficacy, which has been linked with better exercise adherence and long-term success[4].
- **Modeling discipline and consistency** reminds me that my personal commitment to wellness can also positively influence those around me, reinforcing the values I seek to embody in leadership.
