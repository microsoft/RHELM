**Date:** June 20, 2024

## Executive Summary

As of June 20, 2024, the Summer Outreach Workshop materials have undergone a comprehensive revision to strengthen financial literacy for middle school students, with a particular focus on engagement, clarity, and relevance. These updates were driven by direct feedback from Eastwood Middle School stakeholders, recent educational research, and the need for well-aligned visual components—especially the thoughtful inclusion of Jasper’s artwork. The goal was to create a more interactive and accessible learning experience, making key financial concepts easier to understand and apply. The anticipated outcome is a marked improvement in students’ grasp of financial principles, better retention through visual and participatory elements, and increased flexibility for educators to adapt the materials to different learning styles.

## Rationale Behind the Changes

### Responding to Stakeholder Needs

Eastwood Middle School faculty and students provided valuable input through surveys and feedback sessions. Teachers expressed a desire for more kid-friendly definitions, especially for terms like “budget” and “interest”, and highlighted the importance of age-appropriate examples. There was a clear demand for engaging, relevant visuals, with students noting that illustrations and comics helped them remember and relate to financial concepts.

### Applying Educational Research

Current research in financial literacy education demonstrates that visual aids and interactive activities significantly improve retention and comprehension. According to the Jump$tart Coalition National Standards and Richard Mayer’s principles on multimedia learning, reducing redundancy and preventing cognitive overload enables learners to fully grasp new material. Interactive scenarios and peer discussions, as recommended by the OECD PISA Financial Literacy Framework, allow students to connect theory with practical, real-world application and encourage collaborative problem-solving.

### Alignment with Workshop Goals

Every change made directly supports the workshop’s overarching goals:

- **Comprehension:** Simplified definitions and expanded examples ensure core concepts are clear to all students.
- **Engagement:** Integrating artwork and interactive elements makes the material inviting and memorable.
- **Independent Thinking:** Activities like peer discussions and decision-making flowcharts encourage critical evaluation and personal reflection.
- **Tools for Continuing Growth:** The addition of checklists and progress trackers equips students to actively monitor and assess their own financial knowledge over time.

Jasper’s artwork was featured throughout the materials, not simply to decorate, but to forge emotional connections and make abstract concepts tangible.

## Recommendations for Further Improvement

Building on this revision, the following recommendations address opportunities for ongoing enhancement:

1. **Expand Interactive Elements:** Adding more scenario-based activities and peer discussions will further support practical skill-building and active participation.
2. **Diversify Visual Representation:** Inviting other students to contribute artwork—in addition to Jasper—will personalize the materials and broaden representation.
3. **Introduce Adaptive Tools:** Launching digital checklists and progress tracking sheets will help students monitor their financial literacy growth continuously.
4. **Establish Regular Review Cycles:** Frequent feedback sessions with both school partners and the student panel will support iterative refinements and maintain relevance.
5. **Implement Data-Driven Assessment:** Collect and analyze student learning data before and after the workshop to directly measure impact and guide future updates.
6. **Monitor Policy Changes:** Continuously review evolving educational standards to ensure materials remain aligned with state and national guidelines.

**Immediate Action Steps:**
- Distribute a standardized self-assessment checklist at the close of each module.
- Schedule quarterly review meetings with school partners.
- Develop a centralized feedback repository for streamlined updates.
- Facilitate teacher peer-observation sessions to encourage professional growth.
