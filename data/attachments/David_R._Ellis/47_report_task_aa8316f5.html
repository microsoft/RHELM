Foundation Summer Initiatives Action Plan

Advancing Financial Literacy Education

For Board Presentation & Program Implementation
Prepared June 2, 2024

Executive Summary

The Foundation Summer Initiatives Action Plan outlines a clear and actionable strategy for promoting financial literacy education in Summer 2024. Developed through collaboration between David R. Ellis (Financial Literacy Advocate & Consultant) and Alicia Torres (Foundation Program Lead), the plan builds on recent research and best practices to address the diverse needs of children, adults, and policymakers in our community.

Key objectives include the creation of age-appropriate curriculum, integration of experiential learning models, regular program evaluation, and a commitment to continuous improvement. By emphasizing accountability, stakeholder engagement, and evidence-based methods, the plan aims to foster financial inclusion and economic resilience. Board members and other stakeholders in education policy are invited to review the plan and support its implementation to ensure lasting impact.

Summary of Discussion

During the virtual meeting, participants agreed to prioritize financial literacy initiatives based on the most current evidence from academic research and recent policy developments. The group emphasized the importance of adapting content for various age groups using proven strategies, such as interactive workshops for children and self-paced modules for adults. Regular check-ins and progress reviews were identified as essential to maintaining program fidelity and responding promptly to challenges.

A consensus emerged around tracking all action items with clear responsibility assignments and defined deadlines. The team scheduled a mid-summer evaluation for late July, to assess program outcomes and make any necessary adjustments. The plan also draws extensively on the Foundation’s recently published co-authored report (Ellis & Torres, 2024), ensuring that curriculum and stakeholder engagement practices reflect the latest research. Some meeting logistics—such as full attendee lists, detailed agendas, and finalized evaluation metrics—will be confirmed following further board input.

Supporting Evidence and Rationale

The decision to design age-appropriate curriculum and utilize experiential learning is supported by a substantial body of research. Studies have shown that financial literacy interventions are most effective when tailored to cognitive development, and when they integrate hands-on activities. Additionally, nonprofit management literature highlights the importance of milestone tracking, collaborative evaluation, and adaptability to changing community needs. Insights from the Foundation’s recent co-authored report underscore the value of assessment protocols and community involvement in driving meaningful outcomes.

Next Steps & Recommendations

1. Strengthen Collaboration and Feedback Loops

  • Bi-weekly Team Meetings:
    Regular check-ins will keep implementation on track, promote transparency, and allow the team to respond quickly to any challenges.
  • Stakeholder Engagement:
    Educators, community partners, and program participants will be invited to provide feedback at key points, supporting iterative improvements.

2. Tailor Education for Different Audiences

  • Children:
  • Launch interactive, developmentally appropriate workshops, utilizing games and real-life simulations to build foundational skills.
  • Employ activities highlighted in the co-authored report to enhance engagement.
  • Adults:
  • Offer virtual modules designed for self-paced learning, incorporating current policy updates and practical financial tools.
  • Organize peer learning sessions to encourage sharing of experiences and foster a sense of community.
  • Policymakers:
  • Share interim data and findings from ongoing assessments to inform public discussions.
  • Prepare advocacy materials based on program outcomes to support future funding and expansion.

3. Ensure Accountability and Rigorous Evaluation

  • Assessment Protocols:
    Use research-backed pre- and post-program surveys to measure improvements in financial knowledge and behavior.
  • Structured Reporting:
    Progress and outcomes will be documented through consultative assignments and regular reports, ensuring transparency at every stage.
  • Scheduled Reviews:
    Formal evaluations will be carried out at mid-summer and after program completion, with recommendations for further action.

4. Embrace Continuous Adaptation

  • Flexible Program Design:
    The initiative will respond to participant feedback by refining curriculum, delivery methods, and resources, ensuring relevance to diverse community needs.
  • Adjustment Based on Data:
    Interim results will inform any necessary changes, maintaining a cycle of continuous improvement.

5. Finalize Outstanding Elements

  • Board Review:
    Board members will confirm the composition of the program evaluation team and agree upon specific success metrics during this month's meeting.
  • Stakeholder Outreach:
    The final list of community partners and outreach plans will be completed by the end of June to maximize participation and support.

All board members and program staff are encouraged to approve this action plan, initiate remaining assignments, and maintain a focus on rigorous review and adaptation. The ultimate goal is to maximize the initiative's positive impact on financial literacy and community wellbeing. All updates and adaptations will be included in the final Foundation Summer Initiatives Evaluation Report.
