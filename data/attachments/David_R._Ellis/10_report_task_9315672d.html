Session Descriptions

1. Welcome & Introductions

Objectives:
Start the day by establishing a supportive atmosphere and getting to know each other. We’ll discuss personal financial goals and set intentions for the workshop.

How We’ll Do This:
We'll kick things off with a “Money Memory” icebreaker, inviting participants to share a brief story or thought about money from their life. Next, we'll introduce ourselves and discuss what we hope to gain from the workshop. This open sharing helps build consensus about the topics and skills most important to the group.

Why It Matters:
By voicing individual goals, participants lay the groundwork for focusing their learning throughout the day and set the tone for collaborative growth.

2. Foundations of Financial Literacy

Objectives:
Establish a foundational understanding of key financial concepts—covering earning, saving, spending, and credit. Encourage participants to reflect honestly on their financial strengths and challenges.

How We’ll Do This:
In small groups, we’ll talk through real-life “budget blunders and wins”—lessons learned from managing money. We’ll use concept mapping to build shared financial vocabulary and identify patterns within habits.

Why It Matters:
Participants will use the starter worksheet to assess their financial habits. This activity provides a concrete snapshot of current practices, setting up personalized learning for the rest of the workshop.

3. Budgeting Workshop with Dr. Greene

Objectives:
Dive deeper into effective budgeting strategies that are relevant for adult learners. Address common stumbling blocks and empower participants with solutions tailored to realistic scenarios.

How We’ll Do This:
Dr. Greene will lead a dynamic Q&A session, drawing from participants’ pre-submitted questions about budgeting challenges. Together, we’ll analyze relatable scenarios—focusing on practical adjustments that work for Nashville residents and beyond.

Why It Matters:
Participants will build mock budgets using local expense data, strengthening their ability to adapt budgeting principles to their unique situations.

4. Interactive Budgeting Activity

Objectives:
Strengthen budgeting skills by practicing with real-world numbers and case studies. Encourage teamwork and consensus-building around financial decision-making.

How We’ll Do This:
Working through sample data and clear instructions, facilitators will guide participants as they build budgets and negotiate trade-offs in group settings. The activity concludes with a review and open feedback.

Why It Matters:
By actively engaging in budget-building—and discussing decisions as a team—participants learn practical strategies and gain constructive feedback that they can apply to their own finances.

5. Policy, Advocacy & Local Tools

Objectives:
Increase awareness of community resources and policies that support financial wellness. Motivate participants to put their learning into action beyond the workshop.

How We’ll Do This:
We’ll organize a resource “scavenger hunt,” encouraging participants to explore tables and online materials. Short presentations by local advocates will introduce relevant programs and policy updates.

Why It Matters:
Participants will draft personal resource action plans, pinpointing which tools and organizations can best support their financial goals.

6. Consensus Building & Next Steps

Objectives:
Summarize workshop takeaways and solidify individual and group commitments. Foster ongoing accountability for financial improvement.

How We’ll Do This:
In a closing group circle, we’ll define short- and long-term financial goals, and participants can sign up for post-workshop accountability partnerships. Each person completes a goal checklist and reflection worksheet to guide their next steps.

Why It Matters:
Participants leave the workshop with a clear action plan, new community connections, and tools to keep moving forward.

Budgeting Activity (Dr. Greene’s Revised Approach)

Dr. Greene’s budget activity is designed to demystify financial planning for adults, providing clarity, context, and actionable steps. Here’s how the process unfolds:

Step Instructions Sample Scenario Data Expected Learning Outcome
1 Begin by reviewing a scenario: a single adult renting in Nashville with typical monthly expenses. Monthly Income: $2,800
Rent: $1,000
Utilities: $150
Groceries: $350
Transportation: $120
Health Insurance: $200
Phone/Internet: $90
Savings: $100
Discretionary: $180		 Participants identify fixed vs. variable expenses and visualize a complete budget.
2 Discuss in groups where adjustments can realistically be made. For example: reducing grocery or discretionary spending, comparing transportation options. Learn how to reach consensus on spending priorities and negotiate possible trade-offs.
3 Individually revise the budget based on group input and personal priorities using provided templates. Each participant adapts the sample numbers, considering both group recommendations and personal constraints. Develop hands-on budgeting skills and apply critical thinking to one’s own financial situation.
4 Review final outcomes and discuss the emotions and challenges that come up when making financial changes. No new data required—focus is on sharing reflections and learning through open conversation. Build financial resilience and confidence in adjusting plans in response to real-life circumstances.

Expected Learning Outcomes:
- Ability to distinguish essential from optional expenses
- Confidence in updating and adjusting budgets for personal goals
- Greater skill in negotiating financial choices and understanding their consequences
- Enhanced self-awareness about the emotional impact of money decisions
- Increased practical competence in managing living expenses and resource allocation
