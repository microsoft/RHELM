### Target Participants  
We welcome:
- K-12 educators from public and private schools, as well as those working in adult education
- Leaders and staff from community organizations
- Youth advocacy coordinators  
Additionally, we extend invitations to:
- Policymakers working in education or financial equity  
- Nonprofit volunteers focusing on community or family finance

### Intended Learning Outcomes  
By the end of the workshop, participants will:
- Master the key principles of personal and family financial management
- Gain practical tools for facilitating engaging activities for all age groups—children, teens, and adults
- Apply culturally responsive techniques to ensure relevance across Nashville’s diverse communities
- Identify, access, and tailor recommended financial literacy resources and technology tools (like calculator apps)
- Develop actionable plans to incorporate financial literacy into educational programs and community initiatives

## Guidance and Feedback from Dr. Samuel Greene

During a recent phone consultation, Dr. Greene emphasized several strategies that underpin an effective and inclusive financial literacy workshop:

- **Accessibility First:** Every handout, activity, and resource should be accessible—considering braille, large print, and clear layout options. Whenever possible, offer simplified versions suitable for neurodiverse participants.
- **Engagement Across Ages:** Stories and visuals help capture the interest of children, while adults connect with real-world scenarios. Policymakers and advocates benefit from case studies and data-driven examples.
- **Inclusive Facilitation:** Structure group activities to encourage contributions from all participants. Use open-ended prompts and flexible formats to cultivate a broad spectrum of ideas and perspectives.
- **Pacing and Movement:** The agenda should build in regular breaks and include a variety of activity formats (individual, pair, group). Flexible room arrangements allow participants freedom to move and refocus.
- **Technical Reliability:** Arrive early for setup and thorough device checks; always have backup solutions available for critical tech, including slides and apps.
- **Responsive Logistics:** Dietary needs should be confirmed well in advance, and logistical information—such as directions and emergency contacts—should be distributed clearly before the workshop.
- **Dynamic Curriculum:** Trust in the foundation of the curriculum, but be ready to adapt according to participant feedback and discussion. When participants share their experiences, allow those stories to guide deeper exploration.
- **Consensus and Adaptation:** Employ consensus-building methods for gathering group feedback, and remain ready to adjust activities in real time to maximize engagement and learning.

Dr. Greene’s recommendations have informed multiple planning decisions, particularly our approach to inclusion, pace, and real-time responsiveness.
