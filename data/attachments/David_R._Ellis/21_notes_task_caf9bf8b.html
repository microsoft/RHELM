Spotlight Recipe: Hearty Lentil Soup (🟢)

This week I really wanted to try a new, protein-packed soup that I could make in a single pot for easy cleanup. Here’s how I made it:

Ingredients: - 1 tbsp olive oil - 1 yellow onion, chopped - 2 carrots, peeled and chopped - 2 celery stalks, chopped - 3 garlic cloves, minced - 1 tsp ground cumin - 1 tsp smoked paprika - 1 cup dried green or brown lentils, rinsed - 1 can (400g) diced tomatoes - 6 cups low-sodium vegetable broth - 1 bay leaf - Salt and pepper (to taste) - 2 cups chopped spinach (optional, but great for nutrition)

How I Made It: 1. I heated the olive oil in a large pot over medium, then sautéed the onion, carrots, and celery for about 5–6 minutes until everything softened. 2. Next, I stirred in the garlic, cumin, and smoked paprika, letting them cook for a minute to bring out their aroma. 3. I added the lentils, diced tomatoes, broth, and bay leaf, then brought everything up to a boil. 4. Once boiling, I reduced the heat to a gentle simmer and cooked the soup uncovered for about 30–35 minutes (just until the lentils were soft). 5. After fishing out the bay leaf, I added the chopped spinach and let it wilt into the soup for extra color and vitamins. 6. Salt and pepper went in at the end. I served this with whole wheat rolls, and everyone was happy—even Jasper snuck a few bites.

Using Every Sweet Potato (🥔)

I bought a batch of sweet potatoes on Monday determined not to let any go to waste. This week, I’ve spread them out across several dinners:

  • Wednesday Dinner: Roasted alongside chicken thighs and broccoli for an easy sheet pan meal.
  • Saturday Dinner: Cubed and stir-fried with tofu and other veggies served over rice—so filling and colorful.
  • Monday Dinner: Tossed with sausage and peppers, roasted all together on a sheet pan.

For all these, I used about 1–2 medium sweet potatoes per meal, either cubed or sliced depending on the dish. The leftovers make for quick snacks or additions to salads too.

Our Family’s Food Needs and Preferences

Planning has become second nature for me, but I’m always careful to check off a few important things:

  • Vegetarian Swaps: When anyone in the family wants a vegetarian meal, I make easy swaps (like tofu or beans) for any meat.
  • Gluten-Free Options: With some friends and family sensitive to gluten, I keep gluten-free pasta and bread on hand for simple substitutions.
  • Nut Allergies: I avoid nuts in snacks if we’re hosting, or swap in sunbutter for things like celery sticks.
  • Dairy-Free Choices: Greek yogurt and cheese are staples, but I always have plant-based options in the fridge too.

How This Routine Supports Our Family’s Wellness and Budget

I’ve found that a simple meal plan makes a surprising difference, both financially and in keeping me sane through the busy weeks. Here’s what has helped us most:

  • Repurposing Ingredients: Sweet potatoes, spinach, and lentils pull double or triple duty, so I get my money’s worth and cut down on forgotten food at the back of the fridge.
  • Affordable Choices: Making soup or using what’s left in the pantry first means our grocery bill stays reasonable. Listing out everything keeps me focused at the store and curbs impulse buying.
  • Batch Cooking and Leftovers: Recipes like lentil soup or sheet pan meals are perfect for making extra. Leftovers turn into lunch for the next day or quick snacks.
  • Family Input: Including favorites—especially for picky eaters like Jasper—means fewer dinner battles and a more positive vibe around meals.
  • Building Healthy Habits: There’s a little of everything—lean protein, whole grains, lots of fruits and vegetables. I want these meals to lay the groundwork for everyone’s lasting health and happy mealtimes.
