## Purpose of Meeting

This meeting centered on advancing current efforts in financial literacy education policy. The conversation built on best practices recommended by the OECD and the National Endowment for Financial Education (NEFE), ensuring our actions are aligned with global standards and have measurable impact.

## Discussion Highlights

David Ellis opened by reaffirming the necessity for a structured, evidence-driven approach in integrating financial literacy across academic and community platforms. He emphasized that our strategy should be informed by reliable outcome metrics and established frameworks, referencing OECD’s documentation on education policy impact.

Alicia Torres presented a comprehensive summary of the current financial literacy pilot program. The program’s foundation rests on randomized controlled trials and systematic feedback loops—methods that have consistently shown utility in tracking behavioral changes and learning gains. Alicia highlighted early results that suggest increased understanding of core financial concepts among participating students, particularly in the areas of budgeting and saving.

Reviewing stakeholder feedback, we focused on constructive input received from community educators, parents, and policy officials in recent months. Key themes emerged: a demand for more transparent communication, a desire for open forums for questions and suggestions, and the importance of flexible program design that responds to community needs. Both David and Alicia agreed on the value of these insights, noting that direct engagement not only builds trust but also leads to better adaptation of materials and formats.

The team discussed adding a formal Q&A session in upcoming public forums—a response to consistent requests from stakeholders for more interactive opportunities. We evaluated how best to facilitate candid dialogue and gather actionable suggestions, determining that such sessions could become a cornerstone of future engagement, making policy efforts more accountable and responsive.

In finalizing next steps, we outlined clear deliverables and timelines based on the SMART framework. Accountability structures were established, including regular check-ins and performance reviews, to keep progress on track and maintain transparency.

---

## Key Decisions

- **Formalizing Q&A Sessions:**  
We agreed to integrate a dedicated Q&A segment into all future stakeholder-facing meetings. This decision directly addresses educator and parent feedback, fostering a more open, participatory environment. By following recommendations from NEFE and OECD, we aim to create robust feedback mechanisms that support ongoing improvement.

- **Evidence-Based Monitoring:**  
Our monitoring protocols will leverage established frameworks and performance metrics to evaluate outcomes and guide iterative program development. This approach ensures our interventions are both rigorous and adaptable.

- **Structured Follow-Up:**  
Quarterly analysis meetings will be established, using a rotating facilitation model. These sessions will allow us to systematically review engagement data and refine strategies as necessary.
