## Introduction

At Eastwood Middle School, we believe financial literacy is essential for students and their families. The Parent-Student Budgeting Challenge encourages families to work together, building practical budgeting skills and fostering important conversations about managing money. This initiative goes beyond math or economics—it’s about setting goals, making choices, and understanding how financial decisions affect daily life.

By inviting parents and students to collaborate on budgeting exercises, we hope to strengthen healthy financial habits, build trust within families, and empower everyone to make more informed decisions. Family involvement brings learning into the home, making it truly relevant, and provides children with role models for positive financial behavior.

Our main goals for this challenge are:
- Helping families become comfortable with creating and adjusting household budgets
- Giving students and parents tools to save and spend wisely together
- Strengthening communication and teamwork around family finances
- Encouraging the use of community resources for financial education

Throughout the challenge, we aim for students and parents to:
- Build a foundation in everyday budgeting
- Enhance discussions and transparency on money matters at home
- Set and achieve tangible financial goals
- Feel confident in accessing local and digital financial support

## Overview of the Budgeting Challenge: Purpose & Learning Goals

The Parent-Student Budgeting Challenge is designed to empower families by offering hands-on experience with family budgeting. Participants work as a team to manage income, track expenses, and work towards savings goals.

### What You’ll Do Together:
- Create a realistic household budget with actual numbers from your family
- Track and record income and expenses each week
- Set savings goals and check on your progress
- Review challenges and strategies after each budgeting period

### Why Family Participation Matters
Active involvement from parents and students helps build trust and open communication. It gives students real-life experience, letting them see firsthand how choices in spending and saving affect overall family well-being. The challenge is adaptable to all kinds of family types—from single-parent households to larger families.

### Our Approach
Research shows that financial education is most effective when it is interactive and family-centered.[1] This challenge uses:
- Collaborative exercises to reinforce learning
- Discussions and reflections to encourage careful decision-making
- Local resources to provide ongoing support

By integrating joint activities and regular reflection, families strengthen both financial and personal relationships.

## Step-by-Step Participation Guide

### Getting Started

This budgeting challenge is structured so families can easily follow at home or at school. Here’s how it works:

1. **Gather Everyone**  
   Invite parents, caregivers, and students to participate. The more voices, the better the learning!

2. **Review Goals Together**  
   Before getting started, discuss why financial planning matters. Talk openly about your hopes for the challenge.

3. **Assign Roles**  
   - **Parents/Caregivers**: Share details about household income and regular bills. Explain family priorities and previous financial decisions.
   - **Students**: Help gather information, fill out worksheets, and suggest creative ways to save or manage money.

4. **Complete the Budget Worksheet**  
   - List all sources of income—wages, support, side jobs, etc.
   - Record regular expenses: rent, utilities, groceries, transportation, school costs, and extras.
   - Set one or more savings goals as a family. These could be for emergencies, special purchases, or future activities.

5. **Make Decisions Together**  
   Review the budget and discuss:
   - Where spending is necessary vs. optional
   - Where savings can be increased or priorities adjusted

6. **Track Progress Weekly**  
   Use the tracking table to compare your planned budget with your actual spending and savings each week. Discuss any changes or surprising results.

7. **Reflect and Adjust**  
   At the end of each period, talk about what worked well and what could be improved. Use the reflection questions to deepen your understanding.

8. **Use Available Resources**  
   If you need more advice or tools, check the appendix for helpful local and digital resources.

### Tips for Success
- Be honest about your income and expenses, even if it feels uncomfortable at first.
- Celebrate small gains—every step toward your savings goals matters.
- Make family meetings fun: bring snacks, listen to everyone’s ideas, and show appreciation for progress.

## Reflection Questions with Principal Carter’s Feedback

The reflection stage is where the most meaningful insights often happen. Here are questions for family discussion at the end of each challenge cycle:

1. Which part of budgeting together did you find most difficult—and how did you handle it?
2. In what ways did discussing financial goals as a family change your ideas about saving or spending?
3. What habits or strategies helped you move closer to your savings goals?
4. Which adjustments to the budget made the biggest positive impact? Why?
5. How will you apply what you learned to future decisions—at home, at school, or in your community?

**Principal Carter’s Message:**  
“I encourage every family to use this challenge as a chance to have honest conversations and really listen to each other. The most important lesson is working together to make choices that benefit everyone. The skills and understanding you gain now will help you handle financial challenges throughout life.”

Write or record your family’s thoughts to share with teachers, counselors, or other participants. These reflections also help educators understand what methods worked best.

## Appendix: Digital and Nashville Community Resources

These resources are available for all Eastwood families, whether you need budgeting advice, ongoing support, or financial education workshops.

**National/Online Tools**
- [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau – Youth Financial Education](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/youth-financial-education/)
- [MyMoney.gov – Money Management for Families](https://www.mymoney.gov/)

**Nashville-Based Resources**
- [United Way of Greater Nashville Financial Empowerment Center](https://www.unitedwaygreaternashville.org/financial-empowerment-center/) – Free financial counseling for families
- [Nashville Public Library – Financial Literacy Programs](https://library.nashville.org/events/financial-literacy) – Workshops, recommended books, and events
- [Tennessee Department of Treasury – Family Financial Literacy](https://treasury.tn.gov/financial-literacy) – State-run financial education support
- **Local Hotline: 211** – Connects families to social services, counseling, and community programs

Exploring these options can help families find answers to specific questions, discover new strategies, and build confidence in long-term financial planning.
