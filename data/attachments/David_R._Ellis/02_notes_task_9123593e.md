## Overview

This resource is designed as a comprehensive and easy-to-use guide for preparing a delicious, family-friendly vegetarian lasagna. I created it with the idea that it can work both for family cooking at home and for educational workshops, particularly those focused on getting kids involved in the kitchen and developing nutrition literacy. You’ll find nutrition highlights for every ingredient, step-by-step instructions that show how Jasper joined in the process, a straightforward nutrition breakdown, and a full week’s dinner plan. I’ve included notes on how we tied meals to gardening, as well as practical tips for building lasting kitchen skills and healthy habits as a family.

### Step-by-Step Preparation

**Prep Time:** 40 minutes  
**Cook Time:** 45 minutes  
**Total Yield:** Serves 8

#### 1. Preparing the Sauce and Vegetables
1. **Sauté aromatics.** I heated the olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat, then tossed in the diced onion until it softened and turned translucent, about 3 minutes. Next, I added the garlic and stirred for another minute, letting the aroma fill the kitchen.
2. **Add vegetables.** In went the grated carrot, diced zucchini, bell pepper, and sliced mushrooms. I let everything cook for five minutes, stirring often. Jasper helped wash the vegetables and was excited to use a child-safe grater for the carrots, under supervision (he loves seeing how the long carrots turn into soft orange mounds).
3. **Stir in spinach.** Once the other veggies softened, I added the chopped spinach and stirred until it wilted, just a few minutes.
4. **Add tomatoes and spices.** I poured in the crushed tomatoes and tomato paste, seasoning with Italian herbs, black pepper, and a touch of salt. While the sauce simmered for about 10 minutes, I talked with Jasper about where each ingredient came from—he remembered picking some spinach from our garden last summer.

#### 2. Mixing the Cheeses
5. In a medium bowl, I combined the ricotta and cottage cheese. Jasper sprinkled in half the parmesan and gently mixed it together, taking pride in each movement.

#### 3. Prepping the Noodles
6. For traditional lasagna noodles, I boiled them as directed on the package, then laid them out to cool. When I use oven-ready noodles, it saves this step. Jasper’s job was to help count out the noodles and arrange them for layering—this became a fun counting and pattern exercise, and it opened up a conversation about different types of grains and the structure of pasta.

#### 4. Assembling the Lasagna  
Jasper especially loves this part.

7. I spread a thin layer of the tomato-vegetable sauce in the bottom of a 9x13” casserole dish.
8. We placed 3-4 noodles over the sauce, edge to edge.
    - Jasper tasked himself with making sure each noodle lined up perfectly (his attention to detail made me smile).
9. We spooned a third of the cheese mixture over the noodles—Jasper worked on this with a small spatula, layering slowly and carefully.
10. Another third of the vegetable sauce followed.
11. We repeated these layers: noodles, cheese, and sauce, for a total of three, using up all the ingredients (ending with the sauce).
12. To finish, we sprinkled the top with mozzarella and the remaining parmesan. Jasper scattered the cheese with gusto.

#### 5. Baking and Serving
13. I covered the dish with foil and baked it in a 375°F (190°C) oven for 30 minutes. After removing the foil, it baked for another 10–15 minutes until golden and bubbly.
14. Once out of the oven, we let the lasagna rest for at least 10–15 minutes—an important step for neat slices. I served generous squares with a fresh salad and some steamed green beans on the side.

## Encouraging Child Involvement & Nutrition Awareness

Having Jasper help in the kitchen turned chores into shared adventures—and learning moments. Here are some practices we used to build both excitement and lasting habits:

- **Give Children Real Tasks:** Even at a young age, kids can wash greens, help measure cheese, or, like Jasper, layer noodles and spread sauces. I always offer choices: “Which veggie should we add next?” or “Do you want to sprinkle or stir?” It empowers them and builds ownership.
- **Connect Food to Its Origins:** We talk about where our food comes from, tracing those carrots or spinach leaves back to a neighbor’s garden or local farmer’s stand. Jasper loves seeing pictures from the garden or helping pick herbs. These conversations bring dinner to life.
- **Welcome New Flavors:** Each week, we spotlight a “mystery” vegetable. Whether it’s funky kohlrabi shapes or the tiny leaves of microgreens, we talk about texture, color, and taste. Jasper sometimes makes up names for new veggies—his “tree broccoli” for Romanesco is a family joke.
- **Celebrate Nutrition Every Day:** As we prep, I point out why each item helps our bodies: “Spinach gives you iron for strong blood,” or “Colorful peppers have vitamin C that helps you heal.” Reading nutrition labels and comparing facts on charts has become a little ritual.
- **Make Meals Educational (and Fun):** Prepping dinner is a chance to sneak in math (counting noodles), science (leaf and root veggies), or adult life skills (cleaning up together). We keep the atmosphere light and cheer their “chef” contributions—sometimes with a silly chef’s hat!
- **Reflect at the Table:** After eating, we each say what we liked best, or share something new we tried. I add these notes to our weekly plan, which helps us remember favorites (and less-popular experiments) for next time.
