# Spring Planting Research Notes: A Family Project with David & Jasper

## Introduction

This spring, our family set out to turn gardening into a shared adventure—and a true learning experience that brings us together. Jasper and I planned our garden as a way to spend time outdoors, teach and learn new skills, and share the responsibility of nurturing something from seed to harvest. For us, this project is about far more than just growing tomatoes and sunflowers. Through every step, Jasper gets hands-on experience in setting goals, managing a simple budget, taking care of living things, and working as a team. As a parent, I love that gardening offers so many opportunities to talk about patience, planning, and making healthy choices, all while having fun together outside.

## Choosing Our Plants

After some conversation and research, Jasper and I decided to focus on two favorites—cherry tomatoes and sunflowers. Both are known for being kid-friendly, quick to grow, and rewarding throughout the season. Here’s an overview of why we picked them and how we plan to care for each:

| Plant Name      | Why We Chose It | Sunlight Needs      | Planting Time               | Care Details                                                                |
|-----------------|------------------|---------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Cherry Tomato   | Sweet, snackable fruit; easy for kids to grow and pick; teaches healthy eating | Full sun (6–8 hours daily) | After last frost (late April to May, depending on local dates) | Water regularly (but don’t overdo it), tie stems for support, pick off old leaves, check for bugs |
| Sunflower       | Tall and cheerful; fast results so kids stay engaged; attracts birds and pollinators | Full sun (at least 6 hours) | After last frost (late April to May)           | Keep soil moist as seedlings grow, weed often, watch and measure growth, harvest seeds after flower heads dry |

*We confirmed our planting times using local frost date charts and university extension guides, so we’re confident we’re starting at the right moment for our zone.*

## Our Family Gardening Plan: Step by Step

### 1. Family Kick-Off and Planning Session

We started with a family meeting to pick the sunniest spot in our yard. Jasper loved measuring out the garden patch and drawing a rough map—great practice for his math skills! Together, we looked over seed catalogs, discussed which plants we were most excited about, and tried to make a list of everything we’d need. To add a practical lesson, we talked through a garden budget, comparing seed and supply prices and deciding which purchases made the most sense.

**Goals We Set:**
- Grow enough cherry tomatoes for fresh snacking and a few salads a week
- Plant enough sunflowers to enjoy seeing them from the kitchen window (and share some seeds with friends at the end of summer)
- Assign everyone responsibilities so we all feel involved

### 2. Gathering Seeds and Supplies

Jasper eagerly helped me list out needed items: seeds, sturdy sticks for tomato support, a small watering can (just his size), gardening gloves, and soil amendments. We compared what the local garden shop offered with a few online options to get the best deals, and I involved Jasper in simple price comparisons whenever possible. This turned out to be a great opportunity to talk about making smart choices and sticking to a budget.

### 3. Preparing the Garden Plot

Breaking ground was a joint effort. I took the lead with the heavy digging but made sure Jasper learned how to safely use a small shovel. He tackled weeds with gusto and helped smooth out the soil, taking pride in preparing the bed “just right” for planting. We talked about why healthy soil matters and looked for earthworms as a sign our spot was ready.

### 4. Planting Day

Planting was a highlight for everyone. Following the seed packet instructions, we tucked cherry tomato seeds into prepared rows and spaced the sunflower seeds so there would be room for their tall stalks. Jasper watered the new seeds gently, learning to check the soil’s feel before adding more water—a lesson in both patience and observation.

### 5. Weekly Garden Check-Ups

We set a regular weekly routine for checking in. Jasper took charge of measuring how tall the sunflower and tomato seedlings were getting, keeping a simple chart in a notebook. Each week, he checked soil moisture, watered as needed, and reported any changes he noticed. Together, we looked for signs of new growth, and I showed him how to tie the tomatoes to their supports and how to carefully pinch off old leaves. Observing the progression from seed to sprout was just as thrilling for me as for Jasper.

### 6. Watching and Celebrating Progress

One of our favorite activities has been watching for the first flowers and the arrival of pollinators—especially bees and birds attracted to the sunflowers. Jasper enjoyed taking photos to track our plants’ growth and changes over time. Harvesting the first ripe tomato and seeing the towering sunflowers bloom brought a real sense of accomplishment. Together, we talked about what worked well and what we could improve for next year, such as considering different tomato varieties or experimenting with companion planting.

### Everyday Lessons and Surprises

As the weeks have gone by, gardening offered lots of spontaneous teaching moments:
- Talking about why certain plants need more sunlight
- Counting and comparing growth rates
- Learning why it’s important to weed regularly
- Discussing how patience pays off, both in gardening and life

I found that involving Jasper in simple decisions—like how much to water or when to harvest—helped him feel responsible and invested. We also started a small “garden fund,” adding up seed costs and savings from homegrown food, making it a fun introduction to basic math and budgeting.

## Reflections and Ideas for Next Season

Every gardening season brings a few questions and surprises:
- What bugs did we notice, and how did we handle them?
- Were there any new techniques or tricks we tried (like mulching, or companion planting)?
- Which part of the project was Jasper’s favorite, and what caught him most by surprise?

Looking ahead, we’ve already started brainstorming for next year. Maybe we’ll add snap peas or zinnias to attract more pollinators, or try growing some herbs for fresh summer cooking. Jasper is curious about keeping a tally of our total harvest, and even floated the idea of sharing or selling extra produce.
