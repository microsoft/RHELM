Meeting Minutes: Financial Literacy Resource Integration Collaboration

Eastwood Middle School – Room 214
Date: January 16, 2024
Time: Mid-morning
Attendees: David Ellis, Ms. Ramirez, Mr. Lee

Meeting Overview

On the morning of January 16, 2024, David Ellis (Math Curriculum Lead), Ms. Ramirez (Social Studies Instructor), and Mr. Lee (School Administrator) gathered in Room 214 at Eastwood Middle School to advance efforts around integrating financial literacy resources into Math and Social Studies courses. The session focused on refining an existing Financial Literacy Resource Packet, ensuring its alignment with the latest state curriculum standards, and increasing its accessibility and impact for middle school students.

Objectives

  • Adapt the Financial Literacy Resource Packet for direct integration into Math and Social Studies curricula.
  • Ensure resource content is fully aligned with current state standards and policy directives.
  • Edit materials collaboratively for clarity, relevance, and accessibility.
  • Achieve consensus on all major changes and establish a clear plan for implementation.

Discussion Highlights

Integrating Evidence-Based Strategies

The group began by evaluating current best practices for teaching financial literacy to adolescents. Emphasis was placed on creating student-centered learning experiences by embedding financial concepts into situations that resonate with middle schoolers, such as personal budgeting, saving towards goals, and navigating choices around responsible spending. David Ellis advocated for hands-on, application-based scenarios, stressing that real-world examples help demystify financial concepts and foster practical skills.

Cross-disciplinary integration was also a central topic. For Math, financial concepts are being woven into existing units like percentages and algebra, with activities touching on topics such as simple and compound interest calculations. Ms. Ramirez outlined several ways Social Studies could leverage the material, including discussions on economics and civic responsibilities—connecting classroom learning to broader societal themes.

To increase accessibility, the team developed scaffolded content and tiered activities catering to diverse literacy and numeracy levels. They agreed to incorporate simulations and project-based assignments, allowing students to engage with financial decision-making in authentic, relatable ways. Case studies and group exercises were chosen to provide additional context and collaborative opportunities.

Building Consensus and Collaborative Editing

Throughout the meeting, suggestions and modifications were discussed openly, with each attendee contributing expertise from their subject discipline. Ideas on revising content were presented in a round-robin format to ensure all viewpoints were addressed. Key decisions were documented as they occurred, particularly on topics like the depth of coverage for advanced financial concepts and the pacing necessary for integration within existing curricular structures.

Where disagreements arose—such as how extensively to cover certain financial policies or the sequencing of module components—the group resolved issues through informed debate, majority agreement, and, where necessary, notes for further review and follow-up.

Live editing of shared documents enabled immediate revision of text for clarity and grade-level suitability. Exercises were reorganized to introduce foundational skills before progressing to more complex applications. The Math team adapted examples to reflect culturally diverse perspectives representative of the student community, while Social Studies activities were enhanced with historical context and links to contemporary financial policies.

Aligning to State Standards and Policy

Mr. Lee ensured that all updates to the resource packet were cross-checked with the most recent state standards for Math and Social Studies. The team referenced specific language from policy documents to confirm alignment—for instance, ensuring modules encourage responsible consumer behavior and foster civic financial literacy, as outlined by state directives. The discussion highlighted the importance of adhering not only to the letter of the standards but also to their spirit, creating learning experiences that prepare students for real-world financial decision-making.

Recognizing the need for sustainable implementation, the group began preliminary planning for teacher professional development. Proposed training sessions will focus on best practices for delivering the updated resources, strategies for supporting differentiated learning, and methods for evaluating student growth in financial literacy.

Next Steps

Moving forward, attendees will complete their assigned edits to both the Math and Social Studies resource sections and finalize the standards alignment review. Plans are underway to host a professional development session for faculty, focusing on effective resource adaptation and delivery strategies.

A follow-up meeting is scheduled for the week of January 29 to evaluate implementation progress and review early classroom experiences with the revised materials. In February, initial classroom data and student feedback will be collected from focus groups, providing important insights for future refinements and confirming the efficacy of the new approaches.

Outstanding Issues and Gaps

  • Compatibility of revised content with existing pacing guides will require full semester mapping before integration.
  • Additional evidence of resource effectiveness is needed from upcoming classroom pilot implementations.

Areas for Resolution

  • Align new examples with recently adopted (Feb 2024) state standards, confirming continued relevance and compliance.
