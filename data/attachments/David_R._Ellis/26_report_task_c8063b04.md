## Educational Handouts

The handouts provided during this workshop are designed to build foundational financial skills through a blend of clear explanations and hands-on classroom activities. Each topic covers key concepts and includes interactive exercises encouraging students to explore and apply what they learn.

---

### Section 1: Understanding Money

**Key Concepts:**  
Students are introduced to the basic definition of money—what it is, why it’s valuable, and the different forms it can take today. The lesson traces money’s evolution from barter systems in early societies to the use of coins, paper bills, and modern electronic payment methods.

**Activity:**  
Students work together to complete a “History of Money Timeline,” visually mapping significant milestones from barter, to coins, to online transactions. This helps clarify how money has changed over time and its impact on daily life.

**Summary Points:**  
- Money is a tool that allows us to buy things, save for goals, and plan for the future.  
- In today’s world, money comes in many forms, including cash, coins, and digital payments.

*Transition:*  
Understanding what money is and how it works is the first step toward making smart financial choices. As students move on to budgeting, they’ll begin to see how these concepts fit together.

---

### Section 2: Budgeting Basics

**Key Concepts:**  
Budgeting involves planning where your money will go before you spend it. The lesson explains how setting a budget helps people separate needs (essential expenses like food, shelter, school supplies) from wants (extras like video games or treats), and prepare for future purchases.

**Activity:**  
Through the “Needs vs. Wants Sort,” students examine familiar items and decide which are necessities and which are optional. This exercise stimulates thoughtful discussion about priorities and decision-making.

**Summary Points:**  
- Budgets give you control over your money, ensuring essentials are covered first.  
- Learning to distinguish between needs and wants sets the foundation for financial success.

*Transition:*  
Once students can set budgets, the next step is learning to save—making sure that today's money can help accomplish tomorrow’s goals.

---

### Section 3: Saving and Banking

**Key Concepts:**  
This section dives into the importance of saving, the various types of savings accounts available, and the role banks play in keeping money safe and growing it through interest.

**Activity:**  
Each student completes a “Savings Goal Worksheet,” choosing a short-term savings target (like a book, a bike, or a class trip), and mapping out a simple, achievable plan.

**Summary Points:**  
- Saving money makes it possible to reach both big and small goals.  
- Banks offer a secure place for money and provide services that can help it grow.

*Transition:*  
With an understanding of saving, students learn how to make smart choices when it comes to spending the money they have.

---

### Section 4: Smart Spending

**Key Concepts:**  
Students learn strategies for spending wisely, including comparing product prices to find the best value and recognizing impulse purchases that don’t fit their budget. Real-world examples, like choosing between different lunch options or comparing two brands of headphones, make these concepts practical and relevant.

**Activity:**  
“Supermarket Smarts”—students compare three products, reviewing price tags and features, and then work in small groups to decide which option offers the best value for their budget.

**Summary Points:**  
- Smart spending means thinking before you buy, making sure every purchase fits your needs and budget.  
- Comparing prices and features helps stretch your money further.

*Transition:*  
Understanding how and when to spend is a key life skill—especially as students become more familiar with credit and debt in the modern world.

---

### Section 5: Credit and Debt

**Key Concepts:**  
This section introduces credit as borrowed money, distinguishing it from debit, and discusses when and why people use credit cards. Students also learn about interest and its impact, both positive and negative, on borrowing.

**Activity:**  
The “Credit Card Quiz” presents scenarios such as deciding when it’s appropriate to use a credit card, and what can happen if bills aren’t paid on time. Students share answers and discuss common myths about credit.

**Summary Points:**  
- Credit allows people to make purchases now and pay later, but it is essential to use it responsibly.  
- Understanding interest is crucial to avoiding unnecessary debt and financial stress.

*Transition:*  
Bringing these concepts together, the final group activities build decision-making skills—helping students become confident, thoughtful financial planners.

---

### Section 6: Financial Decision-Making Activities

**Group Scenarios:**  
Students engage in guided discussions around relatable situations, such as deciding between saving for a larger purchase (like a bike) or buying something smaller (like a video game), and considering what to do if they find unexpected money. Teams role-play shopping trips, practicing real-life financial choices.

**Summary Points:**  
- Every money decision involves weighing options and consequences.  
- Working in teams helps develop stronger decision-making skills and prepares students for life’s financial challenges.

*Transition:*  
After exploring all aspects of personal finance, students are encouraged to ask questions, reflect on their experiences, and consider how they can apply these lessons in their everyday lives.

## Presenter Notes

Thoughtful preparation strengthens both delivery and participation. The following notes ensure clarity, inclusivity, and interactive learning:

**Structured Talking Points:**  
- Set a welcoming tone and establish the session’s objectives.  
- Introduce David R. Ellis, sharing professional background and personal passion for teaching financial literacy.  
- Emphasize the lifelong benefits of financial knowledge and skills, especially when cultivated early.

**Reminders:**  
- Reference Tennessee’s financial literacy curriculum standards to align content.  
- Encourage every student’s input and create a respectful learning environment.  
- Practice inclusive communication, welcoming diverse experiences and backgrounds.  
- Allocate time for questions after each topic to deepen understanding.  
- Use practical, age-appropriate examples to ensure accessibility.

**Consensus-Building Questions:**  
- “Who has saved up for something special? What was it?”  
- “Let’s take a quick vote: Is saving for the future or spending on fun most important right now?”  
- “How would you respond if you had to make a money decision as a team?”

**Inclusive Strategies:**  
- Structure small group sessions for collaborative activities and discussions.  
- Provide large-print and visually accessible handouts for all learners.  
- Encourage students to share perspectives and listen to each other.

**Delivery Checklist:**  
- Review every material prior to session start.  
- Confirm projector and tech setup for slide deck and visuals.  
- Arrange handouts so each student receives one copy.  
- Plan and announce group breakouts for activity sessions.  
- Spotlight Jasper’s drawing during the “Savings” topic to link student voice to the lesson.  
- Conclude with a group Q&A to foster open dialogue.
