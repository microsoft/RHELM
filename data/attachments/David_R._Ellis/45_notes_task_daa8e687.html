Evans Family Weekend Meal Prep Plan

Weekend of June 1–2, 2024

Overview

For this upcoming weekend, I’ve put together a detailed meal prep plan for the Evans family that balances nutrition, affordability, and convenience. The plan covers all meals and snacks for Saturday and Sunday, with a focus on wholesome ingredients, simple preparation, and strategies to save both time and money. Drawing on trusted resources like the USDA’s MyPlate guidelines and Mayo Clinic’s healthy eating advice, I’ve organized everything by meal, highlighted key prep steps, and offered practical suggestions for making the most of each ingredient—whether we’re stretching a batch of chili over two days or making breakfast easy for the kids to join in.

Breakfast

Saturday: Scrambled Eggs, Whole-Grain Toast, Fresh Strawberries

Sunday: Greek Yogurt Parfaits with Blueberries, Granola, and Sliced Bananas

Here’s what I’m planning for breakfast:

Item Quantity How I Prep Make-Ahead Tips
Eggs 8 Whisked with a splash of milk; cooked gently with olive oil Crack and store in a bowl the night before
Milk (for eggs) 1/4 cup Mixed into the eggs for extra fluffiness
Olive oil or butter 2 tsp For the pan
Whole grain bread 8 slices Toasted right before serving Slice in advance if using a bakery loaf
Fresh strawberries ~1 lb Washed, hulled, and halved Prepped and stored in a container
Greek yogurt (plain/low-fat) 3 cups Layered for parfaits on Sunday Portion into jars the evening before
Blueberries 1 cup Washed and sprinkled in the parfaits
Bananas 2 Sliced right before serving to keep them fresh
Granola 1 cup Added to parfaits or served on the side Portion into snack bags for grab-and-go

How I Make It Work:

On Saturday, I’ll whisk the eggs with a little milk, seasoning them with salt and pepper, and cook them gently over medium heat. Toasted whole-grain bread rounds out the plate, with a generous helping of juicy strawberries on the side to brighten up the meal.

For Sunday, I love setting up a build-your-own parfait bar. I layer Greek yogurt, freshly washed blueberries and just-sliced bananas, and a crunchy topping of granola into bowls or mason jars. It’s easy, colorful, and makes breakfast a little special.

My Time-Saving Secrets:
- I crack and store the eggs and prep strawberries Friday night, so Saturday morning starts smoothly. - Parfaits can be assembled in advance and stored in the fridge for anyone who wants to grab breakfast and go.

Lunch

Saturday: Big Batch Turkey & Bean Chili

Sunday: Chili Leftovers with Whole-Grain Rice and Simple Side Salad

Lunch is all about hearty, make-ahead comfort for me:

Item Amount Preparation Notes Advance Prep
Lean ground turkey 1 lb Browned in a large pot Cooked and cooled, can be done Friday
Canned kidney beans 2 cans (15 oz) Rinsed and drained, added to chili Rinsed and refrigerated in advance
Canned black beans 1 can (15 oz) Rinsed and drained
Onion 1 large Chopped and sautéed for flavor Diced and stored ahead
Bell pepper 1 large Chopped and sautéed
Canned diced tomatoes 2 cans (14 oz) Added for sauce base
Tomato paste 2 Tbsp Richens and thickens the chili
Chili powder, cumin, oregano 2 tsp each Mixed and added for classic flavor Blended in a jar for quick use
Olive oil 1 Tbsp For sautéing veggies
Salt & pepper To taste Finishes off seasoning
Whole-grain rice (cooked) 2 cups Pairs perfectly with leftover chili Can be cooked a day ahead
Mixed greens (side salad) 6 cups Quick-tossed with olive oil and lemon juice Washed and ready in the fridge
Carrot, cucumber, tomato 1 each Sliced for a crunchy side salad Sliced and packed up

How I Make It Work:

Saturday’s chili is always a winner in our house. I start by sautéing chopped onion and bell pepper with a splash of olive oil until they’re soft and fragrant. Next, in goes the ground turkey—browned till just cooked through. I add the beans, tomatoes, paste, and my favorite chili spice blend, then simmer everything together for about 30 minutes. The result is a rich, filling lunch that leaves us with plenty of leftovers.

Come Sunday, I reheat the chili and spoon it over fluffy whole-grain rice—a great way to stretch one meal into two without anyone getting bored. A simple side salad of mixed greens and crisp veggies rounds things out.

What Makes This Lunch a Family Favorite:
- The chili tastes even better after it’s had time to sit. - Prepping the veggies, rice, and salad ingredients ahead leaves me with more time to relax at lunchtime.

Dinner

Saturday: Sheet Pan Baked Chicken, Roasted Sweet Potatoes, Steamed Broccoli

Sunday: Whole-Wheat Pasta with Marinara, Steamed Frozen Vegetables

Dinner at our place is always about bold flavors and easy cleanup.

Item Amount How I Prep & Cook Advance Prep
Chicken breasts/thighs 4 (1.5 lb) Tossed with olive oil, garlic powder, paprika, pepper Marinated night before for flavor
Sweet potatoes 4 medium Peeled, cubed, tossed with oil and seasonings, roasted Peeled and cubed ahead
Broccoli (fresh/frozen) 2 heads/24 oz Cut into florets, quickly steamed or roasted Trimmed and bagged in fridge
Olive oil 3 Tbsp For roasting and seasoning
Garlic powder, paprika, pepper 1 tsp each Added to chicken and sweet potatoes Blended in a shaker bottle
Whole-wheat pasta 1 lb Boiled for Sunday dinner
Marinara sauce 1 jar (~24 oz) Heated and tossed with pasta Can use leftovers
Frozen mixed vegetables 1 bag (16 oz) Steamed or microwaved, tossed with a pinch of salt Portioned into containers

How I Make It Work:

On Saturday, I love the simplicity of one-pan meals. The chicken and sweet potatoes get tossed in olive oil and seasonings, then roast together at 425°F. Meanwhile, I steam broccoli until it’s crisp-tender. The roasted chicken comes out juicy, and the sweet potatoes caramelize on the edges—no one complains about this dinner.

Sunday’s meal is designed for ease. Whole-wheat pasta gets cooked, coated in warm marinara, and paired with a medley of steamed frozen vegetables. The best part is how hands-off the whole routine is, giving me time with the family after a busy weekend.

Time-Saving Tips I Swear By:
- I marinate the chicken and cube the sweet potatoes before the kids go to bed on Friday, so all I do Saturday is toss everything onto a pan and bake. - I always cook extra roasted veggies; leftovers go well in lunchboxes or get stirred into the next meal.

  • Sliced apples with peanut butter

Snacks get packed up on Friday night or Saturday morning, making it super simple for everyone to grab a healthy bite—especially helpful when we’re headed out for errands or outdoor activities.

Meal Prep Task Checklist

To keep myself on track and make the weekend smooth, here’s the checklist I follow:

Friday Night / Saturday Morning:
- Wash and chop all produce (strawberries, onions, bell peppers, salad greens). - Crack eggs and store for Saturday’s breakfast. - Pre-mix chili and chicken seasoning blends. - Brown and refrigerate turkey for the chili. - Bag up carrots, portion trail mix, and slice apples (tossed in a splash of lemon water). - Marinate chicken and cube the sweet potatoes.

Saturday:
- Roast the chicken and sweet potatoes. - Steam broccoli for dinner. - Prepare Sunday’s parfait toppings (berries, granola, yogurt) so breakfast is quick and fun.

General Prep:
- Cook the rice and pasta in larger batches when possible. - Label and store leftovers in airtight containers for easy lunches or snack times.

Before Each Meal:
- Quickly reheat chili and rice for Sunday’s lunch. - Toss together salads using pre-prepped greens and veggies. - Assemble parfaits Sunday morning for a special start to the day.

Notes: Family Preferences, Budget, and Tips

Dietary Considerations:
No food allergies or major restrictions in my household, but this plan is flexible—swapping proteins, using gluten-free or dairy-free alternatives as needed. I try to cook meals everyone enjoys, and get input from the kids about their favorites for the week.

Budget Estimates:
- Breakfast: About $1.50 per serving
- Lunch (Chili): About $2.00 per serving
- Dinner: About $3.00 per serving
- Snacks: About $0.75 per serving

That’s roughly $7.25 per person per day, which really helps us keep grocery costs predictable. I keep an eye out for sales and try to plan meals using ingredients that do “double duty” across recipes.

Getting the Family Involved:
One of the best parts of weekend prep is how much I get the family involved. The kids love to wash fruit, layer parfaits, and help mix spice blends. For my oldest, budgeting becomes a lesson—he checks prices, compares brands, and is slowly learning to spot a good deal. We even turn nutrition labels into family reading time while shopping, helping everyone make sense of what’s really in our food.

Shopping and Waste Reduction Strategies:
- I stick with store brands for basics and bulk fruit and veggies. - Frozen and canned produce fill the gaps (especially when sales are slim). - The “cook once, eat twice” chili and roasted veggies have saved me on busy afternoons—and any leftovers get dated and stored for quick grab-and-go meals.

Nutrition Approach:
It’s important to me that every plate feels balanced: I aim for lots of color, at least half the meal from produce, with lean proteins and whole grains making up the rest. These meals align with evidence-based guidelines from USDA MyPlate and the Mayo Clinic; it’s practical, realistic eating that keeps everyone feeling good.
