# Morning Walk & Garden Check Routine  
**Date:** June 3, 2024  
**Location:** East Nashville

## Morning Walk Details

I started my morning walk at 6:20 AM, stepping out into the gentle light of early summer. The air felt crisp and comfortable at 65°F (18°C), with a soft breeze from the northeast and only a trace of humidity—perfect walking weather. I set off from my house on Porter Road, headed south toward Shelby Park, then looped through the greenway trail by the South Inglewood Community Center, and finally returned home along Greenwood Avenue. All in all, I covered about 3.2 km (2 miles) over 46 minutes.

As I walked, several details caught my attention. Along the edge of the trail, a line of wildflowers had sprung up seemingly overnight, splashes of pink and yellow brightening the path. On Greenwood Avenue, I stopped for a moment to admire a vibrant new mural—an unexpected burst of color that made me smile and reminded me of the neighborhood’s creative spirit. I exchanged quick greetings with a few neighbors, and paused when I noticed a local gardener tending to a patch designed for pollinators. These small interactions, though brief, made the experience feel more community-driven and left me feeling more connected to those around me.

Sunlight filtered through the old maples, dappling the sidewalk and casting long shadows. The mix of early sun and the soft rustling of leaves added a sense of calm to the morning. I listened to a playlist that alternated between Mozart—his Piano Concerto No. 21 and Symphony No. 40—and some of my favorite Debussy pieces, including "Clair de Lune" and selections from Suite Bergamasque. The blend of lively and soothing music matched my pace and mood, creating a rhythm that made the walk feel restorative, both mentally and physically.

## Reflections: Routine Impact & Well-Being

After my walk and time in the garden, I noticed an immediate shift in my mood. I felt more grounded and clear-headed, with a gentle uplift that stayed with me as I started my workday. The fresh air, sunlight, and sound of birds put me in a positive state of mind, and the physical activity set a steady pace for everything that followed.

With this energetic start, I found it much easier to organize my professional tasks for the day. Sitting down at my desk, I was able to lay out an actionable, step-by-step plan for both my morning and afternoon work blocks. My focus during the mid-morning session was noticeably sharper—I attribute a lot of that to the mental reset provided by my outdoor routine.

Tending the garden right after walking extends the sense of intentionality and gentle discipline I get from being outside. Each small garden task—whether watering at the right time or adjusting a stake—reminds me of the same incremental, attentive practices I share with clients in my financial literacy advocacy work. Regular monitoring, early intervention, and careful documentation are as valuable in building financial health as they are in growing a healthy garden.

This morning underscored how strongly my personal wellness routines intertwine with my professional mindset. When I take deliberate time for myself in the mornings, I show up more authentically with clients and colleagues. Not only does it reinforce my credibility in talking about routines and sustainable change, but it also helps me stay empathic and responsive to others’ needs. I even jotted down a new idea for a workshop activity: using gardening as a living metaphor for financial habits, focusing on observation, proactive care, and celebrating incremental progress.

Ultimately, returning to a rhythm of movement, mindfulness, and stewardship helps me build resilience in both my personal and professional life. As I observed both in the garden and during my walk, celebrating small wins and staying present to each day’s changes keeps me anchored and motivated.
