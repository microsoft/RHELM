*Nashville, TN – April 24, 2024*  
**Author:** David Lee

---

## Introduction

On April 24, 2024, the Eastwood Financial Literacy Workshop took place in Nashville, Tennessee, bringing together a diverse group of parents, students, educators, and policymakers. The workshop was designed to address the growing demand for practical financial education among families and young people, with a special emphasis on interactive learning and mentorship. By focusing on hands-on budgeting challenges and family-focused activities, the event aimed to provide participants with tangible skills and strategies they can use in their daily lives. The collaborative format helped foster meaningful discussions around financial planning, while the variety of stakeholders ensured that multiple perspectives were considered in refining future programs. Through active engagement and the sharing of expertise, the workshop laid the foundation for ongoing financial literacy initiatives within the Nashville community.

## Mentor Feedback and Program Development: Insights from Dr. Samuel Greene

Dr. Samuel Greene, a recognized specialist in educational program design, offered detailed feedback that underscored both strengths and areas for enhancement. He noted the success of family-oriented budgeting exercises and praised the workshop's interactive approach. Dr. Greene emphasized the importance of more structured mentorship opportunities, suggesting these would help families sustain financial learning and communication over time.

To further strengthen the program, Dr. Greene committed to ongoing collaboration, including hosting quarterly mentorship roundtables and providing direct coaching to facilitators. He will play a critical role in guiding curriculum improvements and developing tools for mentorship.

**Key recommendations from Dr. Greene included:**

- **Expanding the Mentor Network:** Recruit volunteer mentors from local universities, credit unions, and parent organizations to build a regional support system.
- **Regular Family-Mentor Check-ins:** Establish scheduled one-on-one sessions between mentors and families to track progress and address challenges throughout the learning process.
- **Reflective Practice:** Implement reflective journaling as part of the family learning experience, allowing mentors to provide feedback and families to monitor their own development.
- **Mentor Resource Toolkit:** Develop and distribute toolkits with conversation guides, progress trackers, and budgeting templates tailored to different age groups, ensuring mentors are equipped to support diverse needs.
- **Evaluation Framework:** Work with Dr. Greene to introduce clear benchmarks for success, including metrics for budgeting proficiency and enhanced family communication.
- **Sustainability and Scale:** Pilot the mentorship model in the next round of parent-student budgeting challenges and analyze outcomes to inform broader implementation.

These steps aim to build a robust and sustainable mentorship infrastructure, supporting families in applying financial concepts beyond the workshop setting.

## Advancing the Parent-Student Budgeting Challenge

Building on the feedback gathered, we have developed a comprehensive plan to expand the parent-student budgeting challenge. The next phase will introduce clearer instructions and redesigned worksheets, reflecting participants’ requests for greater accessibility and more time to engage with the material.

Key actions and anticipated outcomes include:

- **Challenge Redesign:** All worksheets and instructional materials will be revised for clarity, with input from both students and parents to ensure usability.
- **Assessment Implementation:** Pre- and post-workshop assessment tools will be integrated to measure learning gains and inform ongoing improvements.
- **Diverse Pilot Program:** The next iteration will focus on families from a wide range of socioeconomic backgrounds. We will partner with three local schools to recruit and support participants, prioritizing inclusive access.
- **Community Showcases:** Quarterly events will be organized for families to share and discuss the budgeting projects they have developed, fostering an ongoing learning community.
- **Childcare Support:** Steps are being taken to provide on-site childcare, lowering participation barriers and making the workshop accessible to more parents.

Expected measurable outcomes include:

- At least 80% of participants reporting increased confidence in their budgeting skills, as captured by follow-up surveys.
- Clear improvement in students’ ability to create and manage budgets, supported by pre- and post-assessment results.
- A minimum of 25 families completing and presenting their budgeting projects during the pilot phase.
- Formal adoption of workshop content into the curriculum of at least one Nashville school by Fall 2024.

**Responsibility for these initiatives will be shared among:**

- David Lee (Workshop Lead)
- Local educators and Dr. Samuel Greene (Curriculum Design)
- Financial literacy consultants (Assessment development)
- School administrators and parent volunteers (Outreach and logistics)

**Target Timeline:**

- **May 2024:** Redesign materials; recruit pilot families and mentors.
- **June–July 2024:** Launch pilot program and hold first mentorship roundtable.
- **September 2024:** Host initial community showcase; compile and analyze impact data.
- **October 2024:** Report pilot outcomes; begin steps to integrate curriculum into school programs.

These next steps are designed to iteratively build on what we have learned, ensuring the budgeting challenge continues to evolve and meet participants' needs.
