## 3. Discussion and Outcomes

### 3.1 Opening Remarks & Previous Minutes

Linda Martinez opened the meeting by reaffirming the board’s focus on advancing financial literacy through practical, evidence-driven policymaking. The board reviewed and approved the minutes from the previous session, after making minor adjustments to the outreach schedule.

### 3.2 Updates on Local Financial Literacy Initiatives

Malik Carter shared progress reports from ongoing financial literacy workshops in East and North Nashville, noting a significant 40% uptick in participation since January. Participants have shown increased rates of opening bank accounts and a clear improvement in their understanding of budgeting basics. Malik referenced preliminary findings from the National Endowment for Financial Education, which support the effectiveness of peer-led instruction models. These successes set a promising tone for expansion efforts.

### 3.3 Expanding Community-Based Financial Literacy Programs

The board discussed ways to scale the “Smart Money Saturdays” initiative, which currently operates in two neighborhoods, with a goal to reach five neighborhoods by the end of Q3 2024. Rebecca Thorn highlighted the impact of age-specific curriculum modules piloted in local schools. Data from recent pilot sessions revealed post-test scores improved by 23% among middle school students. The board also examined community survey data that identifies persistent financial literacy gaps in the 18–24 age group, leading to a recommendation to prioritize resources for young adults as part of next-phase outreach.

David R. Ellis contributed briefly when technical access permitted, but his voting on consensus items was limited.

### 3.4 Building Partnerships with Schools and Nonprofits

The board committed to initiating formal discussions with Metro Nashville Public Schools to launch financial literacy programming in the 2024–2025 school year. Alicia Jones suggested leveraging United Way’s “Money Smart” program, which offers proven teaching resources and facilitator training. The group supported a proposal for a summer youth camp focused on financial basics and entrepreneurship, led by community volunteers. This camp aims to introduce practical financial skills to young people and foster early interest in financial independence.

### 3.5 Policy Recommendations for City Support of Financial Education

Jamal Stewart presented draft recommendations for strengthening municipal support:  
- Allocation of grant funding for community financial literacy educators, to begin in July 2024.  
- Request for city council approval of a standardized curriculum for all public middle and high schools.  
- Mandate for annual program effectiveness reporting by organizations receiving city funding, ensuring transparency and accountability.

The board agreed to move forward on these proposals, with Jamal taking lead on formal submission.

### 3.6 Assignment of Action Items

Responsibility for critical tasks was allocated among board members. Each assignment includes clear deadlines and established follow-up procedures consistent with board protocols. These tasks include outreach efforts, curriculum refinement, drafting grant proposals, engaging stakeholders, and improving technical infrastructure for virtual meetings.

### 3.7 Community Questions and Dialogue

Community participants raised four primary concerns:  
1. Making financial literacy resources accessible to those who do not speak English as their first language  
2. Ensuring programs address needs of people experiencing homelessness  
3. Coordinating content with adult education providers to avoid duplication of services  
4. Clarifying grant eligibility criteria and offering application support

The board agreed to consult with advocacy organizations focusing on ESL, homelessness, and adult education, and to provide step-by-step guidance for grant applicants.

## 6. Meeting Summary, Next Steps, and Recommendations

The board reaffirmed its top priority—expanding effective financial literacy programming for both youth and adults in Nashville. Clear action steps and deadlines have been set for program growth, partnership development, and policy support. The next meeting in April will feature a review of progress, new impact data from March and April activities, and updates on partnership discussions with local schools and nonprofits.

To enhance ongoing virtual participation, technical support solutions will be explored, with the goal of ensuring every member is able to fully engage.

Stakeholder input remains fundamental to the board’s approach, especially when planning outreach to underserved groups such as non-English speakers and individuals experiencing homelessness. Annual reporting requirements and use of proven program evaluation methods will support transparency and continual improvement.

**For the April 2024 meeting, key priorities include:**  
- Reviewing SMART goals for each action item  
- Collecting and analyzing early impact and enrollment data  
- Finalizing agreements with partner organizations  
- Preparing a draft annual report on program effectiveness
