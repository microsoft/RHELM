## Phased, Evidence-Based Plan: Returning to Strength Training

### Current Situation & Rationale

Right now, I’m awaiting my upcoming medical review with Dr. Patel on June 28, which marks the milestone for resuming strength training after my recent break. My main intention is to return to my workout routine safely, supporting my professional performance and overall well-being.

Numerous studies have shown a strong link between regular physical activity—especially strength training—and improved cognitive function, better stress management, and higher work productivity ([3]). For me, staying active isn’t just about health; it allows me to set a positive example for clients and reinforces my message of balance and leadership.

### Structured Return Plan

**Phase 1: Post-Doctor Clearance (Week 1: June 29–July 5)**
- I’ll start with very light resistance exercises using bands and my own body weight.
- Two sessions during the week, each lasting 20–30 minutes.
- The focus will be on monitoring how my body responds—assessing for pain, fatigue, or anything out of the ordinary and tracking all activities in my fitness journal.

**Phase 2: Gradual Progression (Weeks 2–3: July 6–19)**
- Moving into moderate resistance with light dumbbells and gym machines.
- Planning for 2–3 sessions per week, aiming for 30–40 minutes each.
- Emphasizing compound movements, I’ll only increase the intensity if all goes well—no pain, no setbacks—maintaining close communication with Dr. Patel if anything unusual comes up.

**Phase 3: Full Routine (Weeks 4+: July 20 onward)**
- I hope to transition back into my usual full-body workouts by mid-July.
- Targeting three sessions per week, 40–50 minutes each.
- I’ll slowly increase weights and complexity of movements while keeping a close check on how I’m feeling, adjusting as needed.

This structured, gradual approach reflects best practices from both medical guidance and exercise science, giving my body the chance to adapt and reducing the risk of injury.
