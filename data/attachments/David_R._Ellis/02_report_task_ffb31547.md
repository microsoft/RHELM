# Media Interview Talking Points – January 2024

**Date:** January 4, 2024  
**Author:** David R. Ellis, MA (Education Policy)  
**Purpose:** To equip policymakers, educators, and the public with clear, evidence-based messages and recommendations drawn from the 2023 Financial Literacy Report, focusing on Tennessee’s current landscape and national trends. The briefing promotes advocacy, outreach, and practical solutions for advancing financial literacy throughout the state.

---

## Executive Summary

The 2023 Financial Literacy Report highlights an urgent need to strengthen financial knowledge and skills throughout Tennessee and across the United States. Financial literacy remains a critical determinant of both personal wellbeing and broader economic stability. Tennessee continues to trail national averages, especially among young people, rural residents, and minority communities, despite some policy advances in recent years. The report’s findings point to concrete opportunities for more effective reforms, stronger collaborations, and targeted educational initiatives. Comprehensive strategies—encompassing schools, families, policymakers, and community organizations—are essential to foster lasting financial empowerment.

---

## Key Messages

- **Financial literacy matters for everyone.** When individuals lack basic financial knowledge, they are at greater risk for debt, fraud, and instability—challenges that ripple across families and communities.
- **Tennessee’s youth financial literacy scores are below the national average.** This gap signals not only a local challenge, but a call to action for statewide intervention in K-12 education.
- **Disparities remain entrenched.** Rural, low-income, and minority populations in Tennessee consistently score lower on financial literacy measures, due to barriers such as limited access to curriculum, resources, and qualified educators.
- **Policy progress is promising but incomplete.** Recent standards for financial education in Tennessee schools mark a positive step, yet implementation lags and teacher training remains underfunded and uneven.
- **National patterns show improvement, yet progress is slow.** Although average financial literacy scores have increased slightly nationwide, competencies in digital tools and practical financial management are still lacking, especially in states like Tennessee.
- **Stakeholder involvement is crucial.** Effectively advancing financial literacy requires collaboration among policymakers, educators, families, and grassroots organizations.
- **Unified, collaborative action will drive sustainable change.** Strategic partnerships—connecting state agencies, schools, businesses, and local organizations—hold the key to expanding access and closing persistent gaps.

The data underscore the need for tailored, locally-driven solutions—especially for rural residents, minority communities, and young people. Ensuring teachers are equipped with appropriate training and resources is a crucial but under-addressed lever.

## Recommendations and Strategies

### For Policymakers

- **Expand funding for financial literacy education**, particularly in under-resourced districts and schools serving disadvantaged students.
- **Mandate specialized training and certification for financial literacy educators,** with incentives and clear professional development pathways.
- **Implement statewide assessment systems** to monitor program effectiveness and guide ongoing improvements.
- **Strengthen multi-sector partnerships,** involving government, non-profits, the private sector, and higher education to broaden access and share expertise.

### For Educators

- **Embed experiential learning in the curriculum:** Real-world simulations, budgeting exercises, and project-based learning help students apply financial concepts.
- **Harness technology and digital platforms** to make financial education accessible and relevant, closing divides in online financial acumen.
- **Build family and community engagement:** Involving parents and local leaders in financial skill-building boosts relevance and impact.

### For the Public

- **Participate in local workshops and community events,** and explore the growing range of online financial education opportunities.
- **Focus on creating emergency savings and understanding debt,** including student loan risks and basic budgeting.
- **Advocate for accessible, high-quality financial education—** particularly in underserved neighborhoods and schools.

### Practical Initiatives

To catalyze change, local pilot programs targeting the unique needs of Tennessee communities—including rural and minority populations—are recommended. Financial literacy fairs, teacher mentoring, and a variety of hands-on learning experiences can make these efforts tangible and impactful. Support for teachers—through easy-to-implement lesson plans, digital resources, and ongoing professional development—is a practical way to ensure high-quality instruction reaches every classroom.

## Professional Reflections

During a recent financial literacy summit in Nashville, I witnessed firsthand the deep commitment of educators and community leaders working to improve student outcomes. It was inspiring to see their passion and dedication, even in the face of persistent obstacles like limited funding and slow-moving policy changes. Teachers spoke candidly about struggling for access to training and resources, while students described challenges understanding basic financial principles. These conversations reinforced my conviction that meaningful progress rests not just on legislative action, but on real, sustained partnerships at all levels—from classrooms to state agencies to local organizations. Tennessee is well-positioned to lead if these challenges are addressed through collaborative, innovative approaches.

| **How does Tennessee compare nationally in financial literacy?** | Tennessee remains below the national average across youth and adult indicators. Disparities are most evident in rural and minority communities, emphasizing the need for targeted reforms and expanded educational strategies. |
| **Why is financial literacy important for policymakers?**    | Financial literacy is strongly linked to personal economic security, reduced poverty, and healthier communities.
