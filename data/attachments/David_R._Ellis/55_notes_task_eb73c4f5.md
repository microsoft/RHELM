## Reflections & Notes for Next Visit

### Vendor Recommendations

The standouts this week were BerryHill Farms and GreenLeaf Organics. The strawberries from BerryHill Farms were the best we’ve had so far this summer—sweet, intensely flavored, and not a sour one in the bunch. GreenLeaf’s basil was fresh and fragrant—and I loved chatting with their staff about organic gardening and picking up new ideas for using herbs at home (the pesto recipe is on my kitchen counter).

Firefly Bakery deserves their early morning crowd—their sourdough has an excellent crust and lasts longer than most loaves from grocery stores. It pays to show up before 9:30 am if you want your pick of their bread.

### Observations on Price & Quality

The market felt truly in-season: strawberries, basil, and tomatoes were at their delicious peak and priced fairly, especially given the quality. Heirloom tomatoes were more expensive than supermarket varieties, but worth every cent for the flavor. Organic salad greens ranged widely in price, so next time I plan to compare more carefully across vendors.

Local eggs continue to be pricier than grocery store options, but the taste and freshness are noticeably better. After speaking with Morningstar Acres, I understand more about the costs—and feel better choosing eggs from smaller, pasture-focused farms.

### Conversations & Farmer Interactions

It’s always striking to me how willing local farmers are to talk about their crops and methods. Sampling before buying makes all the difference, especially with tomatoes and berries. I appreciated learning about how Smith Family manages different heirloom tomato varieties and hearing Morningstar’s insights into hen care. It’s these exchanges that really build trust—and help us make more informed choices.

Both GreenLeaf and Smith Family were happy to discuss discounts if we purchased in larger quantities, something to keep in mind if I’m shopping for an event or want to split with friends. Several popular items (like the pork sausage) sold out by mid-morning, underscoring the value of arriving early.

### Market Strategies & Personal Tips

On mornings when the market is especially busy, it helps to bring both cash and a few reusable bags with cooler lining. Some of the smaller vendors can’t process cards, and it’s easy to find yourself juggling fragile berries or eggs in flimsy bags if you forget.

I’ve started rotating which family member talks to each vendor—and today it was fun to see my son ask how the blueberries get so big. Sharing the shopping tasks keeps everyone involved and helps us all learn a bit more about where our food comes from.

Lastly, I’m keeping detailed notes week to week—it’s making it easier to remember which vendors we prefer for certain items and how prices and selection shift through the season. Saving business cards from top vendors has also made it much easier to find them again.

## What Guides Our Choices

When it comes to deciding what comes home with us, here’s what weighs most:

- **Quality & Flavor:** I seek out in-season, local items, and always sample where I can. Superior taste often means paying a little more, but it’s worth it.
- **Transparency:** I feel more confident buying from vendors who are open about how and where they grow or produce their goods.
- **Budget:** I set a weekly market limit. Staple items come first; specialty or higher-priced treats get saved for a future week if needed.
- **Availability:** Flexibility is part of the experience—sometimes the best plans have to be adjusted when favorites sell out or the ideal size or ripeness isn’t available.
