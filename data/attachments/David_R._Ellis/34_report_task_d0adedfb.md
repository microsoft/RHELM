## Executive Summary

This outline presents a comprehensive update to the Financial Literacy Curriculum, designed to improve financial education outcomes for a diverse student body. Our approach focuses on integrating evidence-based instructional methods, direct input from seasoned educators and financial practitioners, and alignment with nationally recognized standards—including those set by the Jump$tart Coalition and the National Standards for Personal Financial Education.

The revised curriculum centers on three foundational modules: Budgeting, Credit Management, and Investment Basics. Each module incorporates differentiated learning pathways suitable for elementary, middle, high school, and adult/family participants. The objective is to foster not only robust financial knowledge, but also meaningful skill development and responsible decision-making, with a commitment to equity and accessibility throughout.

Curriculum development has been a collaborative effort, guided by detailed stakeholder feedback and best practice benchmarks. The result is a dynamic, up-to-date program that empowers learners to build the confidence and competence needed to navigate real-world financial scenarios.

## Curriculum Design and Innovations

The curriculum is grounded in current research on financial education efficacy, with active efforts to meet learners where they are developmentally. Each module is purposefully structured with clear objectives and engaging activities to accommodate various age groups and learning styles.

| Module           | Description                                                    | Learning Objectives                                                                     | Sample Activities by Age                                                                   |
|------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Budgeting**    | Builds foundational understanding of personal budgeting and financial planning | - Distinguish needs from wants<br>- Create realistic budgets<br>- Track income and expenses<br>- Apply budgeting strategies aligned with Jump$tart NM1, National Standards 2-1 | **Elementary:** Budgeting through storytelling, family budgeting projects<br>**Middle/High:** Digital budgeting apps, peer-driven challenge |
| **Credit Management** | Explores responsible borrowing, credit scores, and debt consequences    | - Identify credit types<br>- Interpret credit reports/scores<br>- Assess borrowing risks & rewards<br>- Recognize impact of debt (Jump$tart CD1)                              | **Middle/High:** Credit simulation game (credit card/loan), group analysis of sample reports<br>**Adult/Family:** Credit repair workshop, debt management role-play   |
| **Investment Basics** | Introduces investment principles, savings strategies, and wealth building | - Describe basic investment types (stocks, bonds, etc.)<br>- Understand risk/return<br>- Build strategies for saving/investing (Jump$tart IN1, National Standards 4-1)        | **Middle/High:** Stock market simulation, investment case studies<br>**Adult/Family:** Retirement planning worksheets, expert speaker series |

Each activity is crafted to be both engaging and meaningful, linking theory with practical application. Family involvement is prioritized, especially in modules addressing budgeting and investing, to reinforce learning beyond the classroom. Multiple pathways for advancement ensure content remains relevant and challenging for all learner segments.

*Referenced standards: [Jump$tart Coalition Financial Education Standards][2], [National Standards for Personal Financial Education][1]*
