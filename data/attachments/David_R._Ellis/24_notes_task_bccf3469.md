## Overview & Guiding Principles

This week, I set out to plan our family’s meals around fresh, seasonal produce—doing my best to cut down on wasted food and extra grocery trips. I wanted to cook meals that shared ingredients, giving us nutritious, affordable options all week. I paid close attention to our pantry so I wouldn’t buy things we already had, and I tried to get Monica and the kids involved in the process, hoping to pass on some practical skills about shopping and budgeting.

**How We Saved Money & Stayed Organized:**
- **Seasonal Buying:** I focused on produce that’s abundant in late March and early April, like spinach, carrots, and apples, to get better prices and fresher flavors.
- **Ingredient Overlap:** We reused ingredients in multiple recipes—carrots, spinach, and turkey kept popping up in different meals.
- **Use What We Have:** I combined new produce with staples like rice, beans, and pasta that we always have in the pantry.
- **Inventory First:** Before shopping, I took stock of what was left so I wouldn’t double up.
- **Everybody Helps:** The whole family got involved—helping plan menus, prep food, and learn about costs.

## Cooking Together & Family Engagement

This week, I made it a priority to get Monica and the kids cooking with me. It’s not just about sharing the workload—cooking together is a chance to teach them how to slice carrots safely, double a recipe (hello, fractions!), and understand what goes into putting three meals on the table every day.

**Ways We Turned Cooking into a Learning Experience:**
- **Cooking Skills:** I let Monica crack eggs, measure ingredients, and help whisk up dressings—she’s learning and having fun.
- **Menu Planning Talks:** On Saturday night, we had a quick family huddle to talk about what veggies everybody liked (or didn’t!) and what we wanted to use before it spoiled.
- **Pantry Check:** Before grocery shopping, I walked Monica through the pantry, teaching her how to spot what was running low and where we could avoid waste by using up what we had.
- **Building the Shopping List Together:** We gathered around the kitchen table, flipping through weekly store flyers and clipping digital coupons together. It turned the sometimes tedious job of list-making into a little team event.
- **Assigning Roles:** We started giving the kids rotating jobs, like “Produce Checker”—they loved getting to be in charge of checking what fruits and veggies needed to be used up first.

I’ve also started celebrating when we beat our grocery budget for the week. Sometimes I’ll make a quick homemade dessert as a little reward—Monica is already asking what we’ll bake next!

## Smart Grocery Habits I’m Trying to Build

- **Stick to the Plan:** Shopping from a meal plan and pantry checklist keeps me from buying random extras I don’t need.
- **Buy in Bulk Wisely:** Only stock up in bulk on things we always eat (like rice, beans, frozen veggies) and that won’t go bad before we use them up.
- **Use Multipurpose Ingredients:** I look for fruits and veggies that can show up in a bunch of meals—carrots, spinach, and apples earn their keep every time.
- **First-In, First-Out:** I put older produce and leftovers right up front in the fridge so we eat them before opening new stuff.
- **Review Spending:** After every shopping trip, I look over the bill—sometimes with the kids—and talk about how we did, and any tweaks we can make to save more next week.
