## Reflections: What We Learned Along the Way

#### Staying Flexible
Packing up that morning, I felt confident watching the forecast—but Asheville weather had other plans. The hike at Craggy Gardens should have been easy, but those icy, muddy trails reminded me how quickly outdoor plans can shift. Cutting the hike short and heading for a warm car wasn’t what I’d pictured, but it felt like the right call. I was glad we’d packed extra socks and hot drinks for everyone, and that we had a backup plan for the afternoon.

#### Getting Creative with What’s Available
Arriving at the Arboretum, the closed greenhouse was a disappointment for the kids. But instead of letting that ruin the mood, we wandered the open gardens and explored a nature art exhibit we hadn’t expected. We talked about making the most of things, and how sometimes unexpected closures lead you to try something new.

#### Budgeting for the Unplanned
The budget review at the end of the day became an informal family circle. I laid out our receipts, and we saw immediately where decisions paid off. Our groceries and gas matched our plan to the dollar, but that $15 spent on hot chocolate and cider was a real, tangible reminder of how small comforts can pop up unexpectedly—and it was worth every cent. We’d planned for some miscellaneous spending and never needed it, which helped cover the café splurge.

#### Dealing with Forgotten Items
Leaving the laptop charger at home initially had us all a little frustrated—especially since it meant no movies in the room that night. But rather than running out to buy a replacement, I suggested a tech-free evening, and we leaned on board games and old-fashioned conversation. In hindsight, it was a win. We laughed more and went to bed feeling connected. It was a great chance to show the kids that “making do” can be both practical and fun.

#### Teaching Financial Awareness
Involving everyone in the budget conversation was probably the most impactful part of the day. The kids helped count up what we’d saved by skipping impulse buys, and we all saw the value in having a buffer for surprises—good and bad. It’s easy to talk about responsible spending, but days like this feel like the real lesson. I’m proud that we enjoyed the trip, handled curveballs calmly, and modeled financial mindfulness together.
