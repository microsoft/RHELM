*Financial Literacy Advocate & Consultant—Nashville, TN & Virtual*

## Introduction

This February 2024 public speaking schedule details a series of events planned for diverse audiences—students, adults, and policy stakeholders—across Nashville, TN, with virtual options included. Building on preparation completed January 20, 2024, the schedule is designed to promote financial literacy through engaging, evidence-based presentations. Each event incorporates targeted content and interactive activities tailored to the audience, with clear strategies for preparation, collaboration, and post-event evaluation. The aim is to advance financial literacy advocacy while meeting strategic professional goals.

## Preparation and Planning

### Audience Research

A thorough understanding of each audience segment is a priority.  
- I plan to collect demographic data using local school records for the youth event and community surveys for the adult webinar.  
- For the policy forum, I’ll consult stakeholder networks and recent studies on state and local financial literacy to target relevant priorities and challenges.

### Material Development

Each presentation is being designed to maximize engagement and effectiveness for its specific audience:  
- **Students (Feb 6):** I’ll create interactive budgeting games and credit score simulations to help teens practice real-life scenarios.
- **Adults (Feb 15):** Case studies and debt payoff calculators will be included so participants can see practical applications and track their own progress during the webinar.
- **Policymakers (Feb 27):** I’m preparing policy briefs highlighting local data and best practices, complemented by clear data visualizations to illustrate gaps and solutions.

### Logistics

- For in-person events, venue bookings and AV setup are being coordinated with contacts at the Nashville Public Library and Vanderbilt University.
- For the virtual session, I will test accessibility features on Zoom and ensure a smooth participant registration process and tech support is available if needed.

---

## Engagement Strategies

Building strong partnerships and fostering active participation are central to all three events:  
- I’m working with school administrators, librarians, and local nonprofit leaders to spread the word, boost attendance, and offer co-hosting opportunities.
- Each event will feature interactive segments—such as live polls, Q&A sessions, and teamwork exercises—to keep audiences engaged and encourage practical dialogue.
- Promotion will be handled through targeted social media campaigns, email outreach, and posts in local news outlets, ensuring messaging appeals directly to the needs and interests of each group.
- For every session, educators, financial advisors, and community advocates are being invited to lend their expertise and encourage a collaborative atmosphere.

---

## Post-Event Review and Impact Assessment

After each event, systematic review protocols will help measure outcomes and inform future programming:

### Feedback Collection
- Participant surveys will be distributed immediately after each session, collecting both ratings and open-ended responses.
- Key collaborators and panelists will be invited to share reflections on the event’s impact and opportunities for improvement.

### Impact Assessment
- For youth and adult sessions, learning outcomes will be measured using pre- and post-event quizzes so I can track shifts in knowledge and confidence.
- The policy forum will feature action pledges and on-site commitments; follow-up check-ins will gauge ongoing progress toward financial equity goals.

### Lessons Learned
- Debrief meetings will be held within two days of each event, ensuring timely discussion of what went well, what could be improved, and next steps.
- Best practices and notable insights will be documented in a shared advocacy playbook for reference in future planning.

### Reporting
- Each engagement will be summarized in a comprehensive report, detailing participant demographics, feedback highlights, and concrete recommendations for expanding reach and improving future financial literacy programming.
