Notes and Inspirations

Favorite Recipes, Made with Local Ingredients

  • Spinach Omelet: I like to sauté fresh local spinach and mushrooms in a bit of olive oil before adding some beaten eggs. A sprinkle of tangy feta on top pulls everything together, making this a regular breakfast go-to.
  • Mediterranean Quinoa Salad: This one is on weekly repeat when tomatoes are at their peak. I toss cooked quinoa with diced cucumber, market tomatoes, fresh basil, a handful of feta, a swirl of olive oil, and a bit of lemon juice.
  • Overnight Oats: Before bed, I mix old-fashioned oats with chia seeds, chopped walnuts, juicy peach chunks, and pour over milk or Greek yogurt. It’s always ready and waiting for me in the morning.
  • Stir-fried Chicken: Quick and colorful for a weeknight—sauté bite-sized pieces of chicken with chopped local bell peppers and broccoli, then serve with a scoop of brown rice.
  • Caprese Salad: I never skip this in July. Layers of just-picked tomato, creamy local mozzarella, and a basil leaf on each slice, drizzled with olive oil and a splash of balsamic. Pure summer!

Eating for Recovery: Nutrition Focus

Because back recovery is my main priority, I put extra emphasis on certain types of foods. Protein (from chicken, fish, Greek yogurt, and eggs) is key for tissue repair, while anti-inflammatory omega-3s from salmon, walnuts, and flaxseed help calm soreness and promote healing. I load up my menu with high-fiber fruits and veggies for overall health and digestion, and make sure to get a regular dose of vitamins—especially vitamin C (from berries, peaches, peppers) and vitamin A (from spinach and sweet potatoes).

I keep processed foods, added sugars, and unhealthy fats off my list, as they can slow down recovery by encouraging inflammation. Hydration is just as important: I drink water often and snack on water-rich produce like cucumber, peaches, and melon. Since I’m moving less than usual, I watch my portions and sometimes opt for smaller, more frequent meals.

Planning Ahead for Next Week

Looking ahead, I’ll do a quick check of my grains, dairy, and protein staples before shopping, to ensure I only restock what I truly need. The market usually starts offering the first apples and juicy blackberries soon; I’ll be on the lookout for those. With busy weeks, I try to keep extra containers on hand for meal prep or freezing leftovers—nothing beats pulling a homemade soup out of the freezer on a late night.

My Go-To Prep and Budget Strategies

Meal prep saves sanity—batch-cooking grains, hard-boiling eggs, or prepping chicken for a few days in advance makes healthy eating much more realistic. Shopping with a detailed, intentional list helps me stick to my budget and keeps food waste low. I find the freshest, most affordable produce when I let the season decide most of my menu, and keep a couple “flex meals” on hand (like a grain bowl or a big salad) to use up whatever’s left in the fridge by the weekend. Bulk purchases of basics like nuts and rice cut costs in the long run.
