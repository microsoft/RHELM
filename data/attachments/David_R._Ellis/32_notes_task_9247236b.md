# Ellis Family Gardening Day: Complete Shopping List & Planning Guide

## Overview

For our Ellis Family Gardening Day, I put together this shopping list and planning guide to make sure everyone has what they need for a productive and fun day outdoors. I included all the items we specifically mentioned in our planning, as well as a few optional essentials and ideas for making the most of our efforts—financially, environmentally, and as a family team. This guide is flexible, so we can easily adjust it for future gardening days or share it with others who want to organize their own group gardening events.

## 2. Cost-Saving and Eco-Friendly Strategies

I’m always looking for ways to keep our gardening both affordable and sustainable. Here’s what works for us:

- **Buy in Bulk:** When possible, I choose multi-packs—especially for seeds, gloves, and produce—to cut down on packaging and cost per item.
- **Free Community Compost:** Many municipalities (like ours last year!) offer free compost. Always check the city or community garden website before heading to the store.
- **Reuse from Home:** Before rushing out to buy anything new, I search the house and garage for items we can improvise—like old containers for plant markers, or big pitchers as watering cans.
- **Store Brands:** Home Depot, Lowe’s, and local grocery chains often have “house” brands that deliver solid performance for less. These are great for gloves, tools, and even sunscreen.
- **Eco Choices:** I make a point to buy compostable trash bags and steer clear of chemical or non-organic gardening products when possible. Small steps add up.

## 3. Recommended Brands and Where to Shop

To keep things simple and reliable, here’s where I’ve found the best value and quality for our core supplies:

- **Gardening Tools (Trowels, Gloves, Shears):**  
  - **Trusted and Affordable:** Fiskars, Corona, Wells Lamont, G & F Florist Pro (often on sale at Home Depot, Amazon, or Walmart).
- **Seeds:**  
  - **Best Value:** Botanical Interests, American Seed, Burpee. They’re available at Home Depot, Lowe’s, and sometimes directly from seed company websites.
- **Compost and Soil:**  
  - **Go-To Brands:** Miracle-Gro, Vigoro, and local garden centers or municipal suppliers offer consistently good results.
- **Yoga Mats:**  
  - **Budget Brands:** Amazon Basics and Gaiam Essentials hold up outdoors—but I also ask around for spare or older mats.
- **Grocery Items (for lunch and snacks):**  
  - **Where I Shop:** Store brands at Aldi, Trader Joe’s, or Walmart Great Value, all have dependable produce and pantry staples.
- **Sunscreen:**  
  - **Consistent Savings:** Target Up & Up or Walmart Equate give the protection I trust without unnecessary cost.

## 4. Making the List Work for the Whole Family

Since we’re usually juggling multiple schedules and preferences, these collaboration tips keep us organized:

- **Go Digital:** In advance, I upload the shopping list to Google Sheets and send everyone the link. This lets us all pick what we’ll handle, mark what’s done, and suggest extras.
- **Print for the Day:** On Gardening Day, a printout helps everyone tick off completed tasks and add spontaneous reminders or tweaks.
- **Continuous Improvement:** After each event, I update the list with lessons learned—like which gloves lasted longest, or what snacks ran out too soon—so each year gets a little easier.

---

## 5. Optional Extras & Personal Touches

Sometimes the basics aren’t enough! Over the years, we’ve found these extras can really enhance the experience:

- Hand rake or weeder (for stubborn roots)
- Wheelbarrow or garden cart (always handy—borrowing from a neighbor works great)
- Knee pads or extra yoga mats (for kneeling comfort)
- Wide-brimmed hats and lightweight long sleeves (for extra sun protection)
- Insect repellent (especially for afternoon gardening)
- Extra snacks and beverages (watermelon, iced tea, trail mix—big family favorites)
- Notebook or phone app for recording planting dates, weather, and garden notes

---

## 6. Customization and Family Notes

I always leave blank columns or rows in our digital and printed lists, so anyone in the family can add items for personal preferences, seasonal plants, allergies, or if friends are joining. We also initial next to items once claimed, and update brands or quantities if we discover a better deal week-of. It keeps everyone involved and accountable.
