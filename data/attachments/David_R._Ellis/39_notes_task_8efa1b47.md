## Reflections on STEM, Perseverance, and Diversity

Reading about Katherine Johnson wasn’t just inspiring—it was eye-opening. As a family, we paused several times to talk about how her love of learning and stubborn determination carried her through school, college, and finally NASA, despite being treated unfairly because of her race and gender. There was a moment in the book where Katherine was left out of important meetings simply for being a woman; discussing this together helped us understand that perseverance isn’t just about never giving up on puzzles—it’s also about standing up for yourself and others.

Katherine’s vital role in the Apollo 13 mission reminded us how diverse perspectives make STEM stronger. Without her expertise and courage, the story of space exploration might have turned out very differently. We all agreed it was powerful to see someone who looked like us—or might have faced similar obstacles—being the hero who solved impossible problems. Stories like Katherine’s remind us that STEM isn’t just for a select few; it’s for everyone, and we all have something unique to contribute.

## Next Steps: Actionable Plan for Our Next Library Visit

Inspired by Katherine Johnson, we came up with a family plan to keep nurturing our love for STEM and build skills that matter:

- **Explore STEM Together:** We’ll look for biographies and stories about other pioneers in science and engineering—especially those whose voices haven’t always been heard. We want to fill our reading list with trailblazers from diverse backgrounds, so everyone in our family can see themselves reflected in history.
- **Grow Our Curiosity:** Next time at the library, we’ll each choose a book on a STEM topic that puzzles or excites us—whether that’s space missions, basic coding, or a tricky math problem. By reading together and trying out hands-on projects, we can bring these ideas to life.
- **Practice Problem-Solving:** To flex our thinking skills, we plan to pick up a puzzle or logic activity book. Solving challenges as a team will help us appreciate the kind of creative problem-solving Katherine used every day at NASA.
- **Build Representation & Financial Skills:** We’ll seek out a children’s book that introduces concepts like saving, budgeting, or the basics of money. Inspired by Katherine’s math achievements, we want to show how math skills can help with everyday tasks, like planning for something special as a family.
- **Set Personal Learning Goals:** Before our next visit, each of us will write down a question about space or math that we’re curious about. During our library trip, we’ll hunt for books or resources that help answer those questions, turning our curiosity into real knowledge.

We hope these steps will help us stay curious, improve our real-world math and financial skills, and build our confidence as problem-solvers—just like Katherine.

## Prompts for Follow-up Activities & Discussions

To make our reading time interactive and spark new ideas, we came up with some simple prompts and activities we can use at home or at the library:

- **Critical Thinking:**  
   - What problems did Katherine solve at NASA? How do problems in our lives require similar creative thinking?  
   - Why is teamwork—especially among people from different backgrounds—important for tackling tough challenges?
- **Growth Mindset:**  
   - Katherine faced setbacks, but kept going. Can you think of a time when you didn’t give up, even when it was difficult? How did you feel afterwards?  
   - What can we learn from mistakes, and how can that help us get better next time?
- **Representation:**  
   - Seeing Katherine’s story told in a picture book made us feel proud and motivated. How does seeing people from different backgrounds in STEM make you feel?  
   - Why do you think it’s important for books and movies to show a wide variety of role models?
- **Financial Literacy (for young readers):**  
   - Katherine used math to solve huge problems at NASA. How do we use math together in daily life—like at the grocery store, or when managing our savings?  
   - Could we sit down together and make a simple family budget, using what we know about addition and subtraction?
- **Library Activities:**  
   - Research other “hidden figures” or lesser-known inventors, and make a poster or short presentation to share what we learned as a family.  
   - Try a “math scavenger hunt” at the library: find books that connect numbers to real life, whether in recipes, sports, travel, or even our favorite games.

We love that these activities bring our reading to life and inspire us to think deeper, ask questions, and be creative as a family.
