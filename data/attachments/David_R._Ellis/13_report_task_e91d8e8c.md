# After-Action Report  
## February 10, 2024 Statewide Financial Literacy Teacher Training Workshop

---

## Executive Summary

On February 10, 2024, educators from across Tennessee gathered at the Nashville Convention Center for a Statewide Financial Literacy Teacher Training Workshop—a pivotal event aimed at strengthening financial literacy teaching through targeted professional development. The workshop welcomed 168 teachers representing a diverse mosaic of regions, backgrounds, and grade levels. Together, participants engaged in sessions designed to address the newly updated state financial literacy standards, share evidence-based instructional practices, and establish deeper collaborative networks.

Throughout the day, the workshop delivered clear gains in participants’ understanding of curriculum integration strategies, fostered consensus on overcoming instructional challenges, and highlighted certain logistical and technological issues that impacted session delivery. These experiences offer valuable insights into ongoing policy refinement needs and reinforce the importance of thorough event management—especially in areas of technology and scheduling—when organizing large-scale professional learning initiatives. The findings and recommendations presented in this report are intended to inform and strengthen future efforts that support Tennessee’s statewide financial literacy education goals.

## Event Overview

**Date:** February 10, 2024  
**Location:** Nashville Convention Center

**Purpose and Objectives:**  
- Equip Tennessee educators with effective strategies and resources for teaching financial literacy  
- Ensure classroom practices are closely aligned with the updated state financial literacy standards introduced in 2024  
- Encourage collaborative sharing of classroom resources and teaching approaches  
- Promote ongoing professional growth in accordance with Tennessee Department of Education policies

**Key Personnel:**  
- **Lead Organizer:** Statewide Financial Literacy Program Coordinator, responsible for workshop planning and policy alignment  
- **Workshop Facilitators:** Six certified financial literacy specialists with experience in curriculum integration and adult learning  
- **Technical Support Staff:** Two on-site IT specialists dedicated to audiovisual troubleshooting  
- **Registration and Logistics Team:** Three administrative staff handling registration, materials distribution, and session logistics  
- **Guest Presenters:** Representatives from Tennessee Department of Education and local policy advocacy organizations, delivering keynotes and policy updates

## Lessons Learned

Several key lessons emerged from the day’s experiences:

- **Technology Preparedness Is Critical:** Regular equipment checks and backup AV arrangements should be standard. Even minor disruptions can hinder session effectiveness, so the availability of alternative teaching tools (e.g., whiteboards, printed materials) should be ensured.
  
- **Weather Contingencies Should Be Built Into Planning:** Tennessee’s winter weather can be unpredictable, and schedules should always include buffers for travel delays to avoid a cascade of timetable shifts.
  
- **Collaborative Incident Response Enhances Resilience:** Facilitators who coordinate in real time can minimize disruption and maintain the quality of participant experience. However, these processes can be further streamlined with predefined communication protocols.
  
- **Inclusive Leadership and Problem Solving Sustain Engagement:** Involving all staff in solutions—whether logistical or content-related—helped maintain group cohesion and teacher buy-in even when challenges arose.
  
- **Allow More Time for Practical Application:** Teachers consistently seek opportunities to discuss real-world classroom application. Event schedules should prioritize interactive segments and avoid compressing such time due to earlier delays.
  
- **Policy Linkage Must Be Continually Strengthened:** Clearly connecting new policy directives and standards to classroom realities is essential in helping teachers understand both the “why” and the “how” behind changes.

---

## Recommendations for Future Workshops

To build on strengths and address areas for improvement identified this year, organizers should consider the following recommended actions:

**1. Implement Redundant Technology Systems:**  
Ensure availability of backup projectors, laptops, and AV equipment. Conduct full-scale technical rehearsals before the event to reduce the risk of equipment failure.

**2. Proactive Weather Planning:**  
Adjust check-in and session start times when inclement weather is forecast. Include explicit arrival buffers in all winter registration schedules.

**3. Strengthen Communication Channels:**  
Establish digital messaging (text and email) for rapid real-time updates on schedule changes and session adjustments, improving attendee awareness and satisfaction.

**4. Expand Opportunities for Q&A and Collaborative Discussion:**  
Restructure session timings to prioritize participant interaction, and provide extended periods for direct engagement with panelists and content experts.

**5. Use Pre-Event Surveys to Personalize Policy Updates:**  
Gather targeted input from teachers ahead of workshops to shape policy sessions around their most pressing interests and classroom challenges.

**6. Formalize Facilitator Response Plans:**  
Create and disseminate protocols for incident response and collaborative decision-making among staff to enhance coordination and reduce the impact of unexpected delays.

**7. Employ Validated Feedback Evaluation Tools:**  
Utilize standardized evaluation instruments, such as Guskey’s professional development assessment model, to measure participant satisfaction, learning gains, and inclusiveness, thereby supporting program accountability and continuous improvement.
