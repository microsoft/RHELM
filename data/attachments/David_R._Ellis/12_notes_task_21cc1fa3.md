## 1. Sweet Potato and Kale Skillet: Recipe Overview

At the heart of this week's prep session is a hearty, affordable sweet potato and kale skillet. Packed with fiber, vitamins A and C, and plant-based protein from the addition of chickpeas, this one-pan meal is both satisfying and adaptable for different dietary requirements. Suitable for vegans and vegetarians (with the option to add feta or other proteins), it fits seamlessly into batch-cooking and meal-planning routines.

- **Makes:** 4 main-dish servings  
- **Total Time:** 35 minutes

---

## 2. Ingredients List and Substitutions

Below is a full list of ingredients, along with sensible substitutions to accommodate budgets and preferences:

| Ingredient                | Amount        | Substitution Ideas                                         |
|---------------------------|--------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Sweet potatoes            | 2 large (~600g) | Butternut squash, carrots, or standard potatoes           |
| Kale (fresh)              | 1 large bunch (about 150g, stemmed & chopped) | Spinach, collard greens, Swiss chard        |
| Olive oil                 | 2 tbsp        | Any vegetable oil, or reduce oil for a lower-calorie dish |
| Garlic (minced)           | 3 cloves      | 1 tsp garlic powder or omit for a low-FODMAP option       |
| Red onion (chopped)       | 1 medium      | Yellow onion, leeks, or can be omitted if needed          |
| Chickpeas (canned)*       | 1 can (425g/15oz) | Cannellini beans, lentils, or omit for reduced carbs   |
| Smoked paprika            | 1 tsp         | Paprika, chili powder, or omit for a milder flavor        |
| Salt & black pepper       | To taste      | Season to personal preference                             |
| Lemon juice               | 2 tbsp        | Lime juice, or white wine vinegar                         |
| Feta cheese (optional)    | ½ cup (crumbled) | Plant-based feta or omit for a vegan version           |
| Toasted pumpkin seeds (optional) | 2 tbsp   | Sunflower seeds, walnuts, or omit for simplicity       |

*Opt for store-brand chickpeas and bulk sweet potatoes for better value. Seasonal greens and bulk staples keep costs low.

## 3. Step-by-Step Preparation Instructions

### Setting Up the Kitchen

**Recommended Equipment:**

- Large skillet or sauté pan  
- Medium pot (if pre-cooking sweet potatoes on stovetop)  
- Knife and cutting board  
- Measuring spoons and cups  
- Spoon or spatula

**Tips for Efficiency and Safety:**
- Wash hands and all produce thoroughly before starting.
- Organize ingredients and equipment for a smooth cooking flow (mise en place).
- Read through instructions before you begin to avoid last-minute confusion.

### Preparation & Delegated Tasks

#### Step 1: Produce Prep (10 minutes)

- **David:** Peel and cut sweet potatoes into uniform, ½-inch cubes.
- **Monica:** Rinse, destem, and chop the kale; chop red onion and mince garlic.

_Prepping everything upfront speeds up the rest of the process and ensures even cooking._

#### Step 2: Precooking Sweet Potatoes (5–7 minutes)

- **David:** Put the diced sweet potatoes into a microwave-safe bowl, add 1–2 tablespoons of water, cover loosely, and microwave on high for 4–5 minutes until just tender. Alternatively, simmer or steam them on the stovetop for about 6–8 minutes.
- **Monica:** Heat 1 tablespoon of olive oil in the large skillet over medium heat while the potatoes cook.

#### Step 3: Sautéing the Aromatics (5 minutes)

- **Monica:** Add onion to the pan, sauté for 2 minutes until translucent, then add minced garlic and cook for 1 more minute until fragrant.
- **David:** If using canned chickpeas, rinse and drain them thoroughly.

#### Step 4: Combine and Sauté Vegetables (10 minutes)

- **Both:** Add the pre-cooked sweet potatoes to the skillet, stir in chickpeas, and season with smoked paprika, salt, and pepper.
- **Monica:** Gradually add the chopped kale, stirring as it wilts (over 2–3 minutes).
- **David:** Squeeze lemon juice over the mixture. Continue to cook, stirring occasionally, until the kale is completely wilted and flavors meld—about 5 more minutes.

#### Step 5: Final Touches (2 minutes)

- **Both:** Taste and adjust seasoning as needed. Serve immediately or transfer to meal prep containers.
- **Optional garnish:** Top servings with crumbled feta and pumpkin seeds.

### Workshop and Educational Highlights

Engage participants in each step to promote hands-on learning. Emphasize these practical strategies:

- **Batch Cooking:** Preparing meals together saves time and reduces overall food costs.
- **Flexible Substitutions:** Adapting recipes based on sales, seasonal availability, or pantry staples helps manage grocery budgets.
- **Reducing Waste:** Kale stems can be saved for smoothies or soups, and leftover lemon juice can be used in dressings.
- **Efficient Teamwork:** Clear division of tasks, combined with group steps, maximizes productivity and makes the process enjoyable and educational.

## 4. Teamwork and Collaboration Strategies

**Before Cooking:**
- Review the recipe as a team, decide on ingredient swaps, and clarify any dietary restrictions.

**During Cooking:**
- Divide tasks for efficiency—each person is responsible for different aspects of prep before coming together for combined steps.
- Practice orderly kitchen habits: set out everything needed before starting; communicate often, discuss flavors, and adjust as needed.

**After Cooking:**
- Reflect on what worked well, where the process could improve, and how collaboration made prep smoother. Note changes for future sessions.

**Workshop Application:**
- Rotate cooking roles and encourage all participants to try new tasks.
- Use the activity to draw connections between kitchen collaboration and broader principles of financial literacy—effective budgeting, time management, and shared goals.

### Dietary Considerations

- **Vegan:** Omit feta, or use plant-based alternatives.
- **Gluten-Free:** All listed ingredients are naturally gluten-free—check labels on grains and canned goods.
- **High-Fiber, Low-Glycemic:** Sweet potatoes and kale provide slow-releasing carbs and fill you up.

### Storage and Food Safety

- Cool cooked food before sealing in containers.
- Use shallow containers for faster cooling and to maintain freshness.
- Label each container with the prep date.
- Refrigerated dishes are best used within 3–4 days; freeze leftovers if not eating within that window.
- Reheat meals to at least 165°F (74°C) to ensure food safety.

For further details, see USDA and FDA resources on food storage ([USDA MyPlate](https://www.myplate.gov/), [FDA Food Safety](https://www.foodsafety.gov/)).

### Smart Meal Prep for Financial Wellness

- **Portion Meals Ahead:** Split batch-cooked meals into individual servings to control portions and avoid food waste.
- **Smart Shopping:** Plan grocery lists around weekly sales, home inventory, and recipe overlap.
- **Bulk Prep:** Cooking and freezing meals in advance reduces weekly takeout and lowers grocery costs.
- **Track Your Savings:** Calculate per-serving meal costs and compare with local takeout for a motivating financial snapshot.
- **Creative Use of Leftovers:** Repurpose extras for salads or breakfasts to stretch your grocery dollar.
