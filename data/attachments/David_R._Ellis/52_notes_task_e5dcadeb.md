## Recommendations for Improving Engagement in Future Sessions

Drawing from established approaches in educational coaching and best practices in fostering student engagement, I’ve outlined several strategies to help Jasper build positive experiences with reading. Each recommendation is rooted in research on motivation, learning, and student autonomy.

### 1. Set Clear, Attainable Goals for Each Session

Establishing short-term, concrete goals at the beginning of each session can offer Jasper structure and a sense of accomplishment. For example, we could agree together: “Today, let’s read one chapter and focus on understanding three new vocabulary words.” Clear goals provide benchmarks to measure progress and help scaffold his motivation.

Research consistently finds that setting incremental, specific goals cultivates motivation and self-efficacy in learners (Locke & Latham, 2002).

### 2. Foster Personal Relevance and Connections

Engagement grows when Jasper sees how the reading material relates to his own interests and experiences. I can invite him to connect elements of the story with situations from his life, family history, or even current events. For example, when reading about a character facing challenges, I might ask, "Have you ever tried something difficult and wished for a little magic, like Charlie?"

Relevant content is more memorable and meaningful, encouraging greater participation and deeper learning (National Research Council, 2000).

### 3. Recognize Effort With Positive Reinforcement

Acknowledging Jasper’s efforts—no matter how small—is critical. Positive, specific feedback can help shift his mindset (“I saw you stick with that tricky paragraph—great perseverance!”). Over time, introducing simple incentives, like earning points towards a small reward or special activity, can help reinforce focused participation and progress.

Studies show that positive reinforcement is a key driver for persistence and positive attitudes in young learners (APA, 2014).

### 4. Offer Choice and Autonomy

Allowing Jasper to exercise choice increases his investment in learning. At various points, I can offer him options—such as which chapter to start with or whether to engage in vocabulary work or discussion first. Giving him some control supports his independence and builds a sense of ownership over his accomplishments.

Providing children with choices supports autonomy and enhances motivation and engagement with challenging tasks (Deci et al., 1991).

### 5. Emphasize Growth Mindset and the Learning Process

It’s important to approach challenges as learning opportunities rather than setbacks. When Jasper encounters difficulty, I will calmly explain that making mistakes is part of learning, and improvement comes through ongoing effort (“It’s normal to struggle at first—the more we work at this, the easier it will feel”). I will also model self-reflection by talking through my own problem-solving process, demonstrating resilience and persistence.

Interventions centered on a growth mindset have been shown to foster resilience and long-term academic engagement, particularly in response to setbacks (Dweck, 2006).

### 6. Structure Brief, Energizing Breaks

To address signs of fatigue and waning attention, I plan to schedule short, structured breaks between tasks. We can incorporate physical movement (stretching, a quick walk, or a short game) to refresh Jasper’s focus and ease any frustration. These brief interludes can help regulate mood, boost attention, and create a more productive learning environment.

Neuroscience research supports the role of breaks in maintaining focus and emotional regulation during learning sessions (Jensen, 2005).

## Moving Forward

Although today’s session presented several challenges, I remain optimistic about guiding Jasper toward more productive and enjoyable reading experiences this summer. By consistently applying these evidence-based strategies—setting clear goals, building relevance and autonomy, modeling resilience, and supporting Jasper’s efforts with encouragement and breaks—I aim to create an atmosphere where Jasper feels supported and capable as a reader. With patience and adjustment, I am confident we will see increased motivation, sustained engagement, and stronger reading skills emerge over time.
