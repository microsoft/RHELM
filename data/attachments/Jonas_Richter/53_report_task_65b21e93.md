## Executive Summary

The “Forest of Trust” initiative is a cross-regional privacy education project inspired by the rich natural and historical environment of the Harz mountains—an area long associated with both division and resistance at the heart of Europe. The project draws directly on the personal and collective experiences of hiking through the dense, storied forests of the Harz, where, amid the legacy of state surveillance during the Cold War, ordinary people found creative ways to resist oppressive systems. By anchoring privacy education in the narratives and lessons from this unique landscape, we aim to provide urgently needed privacy literacy rooted in place, history, and lived experience.

In today’s rapidly shifting digital landscape, where surveillance technologies outpace public understanding, it is vital to empower citizens—particularly youth and community leaders—with the skills and critical awareness to protect their digital rights. Our project will develop and deliver a range of experiential and place-based learning modules complemented by engaging digital resources, all informed by local narratives from the Harz and partner regions. These efforts are intended to foster a culture of strategic awareness and resilience against digital surveillance, while also strengthening regional engagement with digital rights policy frameworks. Through these activities, the “Forest of Trust” initiative seeks to create scalable models for privacy education that can be adapted across the European Union and beyond.

## Background and Rationale

### Legacy of Resistance in the Harz Mountains

The Harz mountains straddle what was once one of the most heavily fortified borders in Europe: the line separating the German Democratic Republic (GDR) from the Federal Republic of Germany (FRG). This area witnessed decades of intense state surveillance, with the Stasi employing a wide range of techniques: informant networks, electronic monitoring, frequent patrols, and strict border controls. Their goal was clear—to prevent escape, suppress dissent, and maintain control over every aspect of daily life.[1][2]

Despite this tight grip, communities in the Harz developed innovative forms of resistance. The forest became both shield and sanctuary for those seeking privacy and freedom. People held clandestine meetings among the trees, relying on the terrain to hide written messages and using coded signals—flashes of light, particular sounds—to evade informants and communicate discreetly across the border. Churches and civil organizations quietly offered spaces where individuals could gather anonymously, providing solidarity and support despite the risk of surveillance.[1][2][3] Secret paths and “green border” routes allowed some to slip past both physical and technological obstacles in their search for a freer life.[2][3]

Many of these analog techniques—out of necessity—mirrored tactics we now employ to protect our digital privacy: fragmented and indirect communications, trusted intermediaries, compartmentalization, and the deliberate creation of untraceable trails. The legacy of the Harz region’s resistance offers real, human stories that resonate with today’s digital advocacy strategies such as end-to-end encryption and the use of open, decentralized tools.[3][4]

### Contemporary Relevance

In the present day, countries across Central and Northern Europe are implementing increasingly sophisticated surveillance frameworks that affect nearly every aspect of civic life. Pervasive data collection by state actors and corporations, algorithmic profiling, and expanded police powers are no longer isolated phenomena but part of a broader societal shift. For young people and activists especially, these measures can have a chilling effect on freedom of expression, assembly, and dissent. Worryingly, privacy education in schools and public forums remains limited, often leading to feelings of inevitability or resignation when it comes to surveillance.

By looking to the practical knowledge—and sheer courage—of those who lived under the Stasi’s shadow in the Harz, we find valuable teaching tools for privacy in the digital age. Grounding advocacy and education in tangible experiences, specific places, and real stories helps illuminate the relevance of abstract rights and connects new generations to resilient forms of civic action.[1][2][3][5] The need to close the awareness gap regarding digital surveillance is not abstract; it is urgent and deeply tied to our lived realities.

## Objectives

The “Forest of Trust” initiative is driven by a set of clear, action-oriented objectives:

1. **Raise Awareness:**  
   Build broad public understanding of current privacy risks and rights, directly engaging at least 5,000 participants—including students, teachers, and civil society members—in Central and Northern Europe.

2. **Transfer Historical Lessons:**  
   Integrate primary-source narratives, historical artifacts, and clear analogies from resistance in the Harz into digital privacy curricula, with initial implementation and evaluation at six education or museum partner sites.

3. **Develop and Pilot Educational Resources:**  
   Design and distribute a cross-regional toolkit (print and digital) that uses experiential modules, authentic place-based stories, and interactive activities inspired by the history of the Harz resistance.

4. **Stakeholder Engagement:**  
   Build an active network of at least 15 cross-sector partners, spanning local history, technology, educational organizations, policy groups, and civil society, who will collaborate on joint activities and outputs.

5. **Advocate for Policy Uptake:**  
   Provide evidence-backed policy recommendations for enhanced privacy education, presenting findings at a minimum of two major national or EU-level policy forums and sharing best practices with legislatures and ministries.

Each objective is designed to be practical, measurable, and aligned with existing regional and EU digital rights strategies.

## Use of Original Forest Photography

### Integration in Curriculum

Photographs captured within the Harz forests will be woven throughout educational modules to provide a direct, sensory link between the landscape and narratives of privacy. They will:

- Serve as visual points of reference anchoring learning materials in specific locations and memories
- Illustrate case studies of navigation, concealment, and signaling, echoing both historical and contemporary tactics for maintaining privacy
- Provide immersive backdrops for virtual learning environments, including VR walk-throughs and animated lessons

### Intellectual Property and Accessibility

All photographs will be released under the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike (CC BY-NC-SA) license. This approach guarantees open access for educational users, while preserving attribution requirements and preventing misuse for commercial purposes. Photographers will sign consent forms and IP agreements at the time of collection, ensuring clarity of usage rights. Wherever possible, accompanying metadata and contextual narratives will be provided within the platform, enriching each image with its story and ethical guidelines for reuse.

### Educational Usage

Partner institutions—museums, schools, and NGOs—will have the ability to incorporate this imagery into exhibitions, campaign materials, and classroom activities. All use will be restricted to non-commercial, educational purposes and tracked for impact and reach.
