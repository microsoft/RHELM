Discussion Summary

1. Opening and Objectives Clarification

Dr. Annika Hess opened the meeting by restating the key objectives: finalizing the panel’s thematic direction and ensuring session outputs are evidence-based, aligned with the Forum’s commitment to transparent, inclusive policy dialogue. This approach upholds standards set in previous Forum decisions (e.g., “Commitment to Transparency and Stakeholder Inclusion,” 2023). Each attendee confirmed the agenda, with particular focus on ensuring panel contributions are anchored in the latest developments at the EU and German federal levels.

2. Panel Session Drafting: Thematic Focus and Policy Framing

The panel mapped out the intended structure for the upcoming session, focusing on the intersection of digital innovation and fundamental rights. Discussions centered on themes such as data minimization (GDPR Article 5), transparency mandates, and recent amendments to the EU Data Act (2024 Draft). The group referred back to internal Forum positions—particularly around algorithmic transparency—to provide continuity and build upon previous recommendations.

Jonas Richter encountered persistent audio issues during this segment, preventing him from sharing his analysis of municipal obligations under the new EU Data Act. The discussion acknowledged the anticipated value of Jonas’s points, particularly regarding operational risks for city-level data management, including interoperability and consent challenges. In recognition of the importance of these insights, the panel agreed Jonas would submit his remarks in writing for inclusion in the session draft and broader review, ensuring municipal perspectives are not omitted.

3. Review of Key Stakeholder Perspectives and Invited Inputs

Maria Schmidt presented a synthesized summary of recent positions and contributions from external stakeholders, including submissions by European Digital Rights (EDRi), AlgorithmWatch, and other civil society alliances. The consensus was clear: the civic tech sector’s evolving role in bolstering digital rights should feature centrally in the panel’s discussion, echoing recommendations made in previous Forum sessions (2022 onward).

Elif Güzey emphasized the necessity of integrating discussions about cross-border data flows and privacy harmonization, specifically referencing GDPR recital 101. Her perspective highlighted the ongoing tensions and opportunities within transnational data governance. Dr. Liam Brandt provided essential legal context, explaining recent Constitutional Court interpretations on proportionality in digital surveillance and referencing the German “IT-Grundrecht” doctrine. The panel reflected on the practical differences in focus between local NGOs—who champion data sovereignty—and business actors seeking regulatory clarity. These contrasting priorities will be woven into the panel structure to illustrate the spectrum of needs and concerns shaping digital rights policy.

4. Update on Recent EU Digital Rights Policy Changes

Elif Güzey delivered an update on both enacted and forthcoming EU regulations, covering the Digital Services Act (DSA), Digital Markets Act (DMA), and ongoing Council negotiations surrounding the ePrivacy Regulation. The panel then explored the local implications, particularly for Berlin, with Dr. Brandt stressing how ambiguities in DSA enforcement could present challenges for municipal digital oversight. This conversation drew from recent stakeholder feedback and confirmed the necessity for “living guidance”—practical and adaptable instructions for city administrators—an approach previously advocated by the Forum (Forum Decision, 2023-18).

5. Coordination for External Communications and Media

In light of the session’s policy sensitivity—particularly given the pending Berlin Data Governance Act—Maria Schmidt proposed coordinated preparation for public announcements and media engagement. She recommended organizing briefings in advance and highlighted the importance of clear, consistent messaging. Linda Meier suggested adopting a checklist for communications, building on lessons learned from earlier miscommunications during the March 2024 panels. This process will help mitigate reputational and legal risks, ensuring accuracy and cohesion in external messaging.

6. Technical Rehearsal Planning

Given the technical disruptions during Jonas Richter’s segment, the group agreed to conduct a comprehensive technical rehearsal ahead of the panel event. Planning will include moderator handoff drills, testing of redundant audio solutions, and readiness of backup platforms as needed. Stefan Krüger volunteered to coordinate these efforts with the Forum’s IT provider to strengthen platform reliability and safeguard against similar issues during the live session.
