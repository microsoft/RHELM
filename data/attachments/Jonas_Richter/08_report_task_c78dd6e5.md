## Executive Summary

Advocating for open internet and progressive digital policy in the European Union and Germany has become increasingly complex. Over the past year, we have faced intensified regulatory changes, heightened government intervention, and a rapidly evolving public debate. Our campaign has been acting at the intersection of these forces, aiming to uphold net neutrality, ensure fair platform regulation, and defend digital rights by engaging with policymakers at both the EU and domestic levels.

We began with encouraging momentum: a strong, diverse coalition and hopeful signals emerging from debates around the Digital Services Act (DSA) and Digital Markets Act (DMA). However, the campaign suffered a notable setback when attempts to influence the final DSA text—particularly on platform liability and data access—fell short. This outcome highlighted the strength of competing lobby interests, diverging priorities among EU member states, and the ongoing challenges of sustaining coalition unity amidst a fragmented digital policy landscape.

Recent regulatory developments—such as more stringent online content moderation, robust national security provisions in Germany’s NetzDG, and uncertainty surrounding EU digital sovereignty initiatives—demonstrate the ongoing relevance and necessity of our advocacy. Our central objectives remain clear: safeguard net neutrality, promote transparency and fair access for all, insist on proportional regulation, and rigorously defend fundamental digital rights. The following review addresses key lessons from the past year and outlines a refreshed strategic direction aimed at greater impact and resilience.

---

## Lessons Learned

### Recent Setback: Timeline and Analysis

- **Q1–Q2 2023:** In preparation for final DSA trilogues, the campaign intensified EU-level advocacy, centering on platform transparency and user rights protections. Coalition members were highly engaged, and discussions were robust.
- **Q3 2023:** The emergence of leaked DSA drafts exposed a significant weakening of intermediary liability protections, sparking a surge in lobbying activities—especially from large industry players and certain member states. The coalition struggled to maintain a unified stance.
- **Q4 2023:** The final adoption of the DSA left critical issues on platform obligations and data access unresolved. Communication with both the public and within the coalition weakened, triggering disengagement from several key members and reducing campaign visibility at a critical moment.

Through this sequence of events, we identified several root causes:

- Messaging lacked adaptation for German audiences and the specific concerns of EU institutions, weakening its local and institutional resonance.
- The campaign leaned heavily on a wide-reaching coalition but lacked mechanisms to effectively coordinate diverse priorities or drive unified action.
- The influence of well-resourced government and tech industry lobbies was underestimated, particularly during the decisive phases of policy negotiation.
- Public engagement and media outreach were reactive rather than proactive, missing many opportunities to build momentum and shape the regulatory narrative.

### Challenges and Responses

The following table summarizes core challenges, analyzes their origins, and proposes actionable, systemic solutions for moving forward:

| **Challenge**                                            | **Analysis**                                                                      | **Actionable Solutions**                                                      |
|----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Fractured coalition and limited domestic mobilization    | Primary emphasis on EU-level advocacy overshadowed stronger engagement in Germany  | Enhance collaboration with German civil society via workshops and local events |

These experiences emphasize that timely, coordinated advocacy—rooted in both local relevance and European scale—is vital for defending digital rights in the current environment.

## Revised Messaging Strategy

An effective advocacy campaign must be flexible and context-driven, especially when operating across multilayered political landscapes. Building on recent experience, our revised messaging strategy will:

- Underscore the tangible benefits of open internet principles for EU innovation and competitiveness, ensuring policymakers understand the broader economic and societal stakes.
- Frame user rights and democratic values as central, connecting them to German public sentiment and the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights.
- Highlight proportional, evidence-based risk mitigation approaches, actively refuting characterizations of digital rights regulation as overly burdensome or anti-business.
- Tailor messages to address specific national interests, such as Germany’s focus on digital sovereignty and regional data infrastructures.
- Integrate real experiences and data—highlighting small and medium-sized enterprises, journalists, and everyday users who face obstacles under current platform policies.

#### Comparative Overview: From Past Limitations to Improved Practice

| **Aspect**      | **Previous Approach**                                  | **Refined Approach**                                                                         |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Framing         | Technical/legal, right-based arguments                 | Value-driven and human-centered, emphasizing democracy, digital freedom, and economic growth  |
| Language        | Predominantly English, EU-centric terminology          | Bilingual (German/English); clear, engaging content tailored to the German public discourse   |
| Audiences       | EU policymakers, digital rights NGOs                   | Inclusion of national parliamentarians, mainstream and regional media, SME associations      |
| Evidence Base   | Formal policy briefs, legal opinions                   | Accessible case studies, user testimonials, practical impact data, EU-wide surveys            |
| Policy Context  | Precedents like net neutrality                        | Connection to current EU strategies (e.g. DMA, DSA), with links to wider digitalization goals |

By anchoring our messaging in relatable stories and clear evidence, we aim to resonate more deeply with policymakers and the broader public, while effectively countering misleading industry narratives.

## Conclusion

The dynamic and demanding environment of digital policy in Germany and the EU necessitates renewed strategic clarity. By strengthening local partnerships, refining our coalition, and updating our messaging to resonate more powerfully with both policymakers and the public, our advocacy campaign is positioned to more effectively champion open internet and digital rights through the next wave of regulatory debates.

Sustained, coordinated action—grounded in real-world impact, credible analysis, and diverse voices—remains our best route to securing a free, open, and inclusive digital future for all.
