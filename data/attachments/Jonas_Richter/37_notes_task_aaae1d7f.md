# Comprehensive Travel & Hiking Itinerary for Jonas Richter: Berlin – Leipzig – Malerweg (Saxon Switzerland), October 2024

## Overview

This itinerary outlines my journey from Berlin to Leipzig to attend a technology policy conference, followed by travel to Bad Schandau and a three-day trek along the Malerweg in Saxon Switzerland. I’ve structured the document to meet both my professional needs and personal interests, with a focus on digital connectivity, privacy, and maximizing both productivity and reflection during travel.

I've incorporated reliable resources and the latest best practices in Germany for 2024, making sure the plan supports efficient use of digital tools for ticketing, navigation, and secure communication. Safety during outdoor activities, optimal conference participation, and moments for professional and personal reflection are central to each section.

## 1. Travel Itinerary: Berlin → Leipzig → Bad Schandau

### Train Schedules, Digital Ticketing & Transit Practices

#### Berlin to Leipzig (Deutsche Bahn)

Traveling from Berlin Hauptbahnhof to Leipzig Hauptbahnhof is straightforward, with frequent and fast ICE connections (every 30–60 minutes). I prefer morning departures; for example, the 07:30 ICE 1000 arrives at 08:44, or the 08:30 ICE 1006 which gets me to Leipzig by 09:44.

Booking in 2024 is entirely digital. I use the DB Navigator app on my phone for mobile tickets (stored both in-app and in my Apple Wallet), which means I don’t need printouts. The app is invaluable for real-time updates on delays, platform changes, or seat reservations, and also helps me quickly connect to onboard WiFi.

For privacy, I typically reserve a seat in the “Ruhebereich” (silent carriage) and use my own VPN if sensitive work materials are involved. It’s also helpful to have a power bank and headphones handy for a productive ride.

**Useful Links:**

- [Deutsche Bahn Timetable & Booking](https://www.bahn.com/en)
- [DB Navigator App (iOS)](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/db-navigator/id343555245)
- [DB Navigator App (Android)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=de.hafas.android.db)

#### Leipzig to Bad Schandau

After the conference, I travel from Leipzig to Bad Schandau, typically via a direct IC or RE to Dresden Hauptbahnhof, then catching the S1 S-Bahn to Bad Schandau. The journey is comfortable—approximately two hours in total. I book this leg through the DB app, which issues a single digital ticket covering both train and S-Bahn segments. At Dresden, the S-Bahn connections are easy to track via the app, and I’ve found [VVO Dresden Transport](https://www.vvo-online.de/) useful for last-mile planning or regional ticket options.

**Transit Checklist:**

- Smartphone with DB Navigator, tickets cached offline
- Mobile power bank
- Headphones (preferably noise-canceling)
- VPN for privacy
- Durable backpack for electronics
- PDF and local backups of all travel documents

## 2. Leipzig Conference Navigation: Hauptbahnhof → Kongresshalle

### Cycling to the Venue

Leipzig is a bike-friendly city, and I enjoy cycling from the main train station to the Kongresshalle am Zoo. The route, approximately 2.3 km, passes Goethestraße and Augustusplatz, and takes about 10–15 minutes. I prefer Nextbike for rental: bikes are readily available near the station.

Before any trip, I download the route using [Komoot](https://www.komoot.com/) or [OsmAnd+](https://osmand.net/) to ensure offline navigation if mobile signal drops. I use a sturdy Quad Lock mount for my phone, keeping it visible for navigation and calls. If I need to lock up my bike at the venue, I opt for Nextbike’s digital locks for convenience and security.

**Cycling Checklist:**

- Smartphone with Komoot/OsmAnd+, route preloaded
- Helmet (usually required by conference insurance)
- Laptop stored in a weatherproof pannier or backpack
- Small bike lock or rely on Nextbike system

## 4. Hiking Itinerary: Malerweg, Saxon Switzerland

After the conference, I travel to Bad Schandau to begin a three-day hike along the Malerweg’s first three stages. The trail, totaling roughly 37 km, traverses spectacular sandstone landscapes, dense forests, and picturesque villages. My plan emphasizes both natural immersion and opportunities to observe the intersection of rural infrastructure and digital connectivity—relevant to my ongoing research on tech access in remote areas.

### Sunrise Photography & Reflective Journaling

The misty sunrises on the Malerweg offer not only photographic inspiration but a chance to reflect on digital solitude. I plan early morning shoots at Schrammsteinaussicht (50.92039, 14.20421) and Bastei Bridge (50.96232, 14.07390). My camera settings for these moments: Canon M50, 15–45mm lens, set to f/8, ISO 200, bracketing exposures with manual white balance and tripod for stability.

While hiking, I keep a weatherproof notebook at hand for spontaneous insights—both technical (noting connectivity gaps, signage, etc.) and personal reflections. Prompt examples:
- How could rural 4G/5G pilots I encounter inform national coverage strategies?
- What analog moments challenge or enrich my views on digital dependency?
- Are there LoRa/mesh innovations possible for hikers and rangers in these dead zones?

Field notes with GPS tags provide unique perspective for later policy recommendations on digital inclusion in rural Germany.

## 6. Digital & Technical Best Practices

Staying connected and secure is essential throughout this journey. I always pre-download maps and tickets before entering areas where mobile signal drops out, such as on rural trains or deep valleys along the Malerweg. Public WiFi at conferences and hotels is common, but I consistently set strict privacy controls and assume unsecured networks by default.

During networking, I prefer digital “tap” cards and QR code sharing, which is both private and efficient. For research, geo-tagged notes on WiFi blind spots and infrastructure gaps are saved for integration into future policy documents. I pack both USB and solar charging options: redundancy is key when hiking or working remotely.
