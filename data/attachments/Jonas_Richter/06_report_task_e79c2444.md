# Meeting Notes: Review of EU Parliamentary Amendments on Digital Rights Policy

**Date:** 2024-02-08  
**Time:** 09:00–11:00 CET  
**Location:** Open Internet Foundation, Kreuzberg Office

---

## Purpose of the Review

This review session was organized to systematically analyze the most recent amendments—adopted or under debate—by the European Parliament that affect digital rights policy within the European Union. Our efforts are directly aligned with the Open Internet Foundation’s central mission: safeguarding and promoting open internet principles across European policy frameworks. Practical objectives for this analysis included:

- **Defending Open Internet Principles:** Reinforcing net neutrality, transparency, non-discrimination, and the protection of fundamental rights in digital environments.
- **Anticipating Legislative Developments:** Identifying both immediate and longer-term regulatory, legal, and implementation trends that could impact digital rights and freedoms.
- **Strengthening Civil Society Collaboration:** Highlighting opportunities to broaden advocacy coalitions and respond collectively to legislative challenges.

Through this review, we sought to inform both internal strategic planning and broader advocacy campaigns by tracking notable policy changes, assessing their potential impacts, and outlining practical actions for the Foundation and its partners.

---

## Summary of Key Amendments Reviewed

### Background and Methodology

During the review period, accessing official EU Parliament amendment texts was restricted, which limited direct citation of exact legislative language. Nevertheless, our analysis drew upon established parliamentary cycles, detailed procedural notes, and up-to-date commentary from active civil society groups. We relied on official legislative calendars, session agendas, and secondary reports to identify central areas of amendment activity in 2023–2024. The following themes emerged as focal points of current parliamentary deliberations.

### Focus Areas

1. **Net Neutrality:**  
   Amendments focused on reinforcing or altering net neutrality obligations. In particular, we examined legislative activity around the Open Internet Regulation (EU 2015/2120)—notably Articles 3 and 5, which are pivotal in securing meaningful non-discrimination and openness in network traffic management.

2. **Platform Regulation and Content Moderation:**  
   Proposed changes to the Digital Services Act (DSA, EU 2022/2065), especially Articles 13, 17, and 28, raised significant implications for intermediary liability, algorithmic transparency, and procedures for content removal. Policy discussions continue to revolve around the extent and frequency of mandatory reporting and user redress.

3. **Surveillance and Data Access:**  
   The ongoing debate over the draft ePrivacy Regulation and recent amendments to eIDAS (EU 910/2014) highlighted contentious issues surrounding user consent, mandatory metadata retention, and secure digital identity verification. We noted that legislative proposals aim to strike a balance between security interests and user privacy.

4. **Interoperability and Data Portability:**  
   Amendments to the draft Data Act and the Digital Markets Act (DMA, EU 2022/1925) focus on strengthening users’ rights to data portability and improving interoperability standards. Particular attention is being paid to Articles 6 and 14 of the DMA, which are critical for fair competition and limiting anti-competitive “gatekeeping.”

5. **Encryption and Security:**  
   New provisions seek to address the future of end-to-end encryption in digital communications, with some proposals seeking to maintain robust standards and others threatening to create potential exceptions for lawful interception.

## Analysis of Amendments and Their Impact

The following analysis is structured to address both the immediate and long-term implications of the proposed and debated amendments.

### Immediate Implications

- **Net Neutrality Protections:**  
  Strengthening legal safeguards against discriminatory traffic management remains crucial for ensuring an open and equitable digital environment. Recent amendments aimed at eliminating loopholes in the Open Internet Regulation would limit the ability of internet service providers to create preferential “fast lanes” or restrict certain services based on commercial interests. Dilution of these protections, on the other hand, would set a troubling precedent for prioritization deals and undermine the principle of a level playing field.

- **Transparency in Platform Algorithms and Content Moderation:**  
  Mandated transparency reports and clearer procedures for content moderation, as outlined in DSA amendments, are key to fostering greater accountability by online platforms. Civically, these requirements support users’ rights to information and meaningful redress, especially when automated decision-making affects access to information or participation in public discourse.

- **Surveillance and Data Retention:**  
  New clauses relating to data access and metadata retention stipulate exceptions for national security or law enforcement purposes. These carve-outs risk expanding state surveillance capabilities and undermining public trust—particularly if they are vaguely worded or lack effective oversight.

- **Interoperability and Portability:**  
  Stringent enforcement of interoperability and data portability would increase user autonomy and foster competitive digital markets. However, amendments that weaken such provisions or introduce overly broad security exceptions create uncertainties and open the door to renewed vendor lock-in.

### Long-Term Impacts

- **Regulatory Leadership and Norm Diffusion:**  
  The EU’s evolving frameworks on net neutrality, platform accountability, and encryption are increasingly influential at the international level. Regular updates to these articles establish standards that may shape future digital rights policy beyond Europe’s borders.

- **Risks to Encryption and Digital Sovereignty:**  
  Provisions that provide for new lawful interception capabilities or weakened encryption frameworks pose serious risks to digital rights and information security. Weakening EU commitments to robust encryption would not only harm privacy but could also diminish Europe’s reputation as a global norm-setter for digital rights.

- **Trust and Participation:**  
  The cumulative effect of clear, enforceable safeguards can enhance user trust in digital services and foster a vibrant, participatory society. Conversely, the proliferation of ambiguous or surveillance-expanding amendments can erode public confidence and hamper the EU’s digital sovereignty objectives.

## Action Items and Follow-Up Tasks

1. **Ongoing Monitoring:**  
   Closely track parliamentary committee debates and plenary votes, with special attention to amendments affecting net neutrality and exceptions to encryption standards. Identifying late-stage amendments is essential for timely intervention.

2. **Civil Society Coordination:**  
   Convene a working group including key partners such as EDRi and Access Now. A unified, fact-based response enhances persuasive impact, especially regarding contentious measures on interoperability and encrypted communications.

3. **Targeted Policy Briefs:**  
   Prepare concise, evidence-based briefs on critical issues, especially the transparency and net neutrality amendments, for immediate circulation to Members of the European Parliament (MEPs) and allied advocacy groups. Early, clear communication helps shape parliamentary perceptions and decision-making.

4. **Legal-Technical Analysis:**  
   Commission expert analysis to determine the compatibility and practical implications of proposed amendments within the frameworks of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

5. **Public Communication:**  
   Issue timely statements to highlight significant policy wins (for example, anti-discrimination measures in internet traffic management) and articulate public concerns where digital rights are put at risk (such as the lawful interception of encrypted communications).

**Further Research Priorities:**

- Develop a detailed implementation timeline for major amendments, identifying potential bottlenecks or risks in the transposition and enforcement stages within Member States.
- Monitor industry reactions and lobbying efforts, especially in areas of interoperability and encryption, to anticipate possible counter-arguments or attempts to dilute legislative language.
- Analyze emerging reservations or divergent positions during the European Council phase to refine advocacy tactics and strengthen coalition resilience.
- Once official amendment texts are released, ensure a comprehensive review and confirm alignment with civil society priorities.
