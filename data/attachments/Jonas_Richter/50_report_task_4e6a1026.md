## Feedback from Dr. Marta Nowak: Integration and Implementation

On November 8, 2024, Dr. Marta Nowak provided us with detailed, actionable feedback. Her insights have played a vital role in refining our program’s direction, with a particular focus on inclusivity, partnership building, and robust evaluation.

**Key Points from Dr. Nowak's Feedback:**
1. **Inclusivity for Hard-to-Reach Groups:** Dr. Nowak emphasized the necessity to tailor workshop content for groups such as the elderly, migrants, and people with disabilities.  
2. **Partnership with Public Libraries:** She advocated for leveraging libraries, which are trusted and accessible community resources, as digital learning hubs and outreach partners.
3. **Measuring Impact:** There was an urgent call for more rigorous and quantifiable measurement of digital literacy outcomes, using both immediate and longer-term metrics.
4. **Outreach Documentation:** Dr. Nowak recommended detailed documentation of outreach pilots, including anonymized stories and practical lessons learned.

**Integration into Project Activities:**

We responded to each of these recommendations as follows:

- **Workshop Adaptation:** We have revised our curriculum to include relatable, scenario-based exercises aimed at diverse groups. The November pilot sessions incorporated these changes with positive feedback, particularly from elderly and migrant participants.  
- **Strengthened Library Partnerships:** We approached regional libraries, drafted a memoranda of understanding template, and scheduled staff training sessions to facilitate their role as digital learning hubs.  
- **Evaluation Measures:** A dual-tiered evaluation framework has been developed, combining pre/post digital skills assessments with three-month follow-ups, to provide a clearer picture of impact and areas for further improvement.  
- **Enhanced Documentation:** All pilot events are now documented using a standard template that captures demographic data, anonymized user narratives, and outcome summaries. This approach has improved our reporting transparency and provided a clearer link between activity and impact for all stakeholders.

Dr. Nowak’s feedback has directly enhanced our strategies for reaching underserved populations, strengthened our evidence base for policy dialogue, and brought greater transparency to our programming.

## Innovative Outreach Strategies: Café Einstein Team Lunch

During an informal team lunch at Café Einstein on November 11, 2024, the relaxed atmosphere encouraged an open exchange of ideas, leading to the development of several new outreach concepts. These strategies emerged organically from our shared experiences and observations, and reflect both our ambition and the practical considerations of community engagement.

**1. Micro-Volunteer Digital Coaches**

We recognized that empowering participants as peer-trainers can dramatically extend our project’s reach. The plan involves developing concise, accessible training modules and offering small stipends to volunteers, fostering a sense of ownership within communities. This model proved especially promising in regions with limited resources, as it builds sustainable local capacity and cultivates ongoing engagement well beyond the program’s formal interventions.

**2. Pop-up Digital Literacy Kiosks at Local Markets**

Another idea that resonated with the team was installing mobile kiosks at popular community gathering points like local markets. Designed with portability and flexibility in mind, these kiosks can attract individuals who may not attend traditional workshop venues. We plan to staff them with part-time facilitators using portable hardware kits, lowering barriers to entry and enabling first-time digital engagement for a broad cross-section of the population.

**3. Regional Social Media Digital Ambassadors**

We discussed the potential of leveraging youth and local influencers to act as digital ambassadors within their own social networks. Through a combination of social media training and small grants, these ambassadors are positioned to create and distribute content that resonates locally, encouraging grassroots participation and viral messaging. This approach not only broadens our audience, but also ensures our messaging feels authentic.

By piloting these locally inspired strategies, we intend to supplement our traditional top-down outreach methods. The Café Einstein session highlighted the value of informal settings for surfacing creative solutions and strengthening team buy-in. Full notes from this meeting are documented in our internal repository [1].

Activating the library partnership quickly extends our reach, particularly to communities with limited access to other institutions or connectivity. Early engagement also lays the foundation for sustainable, institutionalized programming.

Testing our micro-volunteer, market kiosk, and digital ambassador models in selected regions will provide practical evidence to inform broader adoption. Collecting data during this phase will be crucial for future scaling decisions.

**Deadline:** December 10, 2024

Systematic preparation for scaling outreach to new areas is essential to fulfill our national objectives. Early groundwork ensures that, pending confirmation of additional resources and staffing, we can expand rapidly and consistently.
