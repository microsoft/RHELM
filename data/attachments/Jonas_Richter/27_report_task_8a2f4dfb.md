## Discussion Summary

### 1. Review of 'Privatbildung Jetzt!' Berlin Expansion Policy Brief

Jonas Richter opened the meeting with an overview of the current draft of the policy brief, referencing the most recent guidelines from the Bundesministerium für Bildung und Forschung regarding private education initiatives. The group examined how these guidelines intersect with Berlin’s regulatory landscape, especially in light of recent developments.

The policy rationale section was reviewed in detail, with particular attention paid to the relationship between private sector engagement and measurable improvements in digital education outcomes, as evidenced by KfW Research (2023). The team discussed refining this section to provide clearer, data-driven arguments for stakeholder endorsement.

Dr. Petra Schulze emphasized the need to further align the proposed framework with updates to the Berliner Privatschulgesetz (2024). Licensing standards and the provision of digital infrastructure were highlighted as crucial points for regulatory compliance. She recommended highlighting the most recent amendments directly within the policy brief to ensure accuracy.

Frau Becker focused on sharpening the policy’s articulation of benefits for underserved communities in Berlin. She suggested adding a dedicated subsection with specific examples and metrics, recognizing the role of digital education in addressing inequities.

### 2. Incorporation and Analysis of Hamburg Case Study Feedback

The team proceeded to analyze key lessons from the Hamburg pilot (2022–23). Detailed feedback highlighted the value of streamlined administrative processes, robust impact measurement systems, and inclusive stakeholder engagement practices.

Jonas Richter presented data from the Landesinstitut für Lehrerbildung und Schulentwicklung (2023) showing significant gains in digital inclusion following Hamburg’s initiatives. He recommended that Berlin adopt Hamburg’s multi-stakeholder governance model, noting it led to improved program ownership and data sharing among stakeholders.

Discussion also touched on the compatibility of Hamburg’s quality assurance methods with Berlin’s legal and operational frameworks. Dr. Petra Schulze advised a careful integration of successful evaluation methodologies, stressing the need to conform to Berlin-specific requirements. She referenced a Senate Chancellery working paper (2024) on cross-state benchmarking and proposed selectively adapting metrics that have demonstrated success.

The group agreed that a comparative overview—detailing where adaptations or deviations from the Hamburg model are necessary—should be added to the policy document to support transparency and rationale.

### 3. Final Edits, Impact Assessment, and Next Steps

The team reviewed a set of recommendations for editing and structuring the policy brief:

- Clarified roles and responsibilities for all participating stakeholders.
- Refined language around legal compliance to more clearly address current licensing and operational standards under the Berliner Privatschulgesetz.
- Strengthened provisions for data transparency, incorporating guidelines for open reporting and external audits.

Dr. Schulze proposed adding an appendix that summarizes third-party evaluations conducted during both the Hamburg and Berlin pilot phases, to reinforce the empirical basis for recommendations. Frau Becker agreed and took responsibility for compiling these materials.

The policy’s anticipated impact was discussed in detail. The revised framework is expected to decrease application processing times by approximately 20%, improve satisfaction among schools and community partners (to be measured by pre- and post-implementation surveys), and mitigate compliance risks through clarified procedures and reporting structures.

All proposed changes and additions were assessed in terms of policy coherence, legal certainty, and alignment with strategic objectives. The team agreed on the necessity of conducting a comprehensive internal review before submitting the final package to the Senate.

## Next Steps

The immediate priority is to revise the policy brief expeditiously, integrating all feedback and explicitly referencing each source to ensure transparency and external auditability. The team will adopt relevant elements of Hamburg’s governance and quality control frameworks, providing a rationale for each adaptation and benchmarking anticipated impact based on Hamburg’s post-implementation data.

Plans are in place to launch a targeted stakeholder communication round in late July, validating the practicality and relevance of the proposed changes with education stakeholders in Berlin. This outreach will help verify best practice alignment and facilitate continued stakeholder buy-in.

Documentation for the Senate submission will be prepared to ensure complete traceability of all analytical assumptions and legal references, in line with the Berlin Education Department’s recent mandates on transparency in public-private education initiatives.

Finally, an impact monitoring protocol will be established to track outcomes following the policy’s adoption. Quantitative benchmarks will be used to assess policy success and inform any necessary future revisions.

## Reflections and Key Insights

Cross-regional knowledge-sharing—particularly learning from the Hamburg case—has proved invaluable to the refinement of Berlin’s policy approach, leading to improved program design and increased stakeholder engagement.

Attending proactively to legal and compliance requirements, especially those spanning public and private boundaries, ensures smoother implementation and minimizes disruptive revisions later in the process.

Grounding policy recommendations in robust empirical data from pilot programs and third-party evaluations not only strengthens the policy’s credibility but also supports its defensibility during legislative review.

A clear, targeted articulation of benefits for underrepresented groups will be essential in building political and public support for the policy’s expansion. Similarly, well-delineated roles and clear timelines will help maintain momentum and accountability as the project moves toward Senate review.
