## Expert Feedback and Integration

Feedback from guest experts Dr. Petra Schulze and Marta Klein has played a critical role in shaping the workshop’s design and content.

### Insights from Dr. Petra Schulze

- **Strengthening Evaluation of PETs:**  
  Dr. Schulze highlighted the importance of rigorous, evidence-based evaluation of privacy-enhancing technologies. She recommended that demonstrations extend beyond presenting functionality to cover potential limitations, vulnerabilities, and practical challenges that arise during deployment.

- **Alignment with Current Standards:**  
  She stressed that all technical content should reflect the most recent guidance from ENISA, as well as updated standards such as ISO/IEC 27701 and recommendations by the European Data Protection Board (EDPB).

- **Tiered Technical Engagement:**  
  Dr. Schulze suggested structuring certain sessions to engage both advanced and intermediate participants, facilitating learning through scenario-based exercises with real-world applicability.

### Insights from Marta Klein

- **In-depth Policy Analysis:**  
  Marta Klein encouraged deeper policy analysis, particularly through comparative reviews of GDPR implementation across EU member states. She emphasized the value of examining practical differences and real policy impacts.

- **Participant-driven Policy Exercises:**  
  To foster critical thinking, she proposed group exercises focused on hypothetical scenarios involving cross-border data transfers, collaboration between supervisory authorities, and issues surrounding digital sovereignty.

- **Incorporating Recent Jurisprudence:**  
  Referencing landmark decisions by the European Court of Justice (such as Schrems II), Ms. Klein recommended exploring their practical implications on data flows and technology rollout.

### Application to Workshop Structure

These recommendations have guided enhancements across all modules:

- Demonstrations now incorporate deeper technical analyses, including risk and benefit assessments of PETs.
- Policy exercises are designed to mirror real multi-country scenarios within the EU context.
- All learning objectives, activities, and resource materials consistently reference authoritative, up-to-date EU documentation and case law.
- Each session is structured to build bridges between policy and technical stakeholders, encouraging collaborative problem-solving.

## Detailed Workshop Modules

### Module 1: The Evolving EU Digital Privacy Landscape

**Facilitator:** Marta Klein

This opening module provides a thorough overview of the EU’s regulatory environment in digital privacy, beginning with key frameworks such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the latest developments in the ePrivacy Regulation. Participants will examine the role and guidance of the European Data Protection Board and will discuss how these directives are put into practice across different jurisdictions. A focused comparison between Germany and France will bring out distinct national enforcement approaches, and the module will reference current ENISA threat landscape reports to illustrate the intersection of security challenges and privacy requirements.

**Key Discussion Points:**
- Structure and intent of GDPR and ePrivacy Regulation  
- EDPB’s interpretative guidance and its impact on compliance  
- Operation of the “one-stop-shop” mechanism and cross-jurisdictional enforcement  
- Analysis of ECJ case law such as Schrems II and its implications for transnational data processing

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Participants will gain an in-depth understanding of the current European regulatory landscape, and will be able to critically compare harmonization efforts and enforcement practices across major member states.

---

### Module 2: Advanced Technical Foundations of Privacy-Enhancing Technologies

**Facilitator:** Dr. Petra Schulze

Module 2 explores the technological backbone of digital privacy through focused case studies and interactive demonstrations. Topics include differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, secure multi-party computation, and advanced anonymization techniques, anchored to international standards such as ISO/IEC 20889:2018 and ENISA’s PET guidelines. The session delves into how these technologies are being adopted in European contexts, highlighting practical strengths as well as limitations and known attack vectors.

**Demonstration and Exercise:**
- Live setup and exploration of a differential privacy toolkit (OpenDP Library), illustrating how controlled noise addition works for privacy protection.
- Breakdown of anonymization standards with practical scenarios, including de-anonymization challenges in synthetic medical datasets.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Participants will acquire both a conceptual and practical grasp of leading PETs, understanding not only their benefits but also current technical and operational challenges. Attendees will be able to apply evaluation frameworks for PETs in hypothetical deployments, considering both effectiveness and risks.

---

### Module 3: Data Mapping & Risk Assessment (Hands-on Demonstration)

**Facilitator:** Prof. Dr. Laura Meier

This session focuses on practical skills in data mapping and privacy risk analysis, anchored in GDPR’s Article 35 requirements for Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs). Participants will use standard templates from EDPB and ENISA to map data flows and identify high-risk processing, leveraging digital tools such as CNIL’s open-source PIA tool.

**Collaborative Exercise:**
- Teams will map the data lifecycle of a fictional cross-border health research project, systematically identifying personal data flows, risk touchpoints, and potential non-compliance issues. Participants will then propose concrete mitigation strategies for identified privacy risks.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Attendees will develop hands-on expertise in documenting data processing activities, assessing privacy risks for complex projects, and recommending actionable risk reduction measures aligned with EU requirements.

---

### Module 4: Policy Impact Assessment Workshop

**Facilitator:** Marta Klein

This workshop module addresses the application of structured methodologies for evaluating policy and legislative proposals. Key methods include Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) and Fundamental Rights Impact Assessments, with special attention to emerging laws such as the AI Act and Data Governance Act.

**Group Work and Simulation:**
- Groups will analyze post-Schrems II challenges, assessing legal risks around cross-border data transfers and the coordination between national supervisory authorities.
- A structured scenario will prompt debate on digital sovereignty and collaborative privacy governance in EU research consortia.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Participants will strengthen their ability to interpret legislative initiatives, evaluate their real-world impact, and formulate policy recommendations supported by sound risk assessment.

---

### Module 5: Integrating Technical and Legal Privacy Approaches

**Facilitators:** Panel with Prof. Dr. Laura Meier, Dr. Petra Schulze, Marta Klein

The concluding module brings together lessons from both technical and policy perspectives. Through a facilitated panel and open Q&A, participants will examine best practices for integrating compliance with technical solutions. Emphasis will be placed on creating sustainable privacy programs that continue to adapt as the regulatory and threat landscape evolves.

**Panel Discussion:**
- Real-world examples illustrating the interplay between technical design choices and compliance obligations
- Discussion on fostering ongoing collaboration across technical and policy domains to meet evolving European privacy expectations

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Participants will leave with a holistic understanding of how to coordinate technical and legal strategies, preparing them to implement effective privacy solutions that meet both organizational objectives and regulatory requirements.
