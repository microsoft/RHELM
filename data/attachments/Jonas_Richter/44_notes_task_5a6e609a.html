Workshop Expansion Proposals

In response to increasing calls for more robust, inclusive, and impactful workshops on open internet governance, stakeholders convened to generate a set of targeted expansion strategies. The following table outlines these ideas, detailing their significance, potential obstacles, and resource requirements to facilitate detailed planning and prioritization.

Table: Expansion Ideas Overview

Idea Policy Relevance & Stakeholder Impact Potential Challenges Resources Needed
1. Integration of Open Standards in All Outputs Facilitates interoperability, supports open internet ideals, lowers barriers for civil society and SMEs. Encourages competitive and transparent ecosystems. Resistance from vendors reliant on proprietary interests; lack of uniform regulatory support (especially in public procurement). Experts in protocol and standard mapping; Communication materials for policy advocacy; Updated procurement policies and guidance.
2. Digital Platform for Stakeholder Participation Enhances inclusivity by gathering a broader range of perspectives and strengthening the legitimacy of outcomes. Encourages vibrant policy debates with direct input from multiple sectors. Ensuring digital accessibility—particularly for marginalized or remote groups; Navigating data privacy legalities (such as GDPR); Safeguarding against undue corporate influence. Secure, open-source platform development; Multilingual support; Materials and funds for outreach and accessibility initiatives.
3. Policy Labs for Regulatory Sandboxing Offers a structured environment for innovative regulatory trials, enabling evidence-based adjustment to policy affecting internet openness. Attracts innovators and fosters adaptive governance. Legal boundaries that restrict regulatory experimentation; Apprehension among regulatory authorities; Additional coordination efforts required. Formalized partnerships with legal authorities; Liaison and advisory staff with compliance expertise; Champions to guide and promote pilot initiatives.
4. Transparency Dashboard for Internet Governance Improves accountability and public trust in decision-making processes; Enables ongoing advocacy rooted in accessible information. Institutional hesitation to share sensitive data; Difficulty in aggregating and presenting complex governance information; Risk of overloading stakeholders with information. Robust data infrastructure (APIs and databases); User-centric design for accessibility; Dedicated team for regular content updates; Regulatory expertise for thorough impact analyses.
5. Continuous Digital Rights Training Empowers all stakeholders to better advocate for user rights and fosters informed uptake of rights-preserving technologies. Cultivates a culture of policy literacy and engagement. Overcoming participant burnout; Reconciling diverse regulatory landscapes and attitudes toward rights education; Limited engagement with ongoing training modules. Multilingual curriculum development; Collaboration with universities and NGOs; Institutional recognition of training achievements.
6. Open Data Repository for Outcomes Makes workshop results transparent and openly reusable, supporting secondary research and evidence-based advocacy. Reinforces the commitment to accessibility. Ensuring full compliance with privacy laws; Risk of data being reused without attribution; Securing long-term funding for ongoing maintenance and updates. Reliable technical hosting; Comprehensive legal and data protection review; Clearly articulated policies on data sharing and citation.
7. Proactive Policymaker Engagement on Open Internet Norms Bridges the gap between domestic policies and international open internet standards, ensuring broader, sustained impact from workshop recommendations. Fragmented policy environments and conflicts between national regulations; Challenges in building cross-border cooperation; Diplomatic nuances. Policy briefs and advocacy resources; Strong coalition networks; Allocated budgets for international dialogue and travel.
8. Multilingual Resource Center Breaks language barriers, broadening participation beyond English-speaking communities, and upholds principles of equity and inclusion. Scarcity of resources for extensive translation and localization; Maintaining both legal precision and cultural nuance during adaptation. Sufficient translation budgets; Guidance from regional legal experts; Technical infrastructure for dynamic localization across digital platforms.
9. Youth and Emerging Voices Scholarship Program Ensures new perspectives and leadership are constantly developing, enriching policy debates and succession planning for advocacy networks. Guaranteeing meaningful, ongoing engagement after initial involvement; Avoiding superficial or tokenistic inclusion; Funding sustainable and equitable participation. Selection committee with diverse representation; Scholarship and travel support; Structured mentorship opportunities with policy professionals.
10. Impact Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) Framework Measures effectiveness, guides iterative improvement, and provides justification for continued or expanded investment. Enables transparent reporting to funders and the public. Aligning measurement tools with desired policy outcomes; Significant resource investments; Sometimes, organizational resistance to external evaluation. Advanced analytics tools; Expertise in indicator and metric design; Long-term funding for independent evaluators.

Notes: - Each proposal explicitly reinforces the foundational values of open internet governance: accessibility, neutrality, transparency, and inclusive participation. These correspond directly to guidelines set by institutions such as the Internet Governance Forum (IGF) and the Open Government Partnership. - Consideration of regulatory and policy challenges, such as privacy protection, international policy divergence, and the need for credible accountability mechanisms, is woven throughout. - The table format is structured in accordance with best practices for transparent, participatory brainstorming and policy planning.

Path Forward

To maximize the impact and feasibility of these proposals, a set of immediate, coordinated steps is identified across policy engagement, advocacy, and technological development:

Policy Initiatives

  • Consultation with Key Policymakers: Early engagement will focus on exploring regulatory sandboxing opportunities and the official endorsement of open standards. Building coalitions and buy-in at this stage lays the groundwork for more effective implementation.
  • Drafting Advocacy Briefings: Preparation of policy briefs targeting increased transparency in internet governance. These briefs will highlight best practices, anticipated benefits, and clear, actionable recommendations.

Advocacy and Outreach

  • Multistakeholder Working Group: Assemble a diverse cohort of representatives from civil society, government, technical community, and marginalized groups to guide the development of the digital participation platform.
  • Targeted Outreach Campaigns: Launch focused initiatives to encourage active involvement from youth and underrepresented communities. This includes facilitating access to scholarship programs and customized digital rights education.
  • Securing Endorsements: Approach respected figures and institutional leaders to champion the multilingual resource center and open data repository, lending credibility and extending reach.

Technology and Integration

  • Platform Assessment and Selection: Conduct a thorough evaluation of available secure, open-source platforms to support participation and the transparency dashboard. Assessment will factor in usability, accessibility, and scalability.
  • Open Data Infrastructure: Begin development of the technical infrastructure necessary to host and share workshop outputs, ensuring consistency with prevailing data protection standards such as GDPR.
  • Localization Support: Develop processes for multilingual support, ensuring that digital tools and training content are accessible in all relevant languages, and sensitive to local legal and cultural context.

Priority will be given to actions that most effectively support inclusive participation and facilitate quick, responsive policy engagement—needs repeatedly emphasized during both structured discussion and informal exchanges.

Stakeholder Insights

In addition to collective brainstorming, one-on-one conversations with experienced contributors provided valuable perspective and practical guidance:

Reflection from Ahmed

Ahmed highlighted the persistent digital divides that continue to undermine inclusive policy development. He emphasized the need to actively engage and build capacity within underserved communities to avoid replicating existing exclusions in digital policy structures. His insights reinforce the importance of equity not only in policy outcomes but throughout the decision-making process.

Observations from Frau Lehmann

Frau Lehmann brought attention to the necessity of sustainable and policy-aligned infrastructure that can deliver lasting transparency and hold actors accountable. She pointed out that independent oversight within impact assessment processes significantly increases trust and legitimacy among stakeholders. She also cautioned that scaling digital tools rapidly, without first securing regulatory support, could backfire and erode trust.

Implications for Open Internet Advocacy and Policy Development:

These perspectives reinforce that the legitimacy, effectiveness, and public trust in open internet initiatives hinge on three foundational commitments: transparency in governance, equitable access, and deep, ongoing stakeholder engagement. International frameworks and best practices from organizations like IGF, UNESCO, and the Open Government Partnership further validate these focus areas. As internet regulation continues to evolve in complexity and significance, designing adaptive and evidence-based frameworks remains essential for safeguarding rights and supporting sustainable development.
