# Meal Prep Plan for Saturday Evening: My Lentil-Spinach Stew from Boxhagener Platz

---

## Overview

Every Saturday, I make it a ritual to visit Boxhagener Platz Markt in Berlin. I love tracing the rows of colorful produce, picking out what looks freshest, and planning my weekend dinners around whatever the market has to offer. For this Saturday’s dinner, I decided on a hearty lentil-spinach stew—a dish that’s become a staple in my home for both comfort and nutrition. Using almost entirely local and organic ingredients, this stew comes together easily in a Berlin kitchen and fits right in with my goals of eating healthily, supporting regional farmers, and keeping things sustainable.

## My Step-by-Step Prep

**1. Ingredient Prep**
- Rinse spinach well—sometimes there’s sandy grit—and roughly chop.
- Kohlrabi gets peeled and diced into small cubes (around 1 cm pieces).
- I peel and finely dice the onion, carrots, and celery for good texture.
- Garlic gets peeled and finely minced.
- Tofu gets cut into cubes, then patted dry with a towel to help it brown.
- Lentils are rinsed thoroughly in a fine-mesh sieve—removes dust and improves flavor.
- Parsley is chopped and set aside for a fresh finish at the end.
- I halve the lemon so it’s ready for squeezing.

**2. Tofu Browning**
- In a large, heavy pot, I warm half the olive oil over medium-high heat.
- Once hot, in goes the tofu cubes. I let them brown all over (about 5–7 minutes), turning for an even golden crust.
- I scoop them out and reserve them for later.

**3. Building the Stew Base**
- I lower the heat to medium, add a bit more oil if needed, then sauté the onion, carrots, and celery for 5–6 minutes until they turn soft and release their aroma.
- Next, I stir in the garlic, kohlrabi, and cumin—sautéing just long enough for the flavors to blend together (another 2–3 minutes).
- I add the drained lentils and toss everything to coat.
- In goes the vegetable stock and bay leaf. I increase the heat until it just starts boiling, then cover partially and let it simmer on low for about 20–25 minutes.

**4. Final Touches**
- When the lentils are tender but still intact, I stir in the chopped spinach and reserved tofu.
- The stew needs just 5 more minutes—enough for the spinach to wilt and the tofu to warm through.
- I season with salt, pepper, and fresh lemon juice, tasting to get the brightness just right. The bay leaf comes out at the end.

**5. To Serve**
- I ladle the stew into bowls and sprinkle with parsley. Sometimes I grate a bit of lemon zest on top or drizzle with a last splash of good olive oil.
- A slice of fresh sourdough from one of the market bakers makes it a complete meal.

**My Tips for Saving Time**
- While the tofu browns, I finish chopping the softer veg (like garlic and parsley).
- Lentils get rinsed while the onions are starting to sauté.
- Cleaning up in small spurts keeps the kitchen calm and pleasant.

## After Cooking: Storage and Serving

**Portioning and Serving**
- The stew gives me about four generous bowls, perfect for sharing with friends or keeping leftovers for the next few days.
- Bread from the market—especially the chewy sourdough—pairs beautifully with the stew.

**Refrigeration**
- Once the stew’s cooled (within a couple of hours), I pack it into glass containers and chill it in the fridge.
- It keeps well for up to four days, and I find the flavors even improve on the second day.

**Freezing**
- For longer storage, I freeze individual portions—handy for busy evenings. I leave room in the container so the stew can expand as it freezes.
- It thaws best overnight in the fridge. The vegetables keep their bite, and the flavors stay vibrant.

**Reheating**
- To reheat, I warm the stew gently on the stove or in the microwave, stirring now and then. If it gets too thick, a splash of water or a bit more lemon brings it right back.
- When I pack it for work lunches, I tuck the parsley and a slice of lemon on the side to finish at the last minute.

**My Best Practices for Freshness and Flavor**
- Parsley and lemon always go on fresh, either when serving or after reheating—this keeps the stew tasting lively and preserves vitamin C.
- Spinach cooks quickly, so I’m careful not to overdo it; that way, it keeps its bright color and nutrients.
- I use glass or BPA-free storage containers—better for health and flavor.

## Why I Choose These Ingredients

**Lentils**
- I love that lentils bring plant-based protein, iron, and fiber to the table. They’re a staple in my diet, filling and gentle on the environment since they fix their own nitrogen and need little water to grow ([The Lancet][1]).

**Spinach**
- Fresh spring spinach from Berlin soils is rich in vitamin K, A, iron, and especially folate. Short supply chains at the market mean these greens carry fewer food miles ([The Lancet][1]).

**Kohlrabi**
- It feels right to buy kohlrabi at a Berlin market—it’s a local favorite and loaded with vitamin C and fiber. Choosing local root veg always makes sense when I want to minimize emissions and eat seasonally.

**Tofu**
- Tofu has become more available from Berlin artisans, sometimes even organically grown. It provides excellent protein and fits with my intention to reduce meat consumption. Plus, local or regional soy has a lower carbon footprint compared to animal proteins ([BzFE][2]).

**Why Organic and Local?**
- I prioritize organic when I can for reduced pesticide exposure and to keep small-scale Berlin farmers in business. The market’s “Bio” and regional stalls make it easy to shop this way and support urban agriculture.

I love knowing that almost everything in the pot came from a few blocks away. The flexible, step-by-step prep makes for a relaxed, efficient evening—even if friends drop by unexpectedly. A pot of this stew is like bringing the Berlin market right into my kitchen.
