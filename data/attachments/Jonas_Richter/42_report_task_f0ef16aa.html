3. Session Summaries

AV Setup and Technical Resolutions

To ensure a seamless hybrid event, time was dedicated to resolving AV and connectivity issues. A firmware mismatch with the main projector briefly delayed proceedings but was swiftly resolved with a patch and backup connectors, enabling participants both in the room and online to fully engage. Initial Wi-Fi instability was addressed by moving to a guest network and employing mobile tethering as a backup, which ensured that streaming and presentations continued with minimal disruption.

Reflections for Future Events:
Technical redundancy proved essential. Integrating a structured AV checklist and designating an IT contact for quick troubleshooting are recommended steps for subsequent workshops, particularly when simultaneous digital demonstrations are planned.

Toolkit Documentation Distribution

Participants received both printed and digital (USB) versions of the Privacy Curriculum Toolkit and associated teaching resources. Dr. Schulze walked attendees through the structure and navigation of the materials, clarifying how both teachers and policy stakeholders could utilize each component.

Immediate feedback highlighted the strengths and areas for development: - Teachers from Hamburg requested more locally relevant examples to better engage their student groups. - Neukölln representatives appreciated the ability to adapt and print modular lesson components, which supports differentiated instruction. - Minor accessibility concerns were noted, such as font size in technical diagrams for teachers with visual impairments, but the concise FAQ annex was widely welcomed.

A formal feedback survey was distributed, and full quantitative results will be analyzed for the curriculum’s next iteration.

Live Demonstration: Digital Privacy Exercise (Frau Becker)

Frau Becker led a live model lesson focusing on interactive, scenario-based privacy teaching. Using a blend of multimedia slides, in-class polling, and student role-play, the demonstration vividly illustrated how personal data can be traced across various digital platforms.

Participants actively engaged in simulating data requests and adjusting privacy settings on familiar digital interfaces. Socratic questioning techniques fostered deeper critical thinking among both students and observing educators.

Feedback:
This session received the highest marks from attendees, particularly for showing how theoretical concepts can be translated into real classroom situations. Attendees suggested allowing more time for Q&A following the session and proposed deeper exploration of risk management scenarios, especially for older or more advanced students.

Encrypted Messaging Group Exercise (Jonas Richter)

Following lunch, Jonas Richter introduced a hands-on group exercise on encrypted messaging. Participants formed small groups and practiced sending, receiving, and decrypting basic messages using introductory tools. The activity was contextualized with a concise policy briefing highlighting European and German perspectives on encryption and the ongoing debates around balancing privacy with child safety.

Policy Discussion:
A productive exchange unfolded when some policy observers called attention to the omission of law enforcement considerations. One group advocated for a discussion on the principle of "privacy by design" rather than technical solutions alone, signaling the need for clearer instructional guidance that helps non-specialist educators connect foundational technical concepts with policy context.

Suggestions:
Teachers valued the accessibility of the practical exercise but noted the importance of age-appropriate materials and guidance for introducing cryptography with younger students.

Practice & Policy Breakout (Marta Klein)

During this interactive segment, participants self-organized by region to discuss real-world challenges of curriculum implementation. Using a "barrier and solution" mapping technique, groups identified obstacles such as inconsistent access to digital devices, variable school administration support, and the complexities of differing regional (Länder) policy interpretations.

From these discussions, several strategies emerged: - Develop school-based peer learning groups for ongoing support - Draft template communications for gaining administrative buy-in - Designate regional “policy champions” to facilitate adaptation and share best practices

The breakouts closed with a consensus on the importance of localized curriculum adaptation and ongoing educator peer networks to sustain momentum. A comprehensive list of proposed strategies will be finalized and shared with all regions post-event.

4. Analytical Highlights

Pilot Feedback from Neukölln

Teachers from the Neukölln pilot site reported improved digital privacy awareness among students, noting visible gains especially within disadvantaged groups. Lesson flexibility proved key—curriculum modules were best received when teachers could adjust content and pacing for varying digital literacy levels. Early evaluation data indicates 60% of teachers integrated the curriculum smoothly, while 30% advocated for more practical, hands-on activities.

Teacher Engagement

Teacher participation was strong, with the majority of core representatives attending at least two sessions. Informal peer-to-peer dialogues were consistently rated as the most valuable aspect of the event. Top teacher requests focused on increased access to real-life classroom case studies, more training videos, and the development of continuing professional development modules.

Policy Perspectives

The event provided a balanced forum for a range of policy opinions. Representatives from the Open Internet Alliance and Freedom Data Foundation welcomed the curriculum’s rights-based foundation, but several observers stressed the importance of also addressing data sharing with public authorities, not solely commercial actors. Constructive dissent, including debate around the limits of student privacy versus safeguarding responsibilities, was documented to inform upcoming advisory processes and national discussions.
