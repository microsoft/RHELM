## Lecture Summary

### The Evolving Digital Rights Landscape (2024)

The lecture opened with an overview of current developments in European digital rights regulation. The landscape is rapidly changing, driven by the ongoing implementation of the Digital Services Act (DSA) and Digital Markets Act (DMA), as well as the near-finalization of the EU AI Act. Dr. Nowak underscored the pivotal shift from purely individual rights such as privacy, due process, and non-discrimination, toward greater scrutiny of platforms’ obligations in a data-driven society.

### Algorithmic Transparency: Concepts and Challenges

A central theme of the session was the complex issue of algorithmic transparency. Dr. Nowak differentiated between technical transparency—clarity on how algorithms operate internally—and procedural transparency—how algorithmic impacts are systematically assessed, communicated, and acted upon.

Legislative attention is increasingly focused on these aspects. For example, the DSA specifically mandates that “very large online platforms” disclose certain parameters of their recommendation and ranking algorithms, with Articles 27 and 34 forming the regulatory core. However, meaningful transparency remains difficult to operationalize. The challenge is to balance stakeholders’ demand for explainable, open systems with the legitimate need for companies to protect trade secrets and other sensitive intellectual property.

The adequacy of current transparency requirements is still a matter of active debate. Participants explored whether the information being disclosed is sufficiently granular to enable independent scrutiny, and questioned whether existing enforcement mechanisms will prove effective in practice.

### Regulatory Frameworks and Governance

Dr. Nowak provided an in-depth comparative analysis of the DSA, DMA, and the forthcoming AI Act regarding digital rights enforcement. The discussion highlighted the tension between the EU’s push for harmonized regulation and the residual discretion left to member states, leading to risks of fragmentation or regulatory arbitrage.

Independent audits and third-party oversight not only enhance public trust in algorithmic decision-making but are also becoming integral to mitigating the unintended consequences of complex digital systems. However, several challenges persist, particularly regarding the independence and effectiveness of audits, and the complexities of cross-border enforcement.

### Policy Developments and Contemporary Events

The lecture referenced several timely developments:
- The EU AI Act recently cleared the committee stage in the European Parliament (October 2024), marking a major advance in the legislative process.
- There are ongoing legal challenges from major platform providers aimed at elements of DSA implementation, particularly those concerning transparency obligations.
- Recent high-profile cases of algorithmic bias, especially affecting public sector decision-making (e.g., social welfare allocation tools), underscore the real-world consequences for discrimination and highlight the urgent need for robust and effective impact assessments ([EU Digital Services Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act-package)).

## Curriculum Roadmap Updates

### New Case Studies to be Integrated

- **Case Study A**: Legal challenge to the DSA’s algorithmic transparency provisions by a major social media platform.  
  _Purpose_: Demonstrates how regulated companies resist disclosure and the resultant friction within the EU regulatory environment. This case will facilitate nuanced debate on the strengths and weaknesses of current policy frameworks.

- **Case Study B**: Documented instance of algorithmic bias in a public sector social welfare allocation system.  
  _Purpose_: Provides real-world context for understanding discriminatory effects of algorithmic systems and the critical importance of robust independent audits.

### Reading List Additions

- **EU Digital Services Act – Articles 27, 34, 42**  
  These articles form the backbone of transparency obligations in the current regulatory landscape.

- **Prokova, E. & Staab, S. (2024). “EU AI Act and DSA: Convergences and Tensions.”**  
  This academic piece bridges the gap between evolving AI governance and digital rights protections.

- **Recent European Commission guidance on DSA enforcement**  
  Offers up-to-date insights on both practical and legal dimensions, ensuring students are familiar with the latest developments.

### Action Points for Upcoming Workshops

- Organize an expert panel on effective models of algorithmic audit, bringing together representatives from both industry and civil society.
- Develop a mock policy review exercise to directly compare DSA and AI Act approaches to algorithmic governance.
- Create assignments that encourage students to explore the risks and realities of regulatory fragmentation within the EU context.

### Rationale for Curriculum Enhancements

These additions are designed to address current policy debates, particularly those centering on transparency, technological impacts, and the tension between EU-level harmonization and national discretion. This approach ensures that the curriculum remains relevant and responsive to the ongoing evolution of digital rights law ([EU Digital Strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act-package)).
