**Date:** 2024-09-12  
**Author:** Jonas Müller

---

## Summary

This review analyzes current feedback from the Technology Policy Program’s efforts to strengthen educator outreach, particularly in promoting open internet advocacy and digital rights. Drawing on input from internal program reviews, outreach initiative reports, and educators’ own perspectives, the report identifies key challenges and areas of opportunity in increasing the effectiveness of outreach activities. The central aim is to ensure that educator engagement strategies are aligned with the program’s goals of advancing open internet principles, bolstering digital rights awareness, and maximizing the impact of policy or technical interventions. In addition to synthesizing stakeholder feedback, this review reflects on project management and workflow observations to support ongoing improvements in both process and outcomes.

## Observations and Insights from the Review

The aggregated feedback reveals a landscape where both the diversity of participants and the complexity of digital rights education demand more adaptive, integrated approaches. Several themes emerged:

- **Diverse Stakeholder Needs:** Feedback from classroom educators, regional partners, and program staff underscores the need for open communication across all levels. Issues reported in one location—such as resource gaps or engagement barriers—often reflect broader trends that require attention, highlighting the value of multi-channel communication.

- **Adaptable Resource Development:** One consistent issue is the lack of tailored, modular resources that recognize local policy realities and technical constraints. In regions with restrictive internet policies, generic outreach materials fall short, emphasizing the need for flexible, context-specific resources developed with direct input from teachers themselves.

- **Intersecting Policy and Technical Challenges:** Clearly distinguishing between needs for strategic policy change and those requiring technical solutions has helped clarify where to focus resources. However, there are significant areas of overlap—for example, secure technology platforms are essential for effective policy engagement in restrictive settings.

- **Operational Workflow Considerations:** Reviewing these materials and synthesizing feedback surfaced some practical challenges. For example, I experienced recurring technical disruptions—specifically, a persistent trackpad malfunction on my ThinkPad whenever there was excess environmental moisture. These interruptions, seemingly minor at first, tended to break concentration and interrupted the analytical process, occasionally leading to oversights or delays in prioritizing feedback. Addressing such workflow obstacles, whether through equipment upgrades or better planning, will be important for maintaining consistent productivity in future reviews.

## Next Steps & Actionable Policy Recommendations

Addressing the identified challenges requires a coordinated approach, with particular emphasis placed on the following priorities:

- **Co-creation of Educator Resources:** Assemble a dedicated taskforce to collaboratively design curricula and toolkits that address regional digital rights issues and open internet policy, ensuring these resources are easily adaptable to different educational contexts.

- **Accelerate Localization and Translation Initiatives:** Allocate additional resources to regionalize and translate outreach materials. Implement regular evaluations to assess their effectiveness in deepening engagement and supporting educators in diverse areas.

- **Strengthen Institutional Partnerships:** Build formal partnerships with educational organizations to provide ongoing support for educator-driven advocacy. This approach can help sustain initiatives, offer scalable models, and ensure alignment with the broader goals of the Technology Policy Program.

- **Expand Professional Development Opportunities:** Increase the scope and accessibility of online professional development, particularly in areas such as privacy, surveillance, and open internet advocacy. Prioritize outreach in regions with historically lower engagement and limited access to such training.

- **Advance Technical Infrastructure for Outreach:** Invest in technology platforms and tools that are secure, accessible, and resilient to connectivity limitations, especially in restrictive policy environments. Frequent user testing and continuous feedback mechanisms will be crucial for ongoing improvement.

- **Systematic Monitoring and Evaluation:** Establish a robust feedback loop to regularly assess the impact of new resources, professional development participation, and outreach activities. Use these findings to inform strategic adjustments and sustain progress toward program goals.
