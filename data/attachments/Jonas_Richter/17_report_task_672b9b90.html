Author: Jonas Richter
Context: Weekend Retreat—Harz Mountains
Intended Audience: Technology Policy & Open Internet Advocates, Germany
Date: 2024-05-18

Executive Summary

The 'Privatbildung Jetzt!' initiative is a strategic push to rethink Germany’s future of education and technology policy, placing private, autonomous learning and strong digital rights at center stage. Rooted in open internet advocacy, our vision is to guarantee equitable digital access, empower individuals through educational self-determination, and promote data literacy as essential rights for all citizens in a digitized society.

This vision isn’t emerging in a vacuum. Current shifts in the regulatory landscape—such as changes to the Netzwerkdurchsetzungsgesetz (NetzDG), the evolving Data Act, and intensifying debates about platform oversight at the national and European levels—present both opportunities and complex challenges. We face tangible obstacles: the inertia of large educational bureaucracies, limited cross-sectoral collaboration, difficulties around GDPR compliance for independent learning platforms, and skepticism toward open-source or unconventional models of digital education. In our intensive retreat in the Harz Mountains, these issues came into sharp focus, particularly through exchanges with experts like Frau Becker. Our discussions highlighted the urgent need for clearer evidence of policy impact, innovative forms of stakeholder engagement, and approaches grounded in robust data.

Key Insights and Inspirations from the Retreat

The retreat in the Harz Mountains offered a rare opportunity for focused, honest conversation among policy experts, educators, technologists, and advocates. Stepping away from day-to-day work and digital distractions, I, along with my fellow participants, experienced a blend of challenge and inspiration. Frau Becker’s perspectives on digital pedagogy stood out, and her emphasis on open-source frameworks left a lasting mark on how I now approach both strategy and advocacy.

Highlights from the Retreat

In-depth Exchange with Frau Becker:
Frau Becker argued persuasively for decentralized learning systems built from the ground up, driven by community needs and committed to digital sovereignty. Her passion for open-source software wasn’t just technical—it was a call to protect learners’ privacy and autonomy in a constantly shifting landscape. This reinforced my own conviction that technological independence and individual agency must be non-negotiable within digital education.

Project and Policy Development:
Following these discussions, I recognized the need for more participatory policymaking—more direct involvement from learners and educators in choosing the tools and methods they rely on. Integrating wellbeing and reflection into our advocacy framework, not as an afterthought but as a core pillar, now seems indispensable. The intersections between privacy, agency, and wellbeing are clearer to me than ever.

Personal Wellbeing and Cognitive Change: The ‘Digital Detox’ Effect

Reflection on the Retreat Experience

Spending several days immersed in the Harz landscape, away from screens, made a concrete difference in how I processed ideas and engaged with colleagues. Without the constant pull of notifications, I found myself listening more intently, contributing with greater presence, and appreciating the subtler cues in group discussions. The act of walking—sometimes in companionable silence, other times in lively debate—enabled me to connect dots between technical regulation, educational practice, and broader visions for societal wellbeing in ways I rarely experience in an office or online meeting.

My creativity picked up noticeably; conversations turned unexpectedly generative, especially while just hiking under the forest canopy. The ritual of mindfulness exercises in the morning and shared reflection rounds in the evenings changed my approach to both problem-solving and planning. By the time we returned, I was more focused and motivated, and I had already mapped out actionable steps for the next project phase.

Importantly, this experience forced me to acknowledge the long-term benefit of deliberate digital breaks for sustaining resilience and innovation—mirroring findings from the Fraunhofer-IAO and European research on digital health.

Project Practice Takeaways:

  • Regularly building structured reflection and tech-free time into both team and solo workflows helps prevent burnout and keeps the advocacy process energetic and creative.
  • I will advocate for making offsite retreats and “digital sabbaticals” a fixed part of our team’s calendar—not as perks, but as core tools for high-quality research and advocacy.

Recommendations for Future Research & Advocacy

  • Make nature-based, cross-disciplinary retreats a regular part of open internet policy development. These settings act as catalysts for authentic dialogue and risk-taking.
  • Institutionalize digital wellbeing strategies within advocacy organizations—not simply for productivity, but to build long-term resilience and creativity into project teams.
  • Expand pilot projects for private, open-source digital education, ensuring strong local stakeholder involvement at every stage.
  • Invest in transparent, rigorous evaluation from project inception, using mixed qualitative and quantitative methods to track both intended and unintended effects.
  • Maintain sustained, proactive dialogue with regulators—aiming for legal frameworks that are flexible and innovation-driven, but never at the expense of digital rights or user autonomy.
