Incident and Attendance Report: Digital Privacy Workshop

Event Title: Digital Privacy Workshop
Date: February 2, 2024
Location: Kreuzberg Community Center, Berlin
Organizing Body: Digital Rights Forum (DRF)

Executive Summary

On February 2, 2024, the Digital Rights Forum (DRF) held a Digital Privacy Workshop at the Kreuzberg Community Center in Berlin. The workshop’s primary aim was to strengthen participants’ knowledge and practical skills surrounding digital privacy, focusing on recent regulatory and policy developments such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the EU Digital Services Act (DSA), and current trends in data retention laws.

The event attracted community leaders, digital rights advocates, and local professionals committed to advancing privacy-friendly policies and protecting digital rights both personally and within their organizations. However, the workshop’s execution was significantly impacted by a series of logistical setbacks, most notably a double-booking at the venue that led to an unexpected relocation, and ongoing Wi-Fi connectivity problems that disrupted planned interactive activities. These challenges not only affected the workshop’s flow and overall participation but also highlighted the structural vulnerabilities commonly encountered when hosting community-oriented digital policy events.

This report documents the chronology of incidents, their effects on attendance and engagement, and analyzes both successful adaptations and areas requiring improvement. Recommendations are provided to mitigate similar risks in future events.

Chronology of Incidents and Issue Management

Venue Double-Booking and Relocation

The morning began as scheduled, with the DRF event coordinator, M.K., arriving onsite at 8:00 am to oversee setup. Upon arrival, M.K. was informed by Youth Theatre staff that they had a simultaneous booking for the main hall. Subsequent verification at the front office confirmed that both groups were officially scheduled for the same space. In response, both parties escalated the matter to center administration.

By 8:50 am, after mediation by the center manager, it was decided—based on established policies favoring events with child participants—that the Youth Theatre would stay in the main hall. DRF was relocated to the smaller multi-purpose room, which offered limited seating and suboptimal audio-visual capabilities.

Event staff quickly adjusted, issuing notifications about the change via email to registered attendees and posting clear signage at the center entrance. Registration activities resumed at 9:15 am in the new room, about 30 minutes behind schedule.

Staff Coordination and Communication

  • M.K. (Event Coordinator): Managed on-site communications with both the Youth Theatre and center administration, escalated the incident within DRF, and ensured that all attendees were promptly notified about the change.
  • S.L. (Lead Facilitator): Adapted the workshop agenda and coordinated technical requirements in the new space.
  • Community Center Manager: Guided the decision-making regarding space usage, basing their rationale on existing protocols that prioritize children’s activities.
  • Support Staff: Assisted in the physical relocation, updated all informational signage, and supported late arrivals during registration.

The decision to move reflected risk assessments that prioritized participant safety, child protection standards, and practical workshop needs. The new room, however, presented challenges: less physical separation from other center activities, limited AV equipment, and overall reduced capacity.

Wi-Fi Connectivity Issues

Compounding the venue disruption, staff noticed intermittent Wi-Fi failures during setup around 8:20 am. Throughout the day, connectivity remained unreliable, which significantly undermined interactive workshop components—particularly live demonstrations of privacy-focused digital tools and accessing up-to-date regulatory materials.

To adapt, facilitators distributed printed guides for practical exercises and encouraged participants to use mobile data where possible. Despite these efforts, network-dependent activities were curtailed or replaced by verbal and printed walkthroughs. Some attendees departed early, reportedly due to frustration with technical limitations and discomfort in the crowded, smaller room. The Q&A sessions, originally intended to involve hands-on tool use, shifted towards discussions of broader policy issues.

Actual Implementation

Relocation caused delays and a more condensed morning schedule. Registration was shortened, and participants had to wait until 10:00 am for refreshments. The opening sessions were combined and abridged due to staggered arrivals; the GDPR and DSA content was presented together in a condensed format.

The Legal Landscape session proceeded mostly as planned, though with fewer visual aids because the projector malfunctioned in the new space. Practical demonstrations—central to Session II—could not be run live; instead, printed step-by-step guides and oral instructions replaced interactive components. Lunch was curtailed, with some participants choosing an offsite venue to alleviate crowding.

Afternoon advocacy discussions were held but with diminished participation, as the cumulative effect of earlier disruptions took its toll on engagement. Facilitators extended the panel and Q&A session to maximize participant value, focusing discussions on high-level advocacy strategies rather than on technical demonstration.

Deviations and Omissions

  • Most interactive digital tool demonstrations were replaced by theoretical discussions due to persistent Wi-Fi and AV issues.
  • Networking and breakout sessions were deprioritized because of time and space constraints.
  • Some handouts, like DSA summary sheets, were distributed only digitally after the event rather than on-site, a compromise made necessary by the day’s constraints.

Underlying Causes

These deviations were directly attributable to the unexpected venue change (resulting in space, equipment, and time loss), Wi-Fi failures, and attendant drop in participant numbers. The facilitation team prioritized essential legal and policy content to ensure key objectives were still met despite challenges.

Analysis: Attendance, Engagement, and Workshop Impact

The day’s incidents had a measurable impact on the workshop’s success:

  • Attendance: Several participants were unable to locate the new room, leading to lower turnout than anticipated. Others left early after experiencing logistical and technical hurdles.
  • Engagement: Attendance and active involvement declined during the event. The smaller, crowded space and lack of reliable internet prevented the kind of hands-on participation central to the program design.
  • Content Achievement: Despite these constraints, the core legal updates and advocacy guidance were delivered, and feedback indicated that those who remained valued the expertise and discussion. However, practical skill-building and networking—other important objectives—were only partially realized.

Recommendations

Incident Preparedness and Mitigation

  • Secure written, double-confirmed reservations for all event spaces, and proactively identify backup room options with adequate facilities.
  • Assign personnel with explicit responsibility for rapid on-site incident management and escalation.
  • Prepare preprinted venue signage and ensure access to both email and SMS communication channels so all logistical updates reach attendees promptly.
  • Invest in portable Wi-Fi hotspot devices or arrange backup connectivity through the venue to support all digital activities.

Agenda Design and Participant Communication

  • Structure agendas to prioritize core content and flexible modules, so key topics can be covered even amid disruptions.
  • Circulate critical handouts and guides to registered attendees in advance.
  • Provide real-time event updates across multiple communication channels (email, SMS), especially when making last-minute logistical adjustments.

Best Practice Implementation

  • Conduct post-event surveys to systematically capture attendee feedback, especially regarding incident response and adaptation.
  • Maintain detailed incident logs in accordance with best practice guides from the Open Rights Group and similar organizations.
  • Explore the integration of distributed workshop tools (pre-recorded video demos, live-streamed remote sessions) to safeguard against local connectivity failures.
