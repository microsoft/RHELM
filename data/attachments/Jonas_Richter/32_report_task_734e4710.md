## 1. Key Decisions and Rationale

### 1.1 Campaign Logistics & Technology Policy

**Decisions:**
- The outreach campaign will champion open internet principles and digital literacy in secondary schools through a blended delivery model, combining in-person events with online resources.
- We agreed to pursue a hybrid approach, integrating both regional school networks and digital platforms, to ensure broad accessibility and compliance with current EU and German digital policy frameworks.

**Rationale:**
- This approach directly supports Germany’s ongoing digitalization in education and adheres to policy guidelines such as the Digitalpakt Schule.
- By combining digital and in-person methods, the campaign can adapt to diverse school infrastructures, promoting inclusivity and minimizing barriers to access.
- Although the hybrid model involves more complex planning, it offers greater flexibility for scaling the campaign across different regions and fits well with advocacy for an open internet.

### 1.2 Pilot Workshop Scheduling & Scalable Engagement

**Decisions:**
- We will begin with two pilot workshops in Berlin and Brandenburg, establishing an adaptable template that can be rolled out nationally.
- Early engagement with a broad pool of stakeholders—including teacher associations, IT pedagogical coordinators, and students—will be prioritized to guide content and delivery.

**Rationale:**
- Evidence from previous education technology initiatives consistently shows that regionally piloted, feedback-driven models are more readily adopted by schools and have greater impact.
- Engaging stakeholders from the outset is critical for institutional support and long-term sustainability.
- While this strategy might modestly delay national rollout, it strengthens the campaign’s foundation and enhances its potential for ongoing expansion.

### 1.3 Resource Allocation: Digital Tools & Open Educational Resources (OER)

**Decisions:**
- The campaign organization and resource hub will run on open-source platforms such as Nextcloud and Etherpad to maximize accessibility and data privacy.
- Existing high-quality OER under Creative Commons licenses will be curated and localized, forming the basis of educator outreach toolkits.
- Project management and content generation will be handled by a cross-functional team, comprising Jonas Richter, Dr. Petra Schulze, and two education interns from a partner higher education institution.

**Rationale:**
- Leveraging open-source tools aligns with both technology policy standards and ethical resource stewardship, ensuring that schools with limited budgets can participate fully.
- Using and adapting OER reduces costs, facilitates collaboration, and guarantees resource availability beyond the life of the campaign.
- Although some initial training will be required for our team and for participating educators, these tools are adaptable and likely to prove sustainable in the long term.
