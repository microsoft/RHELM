# My Daily Meal Plan – Berlin, 13 June 2024

## Introduction

As a technology policy analyst living in Berlin, balancing a demanding professional schedule with my values around health and sustainability is important to me. Given that today is a particularly busy workday, I’ve designed my meals to be simple yet nourishing, built around local, organic ingredients and sustainability-minded choices. With breakfast before work and dinner after a long evening, the plan focuses on convenience, fresh seasonal produce, and minimizing waste. I’m sharing my grocery inventory, step-by-step cooking notes, and reflections on how these choices fit my lifestyle.

## Ingredients and Where I Shop

### Breakfast

- **Rye Toast, Fresh Fruit, Black Coffee**
  - Rye bread: I usually pick up organic wholegrain rye bread from Bio Company or the weekly market at Maybachufer. All the breads at the market are certified—Bioland, Naturland, or Demeter—and the quality is consistently good.[1][2][3][4][5][6]
  - Mixed fresh berries: Berlin markets in June are bursting with strawberries, raspberries, and blueberries. The berries I get come with the EU Bio label, which is reassuring.[7][13]
  - Apple: I always try to choose apples from regional farmers—often at the weekly market or my local organic supermarket.[7]
  - Black coffee: I enjoy my coffee simple and strong. Lebensbaum and Rapunzel are my go-to fair-trade, organic brands[8][9][10], and I love supporting local producers.

### Dinner

- **Seasonal Vegetable Stir-Fry with Herbs**
  - Vegetables: All the veggies—especially asparagus and zucchini—are in season right now. I buy them at the Wochenmarkt or LPG Biomarkt, always looking for those EU Bio stickers.[13]
  - Fresh balcony herbs: I’ve been growing parsley, chives, and basil on my balcony. Picking fresh herbs for dinner feels satisfying and reduces packaging waste.
  - Olive oil: I use organic, European olive oil, usually Rapunzel or anything certified with Naturland.[5][7][9]
  - Garlic: Always local and organic from the market.
  - Soy sauce/tamari: I keep organic tamari in my pantry for a quick flavor boost.
  - Quinoa or rice (optional): Having these grains ready from batch cooking helps on nights I come home late.

## Sustainability in My Shopping and Eating

Living in Berlin gives me access to fantastic local produce. I’m committed to using in-season fruits and veggies both for their flavor and lower environmental footprint.[13] Buying from organic-certified farms—Bioland, Naturland, Demeter—means fewer chemicals and better soil health,[4][5][6] and I love the feeling of supporting local businesses at Berlin’s vibrant weekly markets.[1][2][3] Growing my own herbs on the balcony has become a small daily joy—no transport, no packaging, just a quick snip before dinner.

---

## How I Prepare My Meals

### Breakfast

1. **Rye Toast**
   - I slice fresh organic rye bread, lightly toast it, and usually freeze any leftovers to avoid making extra runs to the bakery.
2. **Fresh Fruit**
   - Berries and apples are washed thoroughly. I slice the apple and pile everything on a plate.
3. **Black Coffee**
   - I usually brew a strong cup or two, using beans from Lebensbaum. I drink it black—it’s bold, satisfying, and keeps me alert for morning meetings.[11][12]

*Prep takes me less than 10 minutes, which is perfect for busy mornings. I get a hearty dose of fiber, vitamins, and just enough caffeine to start the day.*

### Dinner

1. **Vegetable Prep**
   - I rinse and prep all the veggies—trimming asparagus, dicing zucchini, tomato, and carrot. Spinach gets a quick wash and chop. Garlic and balcony herbs are minced.
2. **Cooking**
   - In a large pan, I heat olive oil and sauté the garlic briefly. Carrots and asparagus go in first (they take a bit longer to soften), followed by zucchini, tomato, and spinach. Everything cooks until just tender, and I add fresh herbs right at the end for flavor.
   - I season with tamari, salt, and pepper. Sometimes, I serve it over quinoa or rice that’s already been cooked and chilled in the fridge.

*From start to finish, actual cooking takes about 20 minutes. The stir-fry is packed with micronutrients and tastes fresh, and adding grains makes the meal more filling after a long day.*

## Managing Leftovers and Reducing Waste

Thinking ahead means fewer trips to the shops and less food going to waste. Here’s how I keep meals flexible and sustainable:

| Meal      | Leftovers             | How I Use Them Next Day                       |
|-----------|-----------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Breakfast | Rye bread (frozen)    | Toast for breakfast or snack                   |
|           | Apple (half, unused)  | Add to stir-fry, oats, or as a snack           |
|           | Coffee (extra brewed) | Use in overnight oats or compost grounds       |
| Dinner    | Stir-fry (1 portion)  | Use in a salad wrap, add an egg for breakfast  |
|           | Herbs (extra)         | Toss in omelettes, salads, or freeze           |
|           | Quinoa/rice           | Add to tomorrow’s lunch or breakfast porridge  |

All veggie scraps and grounds go right into my compost bin—Berlin’s municipal composting makes waste management easy.[14] I’ve found batch cooking grains ahead of time makes dinner much smoother, and storing leftovers in airtight containers keeps everything fresh.

## Reflection: Balancing Values and Reality

My daily meal planning is more than just a way to save time—it’s a personal commitment to health and sustainability. Choosing local, organic produce and making use of home-grown herbs fits with my values and supports Berlin’s agricultural community. Preparing simple, tasty meals lets me focus on work without sacrificing nutrition. I follow German and European dietary guidelines for fiber, vitamins, and caffeine intake,[11][12] and I integrate strategies for leftover usage and composting—good for the environment, good for my wallet.

These choices help me harmonize my idealistic goals—healthy living and sustainability—with the pragmatic needs of a busy work schedule. With every meal, I feel that I’m genuinely living my values, one day at a time.
