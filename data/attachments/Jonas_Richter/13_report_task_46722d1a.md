## 2. Agenda and Discussion Points

### 2.1 Workshop Objectives

Jonas and Dr. Nowak agreed the primary goal is to build an in-depth, action-oriented policy workshop, with an emphasis on practical recommendations for safeguarding the open internet and digital rights. Both underlined that a cornerstone of the workshop is robust stakeholder diversity, ensuring balanced representation from academia, civil society, industry, government, and youth groups. The workshop aims to formulate concrete policy suggestions, grounded in evidence and reflective of the multi-faceted digital landscape in Europe.

### 2.2 Workshop Structure and Content Plan

A preliminary structure for a full-day event was agreed upon, designed to foster both broad and focused discussions. The draft agenda includes:

- **Welcome & Opening Remarks** (10 min): Setting the tone and framing key workshop goals
- **Keynote Address** (20 min): Delivered by Jonas Richter or an invited external expert, to contextualize to current European tech policy trends
- **Thematic Panels & Breakout Sessions:**  
    - *Technology Policy and Regulation* – Exploring regulatory challenges and emerging issues  
    - *Open Internet and Human Rights* – Examining digital access, rights protections, and ethical implications  
    - *Inclusive Participation: Regional and Global Perspectives* – Ensuring marginalized voices and regions are included

  Each breakout will be supported by prepared background briefs to guide discussions.

- **Group Work/Interactive Policy Labs** (45 min): Attendees collaborate in small teams to generate actionable policy proposals and strategies for impact.

- **Student-Led Insight Session** (20 min): The Polish student group will share findings from pre-event consultations, focusing on youth perspectives regarding open internet challenges in Central and Eastern Europe.

- **Final Plenary & Recommendations Review** (30 min): Concluding session where teams present their outcomes and engage in collective synthesis and discussion.

It was agreed that moderators and session leads must reflect the diversity of the participant pool. Special attention will be given to balancing expertise, gender, and regional backgrounds. Each group will have facilitation support to ensure both timekeeping and equitable participation.

### 2.3 Participant Selection and Invitation Process

To ensure a dynamic and representative workshop, Jonas and Dr. Nowak mapped out a stakeholder strategy spanning:

- Academic researchers (technology policy, internet law, governance)
- NGO leaders and digital rights advocates
- Industry stakeholders (platform providers, telecom operators, SMEs)
- National and EU policymakers (including regulatory agencies)
- Representatives from prominent institutions (IGF, ISOC, EuroDIG)
- Students and youth advocates—emphasizing meaningful integration of Polish voices

Emphasis will be placed on balanced participation—women, regional minorities, young professionals, and less-represented disciplines will all be actively sought out to promote rich discussion and inclusive policy outcomes. The ideal workshop size is 20–30 participants, balancing interactivity with logistical feasibility.

### 2.4 Role of the Polish Student Group

Including the Warsaw Digital Rights Students’ Network is a deliberate way to anchor the workshop in real-world, youth-driven perspectives:

- **Roles:**  
    - Serving as co-moderators and rapporteurs, especially in youth-focused sessions  
    - Leading pre-event surveys within student circles to capture grassroots concerns about open internet issues  
    - Co-authoring the workshop’s summary, bringing authentic student experiences and priorities to the fore  
- **Benefits:**  
    - Integrates the lived realities of digital natives into abstract policy debates  
    - Fosters inter-generational dialogue and builds future leadership capability among student participants

To ensure effectiveness, a dedicated student liaison will be appointed by Dr. Nowak. Preparatory calls will be held bi-weekly to coordinate assignments, ensure clarity of expectations, and address any logistical needs. Collaboration will use shared Google Docs and a private GitHub repository, with a digital orientation handbook outlining session formats, reporting requirements, and standards for participation.

### Policy Focus

- **Reconciling Divergent Views:** A working methodology will be adopted to synthesize stakeholder recommendations, seeking common ground where possible and transparently documenting areas of disagreement.
