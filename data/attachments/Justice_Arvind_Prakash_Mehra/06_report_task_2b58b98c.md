## 3. Soil Health Assessment

We employed a systematic approach to soil sampling in each principal garden bed, testing for Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, pH, organic matter, moisture, and texture, following ICAR protocols. Results are summarized below:

| Bed Location         | N (mg/kg) | P (mg/kg) | K (mg/kg) | pH  | Org. Matter (%) | Moisture (%) | Texture      | Observations                                                                 |
|----------------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----|-----------------|--------------|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Rose Bed East        | 55        | 18        | 80        | 6.9 | 2.5             | 23           | Loamy        | Fertile soil, suitable pH, balanced moisture                                  |
| Central Lawn         | 48        | 16        | 73        | 7.1 | 2.8             | 21           | Sandy Loam   | Slightly alkaline; somewhat low nitrogen; otherwise adequate moisture         |
| Vegetable Patch West | 60        | 22        | 85        | 6.7 | 3.2             | 25           | Clay Loam    | Highest organic matter and fertility; excellent for vegetable production      |
| Perimeter Shrub Bed  | 46        | 15        | 78        | 7.2 | 2.1             | 19           | Sandy        | Lower organic matter and nitrogen; could benefit from supplementary compost   |

Observations indicate all beds provide sufficient nutrients for plant growth. The vegetable patch stands out for its rich organic content and moisture retention—a model for fertility. The perimeter shrub bed, however, would benefit significantly from additional organic amendments and nitrogen supplementation. The slightly alkaline pH in some locations may affect nutrient uptake for acid-preferring species.

To maintain and improve soil health, I recommend adding well-rotted compost to the sandy perimeter beds, periodically checking soil pH, and continuing with regular organic matter enrichment across all areas.

## 5. Pest and Disease Management

We followed IIHR and FAO protocols for pest and disease detection, noting both direct evidence and risk areas:

| Location              | Pest/Disease            | Observed Impact                              | Suggested Organic Control                                |
|-----------------------|------------------------|----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Rose Bed East         | Aphids                 | Leaf curling, sticky residue, reduced blooms | Bi-weekly neem oil spray (2ml/L); regular manual removal |
| Central Lawn          | Red spider mites       | Minor leaf stippling and yellowing           | Weekly horticultural soap sprays                         |
| Vegetable Patch West  | Powdery mildew (spinach)| Light dusting, no yield loss                 | Remove affected leaves; treat with dilute milk spray     |
| Perimeter Shrub Bed   | Scale insects          | Wilting, slowed growth                       | Neem oil spray; prune affected branches                  |

The aphid infestation in the rose bed is currently moderate but calls for prompt organic treatment. Regular monitoring and early intervention using neem oil, coupled with manual removal, will help contain their spread and preserve beneficial insects. Red spider mites are localized and manageable with horticultural soap. Spinach beds show harmless powdery mildew; removal of affected foliage and milk spray should suffice. Scale insects in the shrub bed require pruning and recurrent neem treatments.

All recommended remedies align with IIHR and FAO best practices and are proven safe for residential gardens.

## 7. Recommendations and Next Steps

To keep the garden healthy and enticing through the next year, I recommend the following:

- **Soil Enrichment:** Incorporate compost and organic matter, particularly into sandy beds, to boost growth and plant resilience.
- **Pest and Disease Management:** Use neem oil and horticultural soap regularly, targeting aphids and mites while supporting organic IPM protocols.
- **Rose Introduction:** Add new rose varieties according to the above schedule, preparing soil thoroughly and using phased irrigation to aid establishment.
- **Regular Monitoring:** Continue quarterly health checks for soil and plants using standardized checklists; adapt management based on findings.
- **Record Keeping:** Maintain logs of pest and disease occurrence to facilitate early detection and treatment.
- **Education and Compliance:** Stay up-to-date by consulting recent ICAR and IIHR extension materials, ensuring ongoing adherence to best horticultural practice.
- **Encourage Biodiversity:** Foster beneficial insect populations and rely exclusively on non-synthetic interventions for sustained ecological balance.

With these steps, I am confident that the garden will flourish further, combining ornamental appeal with environmental sustainability.
