## Selected Passages and Reflections

### Chapter 6: The ADM Jabalpur Case

**Original Excerpt:**  
> “If the law allows the State to detain a person without disclosure of the grounds during Emergency, then the Constitution stands abrogated in spirit, if not in letter.”

**My Annotation:**  
Reading Khanna’s dissent, I am struck by the formidable courage he displayed in an atmosphere overwhelmingly dominated by executive authority. His judgment is not just a legal document—it is the assertion of individual conscience triumphing over expedient consensus. Khanna addresses not only the technicalities of constitutional text but insists on its deeper spirit, emphasizing that the guarantee of liberty is the soul of the Constitution. I find these principles vital, especially as we continue to face new challenges where rights clash with claims of national necessity. Khanna’s dissent remains a reference point—a reminder that the judge’s first loyalty must be to the protection of fundamental rights, even at personal cost.

---

### Chapter 4: Rise to High Court

**Original Excerpt:**  
> “Justice is not a cloistered virtue; it must operate in full view of the public with principled transparency.”

**My Annotation:**  
Khanna’s insistence on transparency and reasoned decisions resonates deeply. The legitimacy of judicial action depends on openness and the ability of the public to understand and scrutinize the rationale underpinning legal outcomes. In my own practice, I have taken inspiration from Khanna’s approach, striving to craft judgments that are accessible and reasoned, always aware that justice rendered in secrecy is liable to suspicion and distrust. His vision of accountability remains essential in maintaining credibility and public trust in our courts, especially at times when the system faces scrutiny or criticism.

---

### Chapter 8: Reflections & Legacy

**Original Excerpt:**  
> “A judge’s conscience must be his only allegiance.”

**My Annotation:**  
This simple yet profound statement encapsulates the ethical heart of judicial life. Khanna argues for steadfast independence from all external influences—political, institutional, or personal. His career is marked by choices that consistently prioritize integrity and principle over convenience or reward. The legacy he leaves is not just in landmark judgments but in the demonstration that true judicial service often demands sacrifice. I am reminded, each time I encounter ethical ambiguity, that the answer lies within one’s own conscience and clarity of purpose. Khanna’s example offers guidance for generations of jurists, shaping the standards we aspire to uphold and reinforcing the importance of moral independence, especially in times of institutional challenge.

## Integrative Analysis and Contemporary Significance

Justice Khanna’s biography is far more than a recounting of judicial milestones; it is a study in the constant vigilance required to preserve constitutional values. His unwavering stand in the ADM Jabalpur case, where he alone opposed the curtailment of basic rights during emergency, continues to serve as a benchmark for judicial dissent and courage. The biography underscores the foundational tension between state power and individual liberty, and offers historical perspective on the necessity of safeguarding rights even under intense political pressure.

Khanna’s approach to jurisprudence—moving from strict textual interpretation to a more purposive and principled reading—remains central to the way constitutional adjudication has evolved in India. His insistence on transparency and public reasoning is now a well-established requirement for judicial legitimacy. For my colleagues and myself, his life story is a continual reminder that the judge’s duty is to interpret the law in light of timeless values, not transient political interests.

Moreover, Khanna’s post-retirement commitment to legal reform and public dialogue demonstrates that service to the Constitution extends beyond tenure on the bench. His legacy is felt not only in judicial decisions but also in the culture of legal ethics and the expectations placed on public office holders today. Contemporary debates about executive overreach, protection of civil liberties, and the role of dissent in democratic institutions continue to draw upon the principles he championed.

Reviewing his biography has reaffirmed for me the importance of inner conviction, reasoned dissent, and transparency—not as lofty ideals, but as non-negotiable standards in my own judicial responsibilities. Khanna’s example challenges all judges to engage fearlessly with dilemmas of law versus equity, the text versus the spirit of justice, and to remember that our ultimate obligation is to constitutional principle and the conscience that guides us.
