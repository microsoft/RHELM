# Chapter Review: Justice H.R. Khanna – A Judicial Life  
**Book:** *Justice H.R. Khanna – A Judicial Life*  
**Author:** Raghavan Srinivasan  
**Chapter Focus:** ADM Jabalpur & Kesavananda Bharati (likely Chapter 7)  
**Judicial Context:** This chapter examines Justice H.R. Khanna’s distinguished tenure on the Supreme Court of India, specifically his role in two landmark constitutional cases—ADM Jabalpur and Kesavananda Bharati—which continue to shape Indian law and the judiciary’s independence.

---

## Chapter Summary and Analysis

The chapter offers a detailed exploration of pivotal moments in Justice Khanna's career, with emphasis on his legendary dissent in *ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla* and his vital contribution to the *Kesavananda Bharati* judgment. These cases stand at the heart of India’s constitutional development, illuminating the enduring struggle between individual rights and state power, and the judiciary's responsibility as guardian of the Constitution.

### Central Themes

**1. Judicial Independence**  
Justice Khanna’s lone dissent in *ADM Jabalpur* is widely regarded as an unwavering affirmation of constitutional rights, even during the turmoil of the Emergency. Standing apart from his colleagues, he insisted on the primacy of habeas corpus—marking out the court’s duty to protect individual liberty against overarching executive authority. His view reinforced public faith in the judiciary’s capacity to act above temporary political expediency, emphasizing that constitutional safeguards cannot be set aside simply because the times are trying.

**2. The Basic Structure Doctrine**  
In *Kesavananda Bharati*, Justice Khanna’s role was essential in articulating the principle that certain core features of the Constitution—such as the rule of law and protection of civil liberties—are immune from legislative erosion. This doctrine has proven itself again and again as the anchor of constitutional stability, shielding democratic institutions from majoritarian impulses and government overreach.

**3. Personal Cost and Professional Integrity**  
The chapter does not shy away from examining the consequences of Justice Khanna’s principled stands. His supersession following the ADM Jabalpur dissent was not only a personal affront, but a vivid demonstration of the risks associated with defending constitutional principles against entrenched power. The narrative captures the real, human cost of judicial independence and underscores the necessity of institutional autonomy in preserving the rule of law.

**4. Refinement of Judicial Process and Standards**  
Justice Khanna’s rigorous analysis in both cases elevated evidentiary and procedural standards, asserting that even under national emergencies, judicial scrutiny must remain robust. His careful reasoning established a template where the integrity of legal process is paramount, reinforcing the notion that constitutional adjudication is not a luxury reserved for periods of calm.

### Commentary

Justice Khanna’s reasoned jurisprudence and moral clarity form a bedrock for the Indian constitutional system. His firm assertion that "law is above government" set a courageous precedent, one that informed the Supreme Court’s later decisions on fundamental rights and state authority. The development of the Basic Structure Doctrine, as detailed in this chapter, continues to underpin the constitutional order, constraining fleeting legislative majorities while protecting enduring principles.

The narrative of his supersession is particularly poignant, serving to highlight both the fragility and resilience of judicial independence when faced with political pressure. Justice Khanna's dissents, far from being merely solitary voices, have grown in significance over time, influencing landmark cases such as *Minerva Mills v. Union of India* and contemporary debates on civil liberties and executive power.

## Broader Insights: Judicial Philosophy and Alignment

Justice Khanna's judicial legacy embodies a steadfast commitment to constitutional fidelity and individual freedoms. Methodical in his reasoning and courageous in his dissent, Khanna set standards that continue to echo through constitutional jurisprudence. His approach to adjudication—grounded in rigor and guided by conscience—reflects the ideal of a judiciary unafraid to challenge the government when foundational principles are at risk.

**Alignment with Judicial Approach:**  
Khanna’s unwavering defense of rights in *ADM Jabalpur* and his role in crafting the Basic Structure Doctrine closely mirror my own professional experience, particularly in cases restraining executive excess and affirming minority protections. His vision of the Supreme Court as the ultimate safeguard of constitutional order remains a guiding force in my own understanding of judicial responsibility, and I recognize the influence of his methodology in later judgments such as *Minerva Mills*.

**Points of Difference:**  
Few have experienced the personal repercussions that followed Khanna’s dissent, yet his readiness to accept loss in service of principle is a powerful reminder: judicial independence often requires sacrifice. This remains an aspirational benchmark, one I reflect on during moments of heightened political scrutiny.

**Related Landmark Cases:**  
- *ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla* (1976): Judicial remedy and liberty during emergencies  
- *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (1973): Basic Structure Doctrine  
- *Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain* (1975): Constraints on parliamentary power  
- *Minerva Mills v. Union of India* (1980): Judicial review, constitutional supremacy  
- *S.R. Bommai v. Union of India* (1994): Minority rights, federalism  
- *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978): Procedural fairness and liberty

Justice Khanna’s career, as chronicled in this chapter, serves as both warning and inspiration—a reminder that dissent, stewardship, and individual integrity form the heart of a vital judiciary. His story strengthens my own belief in the importance of reasoned dissent and the Court’s essential function as a constitutional check on all forms of power.
