## Proposed Seminar Agenda and Action Items

To maximize the depth of discussion and foster meaningful engagement, I have developed the following prioritized agenda and preparatory tasks:

### Discussion Themes

- **Constitutional Morality & Judicial Overreach:**  
  Facilitate a nuanced dialogue on the evolution of ‘constitutional morality’ in Supreme Court jurisprudence, exploring safeguards against judicial overreach and arbitrary value judgments.
- **The Proportionality Doctrine:**  
  Analyze application of the proportionality framework in Aadhaar and subsequent privacy-related cases, focusing on unresolved issues surrounding biometric data and state exclusions.
- **Consultative Bench Models:**  
  Compare approaches to collective decision-making in major benches like Sabarimala and Puttaswamy, considering their practical impact on precedent-setting and institutional legitimacy.

### Research and Preparation

- **Comparative Constitutional Privacy Brief:**  
  Commission a comparative analysis of privacy protections in India, the UK, and the EU to contextualize the Supreme Court’s position within broader international trends.
- **Gender Equality & Religious Practices Cases:**  
  Compile and study recent Supreme Court cases dealing with the intersection of gender equality and religious customs post-Sabarimala, assessing the consistency of judicial reasoning.
- **Evidence Appreciation Reforms:**  
  Examine procedural reform proposals from Chandra and others for advancing fact-finding processes, including the potential role of expert benches and independent fact-finders.

### Argumentation Strategy

- Prepare robust arguments underscoring the necessity of collective judicial reasoning—especially in landmark cases—and formulate counterarguments addressing concerns that reliance on ‘constitutional morality’ opens the door for subjective adjudication.

### Engagement Plan with Justice Banerjee

- Organize a focused roundtable discussion dedicated to judicial ethics and impartiality in constitutional cases.
- Explore opportunities for joint authorship of a scholarly article analyzing evidence-based reasoning in recent Supreme Court judgments.
- Circulate annotated articles and key case notes to participants ahead of the seminar to foster informed, consultative debate.

## Reflective Questions for Seminar Deliberation

To generate thoughtful and rigorous conversation, I intend to pose the following questions:

- **Objective Standards for Constitutional Morality:**  
  What concrete criteria should the Court employ to guide its reliance on ‘constitutional morality’? How do we ensure consistent, transparent application and avoid arbitrariness?
- **Clarity of the Proportionality Test:**  
  Has the doctrine of proportionality, especially as developed in Aadhaar, provided enough clarity for adjudicating data rights and biometric controversies? Where do ambiguities persist?
- **Limits of Consultative Reasoning:**  
  In situations where evidence is ambiguous or absent, how can the judiciary balance consultative decision-making with the need for factual accuracy? Would procedural reforms—like expert benches—effectively address these challenges?
- **Transparency and Inclusivity in Precedent Setting:**  
  How can the Supreme Court embed greater openness and diverse participation, especially in constitution bench formation and landmark rulings under Article 145?
- **Integration of International Norms:**  
  To what extent should Indian constitutional interpretation draw from international human rights standards, and what protections are needed to preserve domestic constitutional identity?
- **Balancing Activism and Restraint:**  
  How should the Court navigate the tension between judicial activism and restraint when resolving complex social issues with limited legislative guidance?
- **Ethical Frameworks in Judicial Reasoning:**  
  What ethical principles should guide impartial and evidence-driven judicial reasoning? How have Justice Banerjee and his peers upheld these standards in recent decisions?
