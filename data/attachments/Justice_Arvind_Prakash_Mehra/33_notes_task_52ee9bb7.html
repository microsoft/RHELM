III. Summary of Garden Sustainability and Status

(a) Organic Composting and Waste Management

This week, we reaffirmed the practice of using only compost generated within the household and garden—primarily from daily kitchen scraps and pruned plant material. We maintain an optimal proportion of green (nitrogen-rich) to brown (carbon-rich) elements, targeting a 40:60 balance for efficient decomposition—a method in keeping with both FAO and Indian Horticulture Board standards.[2][3]. The compost piles are turned twice a month to provide oxygen and encourage even breakdown.

All non-biodegradable waste, such as packaging, is separated and delivered to the local municipal recycling system—an essential part of our waste reduction efforts. As the summer pruning increases the volume of green matter, plans are underway to expand the capacity of our compost bins, ensuring we can keep up with the garden’s growth.

(b) Jasmine Bloom: Status and Importance

As of this morning, the Jasminum sambac (jasmine) along the southern fence has entered its first stage of bloom, with about 30% of buds open. The sight and scent mark the true beginning of summer in the garden. This partial bloom holds special significance, not just for its beauty, but for its tangible benefits as well: in the coming days, I will collect fresh jasmine both for use in herbal teas—a tradition I cherish—and for weaving garlands. The health and vigor of this year’s plants also demonstrate that last season’s soil improvements have paid off, confirming the value of meticulous organic care.

We expect the full bloom to emerge within a week, at which point I plan to begin phased harvesting, which should stimulate prolonged flowering and maximize the garden’s yield, consistent with expert recommendations.[2]
