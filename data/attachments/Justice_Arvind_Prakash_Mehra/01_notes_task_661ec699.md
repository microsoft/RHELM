## 1. Rose Bed Condition Assessment

On New Year’s Day, a detailed inspection was carried out for each rose bush in the garden, with a particular focus on detecting symptoms of fungal diseases. The examination included careful observation of leaves, stems, and surrounding soil conditions, with attention to factors that increase disease risk during Delhi’s cool, humid winter.

| Bush Name/ID             | Fungal Symptoms & Appearance                                                                                     | Severity | Precise Areas Affected                        | Environmental Factors                                                                                   |
|-------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| R-01 (Peace)            | White powdery patches on leaves and young stems; slight distortion; thin white film beneath leaves               | Moderate | Lower leaves/stems, some young shoots         | High humidity overnight (~68%), mild winter temps (10–16°C), damp loam soil                            |
| R-02 (Queen Elizabeth)  | Black circular spots with yellow halos; frequent leaf drop                                                       | Severe   | Middle/lower leaves, areas of defoliation     | Persistent soil moisture, limited sunlight, poor airflow due to bush density                           |

| R-04 (Double Delight)   | Yellowing, mild wilting, some black streaks on petioles, no significant leaf loss                               | Mild     | Leaf bases, internal foliage                  | Elevated humidity, somewhat compacted soil, soil pH at 6.7                                             |
| R-05 (Eternal Flame)    | No infection observed                                                                                           | None     | —                                            | Healthy environment, routine maintenance upheld                                                        |

### General Garden Observations

During this inspection, overnight dew and fog persisted, with temperatures ranging from 10°C at night to 16°C during the day and humidity fluctuating between 65–72%. These conditions are routinely conducive to fungal outbreaks in roses, particularly when soil remains damp and air circulation is insufficient. Soil analysis showed a moderately compacted loam with a pH of around 6.7 and stable organic matter levels. According to recommendations from the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) and regional weather summaries, fungal infections such as Powdery Mildew, Black Spot, and Downy Mildew are prevalent in Delhi winters, especially in gardens with dense planting and moist conditions [1].

## 2. Immediate Maintenance Actions

After diagnosing each bush's condition, targeted treatments and cultural interventions were applied. All procedures adhered strictly to the most current guidelines published by ICAR and the Ministry of Agriculture, using registered products and following safety protocols for pesticide application.

### Summary Table: Fungicide Treatments and Safety Measures

| Bush Name/ID             | Fungicide & Treatment Method                                        | Dosage/Volume                    | Safety Measures                                                                                                   | Rationale & Reference                                                             |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| R-01 (Peace)             | Sulphur Dust (Bayer Garden Sulphur 80WP), foliar spray             | 2g per L water, 500ml per bush, applied pre-8am             | Nitrile gloves, N95 mask, goggles, shields to prevent drift, bystander exclusion (>10m), 24h restricted entry    | Sulphur is highly effective for Powdery Mildew, minimal residue; suited for Delhi winters [1][2]                 |
| R-02 (Queen Elizabeth)   | Mancozeb (Indofil M-45, 75% WP), foliar spray                      | 2g per L water, 600ml per bush, before 9am                  | PPE kit including gloves, respirator, goggles; no spray near rainfall; careful hand washing post-treatment         | Mancozeb is the recommended solution for Black Spot and prevents leaf drop [1][2][3]                           |
| R-03 (Papa Meilland)     | Copper Oxychloride (BASF 50WP), targeted spray on affected leaves  | 3g per L water, 350ml per bush                              | Full PPE, avoid overspray on blooms, confirm wind direction, log restricted-entry intervals                       | Copper-based fungicide is rapid and resistant to Downy Mildew, especially in early-stage symptoms [1][2][3]     |
| R-04 (Double Delight)    | No fungicide; improved drainage and airflow, debris removed        | Not applicable                                               | Gloves for handling debris                                                                                         | For mild, non-progressive symptoms, ICAR recommends prioritizing non-chemical measures [1]                      |
| R-05 (Eternal Flame)     | No action required                                                 | Not applicable                                               | Not applicable                                                                                                    | No symptoms observed; preventive monitoring continues [1]                                                       |

#### Additional Safety Measures

- All applications were conducted before 9am to limit wind drift and maximize fungicide effectiveness.
- Protective clothing (coveralls, gloves, masks, goggles) was worn throughout by all staff involved.
- Spray zones were clearly marked, with a 10-meter buffer maintained from all water sources.
- Restricted-entry intervals were enforced per product specifications.
- Entry logs, staff signatures, timestamps, and product batch numbers were documented for compliance.

#### Documentation

A full record was maintained for each intervention, referencing horticultural best practices and product registration details, ensuring traceability for all chemicals used as required by local authorities [1][2].
