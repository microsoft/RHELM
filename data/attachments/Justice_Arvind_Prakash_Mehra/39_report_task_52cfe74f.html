Backup Procedures and Safeguards

Effective backup and data protection procedures were implemented to support both daily operations and long-term archival consistency. Technologies and protocols in use include:

  • Cloud Backup:
  • All folders synchronize to a judiciary-approved cloud service such as NIC Cloud or an ISO 27001-certified provider.
  • Data is encrypted both during transmission and at rest (AES-256).
  • Access to cloud backups requires two-factor authentication (2FA).

  • Daily incremental backups run automatically; full backups are performed weekly, matching eCourts recommendations [2].

  • Local Backup:

  • Dedicated external HDD and SSD devices store encrypted backups.

  • These drives deploy hardware-level encryption and require either PIN codes or biometric authentication, ensuring protection against physical theft or ransomware.

  • Encryption and Authentication:

  • Sensitive files, including draft judgments and confidential correspondence, are protected using AES-256 encryption.
  • Secure file transfer protocol (SFTP) is enforced between local and cloud storages.

  • Role-based access permissions restrict access to sensitive materials; all login attempts and file accesses are logged for oversight.

  • Preservation and Archiving:

  • Adherence to ISO 15489 principles is maintained, with file naming and directory structures standardized by date, case number, and content type.

  • All draft and finalized judicial documents are version-controlled, and change logs are kept for every revision.

  • Integrity and Auditability:

  • Regular hash checksum verification ensures all backup copies remain intact and unaltered over time.

  • Authentication, access controls, and audit trails are implemented in compliance with national law and professional rules.

  • Backup Frequency and Audit:

  • Case-critical records sync daily.
  • Full backups for static and closed case folders occur weekly.
  • External audits are scheduled monthly to confirm backup effectiveness and completeness, following established audit SOPs.

These measures collectively create a robust and secure framework that aligns with judicial information governance rules and protects record authenticity throughout their lifecycle.

Recommendations for Sustained Management

To maintain system reliability and legal compliance moving forward, the following practices should be continued and improved:

  • Regular Review and Auditing:
    Conduct quarterly audits of folder structures, access controls, and backup completeness, in accordance with eCourts and ISO 15489 guidelines [3]. These reviews help ensure files are organized correctly and accessed only by authorized personnel.

  • Automated Version Control:
    Employ specialized legal document management platforms that maintain comprehensive version histories and audit trails for all judgments and case files. This not only boosts transparency but also protects against accidental data loss.

  • Enhanced Access Management:
    Persistent enforcement of strong authentication protocols—2FA, comprehensive audit logs, and granular encryption—should remain a priority, with sensitive folders always restricted to those with proper authorization.

  • Risk Mitigation:
    Schedule routine data recovery drills to guarantee backup and restoration methods function correctly under real-world conditions. Deploy endpoint security solutions to guard against malware and unauthorized intrusion.

  • Compliance Assurance:
    Systematically segregate personal and judicial files, following prescribed retention schedules and disposition policies to meet IT Act requirements and uphold judicial confidentiality.

  • Training and Awareness:
    Organize regular training sessions for all judicial staff on digital hygiene and compliance updates, keeping everyone informed of evolving standards and security threats.

  • Continuous Improvement:
    Monitor updates from the Supreme Court, Bar Council of India, and relevant international bodies. Adjust protocols promptly to reflect new recommendations and best practice models.

Sustained commitment to these practices not only ensures legal and regulatory compliance but also enhances the efficiency, security, and reliability of judicial recordkeeping.
