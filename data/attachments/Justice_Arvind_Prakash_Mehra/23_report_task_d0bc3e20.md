The article juxtaposes Indian Supreme Court jurisprudence with select foreign decisions, highlighting both strengths and risks of adopting activist approaches. Kapoor explores how documentary and testimonial evidence is weighed in constitutional litigation, arguing for an approach that balances constitutional mandates with legislative intent. ## Reflections and Recommendations

Justice Mehra’s annotations and observations throughout both articles convey an unwavering commitment to judicial integrity and constitutional fidelity. He reiterates the necessity for the judiciary to continually adapt doctrinal interpretations in light of India’s social transformations, particularly regarding privacy rights and the complex realm of digital evidence. Justice Mehra further warns against uncritical judicial activism and stresses that courts must operate within the bounds of constitutional text and legislative intent to preserve democratic balance.

Comparative analysis, he notes, is valuable but should never substitute for reasoning rooted in India’s unique legal context. Justice Mehra emphasizes that challenges arising from technological and societal changes require ongoing doctrinal review and an openness to reform—especially in the areas of evidentiary law and statutory interpretation.

**Recommended areas for further research and seminar exploration include:**
- Examining frameworks for the admissibility of digital evidence in constitutional litigation, considering privacy and data protection concerns
- Developing principled approaches to judicial activism that maintain respect for legislative processes and constitutional limitations
- Systematically reviewing recent Supreme Court decisions to keep doctrinal debate relevant and current
- Encouraging seminar participants to suggest concrete reforms in evidentiary standards and statutory interpretation to address new challenges posed by societal and technological change
