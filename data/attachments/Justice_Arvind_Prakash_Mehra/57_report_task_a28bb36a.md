## 2. Objective

**Purpose and Scope**

The primary objective of this experiment was to assess the effectiveness of neem-based organic pest control on jasmine (*Jasminum sambac*) and marigold (*Tagetes erecta*) plants, particularly targeting aphid infestations common in urban Indian home gardens. The project aimed to evaluate both immediate reductions in pest populations and the extended sustainability of using neem as part of an integrated organic management protocol.

**Research Hypothesis**

Spraying jasmine and marigold plants with neem oil at established organic concentrations would result in a marked decrease in aphid populations, and do so without negative impacts on plant health or evidence of chemical residues. 

**Expected Outcomes**

- Significant reduction in observable aphid colonies, with marigold anticipated as the more vulnerable species based on prior experience and literature.  
- Maintenance or improvement of visible plant health (leaf gloss, color, vigor).  
- Absence of discernible chemical residues, in alignment with organic gardening standards.

**Context & Relevance**

This work is grounded in the need for environmentally responsible pest control practices. Neem, a locally available and biodegradable biopesticide, serves as a model solution for urban gardeners seeking to manage pests without resorting to harmful synthetics. Our results aim to contribute practical insights for sustainable gardening across Indian cities, where ecological stewardship and pest resistance are both growing concerns.

## 3. Methodology

### Preparation of Neem Spray

- **Ingredients:**  
  - Cold-pressed neem oil (Azadirachta indica) – 5ml  
  - Mild organic liquid soap (serving as emulsifier) – 1ml  
  - Purified tap water – 1 liter

- **Protocol:**  
  1. Combine neem oil with liquid soap directly in a clean container, mixing thoroughly until emulsified.  
  2. Add the emulsified mixture to one liter of water, stirring vigorously to obtain a uniform solution.  
  3. Prepare fresh solution before each application to ensure stability and effectiveness of active compounds.

### Application Process

- **Plant Coverage:**  
  Complete spraying of all above-ground portions, focusing especially on the undersides of leaves where aphids congregate.

- **Application Method:**  
  Applied manually using a calibrated hand-pump sprayer to regulate coverage and minimize waste.

- **Spray Timing:**  
  - First spray administered at 8:00 AM, before exposure to direct sunlight, to reduce evaporation and maximize persistence.  
  - Application was halted during an unexpected 10:50 AM drizzle, then resumed about an hour later after leaf surfaces had dried.

- **Weather Adaptations:**  
  Brief rain required a pause in spraying, with affected plants earmarked for selective reapplication. Monitoring continued on all treated specimens to account for potential wash-off and ensure coverage.

- **Safety and Compliance:**  
  All materials certified organic; no synthetic chemicals employed. Mr. Ramesh wore protective gloves and mask throughout handling and spraying. Care was taken to restrict access to the area for children and pets during and after treatment.

## 7. Conclusions and Recommendations

Neem oil, applied as per standard organic protocols, proved highly effective in reducing aphid infestations on both jasmine and marigold plants under typical urban garden conditions. The treatment had no adverse effect on plant appearance or vigor. Weather variability, specifically the midday drizzle, required some flexibility in the spraying schedule and highlighted the need for adaptive field practices.

For future trials, I recommend the following improvements:

- Target application times for early mornings on clear-weather days whenever possible, or explore the use of temporary physical covers during spraying if light rain is expected.
- For persistent infestations, especially on marigolds, consider increasing neem concentration slightly (up to 7ml/L) or adding successive applications at short intervals.
- Continue combining handwritten and digital documentation to safeguard data, and plan for redundancy by utilizing multiple recording devices.
- Expand the scope to include systematic pest counts, and document any effects on beneficial non-target species to build a more comprehensive picture of ecological impact.

These practices not only align with personal and constitutional commitments to ecological stewardship, but also foster collaboration and transparency within our research group. Consistently following organic gardening protocols ensures our methods remain sustainable and safe for broader community use.
