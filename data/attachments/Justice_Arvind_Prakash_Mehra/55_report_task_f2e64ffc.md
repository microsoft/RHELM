## 3. Discussion Summary

### 3.1 Case Introduction and Context

Advocate Neelam Singh opened with a comprehensive overview of her pro bono constitutional case. The petition centers on the right to equality before law under Article 14 and seeks efficacious remedies through Article 32 of the Constitution. The central issue involves alleged arbitrary administrative decisions impacting marginalized communities, with the claim that these practices infringe both the principle of equality and the right to judicial redress.

Justice Mehra listened attentively and clarified points for a full understanding of the factual matrix and legal context.

### 3.2 Development of Constitutional Arguments

Justice Mehra offered substantive guidance on structuring the legal approach:

- **Article 14 – Equality Before Law:**  
Justice Mehra emphasized the importance of clearly arguing how state actions violate the requirement of non-arbitrariness. He cited *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978), reinforcing that the principle of reasonableness and procedural fairness is now intrinsic to equality. He recommended using *E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu* (1974), which establishes that arbitrariness is wholly opposed to equality, as the backbone of arguments challenging discretionary government power.

- **Article 32 – Judicial Remedies for Fundamental Rights:**  
He stressed the significance of Article 32 in facilitating direct access to the Supreme Court for the enforcement of fundamental rights. Referring to *People’s Union for Democratic Rights v. Union of India* (1982), Justice Mehra underlined the judiciary’s proactive role in supporting claims raised through Public Interest Litigation (PIL), especially in cases where vulnerable groups lack ordinary avenues of redress.

Justice Mehra advised Neelam Singh to firmly establish both the facts and the legal foundation in pleadings, demonstrating a tangible pattern of discrimination or arbitrary conduct by state authorities.

### 3.3 Key Precedents and Legal Reasoning

Justice Mehra meticulously discussed major judgments to guide litigation strategy:

- **Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978):**  
This case expanded the interpretation of fundamental rights, particularly emphasizing a broad reading of procedural fairness under Article 14. Justice Mehra suggested framing arguments around procedural justice and substantive fairness, going beyond mere formal equality.

- **E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu (1974):**  
The principle of arbitrariness was highlighted as antithetical to equality, crucial for challenging discretionary administrative action. Justice Mehra proposed connecting the facts of the current case to this doctrine, using concrete instances of unreasoned administrative determinations affecting the petitioner.

- **People’s Union for Democratic Rights v. Union of India (1982):**  
He discussed how this decision validated a more accessible approach to the courts for public interest litigants. He encouraged the use of public interest language to frame the case as one not only seeking personal relief but broadly addressing systemic injustice.

- **Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India (1984):**  
Justice Mehra pointed to the court’s readiness to intervene for the disenfranchised, noting the importance of liberal standing and an expansive judicial role in ensuring fundamental rights.

Other references included *S.P. Gupta v. Union of India* (1981), emphasizing openness in granting standing for PILs and an empathetic judicial mindset towards collective grievances.

### 3.4 Review of Legal Materials and Petitions

Justice Mehra reviewed Advocate Neelam Singh’s draft petition and supporting case materials. His feedback focused on:

- The necessity of detailed, fact-specific averments rather than broad, generic claims
- Improving the petitioner’s narrative by contextualizing the impact on affected communities
- Integrating robust empirical data and expert opinions to substantiate allegations
- Organizing case law and legal arguments in a comparative format for clarity and impact

He suggested revising certain sections for precision and relying on objective evidence whenever possible.

### 3.5 Evidence, Expert Engagement, and Strategic Recommendations

Justice Mehra recommended a thorough collection of documentary and testimonial evidence to demonstrate the adverse impact of administrative practices. He emphasized the value of collaborating with independent experts to deliver objective opinions on governmental conduct. Particularly, he encouraged an innovative approach in framing legal remedies, proposing that the Supreme Court be prompted, where appropriate, to monitor compliance and institute welfare-driven directives as established in evolving PIL jurisprudence.

Further, he stressed the importance of maintaining a disciplined and concise argumentative style — noting that courts respond best to clear, focused written submissions supported by authoritative precedent and factual documentation.
