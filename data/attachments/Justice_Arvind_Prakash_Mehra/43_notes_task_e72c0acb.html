Shopping Audit Report: Morning Visit to INA Market, New Delhi (21 August 2024)

Introduction

This report details my morning trip to INA Market in New Delhi on 21 August 2024. The purpose of the visit was to pick up some essential fresh produce for the kitchen, along with gardening supplies I’ve been meaning to buy for a while. What follows is a clear breakdown of what I intended to buy, what I actually managed to purchase, and an honest assessment of why I couldn’t complete the full shopping list. Throughout, I’ve tried to document both actions and omissions with transparency, ensuring there’s a reliable record of how the errand unfolded and what decisions shaped its outcome.

Narrative of the Shopping Experience

I set out early in the morning to beat the rush at INA Market, carrying a short list focused on kitchen staples and a couple of things for my balcony plants. Tomatoes and coriander were at the top of my list, both of which are staples at home. I headed straight to my regular vegetable vendor, who always has a fresh selection. Despite some crowds starting to build even at the early hour, I was able to pick up 1 kg of ripe tomatoes and two fresh bunches of coriander without any hassle, ticking off the first half of my list.

My next goal was to visit the gardening supply stall further down the market to pick up a bottle of organic neem oil—an essential for pest control on my balcony garden—and two new planters to replace some aging pots. However, just after finishing the vegetable purchase, I started feeling unexpectedly unwell. The discomfort came on quickly enough that I didn’t feel comfortable continuing with the rest of my shopping as planned.

Recognizing the need to put my health first, I decided to leave the market immediately rather than risk making things worse. As a result, I wasn’t able to stop at the gardening stall at all. This meant both the neem oil and new planters had to be left unpurchased for now.

Assessment of Outcome and Deviation

Out of the four items on my list, only half were purchased—both intended for immediate cooking needs. The more optional or less urgent gardening supplies weren’t acquired this time, directly due to my early departure.

Primary Constraint:
The incomplete shopping was entirely due to the health issue I encountered at the market. Had this not occurred, I would have continued with the rest of my list, as neither neem oil nor new planters are typically difficult to find at INA.

Impact:
Not being able to bring home the gardening supplies means I’ll need to postpone some planned garden maintenance and repotting that required those materials. While disappointing, I feel fortunate to have at least secured all the kitchen items needed for this week’s meals.

Documentation Approach:
In line with good record-keeping, I’ve specified both the reason for leaving the market early and the direct connection between this constraint and the incomplete shopping.

Reflection on Accountability and Transparency

I believe it’s important to be upfront and specific about the reasons behind any deviations, especially when regular routines are disrupted. In this case, documenting the health incident provides both accountability and clarity for anyone (including myself) tracking household or project expenses and resources.

Although the garden supply purchases remain pending, I did meet half of my objectives for the trip. I see this as a prompt to plan for contingencies, especially given how unpredictable simple errands can become when health or other personal factors intervene.

Recommendations and Follow-Up Actions

  • Immediate Next Steps: I will prioritize a follow-up trip specifically for the gardening supplies—ideally within the next week, depending on my schedule and wellbeing. If I foresee similar health concerns, I’ll ask a friend or family member to accompany me, or even delegate the purchase if possible.
  • Shopping Strategy: For future errands involving multiple types of purchases, it might be sensible to split them across separate trips, so that an unexpected disruption doesn’t derail the entire plan.
  • Ongoing Records: I’ll continue keeping detailed notes on what is and isn’t purchased as planned, alongside the reasons for any missed items, to make household management more streamlined and transparent.

Alignment with Good Audit Practice

While there isn’t a fixed template for casual shopping audits, I have tried to apply best practices from formal procurement audits: clear itemization, explicit documentation of deviations, and constructive recommendations. This approach supports not just accuracy in household records but also continuous improvement in personal organization and accountability.

Conclusion

My visit to INA Market on the morning of 21 August 2024 achieved only partial success due to an unexpected health setback. Although I returned home with the key ingredients for upcoming meals, the gardening purchases had to be deferred. This report lays out each item, the status and purpose, and an honest accounting of the shortfall, along with reasonable plans for follow-up. In documenting both the practical side and the personal context, I aim to ensure household shopping continues as efficiently and responsibly as possible.
