Gardening Journal Entry: February 5, 2024

Justice Mehra’s Garden, New Delhi

Date

February 5, 2024

Weather Conditions

Today in New Delhi, the weather was pleasant and encouraging for outdoor gardening tasks. Early morning temperatures hovered around 17°C, gradually rising to a comfortable 24°C by afternoon. Humidity stayed moderate at approximately 48%, with only a hint of haze lingering in the early hours before the sun broke through clear skies. A gentle eastward breeze (5 km/h) kept the air fresh, and there was no rainfall recorded in the preceding 24 hours. Since I watered the garden yesterday, the soil still held enough moisture, making today ideal for routine inspections and organic treatments.

Activities Performed

I began with a thorough inspection of all plants and saplings along the garden perimeter, keen to note any changes since my last visit. Following the inspection, I prepared and applied a diluted, organic neem oil spray—carefully adjusting the mixture for each plant type, in line with sustainable horticultural practices recommended by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR).

Any visible pests and damaged leaves were removed manually, a task made easier and more efficient with Mr. Ramesh’s help. Together, we redistributed the mulch layer to better conserve soil moisture and suppress emerging weeds—a step I find increasingly crucial as the drier weeks of winter arrive.

Throughout our work, I made sure every tool and substance used was organic and responsibly sourced. Mr. Ramesh played an active role, not only in pest management and record-keeping, but also in assessing the health of ongoing interventions. We regularly checked our methods against urban horticulture guidelines issued by New Delhi’s municipal authorities, confirming all our actions were compliant and environmentally responsible.

Observations

Several notable trends and issues arose during today’s inspection:

  • Gulmohar Saplings: Growth continues steadily, although I noticed slight yellowing of leaves. This discoloration appears mild and is likely linked to minor nutrient deficiencies or perhaps recent weather fluctuations.
  • Rose Bushes: New buds have begun to form, signaling healthy development. On closer examination, a few leaves showed sporadic aphid activity and early signs of fungal spots. These issues were addressed with neem oil applications and careful pruning.
  • Marigolds: Blooms remain vibrant along the garden border. While most plants are thriving, a handful displayed evidence of leaf miner larvae, which I managed by manual removal and targeted neem oil spraying.
  • Jasmine Vines: These are producing fresh shoots, a reassuring sign of vigorous health. I found no significant pest issues during inspection.
  • General Pest Control: All neem oil treatments were performed in the cooler morning hours to avoid phytotoxicity, and coverage was restricted to affected foliage as advised by current Indian horticultural standards.
  • Organic Compliance: At no point did I resort to chemical pesticides or synthetic fertilizers. Our methods remained wholly organic and aligned with New Delhi’s urban gardening regulations.

Next Steps

Looking ahead, I have several priorities to maintain the garden’s vitality and regulatory compliance:

  • Soil Testing: Conduct detailed soil pH tests and supplement with compost as necessary, especially for the gulmohar saplings and hibiscus shrubs, both showing early signs of nutrient deficiency.
  • Neem Oil Routine: Continue with weekly applications of neem oil, adjusting the frequency and coverage according to plant health and seasonal weather conditions, ensuring ongoing protection against pests.
  • Pest Surveillance: Maintain close monitoring of aphid and leaf miner activity; if neem oil efficacy declines, I am prepared to escalate controls using biological methods recommended for urban gardens.
  • Record Keeping: Document all interventions and consistently review processes to fulfill New Delhi’s sustainable gardening guidelines.
  • Mulch Management: Increase mulch depth in anticipation of upcoming dry spells, which should help conserve moisture and minimize weed growth.
  • Community Engagement: Expand collaboration with Mr. Ramesh and consider joining local community gardening programs to share our experiences and promote sustainable horticultural practices.

Every intervention in the garden today reflected my commitment to organic, sustainable practices. I made a conscious effort to remove pests manually wherever possible, preferring ecological methods that minimize harm to non-target organisms and support overall biodiversity.

Composting and mulching remain integral to my approach, helping to enrich the soil, conserve water, and promote long-term sustainability.
