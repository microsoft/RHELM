## Morning Observations

### Assessment Approach

Garden monitoring was conducted using Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) standards for urban horticulture. This systematic approach involved:

- **Soil Quality Checks:** I selected five random locations from various planted beds, evaluating the soil’s texture and compaction both manually and with a standard probe, referencing the ICAR Soil Inspection Schedule [2].
- **Moisture Monitoring:** After using the traditional finger test for initial assessment, I confirmed findings with a calibrated moisture meter at the root zones of each plant type to ensure reliability.
- **Visual Plant Health Inspections:** Each bed was meticulously checked for signs of stress, leaf discoloration, and presence of pests or diseases. Results were compared against the Indian Agricultural Research Institute (IARI) diagnostic charts [3] to maintain accuracy in identification.

#### Key Findings

- **Soil Condition:** Two sampled locations showed dry, slightly compacted sandy loam. This indicated a need for supplemental irrigating and light mulching, especially ahead of forecasted hotter afternoons.
- **Marigold Beds:** Lower leaves on marigolds appeared mildly drooped, and moisture readings at the roots registered 18%—below the optimal range (20–30%) for vigorous growth.
- **White Damask Roses:** On one white Damask rose, I spotted early signs of fungal infection (small dark spots) along with a just-satisfactory soil moisture of 21%. The inspection also revealed white flies and fresh eggs clustered on leaves across three rose plants, confirmed by both sticky trap counts and direct visual checks.
- **Photographic Documentation:** All significant observations were recorded with corresponding photographs, attached with time stamps for future reference and to support ongoing treatment evaluation.

## Pest Management

### Monitoring and Analysis

Regular pest monitoring revealed two principal threats in the rose beds:

- **White Flies:** Concentrated primarily on the undersides of Damask rose leaves. Signs included light honeydew deposits and clear clusters of eggs.
- **Aphids:** Minor infestations were starting to appear, as noted during detailed leaf checks and corroborated by sticky trap counts.

Both pest populations were at manageable levels but warranted prompt attention to prevent escalation.

### Targeted Interventions

To counter these pests—and drawing on successful approaches documented in previous months—I implemented the following protocol:

- **Neem-Based Spray:**  
  Azadirachtin-based neem spray was mixed at 2ml per liter of water. Early morning applications ensured that the spray adhered effectively with minimal risk of phototoxicity. I plan to repeat the application every seven days, for two or three cycles, while monitoring leaf health and pest counts after each round [4]. This cycle has been adapted from previous journal entries, supported by regional extension resources and affirmed by the District Horticulture Officer’s 2023 report, highlighting both efficacy and minimal adverse effects [5].

All interventions are documented with photographs and log entries, enabling comparison of outcomes and informed adjustments to pest management strategies.

## Companion Planting Insights

Scientific research and regional horticultural practices indicate several beneficial plant pairings for New Delhi’s dry, subtropical climate during April:

- **Marigold & Tomato:** Marigold acts as an effective biological shield against nematodes and aphids, improving tomato health and yields [6].
- **Basil & Roses:** Basil’s proximity helps deter thrips and has proven to enhance rose vigor and reduce pest pressure, especially in humid spring conditions [3].
- **Onion/Garlic Borders:** Planting onion or garlic along bed edges provides natural protection against fungal diseases and serves as an insect repellent, supporting healthy plant transitions per IARI recommendations [3].

These combinations enhance biodiversity and natural resilience, reinforcing sustainable gardening principles and establishing ecological balance within the garden.
