2. Connectivity Disruptions and Their Impact

Overview

Throughout May 19, 2024, my household was affected by frequent and sustained disruptions to both internet and electricity supply. These issues made it impossible to successfully pay any of the major utility bills, whether online or in person.

Technical Observations

  • Internet Connectivity Loss:
    The home router logs show repeated "WAN Disconnected" alerts, starting from about 8:45 AM and continuing into the afternoon. Each unsuccessful payment coincided with a loss of internet connectivity, evident both from the error messages on payment platforms and from the router indicator lights, which I observed going dark or blinking.

  • Web and App Errors:
    Every major online platform I tried (utility billing sites, State Bank portal, and utility mobile apps) returned network or timeout errors almost instantly after each submission. Typical messages I saw included "Network connection unavailable," "No internet connection," "504 Gateway Time-out," and "Payment service unavailable." In UPI apps like Google Pay and PhonePe, payment processes stalled at the authentication or processing stage, showing looping "processing" screens or connection errors. I took several screenshots for my own records.

  • Bank Platform Issues:
    I tried processing the internet bill via NEFT transfer, but the process failed after verifying the OTP. The portal displayed a "Session expired" notice, which matched the time broadband connectivity dropped out.

In-Person Payment Barriers

When all online avenues failed, I attempted to pay bills at the physical offices for both Electricity and Water. In both cases, I found the counters non-functional. At the electricity utility office, the doors were locked, and a sign cited a local outage for closure. At the municipal water office, staff at the entrance explained all payment terminals were down due to an ongoing citywide power cut. There was no way to make a payment, electronic or otherwise.

Institutional and Public Announcements

Ahead of the outages, I received a municipal SMS announcement warning of possible scheduled maintenance across much of the area. Additionally, the utility websites displayed banners about ongoing city-wide electrical work. Both online notices were accessible earlier in the morning, but became unreachable during the extended mid-day outage.

Personal Observations

I noticed that the Wi-Fi network would drop out suddenly, and several times my phone also lost its cellular data signal, especially during the heaviest outage hours. Trying to access simple websites often resulted in "No internet" errors, and transactions never progressed beyond initial authentication screens. Even power at home was intermittent, which explained the inability to use network-dependent devices.

3. Analysis and Summary

The events of May 19, 2024, illustrate how the simultaneous failure of both internet and power infrastructure can immobilize essential financial activities. The need to pay for electricity, water, gas, and internet—each of which relies on digital networks—became impossible due to external factors entirely out of my control.

Every technical error was matched by visible signals of network failure at home. Direct attempts to pay at physical offices were closed off by the same infrastructure issues, with staff confirming their inability to operate payment terminals or cash counters without electricity.

Given these circumstances, the inability to complete any utility payments today was not the result of personal oversight or lack of effort, but a cascading effect of infrastructure outages. This log provides a clear, time-stamped, and evidence-supported record of all my attempts, including the platforms used, technical errors encountered, and real-world constraints, and may serve as reference in the event of billing disputes or legal requirements for proof of good faith payment effort.
