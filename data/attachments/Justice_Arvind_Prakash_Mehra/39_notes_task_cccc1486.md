## 1. Weather and Setting

I began the day early, stepping outside at around 6:00 am to a comfortable 22°C. The air felt fresh, with hints of humidity (about 72%), and a gentle southwest breeze rustled the garden leaves. By midday, the temperature rose to 28°C, but the garden retained a refreshing coolness in the shade. The sky was partly cloudy at dawn, with the light filtering softly through the trees and no sign of rain in the forecast. Atmospheric pressure remained stable at 1011 hPa, a reassuring sign that today would be ideal for tending to the plants.

## 2. Garden Observations and Actions

Early mornings in the garden have become the anchor to my day. I walked through each bed, gently inspecting leaves, stems, and flowers to get a sense of how everything was faring after a few warm days. My routine now feels almost meditative—a careful, hands-on check for any signs of stress or trouble, with interventions made where needed. Here’s a summary of what I noted and did today:

| Plant           | Status              | Issues Observed                           | Actions and Rationale                                                                               |
|-----------------|---------------------|-------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Tulsi (Holy Basil) | Healthy            | Some lower leaves began yellowing         | Trimmed away the yellow leaves and checked soil drainage. Removing discolored foliage helps prevent the spread of possible nutrient deficiencies and reduces fungal risk. Ensured the roots weren’t sitting in water to maintain optimal health. |
| Marigolds       | Flourishing, in bloom | Aphids clustering on new flower buds      | Sprayed a diluted neem oil solution and gently removed persistent aphids by hand. Neem oil remains reliable for organic treatment, and immediate manual removal keeps the pests in check before they spread further. |
| Roses           | Budding; vigorous    | A few leaves with black spots opening up  | Pruned and discarded affected leaves and used a copper-based fungicide spray over the remaining foliage. This targeted approach prevents further spread of black spot fungus and supports the plant’s natural defenses. |
| Mint            | Lush and aromatic    | Minor wilting at the leaf edges           | Checked for compacted soil around the roots—indeed, some of the soil felt dense. I gently loosened it and watered thoroughly at the base. This helps air reach the roots and prevents water stress. |
| Jasmine         | Moderate growth      | New growth a bit pale and yellowing (chlorosis) | Added a generous layer of organic compost around the plant. Compost typically provides the micronutrients (like iron or magnesium) often lacking from my sandy garden soil, and I expect to see the leaves green up in a week or so. |
| Lemon Verbena   | Slightly stressed    | Leaves curling, faint spider mite webbing | Increased the frequency of misting, paying special attention to the leaf undersides, and wiped the infested areas with a damp cloth. Spider mites dislike humidity, and this hands-on removal controls their numbers before they get out of hand. |
| Aloe Vera       | Thriving, no issues  | —                                         | Rotated the pot so all sides receive even sunlight. This simple habit encourages symmetrical growth and prevents stretched, leggy plants. |

These small acts—snipping, spraying, checking the soil—form a ritual that reminds me of the connection between daily care and visible vitality in my plants. It’s reaffirming to see how prompt, gentle interventions can restore balance in the garden, and I’m learning that attentive observation is often the best preventive measure.

## 3. Mindful Breathing Practice (Dr. Suri’s Approach)

After finishing my rounds in the garden, I took ten minutes to sit quietly on the shaded bench under the neem tree, using that time for mindful breathing. I’ve been following Dr. Suri’s general methodology as often highlighted in wellness circles, though direct access to her official publications wasn’t possible this week.

Sitting upright with my hands resting on my knees and eyes closed, I brought my attention to the subtle feeling of air moving in and out of my nostrils. I used Dr. Suri’s “anchored awareness” technique to gently focus on that physical sensation, which helped center my attention and quiet background thoughts.

For the breathing exercise, I completed ten cycles of the 4-4-6 pattern:
- Quietly inhaled through my nose for four counts
- Held my breath gently for another four counts
- Exhaled slowly through my mouth for six counts

Anytime I noticed thoughts wandering—for example, to a case file due tomorrow, or worries about whether the marigolds would recover from the aphids—I simply redirected my attention back to my breathing, practicing Dr. Suri’s instructions about non-judgment and gentle redirection. This flexibility and acceptance around distraction, rather than fighting it, made the practice far more approachable and effective.

**Why this matters:**  
I’ve found that even just ten minutes of making space for mindful breathing profoundly rewires my state of mind for the day. The deliberate rhythm of the breath helps temper my tendency toward stress, provides a clearer sense of focus, and leaves me feeling steadier before shifting to the more analytical demands of my work.

*(For further reading, similar mindful breathing methods are described here: [Mindful Breathing: Techniques, Benefits, and How-To](https://www.mindful.org/mindful-breathing-practice/).)*

## 4. Reflections from My Morning Walk

After tending to the plants and a few minutes of breathing practice, I laced up my shoes for a brisk 45-minute walk around the garden’s edge and into the adjacent green belt.

**Physical Effects:**  
Moving at a steady pace helped loosen up the stiffness I sometimes feel in my lower back and knees. My breathing was easy, even as I picked up the speed. When I finished and paused to check my pulse, my heart rate felt lower and more relaxed than when I began, which matches the benefits I’ve read about in regular walkers.

**Mental and Emotional Effects:**  
I often start these walks dwelling on unfinished tasks or lingering work dilemmas. Today was no different—my mind was busy with unresolved details from a tricky legal matter. But as I focused on the vivid sounds of birds, the crunch of gravel underfoot, and the play of sunlight in the trees, that background hum of stress faded away. After about fifteen minutes, I found my attention naturally drawn toward my surroundings instead of my worries.

By the time I returned, I noticed a marked sense of clarity and calm. There was a kind of mental reset—a readiness to approach the rest of the day’s work with renewed focus and composure. These walks have become as essential to my morning routine as coffee, and they continue to surprise me with their ability to strengthen not just my body, but my outlook and patience as well.

**Professional Insights:**  
Interestingly, I’ve come to appreciate how these morning rituals—be it caring for plants, practicing mindful breathing, or walking—mirror many of the critical skills needed in my legal work: careful observation, responsiveness, patience, and non-reactive attention to detail. Each practice feeds into the other, grounding my day in both self-care and professional integrity.

## 5. Conclusion

Today’s journal entry is a testament to the daily interplay between careful garden stewardship and my personal wellness practices. From pruning tulsi and marigolds, to pausing for mindful breathing, to seeking both physical and emotional clarity on my morning walk, each step is rooted in attentive care and deliberate action. These routines are as much about nurturing my garden as about nurturing myself—creating a sustainable rhythm that supports both personal well-being and professional resilience.
