## Soil Mix & Preparation

As always, I paid close attention to the composition of the soil. My goal was to provide an aerated, moisture-retentive, and organic medium tailored for peace lilies’ tropical preferences.

- **Coco Coir:** I used “Kisan Cocopeat” (India Organic certified) from Tamil Nadu, which is an eco-friendly peat substitute [4].
- **Organic Compost:** Blended in “Vermicompost Gold,” sourced from Organic India (OMRI listed) [5].
- **Perlite:** Locally sourced “GrowPerlite” ensured proper aeration and drainage.
- **Neem Cake Powder:** Added as a preventive measure against soil-borne pests [3].
- **Vermiculite:** “Horticure” brand, distributed in Delhi, to promote moisture retention.

Each component was verified for organic certification and listed standards. On mixing, the soil felt airy, with good structure for both root expansion and water retention. I tested the blend’s pH, which balanced evenly around neutral (6.5–7.0), aligning with recommendations from both RHS and Indian horticultural guides for Spathiphyllum [2][6]. The visible perlite grains contributed excellent drainage, while the coir and vermiculite kept the soil moist without being soggy—critical in preventing root rot given Delhi’s dry spring climate.

## Care Instructions Provided to Mr. Ramesh

I compiled the following concise care guidance for Mr. Ramesh, who helps maintain the household plants:

- **Watering:**  
  - Maintain soil moisture by watering when the top 2cm feels dry, being careful to avoid waterlogging.
  - Use filtered water or let tap water stand overnight before use to minimize chlorine stress.

- **Light:**  
  - Place pots in bright, indirect light; direct afternoon sun must be avoided to prevent leaf scorch (as reiterated by RHS and government extension manuals [2][6]).

- **Humidity Measures:**  
  - Group the peace lilies together to help elevate ambient humidity.
  - Place pebble trays under pots or lightly mist foliage, especially as indoor air is quite dry during this season [1].
  - Watch for signs of dehydration, such as leaf browning at tips or limp foliage.

- **Feeding Routine:**  
  - Fertilize monthly during active growth (spring through early autumn) with “BioGrow” organic feed, following directions and standards set by India Organic [5].

- **Pest & Disease Monitoring:**  
  - Visually inspect all peace lilies weekly for aphids, mealybugs, or fungal diseases.
  - For PL-3 specifically, continue neem-based treatment and keep isolated for 10 days, observing for recovery before returning to the main group.

- **Climate Considerations:**  
  - Avoid placing containers in direct draft from A/C vents or near fluctuating temperatures.
  - A stable, warm environment is crucial for stress-free recovery [6].

Source references for these protocols include the Royal Horticultural Society, National Horticulture Mission, and official Indian houseplant guides [2][3][6].

## Reflections & Follow-up Plan

Today’s transplanting went smoothly. None of the lilies displayed immediate signs of transplant shock—leaves remained upright and turgid, a reliable indicator of resilience. The new potting mix proved excellent; surface dryness was prevented by the coir and vermiculite, and drainage worked perfectly, with no signs of excess runoff or pooling.

Looking back, similar procedures in previous years have led to robust regrowth and improved health. Entries from March 2023 and April 2022 in my gardening journal confirm that root pruning and appropriate repotting almost always favor recovery [7]. A published study in the “Indian Journal of Horticulture” also confirms that using organic substrates with minimal handling stress yields survival rates greater than 95% for peace lilies [8].

For the next three weeks, my plan is as follows:
- **Monitor Soil and Foliage:** I’ll check soil moisture and the overall leaf condition every three days to ensure sustained turgor and root health.
- **Root Inspection:** After one month, I’ll carefully lift the plants to check for signs of fungal growth or lingering rot.
- **Pest and Disease Checks:** PL-3 will be observed more frequently (weekly), while the others get bi-weekly checks, logging each neem application for transparency.
- **Feeding:** Organic fertilizer will be started after two weeks to support new root growth, following plant establishment.
- **Documentation:** On May 7, I’ll record a follow-up journal entry and take photographs to monitor progress and document any changes in leaf color, new growth, or pest activity.

All activities and recommendations strictly follow standards set by RHS and government authorities [2][6], ensuring the lilies' care aligns with certified horticultural methods.
