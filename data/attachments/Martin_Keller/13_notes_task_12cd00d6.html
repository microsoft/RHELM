Menu Overview

For my Mediterranean-themed dinner this evening, I wanted to bring the warmth and vibrancy of the region’s cuisine to my table. I crafted the menu to balance robust flavors with fresh, herbaceous notes, drawing inspiration from some of my favorite chefs and food writers—Ottolenghi, Claudia Roden, and the expertise at Serious Eats[1][2][3]. I focused on dishes that offer both comfort and brightness, ensuring the meal was both satisfying and true to classic Mediterranean principles.

Tonight’s Menu: - Grilled Chicken with Spicy Harissa:
I marinated free-range chicken thighs in olive oil, fresh lemon juice, garlic, and a generous amount of harissa paste, using Ottolenghi’s technique to strike a bold balance between heat and rich flavor. The grilling imparts a lovely smokiness. - Chickpea Salad with Lemon-Tahini Dressing:
For a refreshing contrast, I tossed together chickpeas, cherry tomatoes, cucumber, and fresh herbs, then dressed everything in a creamy, tart lemon-tahini sauce inspired by Claudia Roden’s approach and assembled according to Serious Eats’ principles for balance and texture. - Warm Pita:
Freshly baked, soft pita rounds are a must. I followed Serious Eats’ guide to get the perfect puff, using traditional Middle Eastern bread techniques and serving the bread warm from the oven.

Preparation Instructions

I’ve mapped out each step to maximize efficiency, flavor, and enjoyment. Where possible, I worked steps in parallel to keep everything moving smoothly.

1. Grilled Chicken with Spicy Harissa

  1. Marinating the Chicken (20 min active; 1–6 hours marinating)
    I mixed the harissa, olive oil, garlic, lemon juice, salt, cumin, and black pepper in a big bowl. After tossing the chicken thighs in the mix, I made sure each piece was well coated before covering the bowl and setting it in the fridge. A longer marination means richer flavor, so I usually do this step after lunch if possible.
  2. Preheating the Grill (While chicken finishes marinating)
    Toward the end of marination, I fired up my grill pan to medium-high heat, letting it get hot enough for a good sear.
  3. Grilling & Finishing (8–10 min total)
    I cooked the chicken for about 4–5 minutes per side. The harissa caramelizes beautifully, giving the meat a smoky and spicy crust. I checked the internal temp (74 °C), then set the chicken aside to rest.
  4. Garnishing and Serving
    Before serving, I sliced the chicken and scattered chopped coriander over the top. The color really pops against the fiery red harissa.

2. Chickpea Salad with Lemon-Tahini Dressing

  1. Assembling the Vegetables & Herbs (10 min)
    While the chicken marinated, I chopped all the veggies and herbs. To mellow out the onion, I soaked it in cold water for five minutes.
  2. Making the Dressing (5 min)
    I whisked together tahini, lemon juice, garlic, olive oil, salt, and black pepper. If the dressing seemed too thick, I thinned it with a splash of warm water until it was creamy and pourable.
  3. Mixing the Salad
    In a big bowl, I combined chickpeas, tomatoes, cucumber, red onion, parsley, and mint, then gently tossed everything with the lemon-tahini dressing. I tasted and adjusted salt and lemon as needed. The freshness of those herbs is always striking.

3. Warm Pita

  1. Mixing and Kneading the Dough (10 min)
    I stirred together flour, yeast, sugar, and salt, then poured in warm water and olive oil. Kneading by hand is soothing, and I worked the dough until it felt springy and smooth.
  2. First Rise (1 hour; overlaps with chicken marination)
    The dough went into an oiled bowl, covered, and sat in a warm corner until doubled in size. I love how the aroma of yeasty dough fills the kitchen.
  3. Shaping & Second Rise (15 min total)
    After dividing the dough into 8 balls and resting for 10 minutes, I rolled each into a thin round, keeping them covered to retain moisture while they rose again briefly.
  4. Baking (15–20 min total)
    I baked the pitas on my preheated baking stone at 250°C. It’s always a small thrill when they puff into airy pockets. Stacking them in a towel keeps them soft and warm just in time for dinner.

Preparation Timing and Process Notes:
The tasks fit together neatly: I marinated the chicken and started pita dough at the same time. While the dough rose, I prepped all veggies and threw together the salad dressing. As the second dough rise finished, I grilled the chicken and then quickly baked the pitas—each piece ready and warm for the table.
