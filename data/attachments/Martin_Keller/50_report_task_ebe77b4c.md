## 2. Examination Overview

### Purpose of Visit

Martin Keller presented for a thorough health screening with a specific focus on sports medicine to support his ongoing participation in swim-based athletic activities. The primary aim was to evaluate cardiovascular health, respiratory function, metabolic and hematological status, and to assess musculoskeletal health—particularly the left shoulder—to ensure safe and effective performance in competitive swimming.

### Procedures and Assessment Protocols

The evaluation followed internationally recognized clinical sports medicine guidelines [1][2] and included the following steps:

- **Personal and Family History:** A detailed pre-examination interview addressed personal medical history, family health background, current and past symptoms, and the scope and intensity of physical activity.
- **Comprehensive Blood Panel:** Collection and laboratory analysis of blood samples focused on hematologic, metabolic, and inflammatory markers.
- **Electrocardiogram (ECG):** Twelve-lead resting ECG was performed to assess heart rhythm, intervals, and any electrical abnormalities.
- **Pulmonary Function Testing (Spirometry):** Objective assessment of lung volumes and flow rates, compared to age- and sex-matched reference values.
- **Musculoskeletal Assessment:** Focused examination of the left shoulder evaluated active range of motion, strength, and pain. Comparison was made to normative data for adult males.

All assessments were completed using standardized protocols to ensure accuracy and reliability. Where indicated, results were interpreted in the context of Martin’s athletic status.

## 5. Recommendations

To optimize long-term musculoskeletal health and athletic performance, the following targeted interventions are advised:

### 1. **Swimming Technique and Shoulder Management**
- Consult with a swimming coach or sports physiotherapist to refine stroke mechanics. Emphasize techniques that reduce internal rotational strain—specifically, slightly adjusting hand entry and elbow position during the stroke.
- Include scapular stabilization drills in the warm-up routine to enhance shoulder alignment and reduce overuse risk [3].
- Practice daily active and passive stretching, focusing on regaining full internal rotation. Incorporate specific flexibility exercises, such as the “sleeper stretch,” and rotator cuff strengthening with resistance bands.

### 2. **Core Strengthening and Injury Prevention**
- Dedicate at least one training session per week to core muscle development. Prioritize dynamic stabilization exercises (e.g., planks with variations, anti-rotation drills, rotational work).
- Emphasize closed-chain stabilization and progressive overload, as improved core strength is strongly linked to enhanced shoulder stability and overall swimming performance [4].

### 3. **Metabolic Health and Lipid Management**
- Continue regular aerobic exercise, aiming for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per week.
- Incorporate a Mediterranean-style diet rich in plant-based fats, dietary fiber, and lean protein, if not already in place.
- Repeat annual lipid and fasting glucose measurements to monitor for any significant metabolic shifts.

### 4. **Ongoing Monitoring and Preventive Care**
- Track shoulder symptoms and internal rotation over time. Escalate to a formal orthopedics or sports medicine consult if pain worsens or range of motion declines.
- Schedule a follow-up comprehensive sports medical evaluation within 12 months. Earlier review is advised if shoulder dysfunction increases or new symptoms arise.
- Collaborate with a physiotherapist to develop and periodically reassess a personalized home exercise plan, ensuring ongoing progress.

**Implementation Plan:**  
Arrange a swimming technique assessment and physiotherapist consultation during the next training quarter. Integrate shoulder mobility and core stabilization exercises into each training week, ensuring adherence to preventive measures.
