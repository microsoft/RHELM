## Guiding Principles

### Emphasis on Gentle, Restorative Movement
The core of the routine involves low-impact stretches targeting the entire body, focusing particularly on the neck, spine, hips, and legs. All exercises are selected and adapted to minimize strain on the upper body, particularly the shoulders, in line with current clinical recommendations.[1][2]

### Exclusion of Swimming and Upper Body Strain
All swimming drills are currently excluded, unless reapproved by Dr. Lang in future evaluations. Upper body loading is strictly avoided in every session to prevent aggravation of previous injuries.

### Injury Prevention and Monitoring
Each movement is to be performed with careful attention to form, gradually increasing duration or intensity only as tolerated. The program includes specific cues to avoid pain or discomfort and encourages self-monitoring throughout each session.

### Structured Analytical Framework
Exercises are organized in daily tables. Tracking templates allow for clear data collection and facilitate regular review of progress and symptoms.

## Daily Exercise Instructions (Sample: Monday)

### Monday – Neck, Spine, and Lower Back

**1. Neck Tilt Stretch**  
- Begin either seated or standing, with shoulders naturally relaxed.
- Gently tilt your head towards your right shoulder without forcing the movement; hold for 10 seconds.
- Return to center and tilt towards the left. Repeat three times on each side.

**2. Upper Trapezius Stretch**  
- Sit on your left hand to stabilize the shoulder, then gently tip your right ear toward your right shoulder.
- Hold for 10 seconds, then switch sides.
- Repeat 2-3 times per side.

**3. Cat-Cow Stretch**  
- Position yourself on all fours, wrists under your shoulders, knees under your hips.
- Inhale as you arch your back and look slightly upward (cow position), then exhale as you round your spine (cat position).
- Continue gently for 8–10 repetitions, focusing on maintaining a smooth, controlled motion.

**4. Seated Lumbar Stretch**  
- Sit in a sturdy chair with feet flat on the ground and hands resting on your thighs.
- Gently lean forward from your hips, keeping your back straight, until you feel a mild lower back stretch. Hold for 10–20 seconds, repeating three times.

*For other days, follow a comparable level of detail, focusing on the designated muscle groups. Always ensure movements are slow, controlled, and within a pain-free range. For upper body stretches, arms should remain below shoulder level to avoid excessive strain.*

## Professional Guidance and Injury Prevention

- Maintain slow, controlled movements at all times. Bouncing or jerky motions should be completely avoided.
- Focus on steady breathing throughout each stretch—do not hold your breath.
- Keep the spine in a neutral position and relax the shoulders as much as possible.
- Diagrams or video demonstrations are recommended for each stretch to ensure confidence and correct technique.
- If any exercise proves difficult or uncomfortable, adjust immediately, document the change, and consult with Dr. Lang if uncertainty persists.
