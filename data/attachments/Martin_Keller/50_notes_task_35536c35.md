### Reflections on My Favorite Local Finds

#### The Challenge of Sumac

Finding sumac in a small Black Forest town isn’t always easy. I’ve made it a bit of a personal treasure hunt to ask at every spice stall—it’s something I crave for its lively, tart flavor on salads and roasted veg. If the spice merchants at the market don’t have it, I’ll swing by the Turkish or Middle Eastern food shops in Triberg center. Every so often, I get lucky. (For others hoping to track it down, online expat forums [1] offer some reliable tips.)

#### Celebrating the Season

June is my favorite time at the Wochenmarkt. Stands overflow with local produce: strawberries shine brightest now, and the last of the Baden-Württemberg asparagus is still available—so I make the most of it while I can [2]. I always stock up on radishes, greens, and crisp cucumbers, all of which taste so much fresher than anything at the supermarket.

Potatoes and onions find their way into many of my meals—they’re inexpensive, hearty, and perfect for wholesome German recipes. Apples from the orchard stalls stay firm all week, making for a quick bite, especially before an afternoon walk.

#### Embracing Black Forest Specialties

I never leave the market without a block of regional cheese and a slice or two of Schwarzwälder Schinken. These are local icons that taste of the forest and pasture. Combined with rustic bread from the neighborhood bakery, they’re the foundation for easy, satisfying meals—whether breakfast, a picnic, or a light dinner.

The Landjäger sausages are ideal for snacking during a forest hike or when I need something quick and protein-rich at my desk. I also make sure to pick up a jar of honey or homemade jam; our area beekeepers and jam makers always offer something special based on what’s blooming or growing.

Buying a bunch of wildflowers, though not essential, is a small indulgence I almost never skip. Fresh flowers on my kitchen table are a reminder of the natural beauty just outside town.

## Shopping Strategy and Vendor Tips

Over time, I’ve learned a few tricks for making my market mornings efficient and enjoyable:

- **Fresh produce and herbs:** I start with the local farmers—often the first stalls I see—so I get the pick of the day’s best.
- **Bakery and cheese:** These vendors can have lines, especially leading up to lunchtime, so I swing by early for my seeded Vollkornbrot and regional cheese.
- **Meats and eggs:** I like to chat with the butchers and poultry farmers. They always share cooking ideas, and I trust their recommendations for seasonal picks.
- **Spices and specialty products:** I make a quick round at the end to check for sumac or other spices, since these stalls can be tucked away at the edge.
- **Flowers:** I choose these last, so they stay fresh as I carry everything home.

I always greet the vendors, often picking up a bit of local news along with my groceries. There’s a sense of tradition here that I treasure; it’s not just shopping—it’s being part of a weekly community.
