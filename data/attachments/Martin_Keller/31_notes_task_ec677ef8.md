## Technique Focus: Freestyle Breathing Rhythm & Analytics

### Bilateral Breathing Consistency

I maintained a bilateral breathing pattern (breathing every 3 strokes) on about 85% of the main set laps, which is a noticeable improvement from previous sessions where I typically reverted to unilateral breathing more often. The 3-3-3 drill enforced a consistent pattern throughout, reinforcing the muscle memory needed for effective bilateral rhythm.

### Breathing Timing and Exhalation

I focused on a steady and continuous exhalation through the nose while submerged, which felt natural on most reps—post-turn, I rarely experienced any air hunger, indicating better breath management. There was a brief delay in exhalation through the mouth, particularly when I was concentrating hard on my inhalation timing. On the single arm drill, my exhalation on the right side felt much more fluid than on the left, where I sometimes rushed the inhale because exhalation wasn't complete. This unbalanced rhythm was particularly evident as the main set progressed and fatigue built up.

### Stroke Efficiency and Count

Over the main freestyle set, my stroke count per 25 meters dropped to 18–20, from a previous average of 21–22. This shift suggests more efficient, elongated pulling through each stroke, with improved distance per stroke. I also noticed less variation in my stroke counts between laps—a 15% reduction in lap-to-lap stroke rate variance—indicating a more consistent stroke technique overall.

### Technical Observations and Challenges

While overall form improved, I did encounter a recurring issue with the left arm crossing over the midline during single arm drills. This caused a subtle drift off my center line and a bit of tension in my shoulder by the drill’s end. On the second rep of the 3-3-3 drill, my right arm “catch” was slightly slow—possibly because my attention was divided between exhalation and catch mechanics. Fatigue affected my breathing control in the last two laps of the main set, leading to less efficient (shorter) exhalations and a brief tendency to revert to unilateral breathing. By the end, my exhalation rhythm had become less consistent, likely a holdover from cumulative fatigue earlier this week.

## Session Reflection and Next Steps

**Reflection:**  
This session stood out for the progress I made in both stroke efficiency and breathing control. Dropping my stroke count and maintaining bilateral breathing for most laps signaled genuine improvement in endurance and form. The lingering issue with my left arm crossing indicates that further coordination work is needed on that side. Although fatigue crept in late in the main set and affected my exhalation rhythm, overall I found breathing to feel smoother and less forced compared to previous workouts.

**Next Steps:**  
- Continue with single arm drills, giving special attention to left side entry and alignment to correct crossover.
- Add weekly hypoxic sets (breathing every 5 and 7 strokes) to further advance breath control under mild oxygen stress.
- Regularly monitor stroke count and bilateral breathing percentage to keep tangible track of progress and sustain improvements in efficiency.
