# Black Forest Spring Cycling Tour: Technical Route Plan
**Route Name:** Schwarzwald Spring Circuit  
**Date of Tour:** To be selected; recommended window: April 15 – May 15, 2024 (for optimal spring conditions)

## 1. Overview

This document presents an in-depth technical route plan for a spring cycling tour through Germany’s Black Forest (Schwarzwald), as envisioned by Martin Keller and the Friends Cycling Collective on February 2, 2024. Developed specifically for enthusiastic group riders, the plan covers detailed route logistics, segment waypoints, elevation charts, terrain analysis, group dynamics, safety procedures, and essential pre-ride preparations. All aspects are tailored for seasoned cyclists planning a challenging, yet accessible group ride, with consideration given to spring weather, mixed ability levels, and current best practices in group cycling. Route design is based on established Black Forest cycling circuits, verified through respected mapping and local tourism sources.

---

## 2. Route Description & Rationale

**Route Concept:**  
The Schwarzwald Spring Circuit is a 95 km loop beginning and ending in Freiburg im Breisgau, winding through iconic Black Forest scenery. The carefully chosen course offers a blend of urban rollout, classic mountain climbs, fast descents, tranquil lakeside stretches, and forested gravel sections. Designed to accommodate riders with intermediate fitness and basic group-riding experience, the route balances scenic highlights with rideable gradients and includes several options for regrouping and rest breaks.

- **Total Distance:** Approx. 95 km  
- **Total Elevation Gain/Loss:** Approx. 1,750 m  
- **Estimated Ride Duration (including stops):** 6–8 hours  
- **Seasonal Suitability:** Optimized for spring, when average temperatures and increased daylight make for comfortable riding conditions. Some wet or muddy patches are typical at this time.
- **Route Validation:** Based on widely-used public cycling paths and classic Black Forest segments, validated through Komoot, local tourism boards, and prior group ride data.

This route’s loop design from Freiburg assures convenient logistics for diverse group members and allows for easy access via public transport at multiple points along the tour.

## 5. Segment Highlights & Technical Analysis

**Leg 1: Freiburg Hbf → St. Ottilien**  
The route launches with a smooth urban section, allowing a steady warm-up before transitioning into forest roads. The initial climb, moderate in length and grade, leads the group to St. Ottilien, a picturesque chapel nestled in woodland—an early chance to regroup and take in the scenery.

**Leg 2: St. Ottilien → Schauinsland Pass**  
This climb is a Black Forest signature—well signposted (“Schauinsland-Radweg”), it steadily ascends through forest switchbacks. Riders are rewarded with panoramic views over Freiburg and the Rhine valley, making this segment both a physical and visual highlight.

**Leg 3: Schauinsland Pass → Todtnau**  
A technical descent follows, with several sharp hairpins and rapidly changing road conditions. Riders should use caution, monitor speed, and be alert for variable asphalt surfaces. The descent culminates in the valley town of Todtnau, renowned for excellent bakery stops—well-deserved after the morning’s climbing.

**Leg 4: Todtnau → Feldberg Summit**  
The next leg climbs gently but persistently through high forests toward Feldberg, the Black Forest’s highest peak. Early spring riders may notice lingering snow patches. The scenic “Hohtann” viewpoint en route offers an ideal opportunity to regroup and refuel.

**Leg 5: Feldberg → Titisee**  
A mix of paved and gravel descents brings the group down from Feldberg, opening up wide southern views and passing the alpine Feldsee. This is a popular section for photos, and riders are advised to take care on mixed surfaces, especially in wet spring conditions.

**Leg 6: Titisee → Hinterzarten**  
Riders enjoy an easier, rolling segment partly tracing the scenic rail path alongside the lake. The area features several café/rest options, making it suitable for an extended lunch break or lakeside rest.

**Leg 7: Hinterzarten → Kirchzarten**  
The route follows a gentle, winding descent past meadows and into the valley. Some short stretches may use multi-use trails—cyclists should remain courteous to pedestrians.

**Leg 8: Kirchzarten → Freiburg Hbf**  
A tranquil final segment follows the Dreisam river back into Freiburg, with plenty of opportunities for re-supply and an easy pace as the ride wraps up.

Throughout, the route avoids the region’s most technical gravel sectors, favoring pass roads and high-quality, well-maintained tracks to ensure comfort and safety for less-experienced group members.

## 6. Group Dynamics, Preferences & Procedures

- **Expected Average Speed:** 15–18 km/h (including climbs and breaks)
- **Rest and Regrouping:**  
  - Short stops planned every 13–18 km (generally at major waypoints, every 60–90 minutes)  
  - One major lunch/coffee stop (recommended at Titisee, roughly mid-ride)
- **Group Profile:**  
  - Intermediate: Regular cyclists with some group ride experience  
  - Mixed confidence on descents and unpaved sections: route avoids the most technical features accordingly

**Safety and Cohesion Protocols:**

- Assign a ride “lead” (front) and “sweep” (rear), rotating at major stops to distribute roles
- Enforce regrouping policy at end of each major climb/descent
- Conduct a pre-ride safety and pacing briefing, covering hazard awareness and drop-out procedure
- Pair less-confident riders with experienced partners, especially on technical gravel or descending segments
- Standardize emergency equipment: every participant must carry ID, health insurance info, basic repairs, and an emergency contact card

These protocols, combined with route selection, put group safety first while still allowing for individual challenge and enjoyment of the Schwarzwald’s best cycling.

## 8. Comprehensive Pre-Ride Checklist

### A. Bicycle Technical Inspection

| Component      | Inspection Details                                 | Check  |
|----------------|---------------------------------------------------|--------|
| Brakes         | Pads/discs condition, cable/fluid function        | [ ]    |
| Drivetrain     | Lubricated, no chain stretch, clean shifting      | [ ]    |
| Tires          | Adequate tread, inflated, no sidewall damage      | [ ]    |
| Wheels         | Spinning true, no spoke/transient flex            | [ ]    |
| Lights         | Working front/rear (essential for forests)        | [ ]    |
| Misc.          | All bolts tight, mounts secure, bell present      | [ ]    |

### B. Personal Equipment

- Helmet (approved, properly fitted)
- Layered spring cycling kit (including rain/wind jacket)
- Gloves, eye protection, head buff
- Two water bottles (minimum 1.5 L total), energy snacks
- ID, emergency contacts, health insurance details
- Mobile phone (fully charged), portable charger
- Lunch or hearty snack, small cash (some cafés cash-only)

### C. Navigation Essentials

- Handlebar GPS/computer loaded with GPX file
- Smartphone with offline mapping apps (e.g., Komoot, Strava)
- Printed map or cue sheet as backup
- Established WhatsApp/SMS group for ride communication

### D. Emergency & Contingency Supplies

- Two spare tubes, pump or CO2 inflator, puncture kit
- Multi-tool (incl. chain breaker and spare link)
- Basic first aid (bandage, plasters, pain relief)
- Emergency foil blanket
- Local taxi/transportation contact numbers
- Agreed Plan B cutoffs for severe weather or fatigue

**Checklist Rationale:**  
This robust pre-ride protocol minimizes preventable breakdowns and safety incidents, particularly given the limited roadside support in some Black Forest areas.

## 9. Planning Rationale & Recommendations

**Route:**  
By focusing on well-serviced Black Forest passes and sticking to primary paved roads, the main loop offers one of the most memorable experiences the region can provide in spring. Key bailout points, notably at Titisee and Kirchzarten, make it easy to adapt the ride to changing conditions or group needs.

**Safety:**  
Spring weather in the Black Forest can shift rapidly—temperatures drop above 1,000 m, and rain or local snow is still possible, particularly on Feldberg before mid-May. The chosen segments avoid areas that are prone to lingering winter conditions, whenever possible. The group structure and planned rest intervals encourage progressive pacing and reduce the risk of fatigue or incidents.

**Best Practices:**  
Group cycling standards from German and international sources are reflected throughout—ranging from communication to technical readiness and consistent re-group strategies.

- For early-spring outings (before May), always check Feldberg summit webcam and road status before departure, as late snow may linger at altitude.
