## Overview & Documentation Best Practices

The purpose of this digital recipe log is to create a reliable, accessible archive of recipes that can be referenced and repeated with consistent results. By establishing a uniform format and clear documentation guidelines, I can easily adapt entries for use in Markdown, Notion, or OneNote, while still ensuring each recipe is clear, precise, and actionable.

Every recipe entry follows the same structure: Recipe Name, Date Added (YYYY_MM_DD), Source or Inspiration (with hyperlinks where possible), a detailed Ingredients list with metric measurements, clear and sequenced Preparation Steps, Estimated Cooking Time, and a Personal Notes section broken into observations, adjustments, and ideas for future iterations. After each recipe, I include a focused summary of Optimization & Technical Highlights that have made the dish more reproducible—drawing attention to culinary details or methods that influenced the final outcome. The hyperlinked Table of Contents at the top enables quick access to any recipe, and is especially useful in larger, digital collections.

**Best Practices I Follow:**

- **Consistent Structure:** I record every recipe using metric units and a standard field order, which streamlines preparation and eases comparison between attempts.
- **Clear Attribution:** I always credit original sources or inspirations, with full titles and hyperlinks when available, both out of respect and for traceability.
- **Bullet & Numbered Lists:** Ingredients are bulleted with precise weights or volumes; instructions use nested numbering for clarity, especially in complex steps.
- **Highlighting What Matters:** After testing, I summarize techniques or tweaks that improved the dish or addressed troubleshooting points, making future replication easier.
- **Iterative Approach:** Notes on flavor, texture, equipment, or ingredient swaps help capture what worked (or didn’t), providing a feedback loop for refining recipes over time.
- **Efficient Navigation:** I link every recipe title and section, so I can jump directly to any recipe or revisit notes with a single click.

## Sumac-Roasted Cauliflower

**Recipe Name:** Sumac-Roasted Cauliflower  
**Date Added:** 2024_05_09  
**Inspiration/Source:** Adapted from [Ottolenghi’s “Plenty” cookbook][1]

### Ingredients

- 1 large head cauliflower (about 800 g), cut into bite-sized florets
- 30 mL (2 tbsp) extra virgin olive oil
- 10 g (2 tsp) ground sumac
- 5 g (1 tsp) sea salt
- 2 g (½ tsp) freshly ground black pepper
- 5 g (1 tsp) smoked paprika (optional)

### Preparation Steps

1. Preheat oven to 220°C (conventional) or 200°C (fan-forced).
2. Prepare the cauliflower:
    1. Remove leaves and core, then break into evenly sized florets (~4 cm for even roasting).
    2. Pat florets dry with paper towels—getting rid of any surface moisture is key for caramelization.
3. Toss cauliflower in a large bowl with olive oil, sumac, salt, pepper, and paprika (if using), mixing until each piece is fully coated.
4. Spread the florets in a single, uncrowded layer on a parchment-lined baking sheet.
5. Roast for 28–32 minutes; turn florets halfway through to ensure even browning. The edges should be crisp and deep golden.
6. Remove from oven and let rest for 2–3 minutes before serving.

### Estimated Cooking Time

- 40 minutes total (including prep and roasting)

### Personal Notes

- **Observations:** Roasting in a single, well-spaced layer made a noticeable difference in texture—the caramelization was much more even, and the cauliflower edges crisped beautifully instead of turning steamy or soggy.
- **Adjustments:** I’ve tried using lemon zest and a little tartaric acid when I’m out of sumac, which gives the dish a similar tart finish. This works in a pinch, though sumac’s flavor is more layered.
- **Suggestions for Next Time:** I want to experiment with convection mode for even deeper browning. It might bring an extra crunch—worth testing if it changes the flavor enough to switch up my default method.

### Optimization & Technical Highlights

- **Drying Before Oiling:** Ensuring the florets were thoroughly dried before adding oil meant the cauliflower roasted rather than steamed—key for that deep, toasty flavor.
- **Consistent Floret Size:** Using my scale and being precise with cuts paid off, giving every piece the same caramelization and tender interior.
- **Sumac’s Acidity:** The sumac provided a bright, lemony note that balanced the sweet, roasted flavors really well. I found this especially rewarding with richer mains.

## Spicy Chickpea Stew

**Recipe Name:** Spicy Chickpea Stew  
**Date Added:** 2024_05_09  
**Inspiration/Source:** Adapted from [New York Times – Alison Roman’s “The Stew”][2]

### Ingredients

- 30 mL (2 tbsp) extra virgin olive oil
- 2 medium yellow onions (about 300 g), finely chopped
- 20 g (4 cloves) garlic, minced
- 15 g (thumb-sized piece) fresh ginger, finely minced
- 6 g (1 tbsp) ground turmeric
- 5 g (1 tsp) red chili flakes
- 2 × 400 g cans chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 800 mL vegetable stock
- 400 mL coconut milk (1 can)
- 12 g (2 tsp) sea salt
- 2 g (½ tsp) black pepper
- 40 g fresh spinach or kale, chopped
- 10 mL (2 tsp) lemon juice, to finish

### Preparation Steps

1. Heat the olive oil in a heavy-bottomed saucepan or Dutch oven over medium heat.
2. Start the aromatics:
    1. Add onions and cook until soft and translucent, about 5 minutes.
    2. Add garlic and ginger, stirring constantly for 1 minute until fragrant.
3. Sprinkle in turmeric and chili flakes, stirring for another minute so the spices bloom in the oil.
4. Stir in chickpeas, coating well, and let cook for 2–3 minutes.
5. Pour in vegetable stock and coconut milk, and mix thoroughly.
6. Bring to a simmer and cook uncovered for 25–30 minutes, stirring occasionally, letting the broth reduce and flavors concentrate.
7. Add spinach or kale and cook just until wilted (about 2 minutes).
8. Season with salt, pepper, and finish with the lemon juice. Stir well.
9. Taste and adjust—add a little more chili for extra heat, or another splash of lemon for brightness.

### Estimated Cooking Time

- 45 minutes total (including preparation and cooking)

### Personal Notes

- **Observations:** Letting the stew simmer uncovered really developed its body; the broth thickened and the flavors melded into something rich but not at all heavy. The creamy coconut milk base, balanced by the spices, was especially comforting.
- **Adjustments:** One night, I swapped half the chickpeas for white beans—it made the stew a touch milder and smoother. Upping the chili flakes added a real, satisfying kick.
- **Suggestions for Next Time:** I’m considering topping the stew with a spoonful of yogurt or a handful of chopped coriander for added complexity. I also want to test whether pressure cooking cuts down on timing without dulling the layers of flavor built during the slow simmer.

### Optimization & Technical Highlights

- **Blooming Spices in Oil:** Sautéing the turmeric and chili in oil at the start intensified their colors and depth. This step is essential for a robust base.
- **Simmering Uncovered:** Letting the liquid reduce transformed the broth into a proper stew and kept the coconut milk from curdling.
- **Ingredient Order:** Cooking onions and other hardy aromatics before adding spices prevented burning and gave the dish a solid foundation, both in aroma and taste.
