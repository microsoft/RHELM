# Digital Recipe Log Entry: Martin’s Mediterranean Grain Bowls

### Date: 2024-03-31

## Overview

I’ve always been drawn to the vibrant flavors and nutritious balance of Mediterranean cuisine, especially as an easy-to-prep meal for a busy week. Grain bowls have become a regular fixture in my lunch and dinner rotation, and this Mediterranean variation brings together some of my favorite ingredients—farro, chickpeas, bright vegetables, creamy feta, and a tangy, spicy dressing. Below, I’ve documented the full recipe, preparation techniques, and tips for meal prep, as well as some thoughts on customizing and adapting the dish.

## Preparation Steps

I’ve tried a few methods for prepping, but this workflow minimizes mess and maximizes freshness:

1. **Cook the Grains:**  
   I start by rinsing the farro thoroughly under cold water, then cooking it in a pot of salted boiling water (about 1.2 L) until it’s al dente—usually around 20–25 minutes. Once done, I drain and spread it on a tray to cool quickly. This keeps the grains chewy and prevents them from getting mushy later.

2. **Prep the Veggies and Chickpeas:**  
   While the farro cools, I halve the cherry tomatoes, dice the cucumber (sometimes I leave the peel on—depends on my mood), and slice the red onion thinly. I pit and halve the kalamata olives, then give the chickpeas a good rinse and drain.

3. **Herbs and Cheese:**  
   Chopping fresh parsley is always a highlight—the aroma really sets the Mediterranean vibe. I crumble the feta by hand, which helps keep it tender and slightly chunky (pre-crumbed feta always seems too dry).

4. **Make the Dressing:**  
   In a large bowl or measuring cup, I whisk together the olive oil, lemon juice, minced garlic, sumac, harissa paste, salt, and black pepper. Everything comes together into a vibrant, tangy dressing with just a hint of heat.

5. **Assemble the Bowl Base:**  
   Once the farro is cool, I combine it with chickpeas, tomatoes, cucumber, onion, olives, and parsley in a large mixing bowl. A gentle toss distributes the veggies evenly throughout the grains.

6. **Dress and Mix:**  
   I pour the dressing over the assembled bowl and toss thoroughly to ensure every bite is coated. The colors and aroma at this point are always a sign I’m on the right track.

7. **Portion and Finish:**  
   For meal prep, I divide the mixture into my containers (details below). I like to store the feta separately, adding it right before serving—this keeps it fresh and lets me control how much each bowl gets.

8. **Cool and Store:**  
   If I’m prepping ahead, I let the bowls cool at room temperature for a bit (never more than 90 minutes—for food safety), then cover and refrigerate.

## Seasoning Insights: Sumac & Harissa Paste

### Sumac

Sumac adds a gentle, tart, lemony brightness that really lifts the grains and chickpeas. For this batch (around 1 kg total bowl), I use about 1.5 teaspoons—enough to taste without overwhelming. At about 0.3-0.5% of the finished mass, this amount brightens up the earthy flavors without turning the whole dish sour. If I want a more intense tang, I sometimes increase the sumac by half-teaspoon increments and taste as I go. When I’m out of sumac, lemon zest works, but I start with half as much since it can be punchier.

**Scaling Tip:** For larger batches (10 kg yield), I’ve found 15 teaspoons (about 25 g) of sumac works well.

### Harissa Paste

Harissa brings warmth and complexity from chilies, caraway, and cumin, adding a subtle heat that balances the salad. Two teaspoons per batch (as detailed above) keep it spicy but still accessible. I’ve learned to calibrate the harissa carefully—different brands can vary from mildly smoky to fiery hot! For milder versions, I cut it down to a single teaspoon. Sriracha works as a substitute (same amount); for dried chili flakes, I use half the amount. Before final assembly, I always taste the dressing to catch heat and sourness.

**Scaling Tip:** For 10 portions, 20 teaspoons (or about 6–7 tablespoons) of harissa generally works, but I always taste first and adjust as needed.

## Batch Cooking Notes

Batch prepping this recipe has made my busy weeks a lot easier. Here are the essential lessons I’ve picked up:

- **Default Batch Size:** I usually make four portions at a time—about 1 kg total yield.
- **Portions:** Each main meal bowl is roughly 250 g, which I find filling and satisfying.
- **Scaling:** For bigger batches (say, a couple of days’ worth of lunches), I multiply the ingredients proportionally. Ten portions? Multiply everything by 2.5.
- **Storage Containers:**  
  - I prefer glass meal prep containers (around 750 mL each) with airtight lids. BPA-free plastic works too, especially for carrying to work.
  - If I have divided containers, I keep the feta (or any “wet” toppings) in a separate compartment for texture.
- **Food Safety:**  
  - I never let the bowls sit out for more than 90 minutes before refrigeration.
  - Everything goes straight into the fridge (at or below 4°C) once cool.
- **Freshness Window:**  
  - These bowls keep well in the fridge for up to four days, though the feta and onion can soften a bit. Texture is best in the first two days.
- **Modular Storage:**  
  - For maximum freshness, sometimes I store grains and toppings/dressing separately, then assemble right before eating. This keeps crunchy veggies snappy.

##### Storage Process Flow

```
Batch Cooked Bowls → Cool (≤90 min) → Portion into Containers → Seal → Refrigerate
```

## Variation Ideas & Customization

As much as I love the basic bowl, I like to shake things up depending on my cravings, what’s in season, or who I’m serving:

- **Preserved Lemon:** Finely minced preserved lemon (about 30 g) adds an incredible salty-citrus punch. Sometimes I swap it in for sumac or lemon juice in the dressing.
- **Alternative Grains:**  
  - Barley or millet for a heartier bite; quinoa or brown basmati rice when cooking for gluten-free friends; couscous if I’m in a rush.
- **Protein Boosts:**  
  - Sliced grilled chicken breast (about 120 g per bowl) for extra satiety
  - Baked marinated tofu (100 g per portion) for a vegan version
  - Roasted salmon, lamb strips, or a few falafel when I want to switch things up
- **Vegetable Additions:**  
  - Roasted eggplant, zucchini ribbons, roasted red pepper, and artichoke hearts all work well.
  - Sometimes I empty my fridge’s salad greens drawer and add spinach or arugula as a bed for the grain base.
- **Modular/“Bowl Bar” Method:**  
  - If I’m hosting, I prepare all the ingredients separately and let guests build their own bowls to taste.
  - Setting up a mini “dressing bar” with extra sumac, harissa, and lemon wedges gives everyone control over flavor intensity.
  - For events or meal-train prep, storing toppings separately in bulk containers makes assembly quick and keeps things fresh.
- **Other Dressings:**  
  - Sometimes I make a tahini-lemon sauce, a cooling yogurt-mint dressing, or add a drizzle of zhoug for an herby kick.
  - These alternatives keep repeats from getting boring over the week.
