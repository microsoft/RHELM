## 3. Main Drills and Technical Focus

### Warm-Up (10 minutes, 400m)
I opened with an easy 400-meter freestyle, focusing on relaxed, controlled exhalation and bilateral breathing every three strokes. The aim here was to set a calm, steady breathing rhythm from the start and gently elevate my heart rate while aligning with proper technique.

### Main Set (30 minutes, 400m)
I divided the main set into three targeted drills:

#### Drill 1: Extended Exhalation with Snorkel  
- **4 × 50m (total 200m), 30s rest between reps**
- Used a center-mount snorkel
- Swam each 50m in approximately 1:05, with slight variation (1:03-1:06)
- Focused on a steady, continuous exhalation for the entire face-in-water phase, ensuring I emptied my lungs by the time I reached the wall.
- The snorkel allowed me to isolate the exhalation mechanism and monitor leaks, particularly near turns.

#### Drill 2: Exhalation-Controlled 25s with Tempo Trainer  
- **8 × 25m (total 200m), 20s rest**
- Swam with a FINIS Tempo Trainer set to 1.15 seconds per stroke, holding a consistent rhythm.
- Aimed for 27 seconds per 25m (actual times ranged 26-28s).
- Started exhalation as soon as my face entered the water and sustained it until the last two meters, which really sharpened my awareness of any breath holding or imperfect release.

#### Drill 3: Breath-Hold Progression (Hypoxic Work)  
- **4 × 25m, 40s rest**
- On every fourth lap, I increased to a 5-stroke breathing pattern (the rest used 3 strokes), simulating race-pace breath control without oxygen debt.
- Sought to minimize unnecessary micro-inhalations by deliberately extending the exhalation phase and staying relaxed, despite natural tension from the hypoxic work.

### Cool-Down (5 minutes, 100m)
Closed the session with a gentle 100 meters of mixed backstroke and easy choice strokes. Here, the focus was on passive exhalation and unwinding any lingering tension. I paid attention to returning my heart rate toward its baseline, finishing the session feeling refreshed.
