## 2. Personal Reflections on Progress

Looking back over the last week, I’ve noticed measurable improvements in both strength and flexibility. I can now hold a standard plank for 60 seconds (that’s a 10-second increase from the week before) and manage a steady side plank for 40 seconds—a modest but encouraging jump. On the flexibility front, my seated hamstring reach has improved by 3 centimeters; my fingertips now stretch 8 centimeters beyond my toes, which feels physically rewarding.

Day-to-day tasks have felt smoother. Transitions like moving from sitting to standing, or navigating stairs, are noticeably steadier and require less mental focus on every movement. I don’t fatigue as quickly when standing for longer periods—standing for more than 30 minutes used to be tiring, but my endurance is definitely better now.

That said, the tightness in my lower back still greets me every morning, especially after exercise or extended activity. It’s less intense than before, but persistent. There’s no shooting pain into my legs, nor any numbness or weakness, which is a relief. Since introducing more lateral leg work, I’ve felt a mild, on-and-off ache in my right glute—nothing sharp or concerning, but worth keeping an eye on as I continue to tweak my training.

## 3. Updated Training Plan

**Rationale for Modifications**

Building on Dr. Lang’s recommendations and my observations, I’ve updated my training plan to target both persistent lumbar tightness and overall core strength. The focus is on incorporating dynamic stabilization—specifically, adding planks with alternating leg lifts to intensify trunk engagement and stimulate gluteal activation. Stretching is now a daily commitment, especially for the hip flexors and hamstrings, with increased duration to further improve flexibility and address that lingering morning stiffness.

I’m maintaining a progressive approach, adjusting exercise difficulty and volume according to how my body responds each day. If I notice any negative changes in pain or form, I’ll scale back and notify Dr. Lang as instructed.

**Exercise Plan**

| Exercise                     | Frequency/Week | Target Reps/Sets               | Notes & Purpose                                           |
|------------------------------|---------------|---------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Plank (standard)             | 4             | 3 x 45-60s                     | Baseline for endurance; pre-fatigue before progressions   |
| Plank with leg lifts         | 3             | 3 x 10 reps (each leg)         | Improves hip/lumbar stability and dynamic trunk function  |
| Side plank                   | 3             | 3 x 30-40s (each side)         | Works obliques & lateral core control                    |
| Bird-dog                     | 3             | 3 x 10 reps (each side)        | Focuses on coordination and anti-rotation stability      |
| Hip flexor stretch           | Daily         | 3 x 30s (each side)            | Maintains and advances flexibility gains                 |
| Hamstring stretch            | Daily         | 3 x 30s (each side)            | Maintains reach improvement, helps relieve morning tightness |
| Lumbar rotation stretch      | 5             | 3 x 15 reps                    | Targets morning back tightness, mobility                 |
| Glute bridge                 | 3             | 3 x 12 reps                    | Strengthens posterior chain, glute activation            |

Core stability exercises (planks, bird-dogs) are my foundation, while daily stretching helps tackle the lingering stiffness. The new plan puts an extra emphasis on flexibility maintenance and dynamic trunk engagement. Progress is logged daily, and symptomatic feedback is noted immediately for ongoing trend analysis.
