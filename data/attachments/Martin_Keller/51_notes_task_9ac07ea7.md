# Recipe Log Entry: Mediterranean-Style Roasted Vegetable & Chickpea Salad  
**Date Prepared:** 2024-06-21

---

## 1. Overview

On June 21, 2024, I prepared a Mediterranean-style Roasted Vegetable and Chickpea Salad, documenting each step to ensure clarity and reproducibility. My aim was to create a harmonious balance of flavors and textures, with particular attention to the integration of Frau Bauer’s sumac blend—a spice mix I’ve been eager to experiment with for its bright, aromatic profile. Here, I detail my process, ingredient selection, methodological choices, and my reflections on the result. By recording the interplay of technique and ingredient—the layering of spices, roasting variables, and final assembly—I hope to support consistent results in future preparations and provide a clear model for variation.

## 3. Step-by-Step Preparation

1. **Preheating and Setup**  
   I started by preheating my convection oven to 215°C (420°F) and lining two baking trays with parchment. This helps to achieve consistent roasting and easy cleanup.

2. **Vegetable Preparation**  
   I gathered and diced the eggplant, zucchini, and bell pepper into 2 cm cubes, cutting the red onion into wedges and halving the cherry tomatoes. In a large bowl, I tossed all the vegetables with 2g of sea salt, 15 mL olive oil, and 2g of Frau Bauer’s sumac blend, ensuring even distribution. The seasoned vegetables were spread out in a single layer on the tray for even caramelization.

3. **Roasting Vegetables**  
   The vegetables went into the oven for 24–28 minutes. I stirred them at the halfway mark, watching for deep golden edges and slight shriveling on the tomatoes—signs that the flavors had intensified and the sugars caramelized, but with the flesh still retaining a gentle bite.

4. **Chickpea Toasting**  
   While the vegetables roasted, I patted the chickpeas dry in a clean towel. I tossed them with 10 mL olive oil, another 1g of the sumac blend, and 0.5g of salt before spreading them onto the second tray. They roasted alongside the vegetables for 12–15 minutes, shaken gently once, until I saw crisped edges and a slight golden color.

5. **Dressing Preparation**  
   Meanwhile, I assembled the dressing in a small bowl: whisking together the remaining 15 mL olive oil, lemon juice, vinegar, Dijon mustard, honey, 2.5g salt, 1g black pepper, and 1g sumac blend. I whisked until fully emulsified and bright. After tasting, I adjusted for acidity and salt, adding a touch more lemon if the sumac’s tartness needed a boost.

6. **Assembly and Integration**  
   In a large bowl, I combined the hot roasted vegetables, crisp chickpeas, chopped fresh parsley (and a little mint), and about three-quarters of the crumbled feta. The dressing was poured over, and everything tossed gently so the vegetables held their shape and the dressing coated without making things soggy.

7. **Final Plating**  
   I served the salad immediately, at room temperature, on a large platter, topped with the remaining feta and a final dusting of sumac for extra fragrance and color. For a mellower, blended flavor, I sometimes refrigerate the salad and toss again before serving.

## 4. Using Frau Bauer’s Sumac Blend

**Approach and Rationale:**  
This proprietary sumac blend played a central role, adding a vibrant, citrusy tang and a beautiful ruby hue. I divided the total amount of blend across three stages:

- **For the vegetables:** Including sumac in the initial toss amplified tart, earthy notes and encouraged browning through subtle acidity.
- **On the chickpeas:** A coat of sumac before roasting brought a citrus complexity that popped in each bite.
- **In the dressing:** Adding a portion to the vinaigrette tied together the salad’s various notes, giving the dressing a unique, unifying tartness and deepening the umami.

**Flavor and Function:**  
Sumac, with its natural tartness from phenolic acids and anthocyanins, brightens the salad in a way that’s distinct from lemon or vinegar. I found it gave the dish a layered, lively flavor profile, cutting through richness, enhancing both the roasted vegetables and the green herbs, and accentuating the briny feta. Unlike harsher acids, sumac’s aroma and dryness bridge the gap between fresh and roasted elements, creating a cohesive flavor journey from first bite to last.

## 5. Detailed Tasting Notes

**Texture:**  
The vegetables kept their structure—soft centers with crisp edges, no mushiness. The chickpeas added a welcome crunch, and the fresh herbs offered bursts of brightness against the fattier, creamier feta. Each forkful was dynamic, with a satisfying variety in mouthfeel.

**Flavor Balance:**  
Tang and earthiness danced throughout: sumac’s subtle lemon notes complemented the sharper lemon juice and vinegar, balancing the olive oil’s richness and bringing forward the natural sweetness of roasted peppers and onions. The salt was just right, bringing out the feta’s briny edge without over-salting the vegetables. Dijon added complexity and a gentle kick, while honey—optional but, for me, essential—rounded out the dressing.

**Sumac’s Impact:**  
On the nose, the salad had a smoky-citrus aroma. The first bite revealed sumac’s signature tartness as a top note, which lingered and brightened each flavor without overpowering the acids or masking the vegetables’ sweetness. The salad felt cohesive, thanks to sumac’s clever placement throughout; the flavor “lifted” as I ate, and the complexity held my interest to the very last bite.

## 6. Ideas for Future Iterations

**1. Alternative Roasting for Enhanced Texture**  
Next time, I’d like to try roasting at a higher temperature—230°C—for 18–20 minutes, then finishing the vegetables under the broiler for a few minutes. I suspect this approach will deepen the caramelization on the outside while keeping the interiors juicier, creating an even bolder contrast in textures.

**2. Seasonal and Dietary Substitutions**  
I want to experiment with swapping in fennel for its lovely aniseed note or sweet potato for extra creaminess and natural sweetness, adjusting the sumac and acids to balance as needed. For a vegan version, I’ll try almond-based cheese to replicate feta’s salt and richness without dairy.

**3. Customizing the Spice Blend**  
I’m also considering blending my own sumac mix next time—maybe with za’atar for added herbal earthiness or Aleppo pepper for a gentle heat. Customizing the mix will let me tweak the flavor profile and play with new dimensions.
