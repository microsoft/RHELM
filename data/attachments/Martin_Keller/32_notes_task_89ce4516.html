Date: 2024_03_31

Purchases This Week

This week’s trip to the weekly market was all about picking up some Mediterranean essentials for my cooking. I focused on fresh, flavorful ingredients that would work in salads, grain dishes, and main meals. Here’s what I bought and how I’m planning to store and use everything:

Item Quantity Vendor/Source Notes on Storage and Use
Cherry tomatoes approx. 500 g Stall near main entrance (vendor name not recorded) I keep these in their original punnet in the fridge, but when serving them raw, I let them sit out for about 30 minutes so their flavor really shines. They’re best within a week.
Kalamata olives 200 g (pitted) Noted: Grocery counter, specific stall uncertain I always ask for olives with brine and transfer them to a glass container at home. They keep well in the fridge for up to two weeks.
Feta cheese 200 g block Cheese counter (brand and vendor unmarked) To maintain the briny tang, I store feta submerged in some of its brine and keep it tightly sealed in the fridge (always under 7°C). I aim to finish within a week—it’s at its creamiest then.
Sumac 40 g (spice jar) Spice stall, middle aisle (label missing) I store all spices in a cool, dark cupboard after opening them, and sumac is no different. The jar is airtight; I’ll check that it keeps its tangy aroma over the next few months.
Flat-leaf parsley 1 bunch (~50 g) Organic produce seller After a quick rinse, I wrap the bunch in a damp paper towel, tuck it into a perforated bag, and stash it in the vegetable drawer. If I use it daily, it’s usually gone within 4–5 days.
Harissa paste 90 g jar Spoke with Frau Bauer at the condiment stand Frau Bauer helped me pick a locally made harissa with a mild spice level. I refrigerate the jar right after opening and always use a clean spoon—one jar lasts about three weeks if I use it a few times per week.
Preserved lemons 2 whole lemons (~100 g) Specialty import stand, details not recorded These come in a sealed glass jar filled with brine. I keep them in the fridge and rinse before dicing them for dishes. Unopened, they’re fine for months, but once opened, I try to finish the jar within two months.

Reflections and Reminders for Next Week

Midweek Checkpoints and To-Do List

Staying ahead with perishables really keeps food waste down. I’ve set a few personal reminders for the coming week: - Midweek, I’ll check the feta, olives, and parsley to make sure nothing’s languishing at the back of the fridge. If anything is close to expiry, I’ll incorporate it right away (maybe make a herby feta spread with extra parsley). - Before I head out again, I want to quickly check how much sumac I have left—I go through more of it than I think, especially with salads. - I’ll also swing by Frau Bauer’s stall earlier. The local harissa blend goes fast, and I’d rather pre-order than miss out. - I keep forgetting to ask vendors for their names or a business card. Building those relationships might help me get the freshest picks, and it’s just nice for future reference. - I plan to bring my own containers next time, especially for olives and feta, to cut down on plastic packaging.

Vendor Preferences & Shopping Strategy

I find the taste and freshness of olives and feta really varies between stalls, so I’m gradually noting which vendors give the best shelf life and quality. For herbs and produce, I look out for organic certification signs and inspect for crispness and vibrant color. The organic produce sellers consistently impress me, and the little bit extra I pay is worth it for the flavor.

Recipe Inspirations Using Preserved Lemons

With preserved lemons on hand, I’m excited to try some new dishes as well as flavor twists for my usual meals: - Moroccan Chicken Tagine: I love slow-cook dishes, and adding diced preserved lemon with olives and spices to chicken gives a fragrant, tangy kick. It’s hearty, and leftovers develop even more flavor the next day. - Mediterranean Grain Salad: Mixing bulgur (or couscous) with chunks of tomato, parsley, sumac, feta, and little bits of preserved lemon makes for a lively, refreshing meal—great for packed lunches or side dishes. - Stews, Dressings, and Roasts: Finely chopped preserved lemons brighten up vinaigrettes or can be tossed with roasted veggies and fish.
- Meal Prep Idea: One easy lunch prep is marinated feta: I crumble feta in a jar, add chopped parsley, a sprinkle of sumac, and a sliver of preserved lemon, drizzled with olive oil. Great with pita or fresh bread.
