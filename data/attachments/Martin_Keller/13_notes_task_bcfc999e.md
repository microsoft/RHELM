## Wishlist Expansion

To keep my collection fresh and engaging, I’ve been considering new titles that offer unique mechanics and replayability for both casual get-togethers and more dedicated sessions. These are currently at the top of my wishlist:

### Ark Nova

#### Summary
- **Designers:** Mathias Wigge  
- **Publisher:** Feuerland Spiele, Capstone Games  
- **Players:** 1–4  
- **Playtime:** 90–150 minutes  
- **Difficulty:** 3.73/5 (BoardGameGeek)

#### Why I’m Interested  
Ark Nova has generated a lot of buzz in the gaming community, and after reading through various reviews and watching playthroughs, I’m particularly drawn to how it blends rich Euro-style resource management with spatial planning and clever use of action cards. The core mechanic—building and running a modern zoo—really appeals to me, and the action-selection system seems both innovative and engaging. While the ruleset is complex, players consistently praise the way everything integrates smoothly after a few turns, rewarding thoughtful planning without feeling overwhelming once the basics are internalized. Its consistent high ranking on BoardGameGeek further reinforces my interest in bringing it to the table for our more strategy-focused game nights.[1]

### Revive

#### Summary
- **Designers:** Helge Meissner, Anna Wermlund, Eilif Svensson, Kristian Amundsen Østby  
- **Publisher:** Aporta Games  
- **Players:** 1–4  
- **Playtime:** 90–120 minutes  
- **Difficulty:** 3.73/5 (BoardGameGeek)

#### Why I’m Interested  
Revive’s post-apocalyptic setting and modular gameplay offer a refreshing change of pace from standard Eurogames. I’m intrigued by its combination of asymmetric player powers, engine-building, and an unfolding narrative element, which promises substantial replay value. The deck-building and resource management systems add strategic depth, while the “unlocking” progression gives each session a unique arc. Early feedback highlights how the rules are thorough yet accessible once underway, with a gameplay rhythm that keeps everyone engaged. I especially appreciate games that accommodate evolving strategies and offer something new each play, and Revive seems to deliver on both counts.[2]

## Upcoming Game Night Plans

With our next gathering with Jana and Lukas around the corner, I wanted to outline a shortlist of games that should be both accessible and fun for the three of us. Here’s a quick breakdown of my selections and the reasons behind them:

- **Azul**  
  Perfect for a relaxed start; it sets up quickly and the rules are easy to pick up, which makes it great for easing into the evening. The abstract design and colorful components are always a hit.

- **7 Wonders Duel**  
  While primarily designed for two, I’d like to try out a mini-tournament where we rotate opponents or have one person act as a “coach” in each round. Its quick rounds and competitive tension are always engaging.

- **Wingspan**  
  This is a solid choice with three; the combination of simple rules and surprising strategic depth means everyone gets involved. The bird-themed art and tactile components add to the table presence.

- **The Crew: Mission Deep Sea**  
  For a cooperative challenge with minimal downtime, The Crew is ideal. Its mission-based structure allows for flexible play times, and the evolving difficulty always keeps us on our toes.

- **Carcassonne**  
  This classic is easy to teach and works well across all experience levels. The tile-laying mechanics encourage friendly rivalry and quick thinking, making it a perennial crowd-pleaser.
