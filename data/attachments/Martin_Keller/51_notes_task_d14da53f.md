## 6. Technical & Subjective Performance Observations

**Freestyle Technique:**  
Technical drills early in the session paid dividends during the main set. My left arm entry felt smoother and more controlled, making the catch phase notably more effective on that side. I was able to connect the pull-through into a single fluid movement after focusing on balance and extension in the drill sets. However, as I fatigued in the last couple of 200m repeats, my right-side body roll became inconsistent, requiring conscious attention to restore symmetry.

**Shoulder Mobility:**  
External rotations and arm circles on poolside felt easy today; my left shoulder range of motion subjectively reached about 75% of what I’d consider ideal, and there were no signs of discomfort. The backstroke cool-down further helped recover shoulder mobility, and I finished without any soreness or pinch.

## 7. Reflections and Technical Adjustments

The integration of focused drills before the main set noticeably improved my core connection and rotation timing, particularly following the 6-3-6 drill. This improvement largely held through the entirety of the main set, though I did notice a tendency to lose streamline off walls after flip turns—especially as fatigue set in.

During the main aerobic block, my splits remained consistent across all six 200-meter repeats, suggesting effective pacing and aerobic stability. Bilateral breathing held up through all intervals—a gain over previous sessions where I would often default to single-side breathing in later sets.

Focusing on a balanced arm entry and a purposeful catch, especially on my left, led to greater stroke efficiency. Moving forward, I need to remain vigilant about keeping my left-side roll active and hip-driven, as well as preventing my right arm from crossing over the centerline as I approach fatigue. Mindfully re-forming my streamline after each turn will also be a priority.

For upcoming sessions, I plan to add short, moderate-intensity kick sets to further engage the core and hip drive. I also want to try using a front snorkel during drills to refine my catch mechanics without worrying about head position. Finally, scheduling underwater video analysis should give me clearer feedback on entry angles and streamline.
