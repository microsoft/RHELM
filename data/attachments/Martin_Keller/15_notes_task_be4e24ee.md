# Weekly Meal Prep Plan for Martin Keller (Feb 4–10, 2024)

## Overview

This meal prep plan for the week of February 4–10, 2024, is structured around my goals: minimizing time spent cooking day-to-day, keeping my meals nutritionally balanced, and staying excited by having plenty of variety. Over the years, I’ve learned that investing time in prepping a handful of versatile base dishes on the weekend makes my week so much smoother—and helps me actually enjoy what I eat.

The focus for this week is on five key principles:

- **Efficient Batch Cooking:** Cook larger quantities of core proteins, grains, and veggies on Sunday, so the most time-consuming work is already done when my weekdays get busy.
- **Nutritional Variety:** I want each day to include different proteins—like chicken, beef, salmon, and chickpeas—plenty of vegetables, some whole grains, and fruit, so my meals never feel repetitive or lacking in nutrition.
- **Flavors and Cuisine Rotation:** I love experimenting with different tastes, so I’ve built in themes including Mediterranean, Asian-inspired stir fry, Western classics, and a little Latin flair to keep my palate engaged.
- **Food Safety and Freshness:** After past experiences with food spoilage, I strictly follow USDA and FDA food safety guidelines for cooling, storage, and reheating, using glass and BPA-free containers whenever possible[^1][^2].
- **Practical Constraints:** I have no allergies or dietary restrictions that require workarounds, and my kitchen is fully equipped with all the necessary appliances and plenty of containers for prep and storage.

### How I Make It Work

My meal prep isn’t just about efficiency—though there’s a real sense of relief when the fridge is full of labeled, stackable containers by Sunday night. I rely on batch-cooking multi-use ingredients like brown rice or roasted veggies. Overlapping flavors and ingredients means less time chopping, less waste, and more flexibility: that batch of quinoa works in both salad and grain bowls; a tray of roasted chicken becomes the backbone for several meals. I portion everything after cooling, label each container, and organize the fridge so the freshest meals are used first. By planning which dishes to eat early and which to freeze, nothing goes soggy or stale.

## Preparation, Safety, and Workflow Tips

I’ve developed my own mini routine from years of a busy work-life balance and, honestly, some trial and error.

### Food Safety First

- After cooking, I always let hot dishes cool for about 30 minutes at room temperature (uncovered), then move them uncovered to the fridge until they’re cold. This helps prevent condensation, which can turn leftovers soggy. Once fully cooled, I seal everything up[^1].
- I keep my fridge below 4°C (40°F), double-checking every Sunday, and label every container with both the dish and the date.
- Meals I won’t use within three or four days go straight into the freezer in shallow, airtight containers—the aim is to prevent freezer burn and keep flavors fresh.
- For reheating, my rule is simple: food must be piping hot throughout—at least 74°C (165°F)—and I give it a good stir to make sure there aren’t any cold spots hiding in the middle.
- When thawing, I always move frozen dishes into the fridge a day ahead so everything defrosts safely.

### My Weekend Prep Workflow

Come Saturday afternoon, I shop and pre-chop as many veggies as I can, so that Sunday can be all about cooking and assembly. Here’s roughly how my meal prep day runs:

```
Saturday PM:  Shopping, produce washing and chopping

Sunday:
- Oven: Roast chicken, salmon, and big trays of vegetables in overlapping batches
- Stovetop: Cook brown rice, quinoa, and pasta (often at the same time)
- Simmer: Make chickpea stew while eggs boil in the next pot over
- Assemble: Toss together Greek salad, package overnight oats, and prep fruit
- Portion: Lay everything out and organize into containers, label, then cool and store
```

I try to do as much as possible in parallel—rice cooks while the oven’s full, and I batch-wash all fruit first so that snack box assembly is quick and satisfying. I love seeing the week’s rainbow of meals lined up in the fridge and freezer!

### Keeping the Fridge Organized

```
|----------------|------------------|---------------------|
| Upper Shelves  | Fruits, snacks   | Overnight oats      |
| Middle Shelves | Lunch/Dinner dishes (4-day supply)     |
| Lower Shelf    | Raw meat/fish    | Hard-boiled eggs    |
| Freezer        | Long-term mains  | Spare rice/veg, all labeled/dates visible |
```

It’s not just neatness—it lets me spot what’s running low or nearing its "eat-by" date, so nothing is forgotten at the back (lesson learned from too many wasted groceries in my student days). Any salad greens or dressed veggies, I keep separate from the dressing to help them stay crisp for several days.

Front-loading the effort into one main prep day is a game-changer for me.
