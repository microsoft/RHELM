## 3. Route Overview and Description

For today’s commute, I set out from my current apartment on Talstraße in Schonach im Schwarzwald and headed to the office, located in the Gewerbegebiet on Tribergstraße. I've been experimenting with different routes and chose a slightly longer detour along Waldstraße to escape the often-crowded main road. This detour features a beautiful section of graded gravel near the old mill (Alte Mühle), providing both variety and a quiet stretch through dense forest.

### 3.1 Detailed Directions

1. **Talstraße (Starting Point):**  
   I started out on the residential stretch of Talstraße—fairly quiet at this time and easy to navigate.
   
2. **Onto Waldstraße:**  
   After a few blocks, I turned onto Waldstraße, passing through a quiet residential zone. The road here is smoothly paved, so I was able to get into a steady rhythm while warming up.

3. **Waldstraße Detour (Forest Segment):**  
   About 2 kilometers in, I veered away from the main traffic, taking the forest service road that loosely parallels Waldstraße toward Alte Mühle. The entrance to the gravel segment sits at approximately 48.1465° N, 8.2067° E (per OpenStreetMap data).  
   The gravel section is just under a kilometer long (about 0.7 km at the steepest point), with a mix of medium-firm compacted stone and some embedded rocks—around 2 meters wide. This part climbs at around a 5% grade for roughly 300 meters, before gently descending for 200 meters. The entire area is heavily shaded, with tall spruce and fir forest overhead.   
   After last night’s rain, the air was cool and there was a slight earthy aroma; I noticed a thin layer of residual moisture on the gravel, which made it a little slick on one or two curves.

4. **Old Mill Segment:**  
   The section around Alte Mühle is somewhat looser underwheel, with shallow drainage ruts and the occasional patch of slippery chippings, especially in curves. Passing through, I kept my speed down and focused on stability.

5. **Exit Gravel, Return to Paved:**  
   After the gravel, I rejoined the paved Waldstraße extension, which leads directly back into Tribergstraße, entering the small industrial area that houses my office.

6. **Final Stretch on Tribergstraße:**  
   The last segment is a gentle uphill followed by a flat approach to the office entrance. Traffic here is busier but manageable, with enough shoulder space to feel comfortable.

## 8. Personal Observations and Technical Notes

**Road Surface:**  
- **Talstraße/Waldstraße (paved):** Smooth apart from a couple of shallow potholes at the Waldstraße entrance.
- **Forest/Old Mill Segment (gravel):**  
    - Noticed lower traction in sections with post-rain dampness—especially along the drainage rut before the mill.  
    - Slowed down in the sharpest curve, where loose stones accumulated, dropping speed to 13–15 km/h (as opposed to 22–25 km/h on clean pavement).  
    - Exiting the gravel, my tires picked up a bit of moisture on the sidewalls but stayed clear of mud; no clogging or heavy buildup.
- **Debris:**  
    - Only minor: a few large twigs and one small branch, likely due to storms the night before. Nothing requiring me to dismount or slow significantly.

**Timing Highlights:**  
- **Fastest Segment:** Tribergstraße final straight—1.3 km in 3:41 (avg 21.2 km/h).
- **Slowest Segment:** Waldstraße gravel climb—0.7 km in 3:25 (avg 12.2 km/h).

**Traffic Encounters:**  
- No cars during the entire off-road/gravel portion; just one hiker, who I yielded to at a narrow spot.
- Four cars overtook me on paved roads; each gave me a safe margin, all more than 1.5 meters away.
- The longest stop was when entering the main road, delayed by a busy intersection and a full pedestrian crossing cycle—about 70 seconds total.
- Mechanically, everything was smooth. The bike handled the mix of surfaces well, with brakes remaining crisp on the wet descents.

## 11. Reflections and Forum Feedback

### 11.1 Route Efficiency

The Waldstraße detour consistently adds about 10% to my commute distance and roughly three to four extra minutes, mostly because I have to ease off on the gravel. On the plus side, I avoid the peak-hour stress of the B500 and main drag—and don’t have to worry about squeezing between trucks or aggressive drivers.

The climb on the detour route is only slightly more demanding, and it’s concentrated in the forest segment before Alte Mühle. I actually find it a good mental reset, tucked away from the bustle and road noise.

### 11.2 Comfort, Safety, and Recommendations

The switch between smooth pavement and loose gravel needs a bit of attention, especially after wet weather. With 40c tires, running around 3.5–4.0 bar, I don’t have any traction issues, even on fresh chippings, but narrower road tires would probably struggle. I always have lights on by default, since the forest can be surprisingly dim, especially on gray mornings like today.  

High-visibility gear is just as important as ever, particularly during the busy final stretch on Tribergstraße. I haven’t run into any major hazards on the detour yet, though I know to take it easy near drainage ruts after rain—traction changes fast there.

### 11.3 Advice for Other Riders

If you value a quieter, more scenic ride over shaving a few minutes from your commute, this detour is a solid option around Schonach. I’d definitely recommend wider, well-treaded tires for the gravel segment. Factor in a little extra time; there’s no sense rushing in the forest. After rain, keep an eye out for slick spots, branches, or the occasional hiker—most folks are friendly and give space but be ready to slow and call out.

For those riding with clipless pedals, it’s worth carrying cleat covers as the gravel can sometimes pack into shoe mechanisms. On weekends, expect a few more hikers and dog walkers in the forest, so a bell or friendly greeting makes passing easier.
