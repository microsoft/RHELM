## Detailed Reflections

### Adaptation to New Breathing Rhythms

Adopting new breathing rhythms required a noticeable adjustment period, especially evident during the Hypoxic Pyramid (3-5-7) drills. Synchronizing my inhale-exhale timing with the structured lap counts felt taxing in the first few sets. With the Side-Kick Breaths, however, I found increased comfort holding a streamlined body position, maintaining a straight head and spine while practicing lateral breathing—a significant improvement from previous sessions. The session began with some awkwardness, particularly in the first laps when I tended to hold my breath too long at the push-off and after flip turns, leading to slight breathlessness.

### Technical Challenges

The Single-Arm with Pull Buoy drills revealed a tendency to rush the exhale, especially toward the end of sets. This sometimes resulted in tension in my chest and a somewhat choppier stroke finish. During the most intense portions of the Hypoxic Pyramid, slowing down my exhalation took considerable conscious effort, and I struggled to maintain relaxation as my CO₂ levels built up. On Catch-Up drills, evenly timing the exhale with the recovery phase of the stroke demanded constant attention, and I occasionally slipped into a truncated breathing rhythm. As sets wore on and fatigue increased, my stroke count became less consistent, especially during longer efforts—highlighting a need to improve aerobic stamina and maintain stroke efficiency under more advanced breathing demands.

### Performance Milestones

Despite the hurdles, I noticed tangible progress:

- On the 3-3-3 Breathing Drill, I completed all laps without having to hold my breath or pause for extra recovery, trimming 3 seconds off my average lap time compared to last week.
- Stroke count remained impressively stable during Catch-Up drills, with only a ±1 stroke fluctuation per lap—a strong indicator of improved control and rhythmic consistency.
- The rest period between Hypoxic Pyramid laps dropped from 20 to 12 seconds on average, suggesting improved tolerance to CO₂ buildup and more rapid recovery between efforts.

## Priorities and Targets for Next Session

Building on today’s progress, I’ve set the following micro-goals for my next session:

- **Hypoxic Pyramid:** Limit rests between pyramid laps to no more than 10 seconds. I’ll record whether I can maintain controlled breathing and how easily I recover.
- **Single-Arm with Pull Buoy:** Keep stroke count within a narrow, ±1 stroke range per lap for each arm (if a set starts at 16 strokes, all laps must stay between 15–17). I’ll track this separately for left and right sides to ensure balanced development.
- **3-3-3 Breathing Drill:** Aim to sustain a continuous, relaxed exhale for at least 80% of each lap. In addition to lap splits, I’ll pay closer attention to perceived relaxation and technique efficiency.
- **Heart Rate Monitoring:** I’ll introduce post-set heart rate tracking alongside lap times for each drill. This will help establish a clearer link between aerobic development and improved breathing control.
- **Reflective Practice:** After each drill, I’ll note any changes in breathlessness or recovery rate as qualitative markers for adaptation and confidence with new breathing techniques.
