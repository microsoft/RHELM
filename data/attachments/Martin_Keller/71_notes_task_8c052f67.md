### 2. Ingredients (Metric Units)

For this morning’s breakfast, I prepared enough shakshuka to serve five people, using a heavy 28 cm skillet. I focused on a balanced combination of spices and herbs, making good use of Frau Bauer’s Aleppo pepper, fresh sumac, and my latest za’atar blend. The ingredients and details are as follows:

### 3. Preparation Steps with Times and Temperatures

#### Step 1: Vegetable Prep  
**Time: 00:00–00:05 (5 min)**  
I started by prepping all my vegetables—onion, red bell pepper, and garlic—measured and diced just before cooking. This helps maintain their freshness and ensures even cooking.

#### Step 2: Sauté Onions & Peppers  
**Time: 00:05–00:09 (4 min) | 180°C, medium-high heat**  
I heated 40 mL of extra-virgin olive oil in a 28 cm heavy-bottomed skillet until it reached about 180°C. After that, I added the diced onions and red bell pepper. Stirring them regularly, I let them soften for about 4 minutes, making sure they didn’t pick up any color—a gentle start is key for a sweet, mellow flavor base.

#### Step 3: Bloom Spices  
**Time: 00:09–00:11 (2 min) | Maintain 180°C**  
Next, I stirred in the minced garlic along with the Aleppo pepper, sumac, za’atar, cumin, coriander, and smoked paprika. I kept everything moving in the pan for about 2 minutes to gently toast the spices and bring out their aromas. I took extra care that the garlic warmed through and smelled fragrant, but didn’t brown.

#### Step 4: Tomato Base & Sauce Simmer  
**Time: 00:11–00:21 (10 min) | Medium heat, 95–100°C**  
I added the diced tinned tomatoes (along with their juice), tomato paste, sea salt, and about 30 mL of water. Stirring to combine, I brought it to a gentle simmer. Over the next 10 minutes, the sauce thickened nicely, becoming rich but not too dense. Periodic stirring prevented sticking—especially important given the high tomato content.

#### Step 5: Make Wells for Eggs  
**Time: 00:21–00:22 (1 min) | Maintain 95–100°C**  
Once the sauce reached the right consistency, I used the back of a spoon to make five evenly spaced wells. This helps the eggs hold their shape and cook evenly in the sauce.

#### Step 6: Add and Poach Eggs  
**Time: 00:22–00:30 (8 min) | Gentle simmer, covered**  
I cracked one egg into each of the wells, aiming for neat placement. Then I covered the skillet and kept the sauce at a gentle simmer (95–98°C). After 6 minutes, I quickly checked the doneness. At around 8 minutes, the egg whites were set and opaque, while the yolks remained creamy and just slightly runny—satisfying that perfect breakfast standard.

#### Step 7: Finish and Garnish  
**Time: 00:30–00:31 (1 min) | Off heat**  
After removing the pan from the heat, I sprinkled over chopped fresh coriander and parsley. The dish was served directly from the skillet in the middle of the table—there’s something convivial about everyone sharing from one pan.

### 5. Tasting Notes & Observations

**Aroma**  
As soon as the lid was lifted, the fruity heat of Aleppo pepper and toasty fragrance from the fresh za’atar blend filled the kitchen. Smoky notes from paprika lingered behind, while sumac brought a lively, tart freshness.

**Texture**  
The sauce achieved a balance between richness and body, with intact pieces of onion and bell pepper for gentle bite. Egg whites were gently set, yielding to creamy, golden yolks—precisely as intended.

**Flavor**  
Every bite was layered: the tomatoes provided a deep, savory backbone, while the Aleppo pepper offered warmth without overwhelming. Sumac added brightness, and the za’atar’s herbal and nutty qualities tied everything together. Using just the right amount of salt ensured all these flavors harmonized without overpowering the subtlety of the spices.

**Visual Presentation**  
The finished dish was vibrant—deep red-orange from the paprika and tomatoes, dotted with flecks of bright green herbs and sesame from the za’atar. Each egg remained visually distinct, with glistening yolks at the center. The meal looked as inviting as it tasted.

### 6. Reflections, Adjustments, and Future Experiments

This batch hit a sweet spot between robust flavor and morning comfort, but there’s always room for refinement:

- I plan to adjust the spice blooming duration: experimenting with a shorter bloom might increase up-front aromatics, while a longer one could draw out deeper, roastier notes.
- I’m intrigued by the idea of tweaking the za’atar blend, perhaps increasing the thyme for more herbal punch or adding extra sesame for crunch.
- Next time, I may add 10 mL of pomegranate molasses to the tomato base to introduce subtle sweetness and tartness without unbalancing the sauce.
- To perfect egg texture, I want to try finishing the last two minutes of poaching uncovered, observing whether a glossier yolk and improved appearance result.
- I’m also curious how using a cast iron versus an enameled skillet affects heat retention and browning, particularly along the base.
- Because fresh sumac’s aroma fades quickly, I’ll compare a just-ground batch to one aged a week, to see how the citrus intensity changes.
- Finally, I’ll experiment with the timing of fresh herb garnishes—adding them immediately before serving versus once plated—to see how it affects their aroma and volatile flavors.
