3. Recipes

Spicy Mediterranean Chickpea Stew with Sumac

Ingredients

  • 250 g dried chickpeas (from the Wochenmarkt), soaked overnight
  • 1 medium yellow onion (120 g), diced (market)
  • 3 garlic cloves (15 g), minced (market)
  • 30 ml extra virgin olive oil (pantry)
  • 1 medium carrot (80 g), diced (market)
  • 1 medium red bell pepper (120 g), diced (market)
  • 400 g canned diced tomatoes (no salt, grocery store)
  • 700 ml vegetable broth (homemade or from pantry)
  • 2 tsp ground cumin (4 g, freshly ground)
  • 1 tsp ground coriander (2 g)
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika (2 g)
  • 0.5 tsp cayenne pepper (1 g), or to taste
  • 2 tsp sumac (4 g, fresh from the market’s spice stall)
  • Zest of 1 lemon (market)
  • 10 g fresh parsley (market), chopped
  • Salt & black pepper to taste

Instructions

  1. Chickpeas: The night before, I soaked the chickpeas in cold water (just part of my Friday night ritual if I’m planning a meal the next day). By Saturday afternoon, I rinsed and simmered them in fresh water for about 40 minutes, just until they were barely tender, then drained and set them aside.
  2. Vegetable Prep: I diced the onion, carrot, and bell pepper, and minced the garlic. Prepping all vegetables ahead of time helped me stay organized, especially with other dishes needing similar ingredients.
  3. Sauté: In a large pot, I warmed the olive oil over medium heat and sautéed the onion and carrot for about five minutes, then added garlic and bell pepper and cooked for an extra couple of minutes.
  4. Spices & Tomatoes: I stirred in cumin, coriander, smoked paprika, and cayenne—letting them bloom in the pan for about a minute (the smell at this stage was fantastic). After that, I added the diced tomatoes and let everything cook for another two minutes.
  5. Stew Assembly: I added the drained chickpeas and vegetable broth, stirred well, and seasoned the stew with salt, black pepper, and sumac. I let it simmer for about 20 minutes uncovered, stirring it occasionally until the stew thickened and the flavors melded.
  6. Finishing Touches: Right before serving, I stirred in the lemon zest and tasted again for seasoning. I served the stew in bowls garnished with chopped parsley and a sprinkle of extra sumac for a bright finish.

Notes on Sourcing

Nearly everything in this stew came from my Saturday morning trip to the Wochenmarkt, especially the chickpeas, produce, and parsley. I managed to find incredibly fresh sumac at a spice stall, which made a real difference in the final flavor. The olive oil and some of the spices came from my pantry.

Workflow

I started soaking the chickpeas the night before. On Saturday, while they simmered, I prepped all the vegetables for this and the other dishes. Later, as the stew was simmering, I was able to focus on preparing the roasted vegetables and flatbread dough.

Roasted Vegetables

Ingredients

  • 1 medium zucchini (200 g), thick rounds (market)
  • 1 small eggplant (180 g), large cubes (market)
  • 150 g cherry tomatoes, left whole (market)
  • 1 red onion (100 g), sliced into segments (market)
  • 1 medium red bell pepper (120 g), diced (market)
  • 30 ml extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Leaves from 3 fresh thyme sprigs (3 g, market)
  • 4 g sea salt
  • Black pepper to taste
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice (15 ml, squeezed fresh just before serving)

Method

  1. I washed and chopped all the vegetables into similar-sized pieces to ensure even roasting.
  2. In a large bowl, I tossed everything together—vegetables, olive oil, oregano, thyme, salt, and pepper—until nicely coated.
  3. I preheated the oven to 210°C (fan), then spread the veg over two lined trays in a single layer (overcrowding leads to steaming, not caramelization!).
  4. The trays roasted for about 25–30 minutes, with a quick stir at the halfway point. When the vegetables had golden edges and were tender, I transferred them to a serving bowl and squeezed fresh lemon juice over top.

Sourcing & Timing

All produce and fresh thyme were bought fresh that morning. I did the vegetable prep while the chickpeas simmered and managed oven timing so that the vegetables roasted as the stew simmered, freeing the hot oven for baking flatbread right afterward.

Fresh Flatbread

Ingredients

  • 250 g all-purpose flour (pantry/grocery)
  • 4 g dry yeast (1 tsp)
  • 160 ml warm water (~37°C)
  • 18 ml extra virgin olive oil (8 ml for dough, 10 ml for brushing)
  • 4 g fine salt
  • 1 tsp (4 g) sugar

Steps

  1. I mixed the yeast with half the warm water and the sugar, let it sit for ~5 minutes until frothy, then added the flour, remaining water, olive oil, and salt. Kneaded until smooth (about 7 minutes by hand). Covered with a towel, the dough rose in a warm spot for an hour.
  2. After rising, I divided the dough into four pieces and let them rest under a towel for about 10 minutes before rolling each into a round (roughly 15 cm across).
  3. I cranked the oven up to 240°C, placing a baking sheet inside to preheat, then quickly baked the dough rounds directly on the hot sheet for about 5–6 minutes each batch, until puffed and golden.
  4. Immediately out of the oven, I brushed each bread with a little olive oil. Keeping baked breads stacked and covered in a towel kept them soft until dinner.

Notes

The timing worked well: I mixed and proofed the dough while roasting vegetables, and baked the bread once the oven was already hot. My olive oil for brushing is a particularly nice bottle reserved just for bread.

Homemade Lemonade

Ingredients

  • 5 lemons (approx. 400 g, market), juiced (zest reserved for stew)
  • 1,200 ml chilled water
  • 80 g cane sugar (adjusted based on taste)
  • 5 g fresh mint sprigs (market), for garnish
  • Ice cubes, as needed

Method

  1. I rolled and juiced the lemons (makes them yield more!), straining out any seeds.
  2. Into a pitcher, I stirred the sugar into the lemon juice until it was fully dissolved.
  3. Added chilled water, adjusted the sweetness (keeping it on the tart side), and placed the lemonade in the fridge to chill.
  4. When serving, I poured over ice with a mint sprig in each glass.

Sourcing & Notes

The lemons and mint were particularly vibrant at the market this week. Since I made the lemonade about an hour before dinner, it was icy-cold by the time we sat down.

4. Preparation Workflow

My Saturday Routine

  • Friday night: Put chickpeas in to soak before heading to bed.
  • Saturday morning (7:45–9:00): Hit the Wochenmarkt for produce, herbs, and anything unique (sumac, mint), ensuring I got everything before the crowds.
  • 15:00 and on: Began cooking in earnest—simmered chickpeas, prepped ALL veg in one go (makes clean-up so much easier), and got herbs sorted.
  • Started the stew base and let it gently simmer so the flavors could really develop.
  • Roasted the vegetables, then mixed the flatbread dough—making the most of oven heat and limiting downtime.
  • After veggies were done, raised the oven temp and baked off flatbreads in two batches while prepping the final touches on lemonade.
  • Last stretch: checked the stew’s seasoning, set the table, and made sure everything was ready for a relaxed dinner.

Sequence & Timing Details

Here’s how I paced myself during the busy afternoon: - Chickpeas started simmering as soon as I finished my second cup of coffee. - While they simmered, I chopped all vegetables for every dish at once (I love the efficiency). - I timed the oven tasks so roasted veg went in first, followed by flatbread. - Flatbread dough got its first rise while the stew came together on the stove. - Lemonade was a quick job, and chilling it early freed me up to focus elsewhere. - By 18:15, everything was ready—the only “stress” was deciding which music to play for dinner.

5. Reflections & Guest Feedback

Tasting Notes

Chickpea Stew: The blend of cumin, coriander, and smoked paprika was so warming, with the sumac and lemon zest lifting everything. The chickpeas stayed perfectly tender, and the broth was thick, spicy, and packed with flavor.

Roasted Vegetables: Roasting brought a natural sweetness, especially with the caramelized edges of the eggplant and zucchini, while the tomatoes burst and the fresh herbs gave a light, springy finish. Lemon juice added just the right zing right before serving.

Flatbread: Honestly, this turned out better than expected—chewy centers, crisp-edged, and awesome for mopping up stew. The olive oil brushing gave it a beautiful aroma.

Lemonade: Fresh, not saccharine, with bright citrus and just a hint of mint—a proper palate cleanser.

Reflections on Process

Prepping all vegetables at once made the afternoon surprisingly calm, not chaotic. The only real trick was keeping oven time tight—staggering trays and switching from vegetables to bread quickly. The bread dough schedule fit right in overtime, so I never felt rushed. Making the lemonade earlier meant one less thing to think about just before guests arrived.

Jana & Anna’s Feedback

  • Jana was struck by the bright flavor that sumac brought to the stew. She said it tasted “uplifting” and not something she’d had before. She emailed me a recipe request that same night.
  • Anna loved the contrast in the roasted vegetables, mentioning in particular how the eggplant melted in the mouth and how the flatbread was “perfect for soaking up all that broth.” She wanted the recipes, but in PDF to organize and save (very Anna!).
  • Both went for second helpings of lemonade and joked about bottling some for summer picnics.

Adjustments & Small Wins

  • I doubled the sumac (from my test version) for a bolder citrusy pop. Both guests agreed this was the right call.
  • Cooking chickpeas separately (instead of in the stew) let me get the texture just right, avoiding mushiness.
  • Not overcrowding my roasting trays paid off—better caramelization on everything.
  • I reduced the sugar in the lemonade at the last minute because it was a warm day and everyone seemed to be craving something a bit more tart.
