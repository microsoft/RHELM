Across all three disciplines, I have seen steady gains in speed and aerobic capacity—particularly in cycling efficiency and swim technique. However, my Garmin HRV (heart rate variability) and subjective fatigue scores tend to decline after consecutive high-intensity days. This reinforces the importance of managing training load and allowing for appropriate recovery throughout the training cycle.

Rationale for Program Adjustments

Recent data suggest it’s time to shift the focus slightly. Based on established periodization models and current endurance training research [1][2][3], I am moving toward a more polarized intensity distribution. This means leaning more heavily on easy aerobic work, sharpening key intensity sessions, and emphasizing structured recovery. For running, I am planning more interval sessions that build durability. Swimming workouts will now include targeted technical drills to minimize form breakdown, especially towards the end of each session when fatigue sets in.

Reflection

Progress Toward Race Goals

Cycling:
I have moved beyond my interim race pace targets and am consistently achieving higher power outputs, particularly on longer rides. The improvement in normalized power and the reduction in heart rate drift show that aerobic efficiency continues to improve.

Swimming:
My 100m and 400m times have dropped significantly, reflecting gains in both raw speed and technical efficiency. However, I continue to notice some form degradation in the last sets of longer main sets—a reminder that my technical endurance under fatigue remains an area for development.

Running:
Interval sessions and tempo runs are progressing toward spring race objectives, although late-set fatigue still leads to a slight pace slowdown. I am finding that strength and durability are now the primary limiters, rather than speed or aerobic capacity.

Review of Key Metrics:
Core training indicators—like aerobic efficiency, sustained pace, and technical execution—have tracked steadily upward. The combination of increased volume, improved technique, and carefully structured intensity has paved the way for this progress. I am pleased to see these patterns aligning well with my stated goals for the season.

Areas for Improvement and Evidence-Based Adjustments

Session logs and detailed Garmin analytics highlight a few areas that need focused attention:

  • Running Durability:
    The fade in later intervals during speed sessions points to a gap in muscular endurance and resilience. To address this, I plan to incorporate targeted plyometric work and strength training, as supported by relevant research [1][2].

  • Swimming Technique Under Fatigue:
    Although technique is consistent early on, maintaining efficiency in the final sets is inconsistent. Dedicated technical work at the end of main sessions—and frequent short “fatigue sets”—should reinforce form in high-stress conditions [3].

  • Load and Recovery Management:
    Notable drops in heart rate variability after clusters of intense workouts call for stricter recovery protocols. This will include prioritizing sleep, nutritional support, and careful monitoring of cumulative stress to prevent non-functional overreaching.

Action Steps Moving Forward

  • Running:
    Begin a weekly plyometric routine and add form-focused strides after long runs. Regular video gait analysis will help fine-tune mechanics and detect compensatory patterns early.
  • Swimming:
    Maintain high-frequency technical sessions each week, with a specific focus on executing clean strokes during fatigue. Endurance-based technical repeats will become a key feature of my main sets.
  • Intensity Balance:
    Adhere strictly to the 80/20 polarized training split, ensuring recovery weeks are respected after each three-week block.
  • Monitoring:
    Keep leveraging Garmin and Strava analytics daily. HRV and subjective fatigue will be tracked closely, with adjustments to the plan if early signs of excess stress or overtraining emerge.
