## 4. Preparation Process

### Mise en Place

To start, I thoroughly rinsed and drained the chickpeas to remove excess brine and any lingering can taste. I washed and dried the cucumber, tomatoes, and parsley, making sure all produce was free of grit and debris. Suppliers and batches were cross-checked before use, preserving traceability.

### Vegetable Preparation

I diced the cucumber and cherry tomatoes into 1-centimeter chunks—small enough to mingle with the chickpeas, but big enough to retain crunch. I finely diced the red onion to keep its sharpness subtle, roughly chopped the parsley for freshness, and minced a clove of garlic to integrate into the dressing.

### Salad Assembly

In a large, stainless-steel bowl, I gently combined the chickpeas, cucumber, tomatoes, red onion, olives, and parsley. Folding the mixture together helped keep the beans and vegetables intact.

### Dressing

For the dressing, I whisked extra virgin olive oil, fresh lemon juice, minced garlic, sumac, sea salt, and black pepper until emulsified. The sumac’s citrus notes bring a bright tang and a hint of color to the dressing, which blends well with the lemon.

### Bringing It Together

I poured the dressing over the salad base, tossing gently to coat every ingredient without bruising the vegetables. The feta, crumbled by hand, went in last. Rather than crumbling to dust, I folded it through carefully, so there were visible pieces in every serving.

### Rest and Serve

The salad was covered and placed in the refrigerator for twenty minutes. This brief rest allowed the flavors to meld and the acidity to soften slightly. Before serving, I did a final check for seasoning and presentation, ensuring freshness and food safety by using sterilized bowls and utensils throughout.

## 5. Tasting Notes and Analysis

### Flavor Balance

The flavor profile was robust yet bright. The chickpeas and feta provided a hearty, creamy foundation, balanced by the salty bite of olives and the fresh crunch of cucumber. The lemon juice contributed a clean, zesty acidity, without overwhelming any single ingredient. The sumac, freshly increased mid-tasting (+0.5g), brought out a vivid citrus note that lingered pleasantly alongside the brightness of the vegetables. Salt levels were restrained, relying partly on the feta’s natural saltiness, and the olive oil lent richness without greasiness.

### Aromatics & Texture

Garlic provided subtle warmth, and the black pepper added just enough heat to round out the dish. The structure of the salad held up well: the chickpeas were toothsome, not mushy; vegetables delivered crunch; and the feta stayed firm, providing creamy, tangy pockets within each bite. The olives brought bursts of briny, savory depth.

### Adjustments

During the tasting, I felt there was room for even more liveliness, so I added 0.5g of sumac, and another 5mL of olive oil to address a slight dryness. No other modifications were necessary; the harmony of flavors and textures was largely achieved as planned.

### Panel Feedback

My tasting panel—a few friends and family over for lunch—appreciated the freshness, noting how the cucumber and parsley brightened the salad while olives and feta provided complexity. One recurring suggestion was to heighten the acidity next time, perhaps with a touch more lemon juice to further balance the savory elements. Comments included:
- “Very refreshing, vibrant—a perfect lunch for a sunny day.”
- “I like how the sumac comes through—it’s got that nice, tart lift.”
- “Cucumber and parsley make it taste really clean.”

Average tasting score came in at 8.5/10, with all adjustments and impressions logged for future reference.
