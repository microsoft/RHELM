## Reflections

### Plan vs. Reality

Looking back, today was frustrating in terms of following my plan. The weather forced me to abandon my bike ride, even though I’d been looking forward to it as a way to loosen up and recharge. I tried to salvage the day by stretching indoors, but even that was derailed by persistent Slack pings and work emails. I ended up quitting after only eight minutes—a far cry from my goal.

### Motivation and Distractions

The unexpected change from an outdoor ride to an indoor alternative took a toll on my motivation right away. I find that I’m often much less enthusiastic about working out indoors, especially when the change is sudden and not of my choosing. Still, I wanted to at least get something done. Unfortunately, trying to play music from my work laptop backfired. Notifications kept popping up and I felt my focus slipping further with every interruption. It was surprisingly demotivating, and I abandoned the session much earlier than planned. The only upside is my lower back felt a bit looser, so at least the short session wasn’t a total loss.

### How Tech Helped—and Didn't

Having detailed real-time weather info easily accessible made it obvious that the outdoor ride would be unpleasant at best, risky at worst. It definitely helps to rely on multiple sources—my cycling group's dashboard plus a few weather apps—to confirm severe conditions before heading out. 

On the flip side, today really highlighted a gap in my routine: lack of reliable notification management. My plan was to stream music from my work laptop during stretches, but I hadn’t set up focus or "Do Not Disturb" modes. The result: non-stop work messages, which broke my concentration and made it almost impossible to enjoy the session.

To make matters worse, monitoring my inactivity on my fitness tracker just made me feel more guilty and frustrated. It’s useful for tracking, but not when the day goes off the rails like this. On the positive side, my scheduling software did its job, sending me early weather alerts and suggesting flexibility. It's clear I need to automate more robust backup plans for days like today.

## Lessons & Next Steps

Today’s experience gave me some concrete ideas for improvement:

- **Do Not Disturb as a Ritual:** Setting devices to “Do Not Disturb” before workouts needs to become second nature—especially for indoor sessions, where work and leisure environments blur together. Using dedicated devices for music (like an old phone or tablet) might help minimize the risk of unwanted interruptions.
- **Smarter Backup Planning:** My apps do a good job of alerting me to bad weather, but I should set up automatic prompts for indoor workouts—maybe even have my tracker nudge me if it spots a missed session.
- **Track Disruption Sources:** I’ll add fields in my log for noting what interrupted me or why a session didn’t work. This should help spot repeat issues over the long term.
- **Weekly Reviews:** I want to build a habit of looking back at these logs every week. That way, I can see if certain obstacles (like weather or tech distractions) are popping up more often and figure out new ways to tackle them.

While today felt like a setback, documenting what went wrong (and why) helps me create more actionable solutions. I’m convinced that making small process changes—like securing my workout environment from digital distractions—will make it much easier to stick to my routine.
