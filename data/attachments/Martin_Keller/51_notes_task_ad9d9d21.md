## 1. Reorganizing the Kitchen: Zones and System

This afternoon, I took on a full kitchen reorganization to streamline everything according to 5S and Lean best practices. I grouped storage and activity areas based on both daily workflow and standard kitchen design—making sure that everything I use together is stored together. Here’s how I broke it down:

### A. Pantry Storage
- **Top Shelves:** Canned food, cereals, breakfast bars—the lighter and less frequently used items.
- **Middle Shelves:** All spices, baking basics (like sugars, flours), and backup bottles of essential condiments.
- **Bottom Shelves:** Large bags of rice, unopened pet food, and other bulk dry ingredients.

### B. Refrigerator and Freezer
- **Upper Fridge Shelves:** Dairy finds its home here, along with current leftovers needing visibility.
- **Lower Drawers:** All produce, plus wrapped proteins for this week’s meals.
- **Freezer Compartments:** Prepped frozen dinners, bags of fruit for smoothies, ice packs tucked in one corner.

### C. Food Prep Counters
- **Electronics Counter:** Mixer, blender, and toaster lined up and wiped down for easy access.
- **Main Prep Zone:** Chopping boards and go-to utensils (spatulas, whisks) live here, all within arm’s reach.

### D. Drawers and Cabinets
- **Utensil Drawer:** Flatware, peelers, can opener, and that one melon baller I use once a year.
- **Knife Drawer:** All sharpened and arranged by size—and out of the way of the main prep mess.
- **Baking Drawer:** Measuring cups and spoons, silicone spatulas, rolling pin, cookie cutters.
- **Pots & Pans Cabinet:** Everything nested neatly—no more pans falling loudly on my feet!
- **Cleaning Supplies Cabinet:** Under the sink but safely separated from any food storage.

### E. Sink and Adjacent Zones
- **Sink Basin:** Dishes stack in the drying rack after a quick wash; nothing left to linger.
- **Under-Sink Storage:** Dish soaps, scrubs, extra sponges, and cleaning gloves sorted and labeled.

## 3. Recipe Digitalization: Keeping Ideas at My Fingertips

Every time I stumble on a new recipe I want to try or add to my rotation, I make sure it’s accessible—no more thumbing through loose papers or random screenshots. Today I logged four new recipes into my digital database, spread across platforms I use for different needs:

| Recipe Title                | Digital Storage               | Status          |
|-----------------------------|------------------------------|-----------------|
| Sourdough Garlic Rolls      | Notion “Recipe Notebook”     | ☑ Digitalized   |
| Quick Chickpea Curry        | Google Drive “Recipes”        | ☑ Digitalized   |
| Lemon-Blueberry Oat Bars    | Paprika App                   | ☑ Digitalized   |
| Thai Peanut Noodle Salad    | Notion “Recipe Notebook”      | ☑ Digitalized   |

#### My Recipe Digitalization Process
1. Snap a picture or scan any recipe that catches my eye (magazines, family notes, blogs).
2. Upload those to the right digital tool—Notion for meal planning, Google Drive for easy sharing, Paprika for on-the-go cooking.
3. Tag by type, prep time, cuisine, or dietary needs to make searches painless.
4. Share with friends or family when they want my latest recipes, or to invite collaborative edits.

Over time, this habit has become a lifesaver—much faster than the old manila folder tucked in a kitchen drawer.
