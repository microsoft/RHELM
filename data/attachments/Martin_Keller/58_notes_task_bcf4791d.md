### In-Depth: The Mediterranean Wrap

#### What Went Inside (per wrap):

- **Whole wheat flatbread or pita**: 80g—strong enough to hold the fillings with a nutty, satisfying bite.
- **Grilled chicken breast**: 85g—lightly seasoned with salt and black pepper, grilled ahead for convenience.
- **Hummus**: 40g—creamy base for flavor and texture.
- **Tomato (30g), cucumber (25g), red onion (10g)**: Chopped fresh, adding crunch and juicy acidity.
- **Mixed greens or spinach (20g)**: For freshness and color.
- **Olive oil (5ml)**: Drizzled right before wrapping for extra richness.
- **Sumac (1g)**: Sprinkled on for that sharp, tangy brightness.
- **Aleppo pepper flakes (0.5g)**: Just enough to give subtle heat and complexity.

#### How I Put It Together

1. **Prepped the veggies:** Diced tomato, cucumber, and thinly sliced some red onion. Greens got a quick rinse and pat dry.
2. **Cooked the chicken:** Seasoned with salt and pepper, grilled on a hot pan for about 8 minutes at 180°C, then cut into thin strips once it rested.
3. **Assembly:** Warmed the flatbread in a dry pan (about a minute per side). Spread the hummus evenly, layered on the fresh veggies and mixed greens, then the sliced chicken. Topped it all with sumac and Aleppo pepper, finished with a drizzle of olive oil, and rolled everything up tightly.
4. **Serving:** Sometimes I eat the wrap immediately, but today I wrapped it in foil so I could settle in at my coworking desk without worrying about it drying out.

#### Ingredient Focus: Sumac & Aleppo Pepper

- **Sumac**: This tangy, red powder gives every bite a zesty, citrus-like kick—I genuinely love how it lifts the flavor, especially with rich hummus and grilled chicken. It's high in polyphenols, especially anthocyanins, and delivers both antioxidants and a subtle anti-inflammatory punch. It’s also said to help balance blood sugar and promote gentle digestion, which I definitely appreciate during a workday ([[1]](#sources),[[2]](#sources)).
  
- **Aleppo Pepper**: I’m careful with the amount; it adds gentle, fruity warmth and a pop of color without overwhelming heat. Besides flavor, I know it’s packed with capsaicin, vitamins A, C, and E, and a host of flavonoids and carotenoids—just enough to give the wrap depth and keep things interesting without stomach upset ([[3]](#sources),[[4]](#sources)).
  
When possible, I sprinkle sumac after assembling the wrap (since heat dulls its aroma), but Aleppo pepper can handle a little grilling if I want it infused into the chicken itself.

## 5. Lessons Learned & Practical Tips for Future Coworking Lunches

Reflecting on today’s lunch, a few practical habits help me make coworking lunches both enjoyable and efficient:

- **Prep in Advance:** Grilling chicken and chopping veggies the night before saves time midday, especially when kitchen space is limited or I’m on a programming sprint.
- **Spice Sourcing:** I’ve started buying sumac and Aleppo pepper in bulk at the specialty shop down the street—the quality difference is noticeable, and it’s more affordable.
- **Tools of the Trade:** Keeping a compact food scale or a set of measuring spoons in my coworking tote helps me portion accurately without guessing.
- **Meal Timing:** Sometimes, if I know I’ll have a long day, I’ll double up and prep an extra wrap or add a hard-boiled egg. Staggered meals seem to match the pacing of my work flow—short sprints, then a quick snack, then back to coding.
- **Mindful Breaks:** I treat lunch assembly as a mini break. Stepping away from the screen for even five minutes to build my wrap does wonders for mental reset and focus.
- **Mixing It Up:** I like to swap chicken for grilled halloumi or falafel now and then and rotate different types of greens (arugula, kale, even pea shoots) for variety and extra micronutrients.
- **Hydration Rituals:** I make a habit of filling my water bottle first thing in the morning. I’m thinking about adding a thermos with herbal tea, especially as the weather cools.
