Technical Evaluation Report: GPRS Modules for Industrial Sensor Systems (Europe, 2024)

Executive Summary

This report delivers a comprehensive technical evaluation and comparative analysis of GPRS modules suitable for industrial sensor systems across European markets as of early 2024. The assessment addresses the specific requirements of industrial applications—such as data throughput, environmental durability, regulatory standards, and supply chain reliability. Three top GPRS modules are compared using a detailed attribute matrix, culminating in a weighted decision analysis and clear recommendations to support robust and future-ready sensor system integration. The report further provides expert risk mitigation strategies and practical guidance for engineering documentation and compliance.

1. Introduction

Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) technologies are reshaping a range of sectors—including energy, utilities, transport, manufacturing, and environmental monitoring—by enabling pervasive, reliable sensor connectivity. While 2G networks in Europe are gradually being phased out, GPRS still serves as a practical backbone for many machine-to-machine (M2M) and telemetry use cases due to its wide coverage and low cost. As such, careful selection of a GPRS module remains critical for ensuring the longevity, reliability, compliance, and cost-effectiveness of sensor systems operating in challenging industrial environments.

2. Application Context and Communication Requirements

GPRS modules are commonly at the heart of industrial sensor solutions deployed in remote or complex settings where wired alternatives are either unfeasible or prohibitively expensive. Typical application scenarios include:

  • Monitoring of remote utilities and pipelines
  • Asset tracking for fleets and industrial machinery
  • Smart city systems like waste management and environmental monitoring
  • Agricultural and ecological data collection

Across these domains, several key requirements consistently shape the choice of communication modules:

Data Throughput

Even in modern projects, most industrial sensor applications demand relatively modest bandwidth, with typical transmissions averaging under 10 kB per message. GPRS’s support for up to 85.6 kbps downlink and 42.8 kbps uplink is ample for standard IIoT needs, enabling periodic telemetry, control commands, and simple firmware updates.

Environmental Robustness

Industrial deployments often expose hardware to extreme temperatures (minimum -40°C to maximum +85°C), vibration, dust, humidity, and potential water ingress. To ensure system reliability, selected modules must at least match the resilience offered by the intended enclosure (referencing IEC 60529/IP and IEC 60068 standards).

Power Efficiency

With many sites powered by batteries or solar panels, low idle currents—preferably below 1 mA—alongside efficient operation and fast sleep/wake transitions are essential to maximize system autonomy and minimize maintenance requirements.

Operational Longevity

Sensor deployments frequently run for 5–10 years or more. Module lifecycles and commitments from suppliers need to match these timelines to prevent costly redesigns and maintain continuity, including guarantees on product availability and end-of-life (EOL) notifications.

Regulatory Compliance

All GPRS modules sold in Europe must adhere to CE marking and the Radio Equipment Directive (RED 2014/53/EU). Compliance with RoHS and REACH regulations is equally non-negotiable, both for legal sale and for responsible environmental stewardship.

Space and Interface Constraints

Industrial sensors usually demand compact components (commonly SMD, LGA, or mini PCIe form factors). Robust electrical interfaces—such as UART, SPI, I2C, or USB—and clear voltage compatibility are necessary for seamless PCB-level integration.

Protocol Support

Complete AT command support, embedded TCP/IP stacks, and SMS fallback ensures both flexibility in integration and robustness in communication. With these capabilities, even in areas of inconsistent data coverage, sensors can reliably transmit critical information [1][2][3].

3. Systematic Evaluation Criteria

To thoroughly assess candidate GPRS modules, we followed a set of well-defined technical and commercial evaluation parameters:

  • Electrical Interfacing: Range and robustness of interface options and voltage levels, including ESD protection
  • Communication Protocols Supported: Breadth and flexibility of AT command sets, embedded protocol stacks (TCP/IP, SMS)
  • Operating Temperature Range: Guaranteed temperature performance according to industrial standards
  • Physical Characteristics: Module size, mounting options, and footprint compatibility for varied designs
  • Certification: Full regulatory compliance (CE, RED, RoHS, REACH) and any operator-specific approvals
  • Environmental Ratings: Validation of resistance to shock, vibration, humidity, and dust according to IEC/EN standards
  • Power Requirements: Typical supply voltage, current draw during transmit/receive, and ultra-low idle/sleep operation
  • Supplier Track Record & Lifecycle: Manufacturer reputation, lifecycle commitments, and quality of support, including EOL notifications
  • Firmware Update Capabilities: Both local (UART) and remote (FOTA) update mechanisms, with emphasis on security
  • Integration Support: Quality of documentation, reference designs, ecosystem support, and available development tools
  • Cost/Performance: Competitive unit pricing, ease of integration, and scalability considerations for mass deployment

Detailed Product Analysis

Quectel M66

The M66 stands out for its combination of compact form factor, robust environmental resilience, and extremely low power consumption, making it particularly suitable for battery-operated or space-constrained smart sensor deployments. The module holds all critical European certifications and comes equipped with both local and FOTA firmware update options, ensuring future maintainability and security. Quectel’s detailed documentation and solid commitment to long-term product support provide additional assurance for design engineers.

Thales/Cinterion BGS2

BGS2’s credentials are well-supported by a history of deployment across critical infrastructure in Europe. Its proven security features and broad operator acceptance appeal to organizations prioritizing reliability and regulatory alignment above all. Although slightly larger and more expensive, the BGS2 remains a strong candidate where ruggedness, traceability, and supply continuity are paramount.

SIMCom SIM800C

The SIM800C provides a cost-effective and compact alternative, with good global availability and broad developer support. It is highly attractive for projects designed for less severe operating environments or where project budgets are a primary constraint. That said, its lifecycle commitments tend to be shorter and its historical advance notice ahead of product discontinuation has not been as comprehensive as with the other suppliers, which may pose risks for long-term, maintenance-heavy industrial deployments.

Recommendation Rationale

Drawing from the weighted analysis and technical review, the Quectel M66 is the optimal choice for new industrial sensor deployments in Europe in 2024. It delivers an ideal mix of industrial durability, efficient size and power profile, full certification, and up-to-date firmware management—all backed by a robust supply chain and long-term manufacturer support. The Thales BGS2 remains a preferred alternative for critical infrastructure where maximum operator approvals and established supplier relationships take precedence, despite its greater size and cost. While SIMCom’s SIM800C excels in affordability and ease of integration, its drawbacks in lifecycle assurance and environmental resilience should be carefully considered in context of the project’s expected operating conditions and maintenance horizon.

6. Risk Analysis and Mitigation

Industrial projects face various risks when adopting GPRS connectivity in 2024. Awareness and planning are essential to secure system longevity and avoid costly redesigns.

Key Risks and Mitigation Strategies

  • 2G Network Sunsetting: Although 2G/GPRS networks remain widely operational in Europe, ongoing shutdowns are expected over the next 5–7 years.
    Mitigation: Select modules and suppliers offering clear migration paths to emerging technologies such as LTE Cat 1 and NB-IoT. Design system PCBs with modular, adaptable communication mezzanines or standardized interfaces to futureproof hardware.

  • Supply Chain Disruptions: Industry-wide semiconductor shortages and unpredictable EOL notifications can disrupt manufacturing and service continuity.
    Mitigation: Choose suppliers with strong lifecycle policies, documented EOL processes, and maintain approved alternative or second-source modules. Secure long-term supply agreements wherever feasible.

  • Evolving Compliance Requirements: Regulatory or operator standards may change over a module’s deployed lifespan, impacting product legality or operability.
    Mitigation: Favor modules with comprehensive current certifications and maintain regular dialogue with suppliers for proactive compliance and certification updates.

  • Firmware Vulnerabilities and Security: Increased device connectivity broadens the threat surface for operational and security risks.
    Mitigation: Require FOTA (Firmware Over-the-Air) update functionality to enable prompt patching of discovered vulnerabilities. Work with partners that provide timely threat notifications and structured firmware update processes.

7. Recommendations for Sensor System Design and Integration

To ensure reliable, compliant, and future-ready industrial sensor deployments in 2024 and beyond, the following practical guidance is advised:

  • Module Selection: The Quectel M66 is the recommended GPRS module for most European industrial sensor applications, balancing technical performance with supply continuity and cost.
  • Hardware and Enclosure Design:
  • Validate environmental protection at both module and system levels, conducting IP/EMC/mechanical testing in accordance with deployment needs.
  • Apply proven PCB layout techniques for electromagnetic compatibility and power delivery, particularly shielding and solid grounding strategies.
  • Firmware and Integration:
  • Integrate microcontroller firmware using the module’s full set of AT commands, preferably utilizing reference stacks and SDKs from the manufacturer to reduce development risk.
  • Maintain clear documentation of module firmware versions and establish robust update pipelines for remote management and patching.
  • Life-cycle and Compliance Management:
  • Set up structured processes for keeping module certifications, datasheets, and integration documentation up to date and easily accessible for audits or regulatory inspections.
  • Monitor regional 2G/GPRS network availability and prepare transition strategies as technology shifts occur—reviewing PCB design flexibility and supply chain readiness regularly.

By adhering to these strategies and focusing on supplier partnerships, sensor designers can mitigate future risks, streamline regulatory compliance, and extend the useful lifespan of their monitoring and telemetry infrastructure.
