## 5. Coach’s Feedback (Anna)

Anna’s primary focus for this session was to help me refine my breathing technique during freestyle, as inconsistencies have tended to creep in when I’m under pressure or fatigued.

Key recommendations included:
- Emphasizing a long, smooth exhale underwater, completely releasing air before turning to inhale. This not only helps with oxygen exchange but also keeps the body relaxed and buoyant.
- Rotating my head more decisively for each breath, while keeping my neck and upper back loose to reduce resistance.
- Ensuring true bilateral breathing—using symmetrical body rotation and incorporating hip and torso movement to initiate the breath, not simply turning through the neck.
- Practicing the 3-3-5 breathing pattern (three strokes per breath twice, then five strokes) as a way to build consistency, capacity, and comfort under moderate exertion.
- Being proactive in checking shoulder and upper back tension as I swim, particularly just before breathing, and deliberately relaxing these areas if I notice tightness.

## 6. Personal Reflections

Today's session felt productive overall, and I came away encouraged by a few tangible improvements.

- **Progress:**  
  I managed to complete the full 1,200 meters without any noticeable drop in form, apart from some minor fatigue in the final 200 meters. My bilateral breathing was more consistent than in previous sessions—fewer skipped breaths, and transitions between sets were smoother. Tracking my stroke count in the endurance set, I noticed I was down by one stroke per length compared to last week, which signals incremental efficiency gains.

- **Challenges:**  
  There were a couple of moments in the busier parts of the lane when I had to speed up my breathing or cut my exhale short in order to overtake swimmers, and I could feel minor technique breakdown creeping in during these exchanges. I also experienced a dip in energy towards the end of the final 200m set, suggesting my aerobic base for continuous distance still needs work. Lane drift persists on backstroke, especially when my left hand enters too far over centerline.

- **Next Focus Areas:**  
  Moving forward, I want to make my exhale near-automatic and relaxed even under pressure, and keep my inhale crisp and full—working to prevent rushed or shallow breaths. Anna’s tip about initiating the breath with a whole-body roll (not just the head/neck) helped, and I plan to prioritize that in drills. I’ll add specific work on backstroke entry symmetry and target another reduction in stroke count per length. Regular self-checks for shoulder tension are now on my checklist, especially as this seems to build up late in sets.
