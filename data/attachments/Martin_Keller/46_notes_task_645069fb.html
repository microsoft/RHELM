Mediterranean-Style Grain Salad with Frau Bauer’s Sumac Blend

Introduction

Today’s lunch was all about fresh Mediterranean flavors, built around a grain salad that’s become a staple in my repertoire. The recipe features a blend of tender grains, crisp vegetables, briny accents, and plenty of bright herbs. Farm-fresh ingredients and a generous hit of Frau Bauer’s Sumac Blend make this an ideal midday meal—light, vibrant, and deeply satisfying. Preparing this dish was also an opportunity to set aside a portion for Jana, as requested, ensuring consistency in flavor and presentation.

Ingredients

The following were measured out for about six generous servings:

  • Grain base: 200 g cooked bulgur or couscous
  • Cherry tomatoes: 200 g, halved
  • Cucumber: 1 medium (approx. 150 g), diced (I left the skin on for crunch)
  • Red onion: 1 small (about 50 g), finely diced
  • Flat-leaf parsley: 20 g, roughly chopped
  • Fresh mint: 10 g, roughly chopped
  • Feta cheese: 100 g, crumbled
  • Kalamata olives: 70 g, pitted and sliced
  • Lemon (juiced): 1 large (about 40 ml)
  • Extra-virgin olive oil: 30 ml
  • Frau Bauer’s Sumac Blend: 1.5 tsp (7 g)
  • Salt: 1/2 tsp (3 g)
  • Black pepper: 1/4 tsp (1 g)

Where possible, I aim for fresh, locally sourced produce. The sumac blend is from Frau Bauer's, as specified in our kitchen standards.

Step-by-Step Preparation

  1. Cooking the Grain Base
    I started by cooking bulgur according to package instructions, though couscous is also a good option here. Once done, I spread the grains out to cool—they need to be about room temperature so the herbs and vegetables stay crisp when combined.

  2. Preparing the Vegetables and Herbs
    While the grains cooled, I halved the cherry tomatoes, diced the cucumber, and finely chopped the red onion. I roughly chopped both parsley and mint, making sure not to bruise the leaves—the aroma from the herbs hit as soon as they were cut, promising a lot of flavor.

  3. Assembling the Salad Base
    In a large mixing bowl, I brought together the cooled grains, all the chopped vegetables, and the fresh herbs. At this stage, the colors alone were cheering.

  4. Adding Feta and Olives
    I crumbled the feta cheese and made sure the Kalamata olives were pitted and sliced before folding them into the salad. Both provide a wonderful salty punch—the cheese creamy, the olives sharp.

  5. Dressings and Seasonings
    For the dressing, I squeezed in the juice of a large lemon and drizzled over extra-virgin olive oil, adjusting each by taste. Salt and freshly ground black pepper rounded out the seasoning.

  6. Finishing Touch: Frau Bauer’s Sumac Blend
    The final flourish is a generous sprinkle of Frau Bauer’s Sumac Blend. I tossed everything again to make sure the sumac was well distributed—the sharp, citrussy aroma is unmistakable.

  7. Portion Reserved for Jana
    As requested, I set aside about a sixth of the salad immediately after blending in the sumac, placing it in a labeled container for Jana. This makes sure she gets the same balance of ingredients and seasoning as the main serving.

  8. Resting and Serving
    Covered the salad and left it to rest for 15 minutes at room temperature. This short pause lets the grains soak up the dressing and the flavors really come together.

Clean-up was done according to our kitchen protocols—wiping down and sanitizing all work surfaces and utensils to keep everything safe and ready for the next meal.

Tasting Notes & Technical Reflections

As soon as this salad comes together, there’s a burst of color—deep reds from the tomatoes, bright greens from herbs, creamy feta, and the glossy black of olives. The aroma is fresh and lively, punctuated by the sumac’s tart edge.

The first bite delivers on that promise—each forkful feels distinct yet harmonious. The sumac, paired with fresh lemon, adds a bright tang that lifts the grains and vegetables, while the feta and olives bring everything into balance with their richness and saltiness.

For Jana’s portion, reserving some immediately after tossing in the sumac ensured she’ll get a salad that’s just as vibrant and well-seasoned as the rest.

Reflecting on the process, there are a few tweaks I’m considering for future versions: - If you crave even more tartness, a bit more sumac (up to 2 tsp) would take it there. - The red onion can overwhelm if left raw—soaking it in cold water for about 10 minutes tones down its bite without losing crunch. - For prepping ahead or serving at a buffet, I’d keep the grains and veggies separate, tossing with herbs and dressing just before serving for best texture. - Swapping in farro or quinoa could add more fiber and protein, making the dish heartier. - Documenting the specific brands of feta and olives used will help keep the salt and flavor profile consistent from batch to batch.

Conclusion

This Mediterranean-style grain salad is a dependable lunch option—simple to pull together, endlessly adaptable, and deeply flavorful thanks to Frau Bauer’s Sumac Blend. Preparing and sharing it is always a satisfying ritual, especially when the meal gets to be enjoyed by someone else, like Jana. With a handful of easy adjustments, I’ll keep refining this favorite for new occasions throughout the season.
