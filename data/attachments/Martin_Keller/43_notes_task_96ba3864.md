# Digital Recipe Log Entry  
## May 1, 2024 — Mediterranean Dinner (Postponed)

---

### 1. Date and Meal Type

- **Date:** May 1, 2024  
- **Meal Type:** Mediterranean Dinner

---

### 2. Planned Recipe and Inspiration

I had ambitiously planned to make Roasted Chicken with Sumac and Fresh Herbs tonight—a Mediterranean classic that’s always been a favorite of mine. I drew inspiration from a combination of trusted sources, including BBC Good Food, NYT Cooking, and Serious Eats. The vision was a golden roast chicken, aromatic with sumac and layered with fresh parsley, mint, and dill, all brightened by lemon and olive oil. Unfortunately, despite my efforts, I wasn’t able to pull up a specific recipe link during planning, but the dish structure is pretty standard in Mediterranean cooking and I've made similar recipes many times before.

---

As soon as I realized I was out of sumac and fresh herbs, my heart sank a little. Those flavors are essential for this recipe, giving it the freshness and deeply savory tang that set it apart. With the Wochenmarkt closed for the public holiday and other vendors out of reach, I quickly had to re-evaluate my dinner plans.

---

### 4. Preparation & Change of Plans

Around 12:30 pm, I was ready to start some early prep, but the missing key ingredients stopped me in my tracks. It was frustrating knowing I couldn’t get the fresh herbs or sumac, especially after looking forward to this meal. The local Wochenmarkt (my go-to for high-quality herbs and spices) was closed, something I hadn’t accounted for with the holiday schedule. With no realistic way to source the missing items in time, I ultimately decided to postpone the Mediterranean dinner. At 12:45, I logged the change in my digital meal planner, and carefully returned everything perishable to the fridge.

I do take planning seriously, so it stung a bit to call it off, but better to pause than attempt a watered-down version that wouldn't be true to the dish or my own standards. No food was wasted, no prep work started, and I left the kitchen organized for when I’m able to try again.

---

### 5. What I Did Instead

Not one to go hungry—or waste leftovers—I pivoted. I reheated the Vegetable Lentil Stew I’d made a couple of nights ago (on April 29) as a base. To get a bit more protein and salvage my Mediterranean cravings, I cubed some pre-cooked chicken breast from the fridge and stirred it in. I also tossed a frozen baguette in the oven for 8 minutes at 200°C, which came out perfectly crisp. For a bit of brightness, I grated some lemon zest on top, since I was sorely missing fresh herbs. The whole thing came together in fifteen minutes—simple, comforting, and satisfying, even if it wasn’t my original plan.

I made sure to record all these substitutions and changes in my meal-tracking sheet, tagging the original meal as postponed and noting the reason.

---

### 6. Reflection — What Worked and What I’ll Do Differently

Looking back, catching the missing ingredients ahead of time saved me a lot of hassle. There’s nothing worse than getting halfway into a recipe and realizing you’re out of essentials, so my system of pre-cooking inventory checks paid off. I’m glad I had flexible leftovers and pantry basics to fall back on, which meant I didn’t feel tempted to order out or let anything go to waste.

That said, this experience made it clear I need to pay more attention to market schedules—especially around holidays, when closures can throw plans off entirely. I’ll be adding a reminder in my calendar to check for public holiday closures and syncing the Wochenmarkt schedule with my meal-planning app when possible. For Mediterranean recipes like this, I also want to keep a backup of shelf-stable or potted herbs, and stock extra sumac or za’atar when I see them at the market.

While having to postpone was disappointing, it was a good reminder to double-check my non-pantry ingredients as soon as I pick a recipe—not the day I plan to cook. This little hiccup is a nudge to automate some parts of my process, like ingredient monitoring and calendar syncing, which will make future meal planning smoother. I’ve now documented the reason for postponement in my meal tracker, so I can look out for patterns when planning future menus—especially as the seasons and vendor schedules shift.
