## Overview

This documentation template lays out a comprehensive system for recording each step of my food market visits, from pre-planning and in-market product selection to detailed post-purchase analysis. The structure prioritizes technical detail and data consistency, ensuring that every bit of information I collect can be exported directly to spreadsheet tools for later review. My goal is to streamline acquisition planning, document purchases and vendor experiences, analyze products thoroughly, and improve the process iteratively with clear, actionable follow-ups.

---

## Pre-Market Shopping Checklist

Thoughtful, organized preparation is key to an efficient market visit. I use a pre-market shopping checklist, grouped by clearly defined categories like Produce, Cheese, or Specialty Items, to ensure I focus my energy where it matters most and never miss any critical acquisition.

### How I Organize the Checklist

- **Categories:** I assign each item to a customizable category (e.g., “Bread & Baked Goods,” “Fermented Products,” “Herbs & Spices”).
- **Tracking:** Each item gets a checkbox for real-time tracking on market day (☐ or ☑).
- **Notes Section:** For every item, I have a space to jot down the preferred vendor (if I’ve found a reliable source before), brand suggestions, or special batch notes.
- **Flexible Entries:** I always leave a few blank lines for unexpected finds—markets can be full of surprises.

### Best Practices I Rely On

- I always fill out the checklist before heading to the market, referencing my notes from previous visits and any tips from trusted vendors.
- When an item consistently impresses me, I note the vendor or brand so I can return to them directly.
- I use clear, descriptive item names to speed up decision-making on a crowded day.

## Purchase Log – Structured Table

Maintaining a reliable, detailed purchase log is at the heart of my post-market analysis process. Each entry translates directly into a structured spreadsheet, allowing me to track products for quality, budget, and vendor performance over time.

### Table Fields I Use

- **Timestamp:** Documenting the moment of purchase helps me recreate the day and spot timing patterns.
- **Category:** Organizing purchases by type for future aggregation and comparison.
- **Item Name:** Detailed enough that I can always recall exactly what I bought, even weeks later.
- **Quantity (with unit):** Standard units like grams, liters, or individual counts.
- **Price per Unit:** Logged in Euro (or the relevant currency); useful for tracking inflation or bargain finds.
- **Total Price:** Essential for budget and expenditure updates.
- **Vendor Details:** Stall number, vendor’s name, and any notable location markers help me pinpoint the source.
- **Notes (Batch Codes, Variants, Initial Impressions):** Space for qualitative notes, batch codes, or immediate observations.

### How I Log Purchases

- I fill out each section right after a purchase—details fade quickly once I move on.
- I always double-check vendor details, especially if similar products are available from multiple stalls.
- Currency and unit consistency is non-negotiable, making spreadsheet analysis viable later.

### Export-Ready Formatting

- Every purchase is a new row, with no merged or multi-entry cells.
- Field names are short and standardized for seamless spreadsheet import.
- Data entry conventions stay consistent across visits.

## Tasting Notes & Vendor Recommendations

A systematic review of purchased items is both enjoyable and essential for improving my market strategies. I break down tasting notes by vendor and by product, supporting comparison and documentation of sensory and performance experiences.

### My Organization Method

Each product or vendor gets its own detailed entry covering:

- **Aromatic & Sensory Profile:** I assess nose, flavor, mouthfeel, and visual cues in clear, technical language.
- **Culinary Recommendations:** Vendor suggestions are always noted, along with my own thoughts on best uses.
- **Comparative Analysis:** I benchmark against previous purchases, highlighting any shifts in quality or character.
- **Vendor Recommendations:** Storage, usage, and handling tips from vendors.
- **Additional Notes:** Includes details like product origin, seasonality, or special insights gathered during conversation.

### Sample Tasting Notes

**Sumac (Vendor: Eastern Spices, Stall 17)**
- *Aromatic Profile:* Strong, zesty citrus aroma; underlying fermented notes that create depth.
- *Culinary Use:* Vendor suggested using it as a final flourish on grilled eggplant or whisking it into labneh for extra brightness.
- *Comparative Notes:* Noticeably more potent than last week’s batch from Stall 8—vivid color and tangier finish.
- *Storage Tips:* Keep tightly sealed, away from humidity or direct light.

**Halloumi (Vendor: Frau Bauer, Stall 4)**
- *Sensory Notes:* Dense, resilient texture with clear sheep’s milk character, less brined than other options.
- *Culinary Recommendation:* Frau Bauer enthusiastically recommends thick slices on the grill, paired with watermelon and finished with mint—a bright, refreshing combination.
- *Comparative Analysis:* Smoother texture and better moisture retention than the previous batch. Batch #774, June 2024.

### Best Practices for Analysis

- I always write my tasting notes immediately after the sampling session, while impression details are sharp.
- Vendor advice is recorded verbatim and attributed precisely.
- I reference earlier logs for each product or batch to track changes over time.

## Summary and Actionable Follow-Ups

After each market day, I pull together my main findings and define clear steps for next time. This keeps my process dynamic and ensures continuous improvement.

### Key Takeaways

- **Product Quality & Trends:** I track which products are peaking, seasonal patterns, and standout vendors. For example, this summer’s halloumi batches have shown remarkable improvement in moisture and texture.
- **Challenges:** I document inventory gaps (e.g. “Sumac sold out at stall 17”), unexpected pricing shifts, or logistical hurdles like market layout changes that disrupt my usual route.
- **Patterns & Preferences:** Positive findings and consistent quality inform my evolving vendor map and shopping focus.

## Export & Analysis Guidelines

To maximize ongoing learning and operational consistency, I keep all records compatible with spreadsheet-based analysis. Key measures include:

- Maintaining one-row-per-item data structure across all logs and checklists.
- Consistent formatting for fields that enable future trend spotting for vendors and products.
- Promptly reviewing recent market data against previous records to close feedback loops on product quality, pricing, and vendor reliability.
- Using structured fields, even within “notes,” to support smart filtering and future-proof the logs.
