# Comprehensive Post-Tour Bike Repair Checklist for the Canyon Endurace  
**After the Black Forest Tour 2024**

---

## Overview

Riding through challenging wet and gritty conditions, such as those experienced on the Black Forest cycling tour, places significant demands on your Canyon Endurace. Thorough, systematic post-ride maintenance is essential not only to ensure safety but also to preserve the bike’s performance and extend the lifespan of its components. This checklist offers a step-by-step guide, drawing on Canyon, Shimano, and SRAM guidelines, supported by established industry best practices. Every section details recommended tools, lubricants, wear indicators, and useful references for diagrams and technical documentation.

This resource is organized for both technical thoroughness and practical ease-of-use after a demanding multi-day tour.

## Stepwise Procedural Guidance

Below, each core maintenance step is expanded to offer thorough practical guidance for mechanics and experienced riders:

### **1. Drivetrain Cleaning and Inspection**

Begin by removing both wheels to allow access to all transmission components. Liberally apply a biodegradable degreaser to the chain, cassette, chainrings, and both derailleurs. Use a dedicated chain cleaner where available, along with stiff brushes to dislodge grit typical of wet forest conditions. Rinse carefully with low-pressure water; avoid spraying directly at bearings and sealed components. After drying all parts thoroughly, assess the chain using a chain checker—replace if elongation exceeds 0.5-0.75% per Shimano/SRAM guidelines.

Carefully scrutinize cassette and chainrings under good light, looking for excessively worn, ‘hooked,’ or missing teeth, and document specific findings using diagram references. Any grinding or resistant feeling while rotating the cranks can indicate intrusive grit or internal wear.

### **2. Front and Rear Derailleur Adjustment and Service**

Shift through the entire gear range to rapidly identify issues such as hesitancy, excessive noise, or missed shifts. Adjust cable tension using the barrel adjuster either in-line or at the shifter, referencing manufacturer diagrams for correct alignment. The front derailleur cage should sit parallel to the chainring and maintain a 1–3mm gap above the teeth.

Fine-tune high and low limit screws, being careful to prevent overshifting or chain drops. If persistent shifting issues remain, prioritize replacing cabling and housing, especially after gritty, wet rides. Use the appropriate diagrams to ensure cables are correctly routed and free of obstructions.

### **3. Lubrication of Moving Parts and Bearings**

Post-cleaning, apply wet-style lubricant to the chain, derailleur pivots, brake levers, and pedal cleat springs—these are especially vulnerable to post-tour grime. Wipe excess lube carefully to minimize the attraction of dust and grit. Components such as the bottom bracket, hubs, and headset should be packed with waterproof, high-pressure bearing grease following cleaning or as indicated by inspection. Always take care to keep lubricant away from braking surfaces and rims.

### **4. Inspection for Water and Grit Ingress**

Remove the crankset, per Canyon’s protocol (bearing type-specific), to reveal and clean the bottom bracket shell. Spin the BB by hand—any roughness or noise warrants cleaning, regreasing, or bearing replacement. Similarly, remove wheels and check both front and rear hubs for smooth rotation and excessive play. Open up hub assemblies where necessary to inspect and service the bearings, replacing or repacking as needed. Attention to these steps after wet, muddy rides is particularly important to prevent long-term damage and corrosion.

### **5. Brake System Assessment**

Dismount the wheels to gain full access to pads and discs. Replace disc brake pads that are worn below 1mm; examine each pad and rotor for glazing, scoring, or embedded debris. Clean all braking surfaces with isopropyl alcohol and carefully check rotor thickness and trueness. Confirm pad and rotor bolts are torqued to manufacturer specifications.

### **6. Bolt Torque and Fastener Security**

Systematically check and torque all critical fasteners: stem, handlebar, seatpost, crank arms, derailleurs, brake calipers, rotors, and thru-axles, referencing the specific torque values from Canyon and component manufacturers. Correct any bolts that are over or under-tightened, and log any discrepancies in your maintenance notes.

### **7. Tires, Wheels, and Structural Examination**

Carefully inspect each tire for cuts, abrasions, glass, or debris embedded in the tread and sidewalls. Check tire wear by assessing tread depth and looking for flat spots or bulges. Remove and replace any rim or tubeless tape showing leaks, and inspect spoke tension by squeezing pairs of spokes along the wheel. Spin each wheel to ensure it runs true; correct any wobbles where practical. Inflate tires to the recommended pressure and confirm sealing where appropriate.

Further, test the cockpit for any looseness or play—lean on the bars with both wheels locked, ensure all bolts are correctly torqued, and closely inspect the handlebar and stem for visual cracks or fatigue, especially on carbon components. Tighten or replace bar tape if found saturated from sustained exposure to wet conditions.

### **8. Final Comprehensive Safety and Function Test**

After reassembling the bike, perform a controlled test ride. Shift through all gears, brake forcefully with both front and rear, and slalom gently to stress test the entire assembly. Listen for abnormal noises and check for any looseness, creaks, or inconsistent shifting that may have developed during the tour or reassembly. Record all findings and address any issues immediately to ensure the bike is fully ready for its next adventure.

## Notes and Documentation

Use this section to keep a record of key observations, unexpected issues, or developing wear patterns. Consistent documentation helps track recurring problems or signs of fatigue across rides, and supports early intervention. For instance:

- "Cassette teeth on 3rd and 4th cogs exhibit moderate shark-tooth wear after 8 days of rain."
- "Rear derailleur hanger was visibly out of alignment; required realignment with Park Tool DAG-2."
- "Bottom bracket felt gritty, but spun smoothly after regreasing; minor water ingress observed."
- "Front disc pads measured at 1 mm—replacement scheduled before next event."
