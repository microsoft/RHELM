Daily Exercise Log Entry: Core Strength Training Session

Date: July 3, 2024

Session Overview

Today's workout focused on core strength with the intent to follow my standard circuit. The plan included both isometric and dynamic core exercises, estimated to last about 34 minutes. After a full warm-up, I progressed into three main sets, integrating fundamental movements aimed at trunk stability and overall core endurance. While the session started as planned, I encountered an unexpected issue during the final portion, requiring an early termination.

Session Breakdown

  • Warm-up: 6 minutes (dynamic stretching, cat-cow, standing trunk rotations)
  • Main workout: 25 minutes (core exercise circuit)
  • Early session termination: Occurred during the second plank set

Exercises Performed

27 sec (set 2, terminated)		 Started strong in the first set, maintaining a straight gradient from head to heel and bracing abdominally. Second set was cut short due to an acute symptom, detailed below.

Performance Metrics (from wearable sensors): - Mean heart rate: 108 bpm throughout the session (indicative of moderate effort) - Range of motion: Remained consistent on all repetitions until the last plank, which shows abrupt cessation on tracking data

Reason for Early Termination

During the second set of planks, approximately 27 seconds in, I experienced a sharp, sudden pain localized in the left lower quadrant of my abdomen—roughly 3 cm medial to the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS). The sensation was acute and clearly defined to one spot, without any radiating pain or additional symptoms such as dizziness, nausea, or general weakness. I did not notice any audible pop or palpable shift, but immediately felt a loss of isometric tension on the left side, accompanied by a subtle but involuntary elevation of my pelvis—later confirmed in the movement tracking data.

Upon reflection, I recall that I was starting to feel some cumulative fatigue before this set, and my pelvis may have been slightly anteriorly tilted, pointing to weakened core engagement at the time of the incident. Based on the suddenness and localization of the pain, I suspect an acute strain of a muscle fiber in the rectus abdominis or perhaps minor nerve irritation. Out of caution and adhering closely to established safety guidance, I ended the session on the spot.

Steps Taken

Immediately after the incident, I stopped all core loading activities, following both the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) guidelines and advice from NHS resources. I refrained from pushing through or attempting further exercises—a core principle emphasized by Dr. Petra Lang, PT, DPT, whose protocol I keep on hand for such situations.

Professional Guidance and Recovery Actions

  • Immediate cessation of all core-intensive and load-bearing activity when sharp, localized abdominal pain occurs, with special care during planks and extended isometrics.
  • Dr. Petra Lang’s recommendations reinforce stopping activity at the onset of acute symptoms and monitoring for any pain persisting beyond 24 hours; medical Consultation is indicated if discomfort does not resolve or worsens.
  • Post-session: I spent five minutes on gentle stretching (mainly supine abdominal release and gentle trunk rotation) and intentionally avoided any further resistance work for the rest of the day.
  • All wearable and exercise log data have been flagged in my ongoing records, and I have set a reminder to review for lingering symptoms. A follow-up with a physical therapist is a standing plan if needed.

Deviations from Standard Protocol

Due to the early stop, I did not complete my planned Russian Twists, Lying Leg Raises, or the scheduled cool-down. Hold times and set volumes for the planks and side planks were also less than I had intended. Despite this, my heart rate maintained a consistent, moderate zone, and the objective movement data from my wearable corroborate both the quality and abrupt end of the session.

Key Points and Summary

  • Sudden, sharp twinge in the left lower abdomen forced immediate end to the session during the second plank set.
  • No systemic symptoms (dizziness, nausea, generalized weakness) were present.
  • Immediate action aligned with Dr. Petra Lang’s acute core injury guidelines.
  • Incident and response documented in both manual log and accompanying wearable data for medical review.
  • Will closely monitor for pain resolution within 24 hours; prepared to consult with a physical therapist if necessary.
