## Game Played: Cascadia

### Game Introduction

**Cascadia** is a tile-laying and pattern-building board game designed by Randy Flynn, centered around the wildlife and landscapes of the Pacific Northwest. It accommodates 1–4 players and typically plays in about 30–45 minutes, though our session stretched to roughly 75 minutes to allow for teaching moments and rule clarifications. The goal is to create the most harmonious ecosystem possible by thoughtfully arranging habitat tiles and wildlife tokens according to a unique set of scoring objectives.

---

### Game Summary

#### Objective

Each player aims to construct an interconnected habitat featuring the five Cascadian animal species—Bear, Elk, Salmon, Fox, Hawk—scoring points both for creating large contiguous corridors of habitat types and for meeting specific animal pattern goals. Strategic drafting and placement are key, with careful attention needed to optimize for both habitat bonuses and animal scoring cards.

#### Setup

- Every player begins with a starter hex tile.
- Four habitat tiles with corresponding wildlife tokens (paired in a public display) are available each round.
- Each animal species is assigned a scoring card outlining its spatial requirement (e.g., Elk in lines, Foxes surrounded by different species, etc.), randomly selected at the start.
- Players receive nature tokens during play, used to refine drafting selections.

#### Game Flow

On a turn, a player selects one of the available tile/token pairs and adds the habitat tile to any adjacent space in their growing landscape. The matching animal token is then placed on an eligible tile—either the newly placed one or any existing, empty tile matching that animal’s requirements. Nature tokens allow for drafting flexibility by breaking the tile/token pairings, an advantage increasingly important as the game progresses.

Scoring occurs at the end of the game:

- **Wildlife Goals:** Each animal scores based on spatial patterns set by the scoring cards.
- **Habitat Corridors:** Largest connected groups of each habitat type are scored, with bonuses for having the largest region among all players.
- **Nature Tokens:** Can tip the balance at crucial moments, either by granting flexibility or as tiebreakers.

The winner is the player with the highest total score after all habitat tiles have been placed and all pattern goals tallied.

## Observed Play and Group Dynamics

### Strategy and Decision-Making

All three of us quickly dove into the strategic heart of Cascadia. Early turns were all about expansion—maximizing potential for both habitat corridors and wildlife objectives, while avoiding the pitfall of isolated, hard-to-use tiles.

**Tile and Token Selection**
- Jana demonstrated a disciplined approach, carefully hoarding nature tokens and biding her time for pivotal drafts. Her patience paid off in the late game, as she used saved tokens to assemble high-scoring animal patterns with perfectly placed tiles.
- Anna was generally more opportunistic, often snapping up tile/token pairs that immediately boosted her wildlife scoring, occasionally sacrificing longer habitat corridors in the process.
- I found myself caught in the classic dilemma: chase corridor bonuses or double down on ambitious animal scoring cards? I leaned toward building up Salmon runs but misjudged the timing, leading to a premature commitment that limited flexibility in the later rounds.

**Interaction and Blocking**
While Cascadia isn’t a confrontational game, some competitive tension emerged as we occasionally drafted suboptimal pairs specifically to deny a key token or tile to another player. This “denial drafting” led to a few memorable twists, forcing creative adaptations and new strategies mid-game.

**Wildlife Goals Influence**
The randomly selected animal scoring cards really shaped our approaches. The Bear card rewarded small sets, prompting us to carefully split clusters. Conversely, the Salmon card encouraged long, branching runs—which Anna executed beautifully, securing a chain that nearly wrapped around her entire board. Jana’s focus on wetlands paid off handsomely, as she assembled a record-setting six-tile corridor, earning not only bonus points but also a round of applause.

**Endgame Decisions**
The final few turns demanded a careful balancing act: maximizing animal groups without breaking crucial habitat corridors or leaving empty, unscorable spaces. Our pace slowed, and every move was deliberated, especially when nature tokens were in play.

## Reflections (Martin)

### Enjoyment and Group Atmosphere

Tonight was one of those rare game nights where fun and focus were perfectly balanced. We all came in with energy, and the enthusiasm rippled through even the slower, crunchier turns. The lively banter and well-timed inside jokes kept things light without diminishing the spirit of healthy competition. Everyone encouraged one another, whether a move went off flawlessly or fizzled—something I truly value in these gatherings.

### Comfort and Venue

My apartment’s living room turned out to be a great environment—bright, quiet, and cooled off just right for a July evening. The snack table saw heavy traffic, with both sweet and savory options disappearing over the course of the night. My only minor complaint would be the size of the table: for sprawling tile games like Cascadia, a bit more space goes a long way. Next time, I’ll look into borrowing a larger table or adding a playmat to keep tiles from drifting.

### Looking Forward

The way our tactics evolved across the evening has me genuinely excited for the next session. I’m already curious to see how a reshuffle of wildlife goal cards might shift our strategies—perhaps throwing in an entirely new lineup for variety. Cascadia’s elegance and replay value stand out, but I’d also love to try other nature-themed games in the coming weeks and see how they stack up in terms of player interaction and strategic depth.

### Suggestions for Future Sessions

- **Pre-Game Setup:** Preparing wildlife scoring cards in advance streamlined the rules explanation, but there’s still room to make teaching quicker—perhaps by investing in a summary sheet for newcomers.
- **Table Arrangement:** Securing an expanded play area or using a non-slip mat would help minimize tile-slide and accommodate bigger boards.
- **Scorekeeping:** Testing out a digital score tracker or printed tally sheets could cut post-game accounting time and help maintain the flow.
- **Group Atmosphere:** The lighthearted, open dialogue and willingness to poke fun at ourselves made all the difference—definitely something to encourage at every game night.
