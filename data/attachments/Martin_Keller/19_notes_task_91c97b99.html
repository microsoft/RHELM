Roasted Eggplant & Za’atar Dip

Martin Keller Recipe Log – February 24, 2024

Overview

On February 24, 2024, I documented my process for creating a Roasted Eggplant & Za’atar Dip, which quickly became a kitchen favorite in my apartment. The inspiration came from a leisurely Saturday visit to the Wochenmarkt, where the abundance of locally grown eggplants and vibrant spice blends caught my attention. With the principles of structured recipe documentation in mind—making it easy to revisit, scale, or share—I set out to record not just the recipe, but some of the thinking, substitutions, and flavor experiments behind it.

Ingredients

Ingredient Quantity Notes
Eggplant 700 g Large, firm specimens from "Obstbau Meier" at the Wochenmarkt
Tomatoes 300 g Ripe Roma variety, picked up from "Biohof Schuster"
Olive oil 60 ml Cold-pressed, extra virgin, sourced from "Olivenölhandlung Kramer"
Za’atar 12 g Fragrant Middle Eastern blend, "Gewürzstand Yilmaz" (contains sesame)
Smoked paprika 7 g Spanish Pimentón de la Vera, just a shade on the hotter side
Garlic 3 cloves Fresh, likely from one of the smaller Wochenmarkt stalls
Lemon juice 20 ml Squeezed from a plump lemon bought at the citrus stand
Sea salt 5 g Fine, locally sourced
Black pepper 2 g Freshly ground, also from "Gewürzstand Yilmaz"
Flat-leaf parsley 10 g Roughly chopped, for garnish (Wochenmarkt, optional)

Preparation

1. Gathering and Prepping the Ingredients

After unloading my basket at home, I gave all the produce a good wash. I find it calming to handle the vegetables, especially when they're as fresh as I found them on market mornings. I set my oven to preheat at 220°C (convection), using this time to prep the baking trays with parchment paper.

2. Roasting the Eggplant

I split the eggplants lengthwise, scoring the cut sides in a crosshatch so they’d roast evenly and soak up the olive oil. With about half the oil (30ml), I drizzled them generously, then arranged them cut-side down on the tray. Into the oven they went for about 30 minutes, until the skins had wrinkled and the flesh inside was meltingly soft—my indicator that the flavor had deepened enough.

3. Roasting Tomatoes and Garlic

While the eggplant roasted, I quartered the Roma tomatoes and tossed them with 1 tablespoon of olive oil and the finely minced garlic. These got their own tray, set to roast for about 20-25 minutes. Sometimes I do this step alongside the eggplant to save time—either way, roasting pulls out a concentrated sweetness from the tomatoes and tames the sharpness of raw garlic.

4. Combining Everything

With the roasted vegetables cooled enough to handle, I scooped the creamy eggplant flesh into a large bowl, discarding the skins. The tomatoes and garlic joined next, along with the remaining olive oil, za’atar, smoked paprika, fresh lemon juice, sea salt, and pepper.

5. Mashing and Adjusting

This is where the real magic happens. For a rustic texture, I mashed the mixture with a fork, aiming for something hearty but spreadable. When I want an ultra-smooth dip, I’ll use the food processor and pulse 8-10 times. I tasted along the way, adjusting the salt and lemon, sometimes adding an extra pinch of za’atar if I want the herbal notes to pop even more.

6. Finishing Touches and Serving

Transferring the dip to a serving bowl, I scatter chopped parsley on top and finish with a last drizzle of olive oil. It’s fantastic served warm or at room temperature, especially with wedges of fluffy flatbread or crisp vegetable sticks. If I’m preparing for a group, I garnish with extra paprika for color.

Inspiration & Recipe Notes

Market Influence:
The Wochenmarkt was buzzing that morning, and I couldn’t resist picking up extra eggplants from "Obstbau Meier" and tomatoes from "Biohof Schuster." Sampling various spices at "Gewürzstand Yilmaz" helped steer this recipe: their za’atar was particularly aromatic, and the smoked paprika batch had a pleasant depth and subtle heat.

Flavors and Adjustments:
I intentionally left out tahini, which is traditional in many eggplant dips, to let the za’atar and smoked paprika really shine. This time I upped the smoked paprika to 7g (2g more than I’d usually use) for a deeper background note. Roasting the tomatoes separately from the eggplant was a small effort but gave the final dip a richer, sweeter base. I’m considering trying a version with 50g of roasted red peppers, or even folding in a small spoonful of tahini (about 25g) for a silkier, more nutty profile.
