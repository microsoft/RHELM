# Technical Cycling Log — March 17, 2024  
**Final Day: Black Forest Tour | Schonach → Furtwangen → Stuttgart**

---

## 1. Route, Date & Navigation Details

This was the final leg of my Black Forest cycling tour, connecting the high Black Forest village of Schonach with Stuttgart via Furtwangen. The route spanned both hilly terrain and urban rail connections, with the first part on bike through the winding regional roads, and the latter segment by train.

### Date  
**March 17, 2024**

### Route Overview

- **Start:** Schonach im Schwarzwald (48.1471° N, 8.2014° E)  
- **Waypoints:** Schonacher Hohe summit, Furtwangen Zentrum (48.0568° N, 8.2078° E)  
- **Destination:** Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof (48.7831° N, 9.1829° E), reached via regional train

#### Sections:

1. **Schonach → Furtwangen**  
   I began early in Schonach, ascending and traversing along the Regionalradweg, which loosely follows local routes K5728 and the scenic B500. This stretch covered about 21 km (13 miles), with a challenging elevation gain of roughly 430 m (1,411 ft) and a descent of 310 m (1,017 ft). Crossing Schonacher Hohe at around 1,050 m tested my climbing legs right from the start.
2. **Furtwangen → Triberg (Alternate/Short Transfer as Needed)**  
   Weather depending, I weighed the option of transferring by regional bus or train on this section (about 12 km with a substantial downhill). Ultimately, I cycled into Furtwangen and deferred the choice, but the wet conditions made it a relevant contingency.
3. **Furtwangen → Stuttgart**  
   From Furtwangen I switched to regional rail for the remaining distance. My planned train route was via Villingen (RB 31) to Stuttgart on IRE 3—a journey of roughly 125 km. I aimed for a midday departure to allow for any delays and give myself a buffer for the rest of the day.

**Main Route Numbers & GPS Coordinates:**
- Roads: K5728, B500
- Rail: DB Regio RB 31, IRE 3
- Key points: Schonach (48.1471° N, 8.2014° E), Schonacher Hohe (48.1129° N, 8.1845° E), Furtwangen (48.0568° N, 8.2078° E), Stuttgart Hbf (48.7831° N, 9.1829° E)

## 2. Weather Conditions

### General Summary

The weather set a demanding tone for the day—persistent cold drizzle and a biting northwesterly wind accompanied me from the outset, common for March in the Black Forest’s higher valleys.

### Detailed Meteorological Overview

- **Source:** Referenced DWD (Deutscher Wetterdienst) data for reliability, accounting for both elevation and valley conditions.
- **Temperature:** Throughout the ride, the air hovered between 2°C and 6°C (36–43°F). The summit and early morning were coldest—my fingers and toes stung during the initial climb.
- **Wind:** A steady northwesterly (NW, 18–28 km/h), with periodic gusts up to 35 km/h, noticeably increased wind chill, especially on exposed ridges and descending valleys.
- **Humidity:** 92–98%—the air felt saturated.
- **Precipitation & Visibility:** Light—but relentless—drizzle kept the roads wet and my jacket clammy throughout. In the higher elevations near Schonacher Hohe, fog and low cloud (ceiling <300 m) cut visibility, so I relied more on GPS and physical road markers.
- **Road Surface:** Consistently slick, with minor puddling on less-maintained stretches, making braking and cornering slow and deliberate.

I had anticipated wet conditions typical for March, but the persistent drizzle and low, heavy clouds left no illusions about the comfort challenges ahead.

## 3. Equipment & Mechanical Performance

### Bicycle & Drivetrain

- **Bike:** My loyal touring road bike (fitted with full fenders and panniers)
- **Drivetrain:** Shimano Deore XT RD-M8000, generally very reliable for loaded touring.

#### Mechanical Issues

Somewhere after Schonacher Hohe, I noticed my rear shifting was stubborn: gears skipped, wouldn’t stay indexed, and the lever pull felt sticky. On quick inspection, there was visible grit around the derailleur’s jockey wheels, and cold, damp conditions worsened cable drag—cable tension fluctuated, and friction increased at the shifter. I suspected water and grime had infiltrated the shifting cables or housing, probably compounded overnight by a brief tumble I’d taken the previous day, nudging the derailleur hanger out of line.

#### On-Road Solutions

Out in the drizzle, I did a basic field reset: pushed the derailleur cage by hand to try and re-seat, then gave the gear cable port a squirt of WD-40 to drive out moisture. I avoided multi-gear shifts and stuck carefully to a stable gear range. Rather than risk a cold-fingered barrel adjust in the wet, I decided to deal with less-than-crisp shifting and adopted a smoother, lower-cadence style for the day. It worked—barely—for the rolling climbs, and I was relieved I’d avoided the rockier forest paths.

### Clothing & Cold Management

- **Jacket:** Gore-Tex hardshell, supposed to be fully waterproof and breathable.
- **Result:** Despite the jacket’s ratings, hours of drizzle plus capillary action from my pack straps left my cuffs, shoulders, and the front zipper area damp. Each time I stopped, especially for food, the clamminess and cooling effect set in fast, particularly on my lower back and underarms.
- **Thermal Effects:** My core temperature took a hit—by every break, I felt chilled, and mild shivering started on the train platform later. I had to eat more than normal just to keep warm (confirmed by sports ergonomics studies: [source 5]).

### Electronics: Phone Failure

- **Device:** iPhone 12 mini  
- **Failure:** Sometime mid-morning, my phone froze and then shut off without warning. I couldn’t get it to reboot, even after a few attempts and checking for moisture.
- **Likely Causes:** Drizzle and foggy air probably seeped into the charging port or SIM tray, and cold conditions seemingly sapped the battery. After several days’ riding without a proper dry-out, it wasn’t a surprise in hindsight.
- **Impact:** No more live GPS tracking, no photographs for the final segments, and without my usual digital timetable and communication tools, I was forced to revert to written directions and manual notes. The sense of “flying blind” was real—the last time I’d been forced to navigate without tech was several years ago.

## 4. Food & Rest Stops

### Planned Stop: Café Central Furtwangen

Arriving in Furtwangen just after 9:30, I expected to warm up and recharge at Café Central (Google’s hours showed 9:30 open). Unfortunately, Sunday brought a delayed opening or perhaps a special closure; the lights were off and doors locked. This was a blow—without a reliable indoor venue, my chances to properly dry gloves and layers disappeared, and my spirits temporarily dipped.

### Actual Stop: Bäckerei Tröndle

A quick detour landed me in the warmly-lit Bäckerei Tröndle at the town center by 09:36. Inside, I grabbed a wholegrain sandwich (Vollkornbrötchen with Gouda, lettuce, tomato, a touch of margarine—about 380 kcal) with a hot black coffee. Nutritional breakdown (per German standards: [source 6])—carbs ~52 g, protein ~13 g, fat ~11 g, sodium ~1 g—was decent for a short stop, though hardly a full recovery meal. I lingered only about 15 minutes, not quite enough to warm through, before heading out again. Missing the café meant carrying that residual chill all the way to the train.

### Logistical and Psychological Impact

The closed café didn’t just cost me calories—it meant extra time spent looking for an alternative (about 15 minutes’ penalty). I had to adjust departure timing for the station and sacrificed a key comfort ritual that, on previous tours, made all the difference on cold, wet days.

---

## 5. Transportation (Train: Furtwangen → Stuttgart)

### Railroad Logistics

From Furtwangen station, I planned to return via DB Regio—first the RB 31 line to Villingen, then transfer for the IRE 3 direct to Stuttgart. Scheduled departure was 11:02, for an arrival at 13:18. Normally, this is a smooth regional link, provided lines remain open.

### Delays and Disruption

- **Actual departure:** 12:16 (over an hour late).  
- **Stated reason:** “Streckensperrung wegen Unwetterschäden”—track closed due to weather, with reports of a tree down near Triberg, a fairly routine occurrence for this region when wind sweeps through after long rain.  
- **Source:** Confirmed via station announcements and would have checked real-time info ([source 7]) if my phone had worked.

I used the extra wait time to stretch and walk laps of the platform, trying to stay warm. Though partially sheltered, I couldn’t escape the persistent wind and drizzle. The fatigue from pre-dawn riding and cold seeped in as the minutes dragged on. Arrived in Stuttgart at 14:31, nearly 80 minutes late, with my plans for gear cleaning and meal prep at home significantly squeezed.

## 6. Personal Experience: Physical, Mental & Documentation Reflections

### Physical Condition

By midday, my body felt the strain—moderate-to-heavy fatigue, made worse by unimpressive sleep the night before and ongoing shivering at stops. I kept up on water and food as best I could; that single bakery stop helped, but I still felt behind on calories for sustained cold exertion. Muscles were tired but not in outright pain—just a generalized heaviness, especially after standing stiffly on the cold platform for an hour.

### Mindset & Emotions

The shut café hit my morale: I’ve come to rely on those mid-tour warm-up rituals not only for comfort, but as a reward, a way to punctuate tough segments with something familiar. That, combined with the sudden loss of my phone (and all hope of documenting this milestone day as intended), created a persistent sense of frustration. My mood fluctuated between initial anticipation, then annoyance with mechanical hiccups, disappointment at the missed rituals, and finally a quiet sense of accomplishment—simply reaching Stuttgart, gear intact and tour completed under difficult circumstances, became enough of a victory. The friendly smile and quick exchange at Bäckerei Tröndle were a small but meaningful lift.

### Documentation & Process Lessons

Losing my phone halfway through the day was more than just an inconvenience. It meant no GPS track, no photographs, and no easy reference for navigation or communicating delay updates. Relying solely on a single device for so many aspects of a modern tour was a mistake. The combination of prolonged humidity, cold, and perhaps condensation from previous days overwhelmed the phone’s water resistance. If I’d packed a second GPS logger or even a waterproof camera, the day’s record wouldn’t be missing its final, hardest leg. Next time, I’ll carry backup systems: a dry pouch, power bank, silicone covers, and at least a paper notebook for timestamps and notes.
