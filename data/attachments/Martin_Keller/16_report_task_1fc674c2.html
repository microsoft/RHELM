2. Executive Summary

On February 6, 2024, Martin Keller and Julia Meier convened at our Stuttgart facilities for a focused firmware patch debugging session on the sensor reliability project. The chief aim of the session was to systematically identify and resolve critical firmware bugs that had been hampering sensor performance, reliability, and energy efficiency, especially during extended operational cycles.

Three primary issues shaped the agenda: - Persistent sensor initialization failures following power cycles - Intermittent data packet loss during UART transmissions - Elevated current consumption during designated sleep states

Over the course of several hours, we employed a range of diagnostic methods, including detailed code tracing, register map validation, and live hardware monitoring with embedded toolchains. Our investigation led us to uncover a combination of misconfigured hardware registers and timing-related race conditions within the data transfer routines. By addressing these root causes: - We successfully resolved and committed fixes for both the initialization bug and the UART data loss issue. - The sleep mode current draw problem saw substantial improvement, though final validation is set for the following week.

Additionally, we noted that the use of blue-light filtering eyewear—trialed by Martin Keller—had a tangible positive effect on comfort and focus during prolonged debugging, supporting deeper work and minimizing disruptions.
