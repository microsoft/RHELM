# Mediterranean Snacks Board Game Night Shopping Guide  
*Planning for 6–8 People*

---

## Introduction

Whenever I host a board game night, I love bringing friends together over food that feels festive but relaxed. For a group of 6 to 8, a Mediterranean-inspired snacks board always sets the tone perfectly: vibrant, healthy, and easy to eat while strategizing over the next move. This shopping guide is my tried-and-true checklist for nights like these, especially when I'm serving spicy chickpea salad with sumac, assorted roasted vegetables, and plenty of warm pita. I’ve tailored these notes to make planning and shopping both efficient and enjoyable, whether I’m prepping solo or organizing with a friend.

## How I Shop and Prep: Tips for a Smooth Night

### Efficient Shopping

* Before heading out, I group my list by shop type: first produce and fresh herbs, then to the spices and pantry staples, finishing at the bakery.
* I make a point to support local vendors, especially for bread and produce—the taste difference always shows.
* On busier days, I’ll grab canned chickpeas or even pre-sliced vegetables and greens as needed, knowing it’ll save precious time.

### Keeping Food Fresh

* I buy my vegetables and herbs just a day or two ahead, aiming for unmarked, vibrant produce. Salads taste brightest when greens and herbs are just picked.
* For bread, I pick up pita rounds the morning of. Nothing beats warming them in the oven just before serving—the kitchen smells amazing and the bread stays pillowy soft.

### Prepping and Serving

* I usually roast vegetables before guests arrive; they’re delicious at room temp or after a brief oven reheat (180°C for 10 minutes does the trick).
* Chickpea salad is quickest when prepped near serving time; if I must make it early, I refrigerate it and let it come up to room temperature for optimal flavor.
* I wrap my pita in foil and warm it at 120°C for about 10 minutes so it’s steamy but never dry.
* For casual hosting, I spread everything buffet-style, letting people graze and refill plates between game rounds—everybody appreciates the flexibility.

## Adjustments and Variations

One of my favorite things about these spreads is how easy it is to customize:

* **Vegetables:** I’ll swap in what’s in season or what my friends enjoy most—carrots, mushrooms, even sweet potato sometimes end up in the roasting tray.
* **Dietary needs:** Everything is vegetarian by default, and vegan if I leave out optional dairy dips. I scan labels for allergens (like sesame in tahini) if I know anyone in the group needs it.
* **Extra dips:** Occasionally, I bring in more Mediterranean choice—baba ghanoush, tzatziki, muhammara—especially if someone offers to contribute a dish or I’m feeling inspired.
