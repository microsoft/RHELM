Subject

Summary of LunaLink Workshop Outcomes and Strategic Next Steps for ESA & Inter-Agency Integration

Purpose Statement

On February 14th, the LunaLink workshop gathered senior technical representatives from ESA and partner agencies, most notably JAXA, to intensify the collaborative integration between our respective space system teams. Our shared objective was clear: to establish seamless technical interoperability and ensure consistent, high-quality data flow in support of upcoming lunar operations. By aligning standards and refining integration procedures, we aim to enhance overall mission reliability and introduce a more agile and resilient systems architecture, benefiting all participating agencies involved in future lunar exploration missions.

Workshop Outcomes and Key Developments

Effective Daily Coordination

Each morning of the workshop began with a stand-up meeting jointly led by ESA and JAXA’s systems engineering teams. These sessions provided an opportunity for rapid collaboration and transparent communication, allowing us to address technical challenges, synchronize progress, and iterate on solutions nearly in real time. The practice has noticeably improved efficiency in dealing with distributed teams and fast-evolving interface requirements.

Resolving Data Synchronization Across Agencies

One of the workshop’s central successes came from the joint troubleshooting of telemetry alignment delays. By combining the CCSDS data exchange protocols with ECSS-E-ST-70-41C standards for telecommand packet formatting, our teams were able to pinpoint and rectify issues originating from asynchronous timestamp mechanisms. We collaboratively designed and validated a harmonized timestamp conversion workflow, which has now been documented and will serve as the baseline for subsequent integration activities.

Strengthening Risk Management and Simulation Protocols

Recognizing the importance of rigorous risk management, we established a unified risk register that captures interface-related concerns from both ESA and JAXA. Adhering to the ECSS-M-ST-80 standard, our teams jointly agreed on criteria for early detection of interface discrepancies. This approach includes scheduling cross-team simulations before deployment, ensuring all critical data paths are stress-tested and any weaknesses identified before live operations commence.

Planning for Integration Readiness

To ensure smooth transition from workshop recommendations to operational practice, we concluded with a set of actionable technical next steps. These include final interface verification, expanding the cross-agency Q&A technical forum (to promote ongoing knowledge exchange), and formalizing joint test campaigns to certify LunaLink integration readiness before mission launch windows.

Conclusion & Next Steps

The collaborative efforts during the LunaLink workshop have laid a solid technical foundation for continued integration and operational readiness. By directly addressing data synchronization and risk management challenges, and by reinforcing communication channels through standing meetings and technical forums, our teams are now well-positioned to advance LunaLink system deployment for lunar operations. The assigned action items and joint campaigns will further support transparent, resilient, and interoperable mission delivery moving forward.
