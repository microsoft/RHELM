## Morning Resistance Training

This morning, I started my day with a structured resistance training session, focusing both on overall fitness and on the specific physical demands of my work. Improving biomechanical efficiency and flexibility continues to be a priority—not just for athletic performance but also for preventing injuries, especially to my wrists, given the repetitive, high-precision tasks I regularly handle at the office.

**Training Summary**

| Time  | Exercise     | Focus Area      | Sets x Reps | Weight (kg) | %1RM | Flexibility Progress           | Wrist Protection       | Injury Prevention | Work Relevance                                   |
|-------|--------------|----------------|-------------|-------------|------|-------------------------------|-----------------------|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| 07:05 | Bench Press  | Chest/Shoulder | 3x8         | 55          | 75%  | +2cm in shoulder abduction    | Neutral grip, wrist wraps   | No pain, steady   | Confident posture, upper body stamina for presentations |
| 07:20 | Back Squat   | Legs/Core      | 4x6         | 80          | 70%  | Improved knee flexion (target 130°–145°) | —                     | Controlled descent| Stability for long workdays and travel           |
| 07:35 | Wrist Curl   | Forearm/Wrist  | 3x15        | 10          | 40%  | Full pain-free ROM            | Eccentric contraction, brace | No strain        | Essential for extended typing and device use      |

During the bench press, I felt a noticeable increase in shoulder mobility, adding 2 centimeters to my abduction range. The improved stability from squats was evident—my knees moved more efficiently, and the controlled descent helped protect my joints. For wrist curls, I paid careful attention to form, slowing down the eccentric phase and using a brace as needed. No pain or strain this morning, which is reassuring given past wrist flare-ups during heavy typing periods.

**Reflections**

My resistance routine is clearly benefiting my work life. Robust upper body strength gives me presence and confidence in executive meetings, and lower body endurance makes long presentations and business travel far less taxing. Maintaining joint mobility and strength feels like a direct investment in my workplace performance—and honestly, a buffer against the stress and strain of leadership.
