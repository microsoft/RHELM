## 3. Strategic Meal Planning & Dietary Notes

### Objectives and Approach

My daily meal planning is driven by several core objectives. First, I want to maintain high cognitive and physical performance. I focus on meals rich in protein, complex carbohydrates, healthy fats (especially omega-3s), and abundant antioxidants. These nutrients keep me alert during long technical sessions and help with recovery after stressful days. Second, I strive to make efficient use of my time. Pre-prepped elements, shelf-stable foods, and a clear grocery routine reduce daily friction and support consistent, balanced eating—even during high-pressure deadlines.

The Mediterranean diet remains my template, echoing ESA and NASA recommendations for astronaut health. I emphasize fresh vegetables, legumes, whole grains, lean proteins like chicken and fish, and plant-based fats. Across the day, I make a point to distribute meals and snacks in a way that keeps my energy steady—preventing both spikes and crashes, which could disrupt my workflow.

### Adaptation for ESA Missions

As I select ingredients each week, I keep potential adaptation to ESA missions in mind. Shelf-stable and nutrient-dense foods—like wholegrain crackers, dried fruit, rice, and frozen fish—are cornerstones both at home and in operational environments. The flexible, modular lunches and snacks I prefer can also support team members with different cultural backgrounds; for example, Mediterranean, North African, and Middle Eastern influences blend easily, respecting diverse palates and dietary requirements. This adaptability is crucial for the multicultural crews I often collaborate with.

### Cross-Cultural and Collaborative Aspects

Inclusivity is always a priority for me, both in menu planning and in social mealtimes. I regularly incorporate vegetarian options (such as hummus and salads), and I’m mindful of providing non-allergenic alternatives where needed. Sharing lunch with colleagues isn’t just about nutrition; it’s a valuable opportunity to strengthen relationships and keep our project timelines aligned. These meals often spark new ideas or clarify priorities. I also document the what, where, and when of each meal in detail, following best practices set out by international space agencies. This record-keeping supports transparency and makes it easy to adapt or audit nutrition plans over time.

### Best Practices in Documentation

I keep a clear divide between my daily meal log and the grocery inventory, sticking to standardized units (by weight or count) and using plain-language descriptions. For every entry, I add context—noting operational requirements, social context, or specific dietary needs as relevant. Where I rely on generic or typical supplier data, especially for grocery restocks, I make this clear for future reference. Maintaining this detail allows for trend tracking and supports smooth collaboration during project reviews or mission planning discussions.
