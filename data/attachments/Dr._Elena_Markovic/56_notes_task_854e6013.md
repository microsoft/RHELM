## Detailed Log and Analysis

### Prescription Renal Diet: Timing and Rationale

At 7:45 this morning, I fed Luna her prescribed Hill’s Prescription Diet k/d for cats, chicken dry formula. This diet, specifically recommended for feline chronic kidney disease (CKD), is formulated with lower phosphorus and sodium content, along with reduced but high-quality protein. Such nutritional adjustments are shown to slow the progression of CKD and support overall kidney function. 
- The batch I’m using provides 380 kcal per 100g, with 6.7% protein and 0.4% phosphorus (UPC: 052742003579). These values are in line with current veterinary guidelines for CKD management [1].  
- I weighed out 35g for breakfast, close to Luna’s ideal daily split, aiming to support her energy needs while not overwhelming her kidneys.

I’ve found that maintaining a precise schedule and consistent portion sizes helps Luna anticipate meals, and most importantly, supports her willingness to eat—especially crucial given her recent variability in appetite.

### Hydration Support: Supplementation Approach

Managing hydration is always top of mind for CKD cats. Following Dr. van der Meer’s recommendations, I administered Supportal oral rehydration solution—a flavourless electrolyte mix—by stirring 20 mL directly into Luna’s food. This product is designed to help cats maintain adequate fluid and electrolyte balance without altering the palatability of their food.
- I have previously observed that separating the supplement from the meal can cause Luna to refuse both, so adding it directly to her food at the time of feeding ensures she receives the full dose.
- This routine is based on manufacturer dosing for a 4 kg adult cat and supports both hydration and overall renal health [2].

I’ve been vigilant about monitoring for any signs of gastrointestinal upset or food aversion, but Luna has tolerated this supplement well each time I’ve included it.

### Meal Response: Appetite, Intake, and Behavior

Luna’s response to her meal this morning was markedly positive.  
- She consumed 34g of her 35g meal (over 97%), leaving only a few crumbs. This is a clear improvement from earlier this week, when she regularly left as much as a third uneaten.
- Within the hour after eating, I measured an additional 12 mL of water consumption at her bowl using a marked cylinder. While the supplemental liquid does contribute to her hydration, I believe this continued interest in drinking independently is a sign of good adjustment and comfort with her diet.
- Her eating behavior was enthusiastic—she approached the bowl immediately, consumed her food at a steady pace, and showed keen interest throughout the meal.
- After eating, Luna engaged in her usual post-meal grooming, then batted at her feather wand for about five minutes. There were no indications of nausea, oral discomfort, or meal aversion during or afterwards.

These observations match her typical healthy routines, and I see no signs to suggest any current issues related to her diet or supplementation [3].

### Dental Treat Administration

Dental care remains an integral part of managing Luna’s overall health, especially since CKD can increase oral sensitivity.
- Today, I offered her half a C.E.T. VeggieDent Feline Dental Chew, in line with best-practice guidelines for cats under 5 kg. She accepted it without hesitation and ate the entire piece.
- I’ve learned to monitor for any reluctance, dropping of the treat, or signs of oral pain, as CKD cats are often at greater risk for dental disease or altered taste [4]. No such issues have arisen.

### General Observations and Ongoing Monitoring

**Immediate findings:**  
- Litterbox checks revealed one normal urine clump and well-formed stool before feeding.  
- Luna’s behavior was bright and social, and she actively sought my attention after breakfast, initiating gentle play.  
- Her appetite has shown steady improvement since the start of this week, a welcome contrast to her more sluggish eating patterns a few days ago.  
- Importantly, I have seen no vomiting, no lethargy, and her overall demeanor is consistent with her normal, healthy self.

**Long-term monitoring:**  
- Weight remains steady at 4.0 kg (last weighed two days ago).
- She continues to tolerate the hydration supplement well, with no gastrointestinal disturbances noted.
- I remain attentive to changes in urination, appetite, body weight, and energy, as these are often the earliest indicators of CKD progression [5]. At this time, no acute symptoms or concerning developments have been observed.
