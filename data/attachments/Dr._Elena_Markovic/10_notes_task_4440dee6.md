# **Restorative Evening Yoga Routine Log: Supporting Wrist Injury Recovery for High-Performing Professionals**

## **Overview**

This report outlines a thoroughly developed restorative yoga routine designed specifically for individuals recovering from wrist injuries, with a focus on evening practice. The sequence prioritizes both physical safety and psychological well-being, ensuring that each component supports injury recovery while fostering deep relaxation. Intended for either personal use or inclusion in organizational wellness programs, this routine is especially suitable for professionals managing high-stress, demanding roles. Emphasis is placed on practical, evidence-based modifications and standardized approaches, in line with current yoga therapy guidelines and scholarly research on musculoskeletal injury recovery.

## **2. Routine Structure and Flow**

The routine is purposefully sequenced to guide practitioners through progressive relaxation, nourishment of physiological recovery, and the cultivation of a calm mental state, particularly before bedtime.

**a. Opening Breathwork & Grounding (2–3 min):**  
Begin in a seated posture, ensuring your forearms rest gently on thighs or cushions. Practice several rounds of diaphragmatic breathing, consciously relaxing wrists and hands. This phase anchors attention in the body and signals the shift from work to restoration.

**b. Supported Supta Baddha Konasana (8–10 min):**  
Transition onto your back, settling into this gentle heart opener. Focus on smooth, natural breathing, letting go of tension with every exhale. Arms remain beside the torso, wrists cushioned and unstrained, ensuring no weight or effort is required.

**c. Legs Up the Wall (8–10 min):**  
Carefully move to the wall, arranging legs vertically while your back and head rest on the floor or a thin mat. Maintain full relaxation of wrists throughout, with soft support beneath as needed. This posture aids lymphatic return and soothes the nervous system.

**d. Supported Child’s Pose (5–8 min):**  
Return to a kneeling position, folding forward over a bolster. Protect the wrists by keeping arms close and rested, palms up or neutral, avoiding traditional forward reach. Let the whole upper body melt with gravity.

**e. Reclined Twist (5 min each side):**  
Lay flat, then guide knees into a twist while arms are comfortably positioned to avoid wrist strain—either extended or resting on the upper body. Use cushions to keep wrists neutral and relaxed, promoting full-body release.

**f. Supported Side-Lying Savasana (8–12 min):**  
Roll onto your side, ensuring comfort throughout by using pillows to support both head and knees, as well as under the bottom forearm. This modification is essential for spinal and wrist alignment while inviting a deep sense of safety and surrender.

**g. Seated Meditation with Herbal Tea (5–10 min):**  
Return to a supported seat. Savor a warm cup of herbal tea, holding it so both wrists remain supported by your lap. This simple ritual signals winding down and anchors awareness in the senses, offering a gentle pause before final rest.

**h. Guided Savasana (8–12 min):**  
End by reclining fully. Support wrists and hands to eliminate any lingering tension. Practice a body scan or follow a relaxation script, attending especially to breath, jaw, shoulders, and hands. Remain until a sense of ease and readiness for sleep emerges.

## **3. Reflective Outcomes and Insights**

### **Physiological Outcomes**
- **Pain Level:**  
  Throughout the session, there was a clear reduction in wrist discomfort and forearm tightness. All props and positioning ensured zero aggravation; wrists stayed neutral and free from strain.
- **Mobility:**  
  The practice allowed safe, non-extending movement at the wrists, maintaining their supported alignment. There were no setbacks or limitations experienced during the session.
- **Muscle Tension:**  
  Noticeable lessening of upper body tightness. The routine successfully promoted relaxation without imposing any load on the healing area.

### **Psychological Outcomes**
- **Mood Enhancement:**  
  A pronounced sense of calm developed across the practice, with anxiety levels decreasing notably—especially during breathwork and guided relaxation phases.
- **Mental Clarity and Focus:**  
  The structure and predictability of the sequence supported mindful attention, encapsulating both body and mind. This fostered internal quiet and readiness for rest.
- **Sleep Readiness:**  
  By the end, profound drowsiness and body comfort signaled a strong readiness for sleep, aligned with goals for effective evening recovery.

### **Practical Strategies for Physical Limitation**
- **Prop Utilization:**  
  Bolsters, folded blankets, and strategic pillows provided essential ergonomic support, removing risk to the wrist and encouraging whole-body ease.
- **Awareness of Arm Position:**  
  Mindful check-ins at each transition ensured wrists never dangled, flexed, or bore weight, but always remained gently supported at or above heart level as appropriate.
- **Flexibility in Sequencing:**  
  The modular design of the routine made it easy to adapt or skip particular poses as needed, preserving therapeutic intent without overexertion.

### **Herbal Tea Integration**
- **Purpose and Role:**  
  The inclusion of herbal tea—such as chamomile or tulsi—during meditation served as a sensory anchor, marking the shift from activity to reflection. Its warmth in hand also aided in further relaxing the wrists and deepening parasympathetic activation.
- **Method:**  
  Tea was held delicately with both hands. With elbows supported, I focused on the sensations of warmth, taste, and breath, incorporating mindful sips and pauses.
- **Noted Benefits:**  
  This ritual added to the sense of comfort and evening closure, reinforcing relaxation, and supporting a healthy sleep onset.

## **5. Logging, Metrics, and Best Practices for Sustainable Implementation**

### **Standardized Session Logging**

- **Tabular Log Entries:**  
  Record date, time, pose, duration, specific modification, and subjective metrics such as pain (0–10), relaxation (0–10), and sleepiness (0–10).
- **Reflective Notes:**  
  After each session, respond to prompts about changes in physical sensation, mental state, effectiveness of adaptations, and relaxation techniques used.
- **Routine Templates:**  
  Maintain a consistent pose list and modification table, allowing for simple adaptation to different injuries or personal needs.

### **Key Metrics for Tracking**
- **Pain/discomfort level (0–10)**
- **Joint mobility (“yes/no” per session)**
- **Mood, anxiety, and sleepiness scores (0–10)**
- **Props used: types and effectiveness**
- **Routine completion (“yes/no”)**
- **Integration of herbal tea (“yes/no,” with type)**

### **Organizational Best Practices**
- **Centralized Tracking:**  
  Use secure, shared digital platforms for session logs, facilitating group trend analysis while respecting privacy. This encourages peer support and accountability.
- **Occupational Health Oversight:**  
  Health leads or supervisors periodically review anonymized reports for signs of improvement or need for further intervention, ensuring proactive support.
- **Interdisciplinary Application:**  
  Standardized routines and logs can be rolled out across various teams, fostering a culture of safety and holistic well-being.
- **Continuous Feedback:**  
  Templates and approaches are regularly updated based on participant feedback and evolving best practices, ensuring ongoing relevance and efficacy.
