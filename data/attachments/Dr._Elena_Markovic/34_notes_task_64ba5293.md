# Grocery Shopping List for Dr. Elena Markovic — Albert Heijn, April 13, 2024

## Overview

After a busy morning at the veterinary clinic, I knew I needed to make a quick yet efficient stop at Albert Heijn to restock my kitchen. My goal for this grocery trip was clear: pick up only the essentials for the coming week, focusing on nutritious, uncomplicated ingredients that make daily meal preparation both quick and enjoyable. Since I usually cook for myself, or occasionally share meals with a friend, I wanted to keep things simple—opting for staple foods in reasonable amounts, steering clear of anything unnecessary or indulgent.

To make things as easy as possible at the store, I organized my shopping list by category. This not only helps me remember what I need but also makes it easier to move through the aisles quickly, especially when I'm short on time.

## How I Planned and Organized My List

Before heading to the supermarket, I thought carefully about what would make my week easier: plenty of greens, satisfying proteins, and simple options for breakfast and lunch. 

**Produce** sits at the top of my list because fresh fruits and vegetables form the backbone of almost every meal I make. Salad greens, tomatoes, and cucumber can be easily combined in endless ways, and bananas are a must for a healthy start to the day or an easy snack between appointments.

**Dairy** is next. Semi-skimmed milk is non-negotiable for coffee and cereal, and eggs are an easy fix for any meal—a quick omelette saves me on especially hectic evenings. Yogurt is both nutritious and filling; it pairs perfectly with fruit or a spoonful of muesli.

From the **Bakery** section, I always pick up whole wheat bread and sandwich rolls. Both freeze well if I ever have leftovers, and they make a reliable base for open-faced sandwiches or lunch rolls when I’m running short on time.

For protein, I rely on a simple pack of **lean sliced chicken fillet** and a tub of **hummus**. The chicken adds substance to salads and sandwiches, while hummus is delicious as a dip or spread. I consciously avoid complicating things with too many meat or specialty items—these basic choices are practical and ensure I don’t overspend or waste food.

All package sizes are what I typically find at Albert Heijn and what makes sense for a single-person kitchen; I try to buy just what I’ll use, both to keep things fresh and to minimize food waste.

## Planning for the Rest of the Week

This streamlined list means I can throw together a salad for dinner, make myself a quick sandwich before running out the door, or enjoy a cozy breakfast without extra fuss. With such a routine in place, there’s less mental energy spent each day on deciding what to eat—one less thing to worry about when my schedule gets overwhelming.

To avoid impulse purchases, I make a conscious effort to skip anything that doesn’t have a specific use for the coming week. I also skip thinking about brands; I simply pick whichever option is available and reasonably priced. Most days, I can whip through the aisles in less than 20 minutes, especially with my list ordered to match the usual supermarket layout—starting with produce and finishing with protein and spreads.

Having notes beside each item reminds me exactly what I was thinking when I wrote the list, so I don’t stand in the middle of the dairy aisle second-guessing myself.

---

## Practical Tips I Live By

- **Stick to Basics:** Focusing on core staples helps me avoid clutter in the kitchen and reduces the chances of forgetting key ingredients during the week.
- **No Brands, Just Essentials:** Not worrying about brands keeps my list practical and lets me choose what’s available or on sale.
- **Think Ahead:** By cross-referencing each item with specific meals (like “breakfast/snacks” or “for salads/sandwiches”), I save time both in-store and at home, knowing exactly what fits where in my routine.

This approach covers all my daily needs for breakfast, lunch, and dinner over several days—without leaving my fridge overwhelmed or my wallet lighter from unnecessary extras.
