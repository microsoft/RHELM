## Batch Prep Recipes: Mediterranean Salads & Roasted Vegetables

Making batch cooking work for me means selecting recipes that hold up well throughout the week and improve in flavor as they marry in the fridge. Over the years, I’ve refined a few reliable favourites that I adjust to suit whatever is best at the markets.

### Mediterranean Chickpea & Seasonal Vegetable Salad

**Servings:** 4 (I typically double this for my weekly prep)

**Ingredients:**  
- 400g cooked chickpeas  
- 300g cherry tomatoes, halved  
- 1 large cucumber or kohlrabi, diced  
- 1 bell or sweet pointy pepper, chopped  
- 1 red onion or a few spring onions, thinly sliced  
- 50g Kalamata olives, pitted and halved  
- 80g feta cheese, cubed  
- ½ bunch fresh parsley, chopped  
- **Dressing:** 3 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, juice of 1 lemon, 1 tsp wholegrain mustard, ½ tsp dried oregano, sea salt and black pepper

**My Batch Method:**  
1. I always start by rinsing and thoroughly drying my vegetables, which really improves fridge life.
2. I combine the chickpeas, harder vegetables, olives, onions, and parsley in a large bowl, but I keep the feta and tomatoes separate for now.
3. The salad base goes right into individual containers, ready for grab-and-go lunches.
4. Each morning, I finish off a portion by tossing in the tomatoes and cubes of feta, along with the lemony dressing.
5. This layering keeps everything crisp and vibrant—no soggy salad after a few days.

### Batch Roasted Mediterranean Vegetables

**Makes enough for at least 6 generous side portions.** These are endlessly versatile: I use them in wraps, on salads, tossed with grains, or served alongside proteins.

- 400g eggplant or zucchini/pattypan squash  
- 400g mixed bell peppers, carrots, or beetroot  
- 2 medium red onions, cut into wedges  
- 200g cherry tomatoes  
- 4 cloves garlic, left whole and unpeeled  
- 6 sprigs fresh thyme or rosemary  
- 3 tbsp olive oil  
- Sea salt and black pepper

**Batch Method:**  
1. I preheat my oven to 200°C and chop everything into uniform pieces, which ensures even roasting.
2. After tossing with olive oil, herbs, salt, and pepper, I lay the vegetables out on a large tray (or two).
3. Roasting takes 25–30 minutes with a stir halfway through. I always keep an eye—aromatic veggies are easy to over-roast.
4. Afterwards, I let them cool completely before refrigerating, storing in glass containers that stack neatly in my fridge.
5. If I make extra, I freeze single portions; they reheat beautifully in the oven or air fryer, especially when I want a quick lunch or dinner without fuss.

---

### Strategies for Freshness & Nutrient Preservation

- I always layer my salads, starting with grains or beans and hard vegetables, and finishing with delicate greens and tomatoes on top or adding those fresh each day.
- Dressings live in their own small bottles until right before serving; it’s a little extra step that keeps salads tasting as though they were just made.
- Roasted vegetables must cool fully before going into the fridge to avoid excess moisture, which can wilt everything.
- I chop herbs and delicate greens daily, just before I’m ready to eat—this small act maintains their flavor and color.
- Freezing surplus roasted veggies has saved dinners on countless busy evenings, without sacrificing much on nutrition.

## Shopping List & Sourcing Guidance

My shopping is guided by two key principles: quality and simplicity. I prioritize organic and local foods whenever possible, not only for taste, but also for peace of mind given my workload and the importance of health.

**Proteins:**
- Chickpeas (canned or cooked): 3 x 400g cans, or 500g cooked (organic)
- Feta cheese: 200g (preferably organic, PGI/AOP)
- Halloumi cheese: 200g (optional, organic)
- Salmon: 200g fillet (sustainably sourced)
- Falafel: Ready-made or prepare from dry ingredients
- Eggs: 6 (organic/free-range)

**Grains & Legumes:**
- Quinoa: 250g
- Farro or barley: 200g
- Oats: 150g (for overnight oats)

**Vegetables:**
- Cherry or grape tomatoes: 500g
- Cucumber, kohlrabi, and/or radish: 3 medium
- Bell peppers/carrot: 5 medium
- Eggplant, zucchini, pattypan, and/or pumpkin: about 1.2kg mixed
- Red or spring onion: 3
- Leafy greens (spinach/rocket): 200g
- Garlic: 1 bulb
- Root vegetables (beetroot, turnip, carrot): approx. 300g
- Seasonal frittata greens: 80g (whatever’s freshest)

**Fruit:**
- Mixed berries: 300g
- Apples: 3
- Lemons: 3

**Herbs:**
- Fresh parsley: 1 large bunch
- Fresh mint: 1 bunch
- Fresh thyme and rosemary: a handful each
- Dried oregano: small jar

**Pantry Staples & Miscellaneous:**
- Olive oil (extra-virgin): 200ml
- Wholegrain mustard: 1 jar
- Kalamata olives: 100g
- Flaxseed/chia: 100g
- Mixed nuts/seeds: 150g
- Rye sourdough: 1 loaf (wholegrain)
- Oatcakes: 1 pack

**Dairy/Dairy Alternatives:**
- Cottage cheese: 200g
- Oat milk: 1 liter

**Snacks & Beverages:**
- Herbal teas: peppermint, chamomile, mint, lemon-ginger, green, rooibos (variety, loose or bagged)
- Greek yogurt: 400g
- Honey: 1 small jar

**Where I Shop:**  
For the freshest, organic products, I visit local farmers’ markets when possible. In Germany, Alnatura and Denn’s Biomarkt are reliable, while in the Netherlands, I like Ekoplaza and Noordermarkt in Amsterdam. In Sweden, I head to ICA’s organic section or Bondens Egen Marknad. When in the UK, Planet Organic and Borough Market offer an impressive selection.

I try to shop midweek when markets are bustling but not overcrowded, and I look for EU Organic and Demeter certifications. Whenever I can, I adapt my meal plan to what’s particularly fresh and abundant—late tomatoes, cucumbers, apples, and hearty greens are at their best here in September.

## Dietary Preferences and Meal Prep Notes

### Nutritional Standards

I follow the Mediterranean approach to nutrition, in line with EFSA and WHO recommendations for adult professionals: generous vegetables, plant-based proteins, whole grains, high-quality olive oil, and minimal processed food. I include oily fish once per week for crucial omega-3s and moderate dairy, aiming for balance and variety above all else. My snacks are always nutrient dense—nuts, seeds, yogurt, or fruit—and timed for steady energy through mentally demanding days.

### My Preparation Rituals

Organic produce and dairy are always at the top of my list, and I search out reputable sources for grains and legumes. Each Sunday, batch prep is a routine I’ve come to appreciate; it frees up time during my workweek, leaving me with daily moments to quietly assemble and enjoy freshly finished meals. Cheeses, herbs, greens, and dressings are always kept separate right up until serving to optimize texture and taste.

With the shifting Northern European harvest, I regularly swap Mediterranean ingredients for whatever is in season—kohlrabi for cucumber, or local root veg instead of sweet peppers. I find that embracing seasonal change not only keeps my cooking interesting but also aligns with my sustainability values.

Herbal teas are another small but important ritual for me. A cup of green tea most mornings wakes me up gently, and calming blends like mint or chamomile help me focus or unwind, depending on the day’s demands.

### Special Considerations

My kitchen is nearly free from highly processed foods or preservatives, which supports both my energy levels and cognitive resilience for high-stakes technical work. I carefully portion meals in glass or BPA-free containers, labeling everything so that even on my busiest days, meals are organized and stress-free. Assembly or final reheating rarely takes more than ten minutes, making healthy eating realistic during even the most hectic weeks.

Above all, I find that this planning and batching isn’t just about efficiency; it’s a commitment to my own well-being, and it helps me bring my best focus and creativity to both work and life.
