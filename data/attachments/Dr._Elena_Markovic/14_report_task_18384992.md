## Executive Summary

On February 3, 2024, we conducted a comprehensive field trial of the orienteering mapping app in the diverse landscape of the Noordwijk dunes. The primary goal was to assess the app’s accuracy, usability, and reliability during real-world use across varied terrain, benchmarking it against the current standards for field mapping technologies. The test covered several critical metrics: GPS accuracy, waypoint logging, system responsiveness, user interface, and ergonomic support for different physical needs.

The app was evaluated at multiple pre-selected waypoints and terrain zones, with detailed feedback captured from all users—each bringing unique perspectives to navigation, usage, and ergonomic factors. The test identified both standout strengths and areas for improvement, offering a roadmap for the next development cycle.

**Objectives:**

- Verify the GPS’s precision and stability at curated waypoints under a range of environmental conditions.
- Evaluate user interface design and feedback mechanisms to ensure intuitive navigation for diverse users.
- Discover technical and ergonomic constraints, especially in areas of challenging terrain.
- Collect insights to further refine both the technology and user experience.

**Benchmarks:**

- GPS location accuracy held within ±5 meters at most waypoints.
- System response times rarely exceeded 5 seconds for waypoint logging.
- Users consistently described the interface as intuitive, though noted areas for improvement.
- The app remained largely stable throughout, even under environmental and ergonomic stressors.

**Key Findings:**

- The mapping app demonstrated robust performance in open terrain, with rapid GPS lock and responsive interaction.
- Signal accuracy degenerated significantly within dense vegetation (pine grove), leading to location drift and lag.
- The app’s ergonomic design accommodated users with limited mobility, but opportunities exist to expand adaptive features.
- Field data and user input support future refinement, reinforcing the app’s alignment with contemporary usability and reliability standards.

## GPS Performance: Terrain Analysis

Testing was structured to capture the app’s GPS accuracy, response time, and consistency across five distinct terrain zones. These zones were chosen to represent common orienteering environments—from open dunes to dense bush and water features.

**Analysis:**

- The strongest GPS performance was observed in open zones and near water, where satellite visibility remained unobstructed.
- Dense vegetation in the pine grove created a marked decline in both accuracy and response, underscoring GPS’s vulnerability to environmental interference.
- Terrain density correlated directly with increased error margin and system lag, suggesting a need for enhanced sensor fusion and multi-source positioning (e.g., combining barometric and inertial data).
- App caching and calibration features mitigated some challenges but were insufficient under the most extreme conditions.

## User Experience and Ergonomic Assessment

**Elena Rossi’s Participation and Feedback**

Elena Rossi trialed the app while using a supportive wrist brace due to a chronic strain injury, providing critical feedback on the ergonomic design:

- The single-touch controls and minimal swipe functionality allowed comfortable navigation even with limited wrist movement.
- The brace did not hinder use, but holding the device for a prolonged period caused mild fatigue.
- Elena proposed enhancements such as adjustable button sizes, improved haptic feedback, and flexible screen orientation to accommodate users with varied hand strength and physical limitations.
- She stressed the need for quick-access modes that reduce reach and effort—beneficial for users with joint or motor skill challenges.

**Ergonomic Recommendations:**

- While the app meets basic criteria for accessibility, there is substantial opportunity to advance interface adaptability—enabling safer, more comfortable use for individuals with injuries or restricted mobility.
- Reducing the movement required for navigation, integrating voice commands, and supporting one-handed operation should be prioritized in future versions.

## Recommendations for Development and Field Integration

Based on the outcomes of this trial, the following actionable steps are advised:

**1. Reliability Enhancements**

- Upgrade GPS signal processing using sensor fusion approaches, especially in areas where conventional GPS is likely to degrade.
- Implement pre-caching of terrain data and adaptive waypoint algorithms to maintain accuracy in variable conditions.

**2. User-Centered Design Improvements**

- Expand UI customization—offer variable button sizing, responsive haptic cues, and multiple color contrast themes.
- Integrate hands-free or voice-driven mapping to support users with limited manual dexterity.
- Introduce intelligent alerts for prolonged GPS lag or failed location lock, allowing users to react proactively.

**3. Robust Offline and Contingency Features**

- Provide offline map caching for use in areas without cellular access or satellite reliability.
- Incorporate fallback logging protocols, so users can continue mapping if real-time coordinates are temporarily unavailable.
- Enable risk assessment notifications that factor in environmental data and signal history to guide safe usage.

**Next Steps**

Immediate focus will be placed on resolving technical reliability in dense vegetation and introducing enhanced ergonomic features in the next development cycle. Continued field testing with expanded user groups and a broader range of terrain is planned to validate improvements and inform ongoing refinement.
