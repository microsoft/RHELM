Executive Travel Coordination Report

Subject: Dr. Elena Markovic (ESA) — Trip to Belgrade for Sofia’s Model Rocket Launch

Overview

This report outlines the coordination and planning for Dr. Elena Markovic, Lead Spacecraft Systems Engineer at the European Space Agency (ESA), to participate in and support the upcoming model rocket launch organized by Sofia and the Belgrade youth club. The objective is to harmonize Dr. Markovic’s ESA obligations with the event schedule and to ensure all technical, logistical, and risk management requirements are thoroughly addressed. Where relevant, the report highlights areas with incomplete data and recommends validation once official timetables become available. All proposals align with ESA best practices for project management and cross-institutional collaboration.

1. Proposed Itinerary and Activity Schedule

Given the current lack of access to finalized ESA and Belgrade youth club schedules, several working assumptions underpin the proposed coordination:

  • The rocket launch is targeted for May or June 2024, with a preference for weekends (Friday–Sunday) as expressed by the youth club.

  • Dr. Markovic has recurring meetings on Mondays and Thursdays; mid-week offers more flexibility for travel.

2. Conflict Assessment and Recommendations

Identified Conflicts and Logistical Risks

  • Scheduling Clashes with ESA Milestones: The weeks of 21 June and 28 June overlap with ESA’s quarter-end activities, which are subject to last-minute change and high-priority demands.
  • Youth Club Availability: The period between 7–16 June coincides with local exams. The club will either restrict capacity or close during this period.
  • Compressed Turnaround: Traveling over 24–26 May immediately after a major ESA review leaves minimal time for both event preparation and post-event integration.
  • Travel Feasibility: Early May stands out as the optimal period with high flexibility on both sides and minimal institutional risk.

Mitigation and Planning Strategies

  • Primary Recommendation: Prioritize the weekend of 10–12 May 2024. This window avoids ESA critical periods, ensures high club availability, and leaves ample time for both event and professional responsibilities.
  • Secondary Option: If delays are necessary, 7–9 June 2024 is a viable alternative but should be treated as contingent upon securing club resources ahead of time. Early booking will help manage limited capacity.
  • Back-up Representation: Nominate an alternate ESA representative to step in, in the event Dr. Markovic faces unavoidable ESA conflicts.
  • Flexible Arrangements: Make all bookings refundable or easily changeable to adjust for potential shifts in ESA or club schedules.

Protocol for Unresolved Scheduling

If these windows prove unworkable due to unforeseen changes, initiate discussions with club management and Sofia’s family to explore weekday scheduling, or to defer the event to after the ESA quarter-end period (i.e., post-1 July), when demands are expected to ease for all stakeholders.

Post-Trip

  • EU Passports: No Serbian visa required for short visits.
  • Non-EU Staff: Must verify current visa requirements through the Serbian Ministry of Foreign Affairs.
  • Equipment Transfer: Confirm completion of all technical export and customs documentation according to ESA rules.

Technical Equipment and Risk Management

  • Prepare backup kits to support the event in the event of lost or delayed cargo.
  • In case of inclement weather, coordinate alternative indoor STEM activities with club leaders.

Communication and Stakeholder Engagement

  • Assign clear points of contact at both the ESA and youth club. Establish a WhatsApp/Slack channel for coordination and rapid communication.
  • Seek written event confirmation from all participants not less than two weeks prior to travel.
  • Liaise with the ESA Communications team regarding any media or press engagements.

Pending Items and Outstanding Variables

5. Summary and Next Steps

The optimal window for Dr. Markovic’s participation is 10–12 May 2024, a period offering both high youth club accessibility and minimal professional conflicts. Should those dates become unfeasible, 7–9 June can serve as a backup, provided early reservation and coordination with the club are established. Immediate actions include validating dates with all parties, making flexible travel and venue arrangements, and ensuring all necessary technical and logistical protocols are in place.

To mitigate any last-minute changes, the preparation process should include a backup participant plan, rapid-response communication channels, and diligent documentation at all stages. By adhering to these recommendations, the success of the rocket launch event and the continuous professional obligations of Dr. Markovic can both be assured.
