Morning: Greek Yogurt Protein Bowl

I like to keep breakfast efficient and substantial so I can start work with steady energy. Each morning, I layer Greek yogurt with oats, blueberries, a bit of banana, mixed nuts, and a drizzle of honey—simple but incredibly satisfying. If I portion the oats and nuts into containers the night before, I can put this together in under five minutes.

Prep:
- Scoop 200 g Greek yogurt into a bowl
- Add 30 g rolled oats
- Top with 50 g fresh blueberries, half a banana (60 g, sliced), 10 g mixed nuts, and 5 g honey

Why it works for me: The yogurt offers around 23 g protein, while the combination of oats and fruit provides low-glycemic carbs and plenty of antioxidants. It keeps me alert and satisfied well into the morning, even on days packed with meetings or troubleshooting.

Allergy info: Contains dairy, nuts, and honey.

Lunch: Almond Croissant & Coffee at Bosbaan Café

On Saturdays, I treat myself to lunch at Bosbaan Café. I look forward to a soft almond croissant—rich, with just enough sweetness—and a hot cup of black coffee, sometimes with milk if I want a little comfort. It’s a brief but welcome ritual before the afternoon’s responsibilities.

Why this lunch?
The croissant is indulgent, but it’s a genuine pick-me-up amid the constant motion of my job. It offers quick carbs for energy, and the almond filling provides healthy fats and a hint of protein. The coffee is essential for sustaining afternoon focus and alertness.

Nutritional value:
The almond croissant has about 400 kcal, offering a nice energy boost. Coffee, depending on how I take it, adds up to 20 kcal.

Allergens:
Contains gluten, nuts (almond), egg, and dairy.

Evening: Dinner (Batch-Cooked Ratatouille)

After a long workday, I want a dinner that’s comforting but not heavy. My go-to is a bright, hearty ratatouille. It’s loaded with fresh, seasonal vegetables—eggplant, zucchini, peppers, onions, tomatoes—and elevated by a generous handful of herbs. I always make extra to save time later in the week.

Cooking directions (for 2 generous servings): - Chop all vegetables ahead of time if possible. - In a large pot, sauté onion and garlic in olive oil for a few minutes. - Add diced eggplant and bell peppers. Cook on medium heat until softened (about 10 minutes). - Toss in the zucchini, cherry tomatoes, and canned tomatoes. Sprinkle in fresh thyme and half the basil. - Let everything simmer uncovered for about 25 minutes until the veggies are completely soft and fragrant. - Finish with the rest of the basil, a dash of salt, and black pepper. - Serve warm, with 50 g whole grain bread if I want something extra hearty.

Batch tip:
I always double the recipe and divide it into containers. Ratatouille keeps well in the fridge for up to three days—ideal for effortless lunches or lazy-night dinners.

Nutrition and why I love it:
The dish is low in calories but high in fiber, vitamins (especially A and C), potassium, and antioxidants. After a day on the go, it feels restorative and light, yet still satisfying.

Allergy notes:
Some people (including myself) need to watch for nightshades—eggplant, peppers, and tomatoes. Bread (if added) contains gluten.

Luna’s Prescription Diet Routine

Luna, my cat, is on a strict veterinary renal diet due to her kidney condition. Every day, I serve her 85 g of prescription renal wet food, twice daily. I make sure not to introduce any treats or alternative foods, as even small deviations can set back her health.

  • Product: Vet-prescribed renal formula, picked up from Dierenkliniek Noordwijk.
  • Dosing & compliance: 85 g wet food per meal, morning and evening (confirmed with the vet). Hydration is just as important, so I always check her water bowl.
  • No exceptions: I stick to the prescribed feeding exactly—no human food, no regular cat food, and certainly no snacks, even when she asks! Maintaining her kidney function means giving her diet my full attention.

Time-Saving Strategies and Performance Habits

My job demands a clear head, so I streamline as much as possible: - Batch cooking: Doubling ratatouille saves me from scrambling for healthy meals during the workweek. - Evening prep: I often wash and chop vegetables in advance, and line up my dry breakfast ingredients into containers before bed. - Shopping once: Getting all my groceries at Albert Heijn in a single run makes things simple; I make sure Luna’s prescription is filled ahead of time to avoid last-minute pharmacy waits. - Efficient mornings and evenings: By minimizing time spent in the kitchen, I can dedicate early mornings and late evenings to work—or much-needed downtime.

Allergens and Dietary Considerations

  • Dinner: Ratatouille is gluten-free unless served with bread; contains nightshades, which can be a concern for some.
