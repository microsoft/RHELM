**Date:** 24 January 2024

## Overview

Every week, I put together a grocery shopping list that keeps pace with my work schedule and nutritional goals. Over time, I’ve found a systematic approach works best, especially when my calendar gets packed and errands have to be efficient. This note is designed for both quick reference and long-term usability—easy to print, edit on my computer, or check on my phone. It’s organized to help me remember essentials, manage risks like expired foods, and support healthy routines.

## Inventory & Planning Notes

### Pantry Check  
Before making my list, I do a quick scan of my kitchen—especially bread, milk, cereal, canned goods, and spices. I jot down anything running low or past its date, updating my master list as needed.

### Nutrition Focus  
A balanced diet is important to me. I aim for a colorful mix of vegetables (especially leafy greens and cruciferous varieties) and keep fruit selections seasonal where possible. Each week I review whether I’ve got enough protein, dairy, and carbohydrates to support both busy routines and recovery times.

### Busy Weeks & Contingencies  
On weeks with extra shifts or unpredictable schedules, I stock up on shelf-stable and quick-prep items: nuts, dried fruit, granola bars, canned fish, and beans. I prefer having frozen versions of essentials for stress-free meals if I get home late. For sudden emergencies or to save time, I include quick-fix foods like canned soups, simple pasta/rice dishes, and healthy snack options. If cleaning or paper products (like dish soap or paper towels) are low, I add those, too.

### Preferences & Brand Selection  
My tastes and dietary needs shift over time, so this list remains flexible. If a new favorite appears or I need to accommodate a specialty product, I adjust accordingly. For product specifics, I refer to European Food Safety Authority guidelines or check manufacturer info, such as Danone’s yogurt varieties.

### Document & Version Management  
To keep things manageable, I date each revision and maintain the table in a modular format—easy to update for print or digital use. Saving a copy as a spreadsheet or PDF helps track changes over time and makes edits quick.

---

## Documentation Practices

I've borrowed a few principles from my engineering and academic work—mostly to avoid missing essentials and keep everything streamlined:

- Items are clearly listed with brands, quantities, and notes so I can scan quickly.
- I use a checkbox format for both weekly audits and shopping trips, whether on paper or digital devices.
- Each list is dated for easy tracking and future reference.
- Actionable points and risk notes help make sure nothing important slips through the cracks.

This organized approach simplifies shopping and supports my nutritional goals, no matter how busy I get.
