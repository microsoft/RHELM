2. Morning Yoga: Biomechanics, Equipment, and Occupational Adaptation

Each morning, I carve out time for yoga, focusing heavily on wrist mechanics because both my job and daily routines have me interacting with computers for hours at a stretch. Today, I concentrated on Downward Dog and Plank—poses notorious for stressing the wrists, with loads often above my own bodyweight ([1]). Over the years, I've seen how these microtraumas can add up, especially in tech-heavy professions like mine ([3]).

To protect my wrists, I use a lightweight, soft neoprene brace that provides compression without restricting movement. Research shows these braces lower peak pressure on the carpal region and help with extension-related pain, which I noticed myself—no pain during the session as long as I wore the brace ([2]). My mat is a dense, slightly inclined one, designed to further spread the load away from the carpals.

I made several modifications today: gently elevating my palms using a rolled mat, keeping my fingers fully splayed, and shifting weight more towards the finger pads than the heels of my hands. I also limited the duration of each pose and introduced micro-pauses to give my tendons a break. These changes not only made a difference in comfort, but align with best-practice recommendations for people at high risk of RSI ([3]).

3. Mediterranean-Style Breakfast & Cognitive Health

I’ve found a Mediterranean-style breakfast works best for me, providing the energy and clarity I need for the day. My plate this morning included 200g Greek yogurt, 15g walnuts, a drizzle of honey, a big handful of fresh blueberries, and sage tea. Altogether, this meal totaled about 340 kcal, with 20g protein, 38g carbohydrates, 13g fat, 5.5g fiber, and 1g omega-3s, alongside plenty of calcium, vitamin D, and polyphenols.

The rationale is grounded both in research and my own experience. Studies show such a breakfast supports cognitive performance and emotional resilience—helpful both on Earth and for those of us working towards long-duration space missions ([4], [5], [6]). The anti-inflammatory polyphenols and omega-3s from berries and walnuts help keep my mind sharp, and the calcium from yogurt supports long-term bone health. I find this combination helps my focus last well into the afternoon. I logged all nutritional details in NutriLog for ongoing trend monitoring—a habit I've maintained for several years ([7]).
