## 2. Shopping Incident Summary

Despite my intentions to shop systematically, things didn’t quite go as planned. When I learned Luna wasn't well, my focus definitely shifted. I kept thinking about her as I moved through the store, which made it tough to keep my shopping on track. The time pressure meant I moved quicker than usual. I did a mental checklist as I dashed through the aisles, but I can see now that wasn’t enough—especially in the fresh produce section, where I missed grabbing both the basil and feta cheese. These kinds of items often require more conscious attention, and under stress, it's easy to overlook them.

## 3. Post-Action Reflection and Root Cause Analysis

After unpacking my groceries at home, I saw immediately that I’d missed two crucial ingredients—fresh basil and feta. This wasn't a one-off fluke; it’s happened before, especially when I’m preoccupied. Looking back on the experience, I see several reasons for the oversight:

- **Time Pressure:** The sense of rushing, combined with real emotional distraction about Luna, made it harder to double-check my list carefully.
- **Distraction and Preoccupation:** My mental bandwidth was caught up in worrying about my cat, which made it hard to focus on details like fresh herbs and cheeses.
- **List Management:** Although I had my list on my phone, I didn’t keep it open and actively mark things off as I went—something that always helps but is easy to skip when rushing.
- **Store Navigation:** I went through the store out of my usual order, and the fresh section where basil and feta are located was particularly rushed. Those departments often get missed when I’m distracted or short on time.

Missing these two ingredients was frustrating, as both play key roles in the dishes I’d planned. It was a small but meaningful disruption to my evening plans, and it underscored how small process gaps can have real impacts.

## 4. Strategies for Improvement (Next Time)

Reflecting on this, it’s clear that I need a more robust approach for shopping—especially under stressful circumstances. Here’s what I plan to do:

### 1. Make the List Dynamic and Central
Instead of relying on memory or glancing at a list here and there, I’ll use a digital shopping app with live check-off features. Placing a high-importance "flag" on fresh, perishable items could help. A glanceable list on my watch or phone would make staying organized easier, even when distracted.

### 2. Pre-Organize the Route
I want to arrange my lists to match the store’s typical layout. By putting fresh produce and dairy at the end and using subcategories (like "Herbs," "Cheeses," "Pantry staples"), I'll be less likely to miss essential items, even if I’m in a hurry.

### 3. Build in Contingency Time
I’ll try to avoid last-minute shopping under emotional pressure, but when emergencies arise, I’ll allow a buffer (arriving a bit earlier or delegating the trip if possible). Creating a quick "review before you leave" system—either a notification or a final full-list scan in the checkout line—should also help.

### 4. Enlist Backup When Needed
If I ever find myself this distracted, I’ll reach out to a friend or family member for a checklist review, either before I go or by sending a quick photo of my cart. Sometimes a second set of eyes catches things I’ve overlooked.

### 5. Reflect and Adapt
I'll continue logging missed items and their causes so I can spot patterns or recurring pitfalls. Regular reviews of my approach should help me fine-tune the process, making shopping less prone to errors.
