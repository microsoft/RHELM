3. My Physical Wellness: Focus on Wrist Health

This afternoon, after a long stretch of typing, I noticed mild stiffness in my wrists—particularly after sustained documentation updates. I rated it at 3 out of 10, higher than my usual baseline of 1. Using my goniometer, I confirmed a slight limitation in range: 65° flexion, 68° extension. Importantly, there was no swelling, tenderness, or redness.

I adapted quickly: shifted to a vertical mouse, adjusted my keyboard, and stuck strictly to the ESA-recommended protocol—taking a 5-minute stretch break every hour. Wrist holds were minimized and I paid special attention to posture, especially since fieldwork earlier involved holding my mobile device. By evening, my wrists felt back to normal. These interventions have really paid off—any signs of repetitive strain need to be addressed early, and I plan to continue trialing different ergonomic options moving forward.

All these practices draw from both EU-OSHA and ESA’s own best practice guidelines ([2], [3]), and I’m increasingly aware of the need to preempt wrist issues in myself and my team.

4. Reflections & Project Management Insights

Today was a mix of energizing team collaboration and focused solo work. My mood stayed stable, alert, and upbeat, especially during morning workshops—a testament to the importance of thorough preparation and good nutrition. Cognitive energy dipped slightly after lunch, but a quick walk and water break brought me back on track for the rest of the day.

Key Accomplishments: - We reached consensus on FDIR reliability standards in the workshop, immediately incorporating them into our FMEA draft. That kind of decisive progress is rare and motivating. - The afternoon field test of MapAppBeta was particularly satisfying; iterative, hands-on feedback is invaluable for refining user-facing systems. - By day’s end, I wrapped up all critical documentation, ensuring alignment with both ECSS and ISO frameworks. This not only accelerates compliance sign-off but also sets us up for a smoother mission phase transition.

Areas I Want to Improve: - Wrist stiffness after long writing sessions remains an issue. I’m exploring alternative input methods like voice-to-text and will be stricter about scheduling micro-breaks, particularly as new EU-OSHA findings recommend. - MapApp’s alert logic will need tweaks. Too many false alarms can undermine team confidence, especially when cognitive load is high. I’ll be working closely with the software team to streamline notifications. - Our team’s cross-cultural cooperation is good, but there’s always room to strengthen open communication. We’ll plan more regular workshops focused on multinational collaboration—key to rapid, cohesive incident response.

Actionable Insights for ESA Project Management: - Embedding regular health and wellness monitoring—both for myself and team members (and, of course, for Luna)—directly supports long-term performance and mission safety. ESA and ESA Academy guidance underscores this approach. - Integrating flexible schedules and mandatory breaks into daily workflow isn’t just good practice; it’s essential for sustaining cognitive energy and reducing injury risk. - Structured, real-time feedback from technical field tests (like today’s MapApp UAT) should feed straight into process and product improvements, reinforcing ESA’s emphasis on reliability and agility. - Keeping project documentation transparent and routinely up to date ensures fast, smooth compliance checks, which prevents unnecessary delays when transitioning between mission phases.

Throughout the day, I was reminded again how interconnected wellness and work performance are. Investing in both pays dividends at every level, from project outcomes to personal satisfaction.
