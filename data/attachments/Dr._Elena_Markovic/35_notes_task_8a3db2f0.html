Personal Health & Wellness Plan Update – April 15

Executive Summary

Following a recent comprehensive health screening and wrist evaluation with Dr. Elena Markovic at Medisch Centrum Noordwijk, I received some encouraging results and actionable feedback. My wrist flexibility has improved significantly: extension increased from 50° to 65°, and flexion from 45° to 60°. This marks a return to the functional range for nearly all my daily tasks, including work and music practice. Swelling in my wrist has also decreased—measured at the carpal level, from 0.8 cm to just 0.2 cm—and my pain on the Visual Analog Scale dropped from 4/10 to 1/10. In addition, blood tests revealed a mild vitamin D deficiency, with serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D at 23 ng/mL (where under 30 ng/mL is considered insufficient).

Drawing from these findings, I’ve put together a targeted plan to further support recovery, address the vitamin D deficit, and optimize my work and lifestyle routines. This integrates medical recommendations, physiotherapy, ergonomic adjustments, and personal wellness strategies that fit the demands of a systems engineering workflow.

Prescribed Physiotherapy & Exercise Protocols

Wrist Flexibility & Strength (Physiotherapy Plan)

Building on my therapist’s recommendations, I’m tailoring my daily routine to strengthen my wrist while minimizing overuse. Here’s my updated plan:

  • Wrist Extension Stretch: With my arm outstretched and palm facing down, I gently pull my fingers back, holding each stretch for 30 seconds, three times, twice a day. This helps loosen tight flexors and prevents contracture.
  • Wrist Flexor Stretch: Arm extended, palm up, I pull my fingers downward, again holding for 30 seconds each rep, three times per session, twice daily.
  • Isometric Wrist Extension: Using my other hand or a band for support, I extend my wrist against resistance for 12 slow repetitions, repeated three times per session, twice a day. This strengthens without overloading.
  • Tendon Gliding Drills: I run through a cycle of five hand positions (fist, hook, straight, tabletop, full flexion), doing three sets of five reps each day. These exercises keep my tendons moving freely.
  • Elastic Band Resistance (Yellow Band): Twice a week, I do controlled supination/pronation and gentle extension exercises, stopping if discomfort rises above mild levels.

My physiotherapist reviews my progress every two weeks, making adjustments based on tolerance and range of motion improvements.

Optimized Daily Routine & Ergonomics

Adapting my habits at both my workstation and piano bench has made a real difference in comfort and productivity. Here’s what I’ve implemented:

  • Computer Work: I cap typing sessions at 30 minutes before taking a five-minute movement or stretch break. Using a Pomodoro timer app helps me stay on track.
  • Piano Practice: Sessions last no longer than 20 minutes at a stretch, followed by a three-minute rest where I shake out my hands, rotate my wrists, and check alignment. A soft foam wedge under my wrists encourages a neutral position.
  • Workstation Setup: My keyboard is angled for wrist neutrality, and the desk is set at a slight negative tilt. My chair is adjusted so elbows rest at a 90° angle, with my monitor at eye level.

These changes take some getting used to, but already I feel less fatigue and tension, especially during long sessions.

Nutrition & Supplementation: Addressing Vitamin D Deficiency

Supplement Plan (Per Clinical Guidelines)

To address my mild vitamin D deficiency, I started taking 1,000 IU (25 mcg) vitamin D3 daily, with plans to reassess blood levels in three months. I’ve also increased dietary sources by eating oily fish (like salmon and sardines) twice a week, including more egg yolks, and choosing fortified dairy or plant-based milks. I make a point to get at least 15 minutes of midday sunlight on my forearms whenever the weather allows.

These steps follow recommendations from the Endocrine Society and EFSA ([Holis et al., 2011][6]).

Traditional Wellness: Herbal Tea Ritual

In addition to supplements, I revived a comforting morning ritual with my mother’s herbal tea blend. Each day at breakfast, I prepare a cup using: - 1 tsp dried nettle leaf - ½ tsp lemon balm - ½ tsp St. John’s Wort - ½ tsp elderflowers - 1 tsp honey (added after steeping)

I add the herbs to a teapot, pour in 250 ml of freshly boiled water, and let it steep covered for seven minutes before straining and adding honey. Drinking this tea connects me to familiar routines and supports my overall wellbeing, offering magnesium and calcium for bones, anti-inflammatory properties, and a gentle mood boost during recovery (see [HerbalGram][7], [WHO Monographs][8]). While it doesn’t replace vitamin D, I value the micronutrients and comfort this practice provides.

Digital Health Monitoring & Accountability

To make sure I’m following the plan, I rely on a few tech tools: - Wearables: My Garmin Vivosmart tracks my daily steps, sunlight exposure, and sedentary time. - Mobile Apps: I use MyTherapy to remind me about vitamins and supplements, and Pomodoro timers (like Focus Booster) keep me honest about my work and practice breaks. - Custom Dashboard: I log my stretching, piano and typing durations, pain scores, and supplement intake in a personalized Google Sheet, which I review every couple of weeks—sometimes with my physiotherapist.

This data-driven approach keeps me accountable and helps me notice patterns or setbacks early.
