# IAC Milan Panel Discussion Summary and Feedback Report  
**Event Date:** January 10, 2024  
**Prepared for:** European Space Agency (ESA) Internal Review & External Benchmarking

---

## Executive Summary

The IAC Milan panel discussion brought together leaders, technical experts, and policymakers from major European and international space agencies to address critical issues facing collaborative space exploration today. Participants examined the practical challenges of inter-agency cooperation, the integration of diverse technical policy frameworks, effective risk management strategies, and the complexities inherent in multi-national communication. While the event succeeded in fostering meaningful dialogue, it was also marked by a significant technical delay and a prominent miscommunication with the Roscosmos delegation. These incidents underscored ongoing challenges and pointed to specific areas for improvement in future collaborations.

**Key Insights and Action Points:**

- **Enhanced Cross-Agency Collaboration:** Panelists reached clear consensus on the need to strengthen joint programme governance and to systematically share mission planning and technology transfer best practices.
- **Technical Policy Alignment:** There were explicit calls for harmonizing safety, cybersecurity, and data-sharing protocols among agencies, facilitating smoother and more secure cooperation.
- **Risk Management and Operational Resilience:** A 15-minute technical disruption brought attention to the necessity of consistent contingency planning and standardized responses.
- **Cross-Cultural Communication:** Communication challenges with the Roscosmos delegation highlighted risks associated with language barriers and different procedural expectations.
- **Audience Reflections:** Feedback emphasized the value of open debate but also pointed to the need for clearer implementation strategies and more inclusive audience engagement in Q&A sessions.

To address these findings, the report recommends:  
- Establishing formal inter-agency technical rehearsal protocols and robust contingency systems  
- Standardizing pre-event briefings and documentation for all delegates  
- Expanding cross-cultural training and ensuring professional interpretation services for non-EU participants

## Incident Analysis: Miscommunication with Roscosmos Delegate

**Root Cause Examination**

The difficulties involving the Roscosmos delegate stemmed from several intersecting factors:

- **Language and Protocol Preparation:** Roscosmos did not receive a verified session script or access to a certified interpreter, which deviated from standard ECSS-Q-ST-80-01 protocol for multi-agency events.
- **Technical Challenges:** Audio connection problems disrupted real-time translation, which compounded confusion regarding the schedule and content of Roscosmos' intervention.
- **Coordination Gaps:** Pre-event briefings failed to properly align ESA and Roscosmos expectations, particularly regarding speaking time and the format of Q&A sessions.

**Protocol Review and Shortcomings**

Event coordination did not fully apply established ESA protocols for multilingual sessions (ref: ECSS-E-70). Critical aspects, including pre-event translation checks and detailed protocol briefings, were incomplete for non-EU participants. The lack of contingency planning for live translation failures and escalation measures further exposed operational gaps.

**Immediate and Long-term Steps Undertaken**

- **At the Event:** The moderator reverted to English-only for the Q&A. Written statements from Roscosmos were compiled and shared post-panel to ensure their viewpoints were included in the event record.
- **Post-Event Measures:** ESA and Roscosmos have now scheduled joint debriefings to review protocol lapses, and both agencies have agreed to standardize briefing packets, verify translation arrangements, and include on-demand interpreters for all future panels.

Multiple respondents encouraged organizers to create more time-resilient agendas and to adopt clearer communication protocols for broader, more inclusive participation.

## Recommendations

The following recommendations are proposed for ESA and partner organizations to ensure future events are both effective and resilient:

### 1. Strengthening Technical and Operational Resilience

- Implement robust technical redundancy through additional A/V backups and comprehensive pre-session system checks, in line with ECSS-Q-ST-80-01 standards.
- Designate a technical monitor during live sessions, with authority to address disruptions promptly and to coordinate contingency measures.

### 2. Advancing Cross-Cultural Communication

- Require certified interpreters and fully validated scripts for all sessions involving non-EU delegates, adhering to ECSS-E-70 guidelines.
- Expand pre-event briefings to all agencies, requiring submission of Q&A preferences and specific translation needs in advance.

### 3. Optimizing Agenda and Stakeholder Engagement

- Integrate flexible buffer periods into session agendas to accommodate unforeseen delays without sacrificing quality audience engagement or interactive segments.
- Pilot a system for prioritizing Q&A based on pre-submitted questions, ensuring broader stakeholder voices are heard and that time pressures do not crowd out diverse perspectives.

### 4. Systematic Inter-Agency Benchmarking

- Institutionalize formal post-event debriefings, with participating agencies contributing to joint reports that identify lessons learned and actionable improvements.
- Update ESA’s Knowledge Management System with cross-referenced incident reports, enabling continual refinement of operating procedures and sharing of best practices across the agency network.
