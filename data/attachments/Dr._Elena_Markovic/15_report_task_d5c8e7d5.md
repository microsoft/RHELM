## Meeting Objectives

The purpose of this meeting was to review recent progress on the LunaLink initiative, with a specific focus on advancing cross-agency collaboration between the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA). The session addressed updates surrounding the joint CubeSat deployment proposal, targeting alignment of technical objectives, resource planning, and risk mitigation strategies. Particular emphasis was placed on harmonizing methodologies between both agencies to enable an efficient and coherent project trajectory.

The meeting agenda included:
- Technical debrief on CubeSat subsystem integration  
- Identification and assessment of project risk factors  
- Resource allocation and cross-agency expertise sharing  
- Strategies to enhance operational synergy  
- Evaluation of collaboration processes and documentation standards

## Discussion Highlights

During the meeting, Elena Markovic emphasized the strategic value of the current CubeSat cooperation, recommending an early scoping exercise in the upcoming quarter to assess options for an expanded mission profile. Marcus van Dijk highlighted the necessity of coordinating regulatory review schedules for payload certification, ensuring simultaneous progress through both ESA and JAXA channels. 

An important open question was raised regarding governance for mid-project scope changes, especially as technology demonstrations yield unforeseen adjustments. The LunaLink team collectively suggested implementing bi-monthly risk reassessment meetings to address emerging technical or logistical challenges and maintain transparency.

---

## Next Steps and Recommendations

Moving forward, the team will focus on:
- Refining and unifying technical standards for CubeSat integration and subsystem testing  
- Coordinating with launch providers to secure a stable schedule  
- Deepening collaboration through targeted cross-training and operational framework alignment  
- Streamlining documentation and governance structures to sustain efficiency  

The team remains committed to fostering a collaborative environment that leverages the strengths of both ESA and JAXA for innovative solutions in lunar small satellite deployment. Regular technical reviews and transparent risk assessments will support ongoing adaptability as the project progresses.
