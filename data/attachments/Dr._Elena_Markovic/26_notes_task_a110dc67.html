Summary

Key Clinical Observations and Developments Compared to Previous Days

Luna’s appetite remained robust throughout the day. She ate between 95–100% of each meal provided, showing consistent interest in food both at main feedings and snack times. Food offered consisted exclusively of Hill’s Prescription Diet k/d Early Support for renal support, divided over four meals to maintain even intake and optimize digestion. During the evening meal, I introduced a small portion of the k/d renal chicken wet food sachet alongside her usual dry kibble, which she accepted well and ate almost entirely. This addition was intended to promote hydration and enhance overall palatability, a strategy that will be continued pending favorable ongoing response.

There were no episodes of vomiting or regurgitation overnight or during the day. Luna’s tolerance to the current dietary and medication regimen continues to be excellent, and there have been no GI disturbances or changes in stool or urinary habits.

Her medication protocol continues as prescribed. Amlodipine remains unchanged at 1.25mg orally each morning, in line with Dr. van der Meer’s recommendations, reflecting ongoing control of her blood pressure as recorded during the last assessment. Telmisartan dosage was increased this evening to 0.625mg by mouth, up from 0.5mg, following direct consultation with Dr. van der Meer. The adjustment was recommended to further manage proteinuria based on results from her latest urinalysis and a review of current clinical guidelines.

In terms of behavior, Luna maintains an alert, interactive, and playful demeanor. Today, I noticed a mild increase in her play activity, particularly in the early evening after her meal. Social interactions and her response to environmental stimuli remain normal, with no signs of withdrawal or discomfort.

Physical examination findings—including heart rate (140 bpm), respiratory rate (22/min), temperature (38.1°C), hydration status, mucous membrane color, and capillary refill time—are all within normal limits and consistent with previous observations. She shows no overt signs of dehydration, and urinary output remains appropriate. Her body weight remains stable at 4.2kg.

Trends, Monitoring Points, and Potential Concerns

Following this evening’s increase in Telmisartan, I will closely monitor Luna for any side effects commonly associated with angiotensin receptor blockade, including gastrointestinal upset, changes in appetite, or signs suggestive of hypotension (such as lethargy or weakness). This will be especially important over the next 48–72 hours, as these effects, if present, are most likely to become apparent shortly after dosage changes.

No appetite reduction, vomiting, diarrhea, or behavioral changes have been observed, but daily monitoring remains critical to ensure any developing concerns are quickly identified and addressed.

Luna’s compliance with her prescription renal diet remains high. Continued attention will be paid to her body weight, hydration status, and laboratory parameters as they become available to ensure sustained nutritional and systemic balance.

Log Format and Best Practices for Integration

All log entries include the date and time, with clear, detailed documentation of dietary intake, medication administration, and clinical rationales for any changes. Clinical observations and events are recorded promptly, alongside narrative and objective findings. Any medication adjustments are documented with reference to veterinary consultation, ensuring a transparent and auditable clinical record.

Where applicable, standard veterinary terminology is used for clarity and consistency. This log is formatted for direct integration with electronic health record (EHR) systems, enabling efficient export or import for use by veterinary clinical teams. The data capture structure is designed to support accurate longitudinal trend monitoring, rapid identification of emerging issues, and collaborative, evidence-based management, following international best practices for veterinary medical documentation.
