Discussion Summary

1. Avionics and Software Subsystem

The meeting opened with a focus on avionics, where we reviewed the new firmware (v2.3.8). Testing so far has been promising; all bench validations passed, though integration with the surrounding satellite systems is not yet finalized. During software simulation, a redundant bus anomaly was flagged, specifically a minor timing deviation.

Engineers noted that CPU utilization spikes during navigation transitions—measured at 53% versus the baseline 43%—need additional analysis to ensure performance remains within operational limits. One key challenge persists with CAN bus error frames under electromagnetic interference (EMI) testing, indicating unresolved signal integrity issues.

Risks & Next Steps:
The primary risk lies in possible schedule delays unless the identified software issue is patched before final qualification testing (FQT). Incremental firmware updates are advised, coupled with a targeted root-cause investigation into EMI-related errors. Catarina Sousa will oversee both the firmware update and EMI analysis, with support from QA.

2. Propulsion Subsystem

The propulsion team reported a pressure anomaly in the secondary tank: measurements fell just outside standard tolerance (20.9 bar, specification 21–25 bar). Fast-cycle valve operation revealed intermittent telemetry, occasionally losing real-time readings. Specific impulse is trending 1.2% below expectations, prompting a review of thermal margins and their effect on performance.

Valve calibration continued to pose a challenge, as drift was detected during consistency checks. To address these issues, an extended one-day test cycle is proposed, giving the team additional time to calibrate sensors and analyze actuation logic.

Risks & Next Steps:
Although the risk level is deemed moderate, further sensor calibration and logic review are scheduled, with Jean-Paul Laurent taking lead responsibility on both.

3. Thermal Control Subsystem

Thermal subsystem discussions highlighted increased sensor jitter on Panel B, along with a delayed response—active heating phases lagged by 13 seconds from the setpoint. Radiator efficiency continues to fall slightly below targets, now averaging 2.4% under specification.

Limited real-time data from stress testing was attributed to workstation connectivity problems affecting Elena Petrova, whose ability to participate was further compromised by wrist pain. To minimize disruption, Ingrid Rasmussen has been designated as backup for technical leadership.

Risks & Next Steps:
The subsystem is operational but exposed to personnel continuity risks. Immediate priorities include controller firmware re-validation and more granular thermal sampling.

4. Communications Subsystem

Communications performance was mixed. The team logged temporary downlink signal degradation (2dB drop) during cross-band switches. However, improvement was seen in antenna alignment—protocol changes reduced RMS error from 0.48° to 0.25°.

A further issue arose with the antenna drive motor, which overheated briefly under a 30W sustained load; hardware checks have yet to conclusively explain this. Multipath errors persisted on the ground testbed but remained local in scope.

Risks & Next Steps:
These are considered low priority but warrant ongoing monitoring. Next steps include refining alignment algorithms and expanding cooling verification in subsequent tests, led by Olaf Müller.

5. Payload & Optics Subsystem

Payload discussions centered on intensity drift in the calibration lamp—7% margin was flagged, with root cause analysis ongoing. The vibration isolation mount still requires validation under high g-load conditions. Additionally, the team noted optical signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) 1.8 dB below predictions, likely due to stray light ingress.

Integration with the avionics subsystem has been delayed by bus timing issues, reinforcing the need for a schedule buffer between teams.

Risks & Next Steps:
Stray light analysis is being accelerated, and the test-bed will be upgraded to improve isolation. Marek Novak is responsible for leading these activities.

6. Systems Integration

Integration tests revealed synchronization errors at several subsystem data convergence points, with overall test matrix coverage at 79% (target: 90%). Missing test inputs and resource allocation bottlenecks have contributed to delays. Latency issues have also increased due to repeated interface handshake problems.

Risks & Next Steps:
Risks in this area are medium-to-high due to potential cross-system schedule impact. The integration engineering team is being reallocated to ensure matrix completion, and further workshops are scheduled for coordination.

7. Risk Management & Quality Assurance

Risk review identified three emerging issues requiring mitigation: firmware delays, propulsion telemetry reliability, and resource shortfalls in integration. No critical compliance issues were reported by QA, though some minor documentation discrepancies require attention.

Next Steps:
Key risks are being prioritized for mitigation, and the risk register is set for an immediate update. QA has requested standardized reporting templates to streamline cross-team documentation.
