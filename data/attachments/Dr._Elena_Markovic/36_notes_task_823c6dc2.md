### 2.2 Dish Details

#### Poached Eggs with Wilted Spinach, Cherry Tomatoes & Olives

**Ingredients (per serving):**
- 2 large eggs (~110g)
- 50g fresh spinach
- 80g cherry tomatoes (~6–8 tomatoes)
- 15g pitted Kalamata olives (~4 medium olives)
- 5ml extra-virgin olive oil (1 tsp)
- Water for poaching
- Black pepper, sea salt to taste

**Preparation:**
1. I start by rinsing the spinach, cherry tomatoes, and olives. The scent of fresh spinach always wakes me up a bit. After halving the tomatoes and slicing the olives, I set them aside.
2. Next, I heat the olive oil in a nonstick pan and gently wilt the spinach over medium heat for about 1–2 minutes. I love how quickly it cooks down—it smells bright and earthy. Once wilted, I plate it.
3. For the eggs, I bring about a liter of water to a gentle simmer in a saucepan. Sometimes I add a splash of vinegar to help the whites set neatly. I crack each egg into its own ramekin, then carefully slide them into the simmering water. I always watch the time to get the yolks runny—about 3 to 4 minutes—then lift the eggs out with a slotted spoon and let them drain on a paper towel.
4. Finally, I layer the spinach on a plate, scatter the tomatoes and olives, then nestle the poached eggs on top. I finish with a grind of pepper and a pinch of sea salt.

**Nutritional profile (per serving):**

| Energy | Protein | Fat (SFA) | Carbs | Fiber | B12  | Choline | Iron | Folate | Magnesium | Potassium | Omega-3 (ALA) |
|--------|---------|-----------|-------|-------|------|---------|------|--------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| 255 kcal | 15g | 19g (4.6g) | 7g | 2g | 2.2μg | 280mg | 2.7mg | 140μg | 50mg | 480mg | ~60mg        |

*B12 and choline are especially critical here; both are essential for neuron signaling and overall cognitive clarity throughout long operational days.*

**Why I chose this dish:**  
Eggs are a staple because they deliver lasting satiety and robust protein to kickstart recovery and keep my focus sharp. The spinach and tomatoes add freshness plus fiber, while the olives and olive oil contribute healthy fats, which support cardiovascular health—a foundation for high-endurance work. My mornings often start in front of a bank of monitors, and I find this combination steadies my energy and mood.

#### Greek Yogurt with Walnuts

**Ingredients (per serving):**
- 150g plain Greek yogurt (2% fat)
- 15g raw walnuts (~7 halves), roughly chopped
- 5g honey (optional—sometimes I crave a touch of sweetness)

**Preparation:**
1. I spoon the yogurt into a bowl—simple and cool, a nice contrast to the warmth of the eggs.
2. Walnuts get chopped and sprinkled on top.
3. When I treat myself, I drizzle just half a teaspoon of honey.

**Nutritional profile (per serving):**

| Energy | Protein | Fat (SFA) | Carbs | Fiber | Calcium | Magnesium | Potassium | B6   | Omega-3 (ALA)         |
|--------|---------|-----------|-------|-------|---------|-----------|-----------|------|-----------------------|
| 162 kcal | 10g | 9g (1.2g) | 11g | 1g | 193mg | 38mg | 242mg | 0.14mg | ~1100mg               |

**Performance insights:**  
The yogurt base supplies a blend of casein and whey for both quick and sustained amino acid availability. Calcium and magnesium are crucial, not just for bone health, but for muscle control and nerve signaling—things I need when piloting simulations or conducting hands-on research. Walnuts add not just pleasant crunch, but a potent dose of alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), a plant omega-3 that's increasingly linked with cognitive performance under pressure.

## 3. Ingredient-Performance Rationale

I always reflect on why each component is on my plate:

- **Eggs** offer complex amino acids and choline, which underpin learning, memory, and mental acuity. During periods of intense data analysis or briefings, I can feel the difference on days I have eggs versus when I don't.
- **Spinach** is my go-to for folate, magnesium, and potassium. Folate keeps my neural pathways in good order, and the mineral profile helps with blood pressure and sustained energy.
- **Cherry tomatoes** provide vitamin C and lycopene—these antioxidants help me fend off the oxidative stress that accumulates after long hours in enclosed technical labs.
- **Kalamata olives and extra-virgin olive oil** are loaded with monounsaturated fats, which support cardiovascular health and reduce inflammation from stress and erratic work hours.
- **Greek yogurt** brings in more calcium and B vitamins for both muscle health and nerve function.
- **Walnuts** have more than just omega-3s; polyphenols in them support neuroprotection and can boost problem-solving and memory. After including them more frequently, I’ve noticed greater mental clarity in late-morning tasks.

## 4. Veterinary Nutrition: Luna’s CKD Meal Protocol

### 4.1 Breakfast for Luna (10 kg Dog with Chronic Kidney Disease)

**Diet**  
Luna’s meal follows my veterinarian’s instructions—she’s on a commercial renal-support diet formulated for CKD (such as Hill’s k/d, Royal Canin Renal, or Purina NF). Today, she eats 150g of the wet variety, portioned for her size.

- **Wet food:** 150g at 08:00
- **Dry food equivalent:** 40–50g kibble, as shown on the specific label

I adhere to standards from AAFCO, ACVIM, and WSAVA—keeping her protein, phosphorus, and sodium within precise ranges to protect her kidneys, and ensuring she gets enough omega-3s.

**Sample nutrition (per 150g wet food):**

| Energy | Protein | Phosphorus | Sodium | Potassium | Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) |
|--------|---------|------------|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| 120 kcal | 6g | 0.08g | 0.07g | 0.19g | 100mg         |

Key targets (per day, for 10kg dog):  
- **Protein**: 2.0–3.0g/kg body weight (20–30g/d) – restricted to lower uremic toxin buildup  
- **Phosphorus**: <0.45g/1000 kcal – controls CKD progression  
- **Sodium**: <0.30g/1000 kcal – supports fluid balance  
- **Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)**: anti-inflammatory, helps slow renal decline

**Behavioral Feeding Log (ACVIM/WSAVA standards):**  
- 08:00 – Served meal, Luna approaches bowl right away.
- 08:05 – Eats steadily, leaving less than 5% residue.
- 08:10 – Bowl removed; appetite rated “Good”.
- Water refreshed after meal.
- No signs of nausea, food aversion, or regurgitation.

I keep a detailed log of Luna’s eating, appetite, and hydration (as recommended) and am alert for any signs of distress or changes in intake—vigilance here means timely adjustments to keep her healthy.
