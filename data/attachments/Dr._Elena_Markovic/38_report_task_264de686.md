## 1. Workshop Overview

On April 20, 2024, the Noordwijk Community Center welcomed a diverse group of youth and adult participants for a hands-on model rocketry workshop, led by Dr. Elena Markovic. Dr. Markovic—a seasoned aerospace engineer and ESA consultant—brought her deep expertise in rocketry systems, STEM outreach, and safety management to the event. Her leadership shaped the session into an engaging, technically rigorous experience designed for participants at various levels, with special attention to effective risk management and evidence-based learning.

## 4. Highlights from Group Discussion

Throughout the workshop, participants explored the principles of rocketry as well as real-world safety protocols and teamwork strategies. Dr. Markovic facilitated engaging conversations about hazard identification, incident prevention, and the importance of adherence to ESA standards during both construction and launch phases.

A pivotal moment occurred when Sofia asked:  
> “What design changes can we make to increase altitude while preserving stability and safety in our model rockets?”

This question sparked a group analysis of factors such as aerodynamic streamlining, optimal mass distribution, nozzle modifications, and innovative materials. Dr. Markovic guided the discussion by referencing ESA procedures for iterative prototyping and risk assessment, with participants weighing practical concerns like local wind conditions, payload weight limits, and recovery system reliability.

Additional topics included engine selection and thrust curve trade-offs, the influence of team decision-making on overall safety and engineering outcomes, and methods for supporting youth innovation through mentorship and open Q&A formats. The blend of youth curiosity and adult experience created a supportive environment for technical exchange, with each team member participating in data recording, quality assurance, and safety checks.

## 6. Reflections: Engagement, Learning, and Next Steps

### Workshop Engagement

The collaborative, mixed-age approach encouraged active involvement in each task, from rocket construction to technical questioning. Youth participants, especially Sofia, demonstrated leadership and innovative thinking, supported by adults who provided critical safety scaffolding and technical advice.

### Learning Outcomes

Participants built familiarity with rocketry concepts, safety procedures, and applied engineering skills. The custom design subgroup developed strategies for iterative prototyping and peer review, bringing evidence-based thinking to practical rocket design. The intergenerational teamwork promoted confidence and knowledge transfer among all attendees.

### Recommendations for Future Practice

- **Documentation:** Continue detailed recording of every build and launch, using ESA templates for risk logs and incident tracking.
- **Risk Assessment:** Integrate checklists and pre-session briefings for hazard identification, routinely updating procedures with learnings from each session.
- **Group Dynamics:** Rotate youth leadership roles to nurture developing skills, formalize adult mentorship, and reinforce a culture of inquiry and teamwork.
- **Feedback Channels:** Systematically collect feedback after each session to address challenges—such as sourcing materials—and streamline communication using digital tools where possible.
- **Innovation:** Support youth-led design projects and open Q&A forums, providing technical depth and encouraging agency in STEM activities.
