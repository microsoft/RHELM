## (A) Summary of the Day

Today felt like a string of small problems that kept stacking. None of them were catastrophic on their own, but they wore down my patience and focus.

### Key moments (what actually happened)

**1) Home — Bonsai monthly health review (morning)**  
I started the day with my monthly check on the rescued juniper, and the first thing I noticed was how much worse the yellowing looked compared to the last time I checked. Some leaves were curled, and near the trunk I saw fine white threads that read as fungal growth to me—especially paired with the overall decline. I tried an “emergency” response: I used cinnamon as a stopgap treatment and worked on improving drainage with a specialty organic bonsai soil. By the afternoon, about half the foliage had browned. I took photos because the progression was obvious, and seeing it side-by-side made it hard to stay optimistic.

**2) Solo desert sunrise hike near Short Canyon**  
I went out early for a sunrise hike, expecting the usual dry footing, but there was unexpected overnight humidity and the terrain felt slick. I slipped on loose gravel and scraped my palm, plus picked up a slight bruise on my knee—annoying more than serious, but it changed my mood. I brought my portable camera hoping for macro detail shots, and the macro function kept malfunctioning intermittently. My best bird and beetle moments ended up out of focus, and I suspect sand in the lens barrel from a previous outing. I also tried a new birdwatching app: it failed to sync GPS and then crashed when I logged vocalizations. I left the hike feeling like I’d worked hard for very little usable documentation.

**3) Lunch — team proposal work at Sagebrush Table Café**  
Lunch was supposed to be a reset and a productive work session, but it felt scattered. Emily arrived late because of roadwork delays. Priya was unwell and understandably not fully present. Maria stepped in, but she kept getting urgent volunteer texts and her attention was split. I ordered spicy lentil hand-pies; they were overbaked—dry and uneven—and I ate more from habit than appetite. My energy dipped hard. We tried to share rare animal photos, but the café Wi‑Fi kept cutting out and the moment of shared excitement ended faster than it should have.

**4) Afternoon grant writing**  
I tried to salvage the workday with grant writing, but my laptop froze twice while I was editing a section in real time. I lost several draft updates. That kind of loss hits me in two ways: it wastes time and it breaks momentum. By the second freeze I was working more cautiously than creatively.

**5) Evening**  
I attempted meditation to settle down, but my focus was poor and I cut the session short. I ended up journaling instead, and the through-line was clear: I’m concerned about team morale, annoyed by technical setbacks, and genuinely worried about the bonsai’s decline.
