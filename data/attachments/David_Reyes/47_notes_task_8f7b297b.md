## 4. Phenological Observations and Plant Status

**Leaf Morphology:**  
Since the May 2024 benchmark, I have observed a distinct increase in both leaf length and width. The foliage now exhibits a deep green coloration (chlorophyll intensity at 7 out of 9), with marginal enhancement and minimal curling. Leaf margins are smooth, lacking necrosis or signs of desiccation. Trichome (pubescence) density remains consistent with that seen prior to the spring drought event. Newly emerged leaf pairs are visible at apical nodes, corresponding to BBCH Scale 15 — five leaves unfolded [4][5].

**Plant Vigor:**  
The bonsai is demonstrating moderate to high vigor. Shoot extension has measured +3.2 cm over the past four weeks (previous period: +1.0 cm), suggesting a strong recovery trajectory. Leaf turgor is robust, with no evidence of wilting or water stress. According to the IWMPRAISE protocol, I rated turgidity at level 2, indicating full turgor [5].

**Branching Patterns:**  
Secondary shoots have begun to develop at the 2nd and 3rd nodes, indicating increased ramification compared to the April 2024 baseline. There are no observable signs of branch dieback or regression since the last monitoring session.

**Overall Plant Health:**  
The olive bonsai has no visible symptoms of disease or pest infestation. The bark remains smooth, free of cracks, and there are no indications of nutrient deficiency such as interveinal chlorosis or purpling. Foliage recovery currently aligns with BBCH Stage 15, consistent with post-stress recovery and "visible leaf expansion" phases according to [PHENOMED][5] and [USA-NPN][6] olive monitoring guidelines.
