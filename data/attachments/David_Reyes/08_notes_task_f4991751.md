# Field Journal Entry — Birdwatching Walk (2024-01-18)

## Date / Time
- **Date:** 2024-01-18  
- **Start:** **5:45 a.m. (pre-dawn)**  
- **End:** **Early return** (exact time not recorded; I turned back sooner than planned because the wind-driven sand made it uncomfortable and unproductive)

## Location
- **Gravel trail bordering Ridgecrest’s Desert Community Reserve**  
- **Turnaround / return point:** back to the **Subaru Outback** (retreated due to gusts, drifting sand, and irritation from grit)

## Purpose of the Walk
I went out for a short pre-dawn birding walk with the goal of catching early activity along the reserve edge and getting a few photographs before the day warmed up. The plan was a steady, quiet scan-and-listen walk along the gravel trail.

## Weather and Visibility
The conditions were dominated by **unexpected high winds** pushing **drifting sand** across the trail. The gusts weren’t just noisy—they actively reduced visibility and made it hard to keep my eyes open comfortably. Between the moving sand and the wind masking sound, bird detectability dropped fast and the walk turned into more of a “safe retreat” than a survey.

- **Wind:** strong, sustained gusts with enough force to move sand consistently  
- **Sand:** airborne grit and intermittent sheets of sand across the trail surface  
- **Other weather details (temperature, cloud cover, precipitation):** **not recorded**

## Equipment (carried)
- **Canon EOS 90D camera**  
- **Field journal**  
- **Garmin GPS**

## Conditions, Comfort, and Effect on Effort
Within minutes, the sand started finding every gap—eyes, sleeves, pockets, and camera controls. My **eyes became itchy** and I could feel **sinus irritation** building quickly, the kind that makes it hard to focus on listening and scanning. Even simple tasks (looking into the wind, pausing to write, lifting binoculars/camera) felt like work.

Photography became a non-starter after I noticed **fine grit in the camera’s focusing ring**. The ring started to feel rough and unreliable, and I didn’t want to grind sand into the mechanism. I stopped using the camera and kept it protected for the walk back.

## Bird Observations
Only one bird was detected clearly before the wind and sand overwhelmed both visibility and sound.

| Time | Place | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| ~**5:55 a.m.** (approx.) | Trail edge along the Desert Community Reserve | **Say’s Phoebe** — a **solitary individual**, briefly observed. After that, the gusts and drifting sand intensified and I didn’t detect additional birds by sight or sound. |

### Species note (context)
Say’s Phoebe is a common flycatcher in open country of the western U.S., often seen perched low and making short sallies for insects—behavior that fits the quick, solitary sighting I had during the brief window when I could still scan effectively.  
Source: Cornell Lab of Ornithology [1].

## Reflection — What Disrupted the Survey
This outing was cut short for practical reasons, and the limitations were consistent and immediate:

- **High winds reduced detection:** The wind masked subtle calls and made it hard to hold a steady visual scan. Movement in shrubs and grasses was constant, which further lowered confidence in detecting small birds.  
- **Drifting sand became the main constraint:** Grit in the air reduced visibility and made it uncomfortable to keep my face into the wind long enough to watch for movement along the trail edge.  
- **Eye and sinus irritation:** The itching and irritation escalated quickly, and once that started I had less patience for careful listening and note-taking. Returning to the vehicle was the sensible choice.  
- **Camera contamination risk:** Feeling grit in the focusing ring was a clear stop signal. Continuing to shoot would have risked mechanical wear and unreliable focus.

## Birding Kit Updates — Actionable Checklist (Add/Replace)
To make future windy-desert walks safer and more productive, I’m adding a few specific items and a simple post-walk routine:

- [ ] **Seal-tight eye protection** suitable for wind-driven sand (reduces itching and abrasion risk)  
- [ ] **Respiratory/sinus protection** for dusty, windy conditions (reduces irritation from inhaled grit)  
- [ ] **Camera wind/dust mitigation** (protective cover or wrap to shield body and controls during gusts)  
- [ ] **Field cleaning tools for grit** (blower/brush + microfiber cloth stored in a sealable bag) so sand can be removed before it reaches moving parts  
- [ ] **Resealable bags/pouches** to isolate the camera and journal when sand is actively drifting  
- [ ] **Post-outing maintenance step:** inspect moving rings/controls immediately after windy desert walks; stop use if grit is detected and clean before packing away
