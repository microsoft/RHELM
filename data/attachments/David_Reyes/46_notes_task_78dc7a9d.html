Best Practices for Desert Fieldwork

Camera Gear:
Select equipment designed for rugged environments, prioritizing weather-sealed bodies and lenses. Consistent cleaning is crucial, especially when exposed to airborne particulates, as recommended by Canon. Periodically check for dust infiltration and clean sensors before and after outings.

Backpack & Hydration:
Choosing an ergonomic, well-fitted backpack such as the Osprey Atmos AG 50 reduces fatigue over long surveys. Hydration should be redundant—carry both a primary reservoir and a backup bottle, as dehydration risk is high in arid terrain. Fill all containers with potable water the night before.

Navigation & Data Collection:
Reliable navigation tools are essential. Garmin GPSMAP devices, loaded with updated desert maps, help ensure accurate data logging and safe navigation. Always carry a printed, waterproof map as a backup. Moisture sensors and other site-specific instruments should be calibrated prior to departure, and their readings carefully logged during surveys.

First Aid & Emergency Preparedness:
A well-stocked first aid kit addresses common desert issues like blisters, dehydration, and electrolyte loss. Emergency items—such as a survival blanket, snakebite kit, and multiple communication devices (satellite phone, PLB, radio)—are vital given the isolation and hazards present. Test and charge all devices, and verify expiration dates on medical supplies.

Documentation & Field Notes:
Waterproof notebooks and pencils are dependable tools in extreme conditions, supporting accurate record-keeping for species, waypoints, and field events. Synchronize and update mobile applications (particularly offline maps and identification tools) to ensure access in areas with poor connectivity.

Power Management:
Field operations in remote environments require effective power solutions. Powerbanks and solar chargers rated for outdoor use should be fully charged in advance. Insulate spare batteries from heat to preserve performance, and rotate usage to maintain longevity.

Safety & Environmental Protection:
Familiarize yourself with local protocols for snakebite treatment and heat illness, guided by Wilderness Medical Society recommendations. All refuse—biodegradable and otherwise—should be packed out, following Leave No Trace principles to protect sensitive habitats.

Lighting & Permits:
Test all lighting equipment before departing, and bring backup options to prevent disruption during early morning or dusk fieldwork. Ensure all permits and identification are valid and comply with local conservation authorities; keep documentation dry and accessible.

Several items included go beyond standard checklists to address the unpredictable conditions of desert surveys:

  • Multi-tool/Paracord/Duct Tape: These tools provide critical support for rapid field repairs, constructing temporary shelters, or responding to unexpected emergencies.
  • Biodegradable Waste Bag: Essential for responsibly managing all refuse and protecting the survey environment.
  • Survival Blanket/Bivy: Offers immediate protection in cases of unplanned overnight stays or sudden weather changes.
  • Snakebite Kit: Prepared according to local fauna risk and current medical guidelines.
  • Lighting Equipment: Reliable headlamps and flashlights are indispensable for safe operation during low-visibility periods.

Record field issues systematically, such as device malfunctions ("GPS lost signal at 07:00"), calibration logs ("Moisture sensor adjusted for sandy loam at waypoint #3"), and habitat-specific observations ("Monitor sunscreen effectiveness in high temperatures," "Log insect activity for biodiversity tracking").

Guidelines for Effective Packing and Field Execution

  • Preparation: Conduct a thorough review of the checklist the evening before departure. Charge all electronics, confirm calibration on data collection instruments, and verify that all gear is in working condition.
  • Flexible Use: The checklist’s table format is compatible with both printed and digital platforms, supporting sorting and real-time updates via apps such as Notion, Excel, or OneNote.
  • Field Annotations: Take notes directly in the checklist during surveys to facilitate troubleshooting, accurate data collection, and fast response to gear issues.

Remain up-to-date with the latest safety and field procedures by consulting resources from the Wilderness Medical Society and Leave No Trace. For detailed equipment support, refer to official manufacturer documentation.
