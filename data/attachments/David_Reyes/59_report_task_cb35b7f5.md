**Date of Visit:** August 18, 2024  
**Location:** Piute Canyon, California  
**Prepared for:** Ecology and Conservation Practitioners

## Executive Summary

On August 18, 2024, our interdisciplinary team conducted a comprehensive field excursion in Piute Canyon, California. The project generated critical ecological and biocultural data, integrating both scientific and Indigenous perspectives. Fieldwork covered five established transects, each sampled for soil properties, vegetation structure, water quality, recent rainfall, and the health of sagebrush (Artemisia tridentata) populations, using both physiological measurements and remote sensing metrics. Results highlight that sagebrush cover and vitality reach their peak at mid-elevation sites with limited human impact.

A central part of our visit was the oral history session with Cahuilla Elder Marjorie Silva, which enriched our scientific understanding with traditional ecological knowledge developed over generations. Elder Silva’s insights provided not only historical context for observed ecological patterns but also clear priorities for biocultural stewardship.

Our key management recommendations include intensified monitoring of invasive species, the integration of cultural values into restoration planning (particularly the protection of ethnobotanically important shrubs), and the continued collection of high-resolution ecological data. Moving forward, we advise a more adaptive, collaborative conservation approach that centers Indigenous knowledge alongside scientific observation. This will require ongoing longitudinal research and clear channels for co-management and dialogue with the Cahuilla community.

## Oral History Integration

### Session Overview

A highlight of the field day was the oral history session with Cahuilla Elder Marjorie Silva, facilitated by Ana Santiago. The conversation focused on traditional ecological knowledge related to Piute Canyon, with particular emphasis on the landscape’s plant communities and long-standing stewardship practices passed down through Cahuilla generations.

### Excerpts and Ethnobiological Insights

> “The sage here is our oldest neighbor. My grandmother taught me where the water sits deep—right where you found your greenest bush.”  
> — Marjorie Silva, Cahuilla Elder, at PC-03

> “When the rains come late, we look for the ants. If they move close to the roots, the earth is thirsty.”  
> — Marjorie Silva, Cahuilla Elder

Elder Silva’s insights provided unique, place-based observations. For example, her correlation between greenest sagebrush stands and groundwater presence was confirmed by our physiological measurements and NDVI data at PC-03. Her description of animal behavior as a hydrological indicator paralleled our own findings on drought stress and soil moisture variability. This dialogue directly informed the selection of ecological reference conditions and shaped our restoration benchmarks to include both empirical and cultural values.

## Recommendations and Next Steps

### Adaptive Management

- **Reference Ecosystem Maintenance:** Prioritize protection and monitoring of mid-elevation sagebrush stands (notably PC-03), which exemplify optimal ecological and cultural conditions.  
- **Integration of Traditional Knowledge:** Apply Elder Silva’s guidance regarding microhabitats (“where the water sits deep”) in the selection and monitoring of restoration areas.  
- **Invasive Species Control:** Intensify removal and monitoring efforts in disturbed areas, especially at downstream sites (such as PC-04) where non-native encroachment is most pronounced.  
- **Drought Stress Monitoring:** Continue and expand physiological monitoring (e.g., leaf water potential) to track early signs of plant water stress in the face of increasing climatic variability.

### Priority Research Questions

- **Rainfall Variability and Ecosystem Response:** How does interannual variability in precipitation—as detected through both scientific measures and Indigenous observation—influence sagebrush productivity and community composition?
- **Biocultural Health Metrics:** What thresholds define “good health” for sagebrush, as co-defined by Cahuilla partners and ecological criteria?
- **Microclimatic Refugia:** How and where do microclimatic refugia persist within Piute Canyon, and what management interventions best support their continued function?

### Follow-Up Timeline

- **Q3 2024:** Complete in-depth analysis of vegetation, soil, water data, and oral history integration.
- **Q4 2024:** Host collaborative workshop with Cahuilla representatives and regional land managers to refine priorities and ensure meaningful co-management.
- **Q1 2025:** Submit peer-reviewed publication and initiate pilot-scale restoration, jointly led by academic and Indigenous co-management groups.
- **Every Two Years:** Continue long-term environmental monitoring, update oral history integration, and share progress with the community at biennial symposiums.
