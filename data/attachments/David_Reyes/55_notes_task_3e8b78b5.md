## Chronological Field Notes

### 04:50 — Departure (pre-dawn)
I was up at **4:50am** and packed quietly to avoid waking the nearby campers. I headed out along the **Eastern Ridge above Short Canyon** under low light. The wind stayed **mild**, enough to feel on the ridge but not strong enough to make walking difficult.

### Pre-dawn wildlife activity (times not recorded)
The pre-dawn stretch delivered two clean sightings—exact timestamps didn’t make it into my notes, but both happened before sunrise.

- **Desert horned larks (pair):** I spotted a pair and managed to photograph them with the **Canon EOS 90D + 100–400mm**. The encounter was straightforward and calm—good, steady viewing long enough to get shots of the pair before moving on.  
  - Photo reference: Canon EOS 90D + 100–400mm; **Photo ID:** `[ARCHIVE-ID / filename placeholder]`

- **Kit fox:** The standout moment of the morning was a **kit fox darting between sagebrush**. It moved quickly and cleanly through cover—one of those brief encounters that’s easy to miss if you’re not already scanning the openings and edges. I captured a photo set and later **archived** it, but I didn’t record the archive location or naming details at the time.  
  - Photo reference: Canon EOS 90D + 100–400mm; **Photo ID:** `[ARCHIVE-ID / filename placeholder]` (later archived)

### Sunrise — tea + journal update
At sunrise I paused, sipped **desert herbal tea** from the **Osprey hydration flask**, and wrote in the **spiral-bound field journal**. I focused on **nocturnal movement notes**—the kind of quick summary that’s easiest to capture while the impressions are still fresh. I also made a dedicated sketch of a **juvenile ground squirrel** (see Sketch section).

The movement notes stayed in the paper journal rather than being fully transcribed here. What I did capture in this digital entry is the context: pre-dawn into sunrise is when fresh activity and transitions can be easier to notice before the day warms up and the trail corridor gets disturbed.
