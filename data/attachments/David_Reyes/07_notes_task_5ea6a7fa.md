# Personal Wellness Log Template for Research Ecologists  
**For: David Reyes, Research Ecologist & Conservation Project Lead**

## Overview

As a research ecologist deeply engaged in fieldwork, leadership, and conservation, I recognize that maintaining both physical health and mental wellbeing is crucial—not just for my own stamina but for the resilience and cohesion of my team. This personal wellness log offers a practical, flexible structure for daily self-monitoring starting January 14th. The format is designed for quick updates and deeper analysis, focusing on sleep, physical routines, professional milestones, social and family balance, mood, and resilience. Through intentional reflection and tracking, I aim to identify patterns, support informed self-care decisions, and cultivate steady wellbeing through the variable demands of ecological research and project leadership.

---

## Sleep Quality

I track my sleep each night with both numbers and notes, aiming for honest self-assessment rather than perfection.

- **Hours Slept:** At the end of each day, I enter an exact number—last night, for example, I managed 6.5 hours.
- **Disruptions:** I list anything that woke me, such as sudden wildlife calls near camp or temperature drops. Sometimes stress or worries about the next day's survey pull me out of deeper sleep.
- **Energy/Alertness:** Each morning, I rate how alert I feel from 1 to 10; a simple but striking indicator of how well I'm coping.
- **Qualitative Notes:** I jot down if poor sleep left me slower to react during a wildlife sighting, or if a rested morning meant sharper data analysis.

By looking back over these entries, I notice trends—like how sleep suffers during multi-day expeditions. This helps me plan ahead: improving my tent setup, scheduling less intensive tasks after tough nights, or using relaxation practices that have worked for me.

---

## Morning Yoga Routine

Before heading into the field, I dedicate time to a short, restorative yoga session. I keep records in a table format, listing each pose:

- **Pose/Sequence:** For me, Downward Dog improves agility after sleeping rough, Cat-Cow frees my spine, Low Lunge opens my hips before hours of hiking, Standing Twists loosen my torso, and Child’s Pose grounds me before a full day.
- **Description of Benefits:** Each pose is chosen to prepare my body for movement, protect against strain, and give a mental reset—especially before high-pressure survey days.
- **Duration:** I record the minutes spent—some mornings, I linger in Child’s Pose if I’m feeling tense.
- **Energy (Before/After):** I rate my energy both before starting and after finishing; even a ten-minute routine noticeably lifts my morning sluggishness.
- **Adaptation Notes:** If weather delays fieldwork or I anticipate tough terrain, I adjust—adding stretches that target sore muscles or slowing down to reduce pre-field anxiety.

Consistent practice keeps me limber and ready, letting me tailor sequences to the day’s particular demands.

## Work Accomplishments

I break down each day’s achievements to better appreciate progress and maintain momentum.

- **Research Milestones:** I log specifics—species identified during bird banding, soil samples collected, or successful GPS mapping.
- **Grant/Project Activity:** Each step toward a submission counts, from outlining a proposal to drafting budget sections.
- **Teamwork/Stakeholder Engagement:** I make note of effective group briefings, constructive feedback, or moments when cross-disciplinary input led to breakthrough ideas.
- **Field Anecdotes:** I record personal impressions—a sudden weather shift prompting rapid adaptation, an unexpected animal encounter challenging our survey, or a teammate’s resourcefulness saving precious field hours.

These details help me track productivity, celebrate achievements, and review strategies that worked—or didn’t—at the end of the project cycle.

---

## Social Interactions

Beyond the technical work, I pay close attention to team culture and everyday camaraderie.

- **Workplace Gatherings:** I note who participated and what was discussed—whether it’s a safety debrief or impromptu planning over coffee.
- **Celebrations/Milestones:** Small moments matter—a teammate’s birthday, a grant win, or marking the end of a challenging expedition with dinner by the campfire.
- **Team Engagement:** I reflect on spontaneous collaboration, shared humor, or honest conversations that lifted spirits after a setback.

Tracking these interactions reminds me to support morale and build trust—a foundation for sustained, effective conservation efforts.

---

## Evening Family Check-In

I make a conscious effort to balance my field responsibilities with family connection, no matter the distance.

- **Family Time Notes:** Most evenings, I call home to discuss the day’s events, share stories, and hear about life outside the project. In person, I value slow dinners and walks.
- **Support/Work-Life Balance Assessment:** I assess how supported I feel—whether a short call was enough to recharge, or if I need to carve out more time in busy periods.
- **Quality Observation:** I reflect on emotional stability and how family grounding helps me show up fully in both roles.

Regular check-ins highlight when I need to adjust—an extra call after a tough day, setting firmer boundaries during fieldwork, or simply expressing gratitude for encouragement received.

---

## Mood Tracking

Each day, I use a simple visual scale (0–10) and descriptive notes to monitor my overall mood and resilience.

- **Numeric/Graphic VAS:** I plot my mood after dinner—some days, field challenges drop me to a 4; others, a leadership win boosts me to an 8.
- **Written Descriptor:** I explore underlying factors—optimism fueled by successful teamwork, or fatigue after relentless data collection. I recognize my emotional highs and lows as real and valuable signals.

Consistent tracking helps me identify what supports my mental health and what strategies—mindfulness, exercise, connecting with others—actually make a difference.

---

## Daily Reflection

At day’s end, I use prompts to ground myself and foster growth.

- **Optimism Sources:** I look for sparks: creative problem-solving under pressure, a teammate’s encouragement, or meaningful progress with local stakeholders.
- **Greatest Achievement Impact:** I weigh which accomplishment brought the most satisfaction—sometimes it’s the field results, other times it’s guiding a new team member.
- **Ecological Discoveries:** I make note of the rare moments—a sighting never before recorded in our region, or a particularly resilient ecosystem thriving against odds.
- **Resilience Demonstration:** I reflect on personal or team adaptability, whether navigating sudden weather interruptions or working together to overcome equipment failures.

This reflection shapes my mindset—encouraging a sense of purpose, motivating me for the next day, and strengthening both individual and collective resilience.

---

## Template Organization and Ongoing Review

I organize the log for speed and practicality, using tables and open-ended sections for rapid entries. Editable formats mean I can adjust for changing field conditions or research priorities. Weekly summaries support strategic reviews, revealing trends across sleep, mood, social interaction, and work milestones. These insights guide tweaks—not just in self-care, but also in leading and modeling wellness for my team.

Through consistent tracking and honest reflection, I strive to maintain the stamina, clarity, and adaptability required for rigorous field science and effective leadership.
