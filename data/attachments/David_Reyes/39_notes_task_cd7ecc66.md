# Carlos Reyes Birthday Gathering Dinner: Menu & Grocery Checklist

## Introduction

As I prepare to host Carlos Reyes’ birthday gathering this June in the heart of the Mojave Desert, I've poured a lot of care and intention into planning our dinner menu. Living in this region has taught me how much our land and climate shape the foods we eat. With that in mind, I’ve curated a plant-based meal that not only brings out the best of the Mojave and greater California’s agriculture, but does so in a way that honors water-wise and local ingredients, supports our farmers, and welcomes everyone at the table—whether vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free, dairy-free, or allergic to nuts.

Sustainability guided my choices every step of the way, from limiting food miles to minimizing waste and avoiding unnecessary packaging. In this note, I’m sharing the dinner menu and detailed grocery checklist, with practical notes for shopping, prepping, and adapting dishes. I hope this helps make the occasion as delicious, inclusive, and eco-friendly as possible.

### Why These Dishes?

I wanted each plate to evoke the spirit and resilience of the Mojave and California growers. I also prioritized recipes that use whole ingredients, are easy to scale, and create minimal waste—whether from packaging or leftovers.

## Sustainability & Regional Strategy

I want this dinner to be not just a celebration of Carlos’ life, but a tribute to our region’s landscape and the people who work so hard to feed us, often with very little water or resources. Every dish is built around crops that thrive in arid environments: from golden beets and squash to native mesquite and prickly pear. Sourcing produce straight from Mojave valley farms and nearby markets slashes food miles, strengthens the local food web, and ensures peak freshness.

I also love heading out in the morning to collect purslane along a wild arroyo, or picking prickly pears—always with gloves and a respectful eye for what’s ready to harvest. Foraging adds a beautiful sense of connection to our meal, but I make sure it’s done carefully and never at the expense of plant health or the needs of animals who rely on these same foods.

Keeping the menu vegan and gluten-free (or with easy swaps) means every guest can relax and enjoy the food without worry. By sticking to whole, minimally processed ingredients and simple recipes, clean-up is easier and there’s almost no chance of anything going to waste. Any leftovers are easy to pack up for the next day or share with friends.
