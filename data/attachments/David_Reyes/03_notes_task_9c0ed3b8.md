# Structured Exercise and Wellbeing Journal Template  
_For David Reyes, Research Ecologist: Mojave Desert Fieldwork (June 2024)_

---

## Overview

This journal template is designed for my daily use as a field-based research ecologist working in the Mojave Desert near Ridgecrest, California. June brings extreme heat, low humidity, and high pollen, often challenging my physical resilience—especially given recurring sinus irritation and general fatigue. Maintaining a light, adaptable yoga routine and tracking my wellbeing is essential for both self-care and job performance here. The following template serves as a structured, data-driven record to help me monitor my routines, adapt strategies to changing field conditions, and document the interplay between personal health and environmental variables. This layout supports both scientific documentation and realistic self-care practice in a demanding desert research setting.

### Reason for Change (If Routine Was Modified)

If I modify my yoga routine, I note the specific reasons here. Common factors include:

- **Health:** Sinus irritation, fatigue, signs of dehydration, headaches, or allergy flare-ups.
- **Environment:** Elevated pollen count, low humidity, intense temperatures, gusty winds, or poor air quality.
- **Field Demands:** Unpredictable schedule changes, extended survey periods, or lack of suitable space/time for movement.

For example:  
_"Skipped Downward Dog due to dizziness and increased sinus congestion this morning, likely aggravated by rising pollen levels and a short night’s sleep."_

### Physical Wellbeing Notes

I use the evening to provide straightforward ratings and notes on my health:

- **Sinus health** (pain or congestion): __ / 10 (0 = none, 10 = severe)
- **Sleep quality:** ____ hours (_e.g.,_ restful, disturbed, fragmented)
- **Perceived energy level:** __ / 10 (1 = exhausted, 10 = fully energized)
- **Other symptoms:** headaches, lightheadedness, runny nose, allergies, muscle tightness—plus any fieldwork-related issues such as minor scrapes or insect stings.

These objective scores, together with my comments, help clarify trends over time.

### Daily Reflections and Observations

Here, I reflect on my health and wellness over the past 24 hours, especially focusing on:

- **Changes since previous entry:** Improvements or setbacks in symptoms.
- **Impact of routine deviations:**
    - Was missing yoga (or modifying poses) followed by more fatigue, decreased concentration, or mood changes in the field?
    - Did alternatives like steam inhalation offset sinus symptoms enough to maintain productivity?
- **Links between field conditions and wellbeing:**  
    - For example: _“Noticed days with high pollen counts and low humidity led to more congestion and skipped yoga, which then correlated with lower morning energy and feeling less sharp on data collection rounds.”_
- **Emerging patterns:** Recurring symptoms under certain environmental triggers, or repeated benefit from a specific adaptive strategy.

### Future Wellness and Adaptive Health Goals

Each night, I outline goals and strategies for the coming days:

- **Short-term health goals:** Stabilize sinus irritation, maintain hydration, keep my energy above 6/10 even on the hardest days.
- **Adaptive strategies:**  
    - Schedule yoga or stretching during the coolest hours.
    - Add more hydration checkpoints and keep saline spray accessible in the field truck.
    - Substitute gentle stretching for intense poses during flare-ups.
- **Routine adjustments:**  
    - Adjust yoga plans to be more flexible and realistic on days with heavier fieldwork or adverse weather.
    - Try new sinus care methods (e.g., neti pot in the evening).
- **Data tracking:**  
    - Note when additional data collection (like tracking pollen every two hours) might clarify persistent symptoms.
    - Refine scales or categories as new patterns emerge.

## Research Suitability and Field Journal Integration

This template meets field science and professional documentation standards by combining:

- Structured, objective data collection (including numeric scales and tables).
- Systematic environmental monitoring, enabling meaningful self-experimentation on links between conditions and health.
- Narrative reflection and hypothesis generation: for example, “Frequent skipped yoga sessions on high AQI days seem to predict greater fatigue and sinus discomfort the following morning.”
- A flexible design that supports adaptation over weeks or field seasons, improving both scientific rigor and personal resilience.

I prioritize clarity and brevity—critical for field use—while allowing space for notes and longer reflections if needed.
