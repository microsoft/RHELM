# Advanced Field Observation Log — Pre-Dawn Bird Survey  
**Date:** January 18, 2024  
**Location:** Near Ridgecrest, California, Mojave Desert  

---

## Survey Overview

On January 18, 2024, I conducted a focused pre-dawn avian survey near Ridgecrest, California, in the western Mojave Desert. The survey aimed to document early-morning bird activity and behavior under challenging winter conditions, contributing ongoing data on local population trends and habitat use.

---

## Location and Habitat

- **Survey Area:** Outskirts of Ridgecrest, California (35.6225°N, 117.6709°W), within open desert scrub habitat.
- **Vegetation Structure:** The site comprised primarily creosote bush (Larrea tridentata) and scattered Joshua trees (Yucca brevifolia), interspersed with ephemeral washes and alluvial fan slopes. Vegetation formed patchy clusters, with frequent openings of bare ground.
- **Microhabitats Noted:**
    - Gravelly slopes supporting sparse shrub cover.
    - Seasonal drainage depressions providing occasional denser shrub aggregation.
    - Edges formed by a disused dirt track, representing minor anthropogenic influence.
- The diversity of microhabitats promotes variable foraging conditions, although the morning’s prevailing winds significantly limited shelter availability for birds.

---

## Survey Timing

- **Start Time:** 05:44 PST (shortly before civil twilight at 05:47)
- **End Time:** 06:23 PST
- **Duration:** 39 minutes

Beginning in low light, I timed the survey to capture the period when many species initiate their dawn activities, although adverse weather limited typical pre-dawn vocalizations and movement.

---

## Environmental Conditions

- **Temperature:** 2.3°C (36.1°F) at survey start
- **Wind:** Steady 23 km/h (14 mph), gusting up to 32 km/h (20 mph), from NNE (~25°)
- **Humidity:** 28%
- **Visibility:** Moderate to low, approximately 1.5 km, due to airborne dust from wind erosion
- **Sky:** Partly cloudy with gradual lightening before and during civil twilight
- The combination of sustained wind and dust considerably reduced both auditory and visual detection capabilities and likely suppressed typical dawn bird activity.

## Avian Observations

Despite careful site selection and targeted timing, bird activity was markedly reduced, likely due to wind and dust. Only one individual was detected:

### Primary Species Encountered:

**Say’s Phoebe (*Sayornis saya*)**
- **Ecological Role:** An insectivorous flycatcher, Say’s Phoebe is a typical resident of open, arid scrub, serving a vital function in regulating insect populations.
- **Conservation Status:** Rated Least Concern by the IUCN and not listed as threatened or endangered in California; considered locally stable, though potentially susceptible to future habitat fragmentation.

---

## Additional Site and Behavioral Notes

- The observed Say’s Phoebe utilized a slightly elevated perch at the junction of an open wash and main alluvial slope, a behavior consistent with previous winter records in the same location.
- Notably absent were typical dawn vocalizations from expected species such as Black-throated Sparrow and Loggerhead Shrike; these absences likely resulted from high wind and dust, both of which can suppress singing and foraging at first light.
- Unlike previous winter surveys here, the Say’s Phoebe did not select higher lookout points such as Joshua tree branches, limiting its foraging to lower, more sheltered perches.
- No evidence was found of mate or territorial activity, diverging from 2023 and 2022 early-season surveys where multiple individuals and territorial interactions were observed.
- The lack of vocalizations and limited movement aligns with observed sensitivity to adverse conditions and may reflect temporary adjustments to reduce energy expenditure.

## Field Challenges

### Equipment Performance
- The Canon EOS 90D, especially the lens, accumulated fine dust rapidly, requiring frequent manual cleaning. After moderate sand exposure, autofocus performance noticeably slowed. I exercised caution to keep the lens sealed and switched to manual focus when necessary.
- Windblown dust interfered with writing in the field notebook, with one observation requiring digital backup notes via smartphone to ensure data was captured promptly and legibly.

### Environmental and Health Considerations
- High sustained winds not only masked environmental sounds, reducing auditory bird detections, but also forced many birds to shelter or limit activity.
- Dust in the air regularly limited visibility below 2 km, further hampering observation accuracy.
- About 20 minutes into the survey, I experienced acute eye irritation, most likely due to wind-borne sand. This was temporarily alleviated with a saline rinse kept on hand. In the last quarter of the survey, sinus discomfort and congestion developed, but these symptoms were managed with brief pauses and PPE, minimizing their impact on data collection.

## Recommendations for Future Surveys

### Equipment and Data Integrity
- Bring a dust-resistant cover or protective enclosure for camera gear when working in abrasive windblown environments.
- Utilize a soft lens brush and manual air blower before packing up equipment to prevent long-term wear.
- Employ fully sealed safety goggles rather than standard wraparound glasses in severe wind.
- Carry a secondary recording device—either voice recorder or smartphone—so observational notes are not interrupted by poor pen performance or dust accumulation.

### Survey Timing and Protocol
- Schedule surveys to begin at or after dawn, especially during periods of forecasted high wind, to improve detectability and ensure optimal bird activity.
- Avoid conducting surveys when visibility falls below 2 km, as this significantly reduces detection rates.
- Take frequent health and hydration breaks, and bring saline drops and cough masks to mitigate the effects of dust and dryness.
- Perform immediate double-entry transcription or digital backup within an hour of fieldwork, to safeguard log integrity in difficult conditions.

## Summary and Implications

Despite encountering sustained wind, airborne dust, and cold temperatures that suppressed typical pre-dawn bird activity, the survey produced a high-confidence record for Say’s Phoebe. Detailed behavioral data were collected thanks to a combination of direct observation and adaptive field protocols. The single sighting, along with the absence of other expected species and noted behavioral shifts, reinforces the Mojave Desert’s sensitivity to short-term weather variability—particularly for avian activity at dawn.

Preserving data integrity during harsh conditions required immediate adaptation to both technological and environmental challenges, underscoring the value of redundant documentation methods and robust PPE. These standardized observation logs, when repeated across seasons and years, will provide strong baseline data for tracking avian responses to environmental variability and inform future site management and conservation actions in the Mojave Desert.
