## 2. My Sustainability Approach & Ecological Insights

Choosing local, organic produce and dry goods isn’t just about “going green” for me—it’s about supporting real people and living gently. The closer my food travels to reach my kitchen, the fresher it stays and the less fossil fuel is burned. Regenerative farms I shop from actively restore soil and boost biodiversity, and bulk purchasing of spices further reduces plastic and cardboard waste.

With beans, I always reach for US-grown, organic, and dried varieties: they pack great nutrition, enrich the soil during cultivation, and don’t rely heavily on resource-intensive packaging. Lentils are my ace in the hole when beans run low; they’re easy to store, versatile, and help the planet by fixing nitrogen.

I’m vigilant about certifications—USDA Organic, Fair Trade, OMRI, and locally-sourced. These ensure honest business practices and help guarantee ecological farming standards. For pantry staples, I avoid excessive packaging and always refill when possible. Bonsai care supplies require a special touch, and I stay away from peat moss due to its environmental toll; rainwater collection and biodegradable pest controls keep my gardening footprint light.

## 4. Real-Life Substitutions & Rough Patches

Not every trip goes as planned. Here’s how I adapted:

1. **Chili Bean Omission:**  
When I realized I’d forgotten chili beans, I doubled up on black and kidney beans. Both work well in chili and offer strong protein and soil health benefits. Black beans, in particular, use less water.

2. **No Fresh Cilantro:**  
With no cilantro in stock, I turned to my herb garden for oregano. Oregano’s drought-hardiness not only saves water but brings a unique herbal note to the chili—a happy accident.

3. **Local Parsnips Instead of Carrots:**  
When organic carrots weren’t available, I switched in local parsnips. They’re a great winter crop, offer a similar flavor, and diversify my cold-season vegetables.

4. **Canned Over Dried Beans (due to time crunch):**  
Pressed for time, I popped open BPA-free canned beans from Eden Foods. While processing and packaging aren’t ideal, Eden keeps it US-grown and safe.

5. **Bonsai Fertilizer Change:**  
I swapped out synthetic fertilizer for OMRI-certified organic pellets. It’s reassuring knowing I’m reducing harmful runoff and supporting cleaner waterways.
