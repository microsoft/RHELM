## Challenges

The challenges today weren’t loud, but they were real—the kind that keep repeating until I pay attention.

The kit fox was the cleanest example. It was over in a heartbeat: a quick skitter at the edge of my vision, then nothing. My reflexive urge was to turn it into a “win,” to chase, to make it count. I’m glad I didn’t. The moment was a boundary lesson: observation isn’t ownership. Not every encounter is mine to capture, and if I treat every sighting like a test of skill, I miss what I came out there for in the first place.

The sand beetle photos brought up a different pressure—more internal, more familiar. I wanted the images to be better than before. Today they were: crisp, detailed, worth keeping. But the real challenge wasn’t technical; it was temperamental. The improvement came from slowing down—giving myself time to check focus, adjust angle, and stay present instead of hunting for volume. I can feel how easily I slip back into rushing when I’m excited or trying to prove something.

Grant editing carried its own subtle trap. Proposal work is iterative by nature, so forward progress can still feel like walking across a floor that keeps extending. The challenge for me is staying constructive: revision without self-criticism, refinement without spiraling into endless tweaking. Today’s check-in helped because it created structure—clear decisions, clear next steps—but I want to keep watching that line where “one more tweak” becomes procrastination in disguise.
