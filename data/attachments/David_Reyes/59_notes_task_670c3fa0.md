# Piute Canyon Multidisciplinary Ecological Field Excursion  
## Comprehensive Itinerary, Team Checklist, Protocols, and Post-Trip Requirements  
**Date:** August 18, 2024  
**Location:** Piute Canyon, Mojave Desert  

---

### Introduction

The Piute Canyon field excursion brings together a multidisciplinary team to conduct a comprehensive ecological survey in one of the Mojave Desert’s most diverse habitats. By integrating botanical, wildlife, soil, and microclimate assessments, this fieldwork is designed to support ongoing conservation efforts and substantiate research into the canyon’s unique desert ecosystem. The following report outlines the schedule, participant roles, equipment list, protocols, and follow-up actions necessary to ensure data integrity, safety, and successful collaboration.

## 3. Equipment Packing Checklist

All research equipment is chosen according to federal and professional standards for desert fieldwork to ensure data reliability and operational safety.

**Standard Research Equipment (following USGS, ESA, USFWS specifications):**  
- Disposable nitrile gloves (minimum two pairs per person)  
- Sterile sample containers, with a selection of sizes for various ecological samples  
- Digital cameras (DSLR), macro lenses, additional batteries, secure memory cards  
- Portable acoustic recorders for avian and wildlife detection  
- Rugged handheld GPS units (e.g. Garmin eTrex series)  
- Water-resistant field notebooks  
- Tablets and/or mobile devices loaded with standardized data entry forms, extra power banks  
- Measurement tools: tapes, calipers, soil probes  
- Flagging tape, marking stakes, site boundary flags  
- Data backup devices (USB flash drives and SSDs)  
- Waterproof pens and pencils  
- Light meters, thermometers, hygrometers for microclimate measurements  
- Insulated coolers with ice packs for sample storage and transport  

**Desert-Specific Equipment:**  
- Hydration bladders or packs (minimum 3 liters per person with easy-access drinking tubes)  
- Electrolyte supplements, energy bars, and high-calorie snacks  
- Comprehensive sun protection: wide-brim hats, UV-resistant long sleeves, sunglasses, mineral-based sunscreen (SPF 50+)  
- Satellite phone or Personal Locator Beacon (PLB) for emergency communications  
- Complete first aid kit for desert conditions, including heat stress, snakebite, and severe allergies  
- Emergency evacuation maps and route lists  
- EPA-approved insect repellent  
- Snake gaiters for high-risk habitats  
- Portable shade structures (canopies or reflective tarps)  
- Lightweight, foldable field chairs  
- Multipurpose tools (multitool, duct tape, etc.)  
- Mojave-specific field guidebooks and topographical maps  

All items are selected based on recommendations from USGS, ESA, USFWS, and OSHA. Team members are responsible for regular inspection and maintenance of personal safety gear.

## 4. Field Protocols

### 4.1 Route Management and Navigation

- All GPS units are programmed in advance with survey waypoints and escape routes; paper and digital maps are distributed to all team leads.
- Teams operate under a buddy system to promote safety during navigation and survey work, maintaining at least two members per group at all times.
- Scheduled hourly check-ins between sub-teams by radio or satellite phone, especially during work in remote sectors.
- Hazards such as wildlife burrows, unstable terrain, and exposed brush are recorded, with immediate route adjustments as needed.
- Emergency evacuation routes are highlighted during the pre-trip briefing; all personnel are required to review these routes before departure.

### 4.2 Data Entry and Management Procedures

- All ecological data is logged initially on standardized digital forms using tablets or mobile devices with dedicated survey apps.
- Field teams maintain water-resistant paper logs for redundancy, following ESA field note guidelines.
- Data is backed up daily on portable external drives and uploaded to secure cloud storage during lunch and after debriefing.
- All collected specimens and samples are labeled with exact location, date, time, and collector's unique ID, following USGS/ESA sample archiving protocols.
- During end-of-day debriefs, teams review and reconcile any ambiguous entries and ensure completeness prior to archiving.

### 4.3 Health, Safety, and Desert-Specific Conduct

- Mandatory hydration protocol: team members consume at least 250 mL of water every half hour; electrolyte supplements are distributed at each break.
- Shade breaks are implemented every two hours with temperature and heat stress monitoring; the Health & Safety Officer conducts regular symptom checks.
- Wildlife is observed but not handled except under the supervision of the wildlife biologist, especially in the presence of venomous species.
- The first aid kit remains accessible at all times, and the Health & Safety Officer briefs the team daily on emergency procedures.
- All participants complete a medical check and submit personal medical information prior to fieldwork; epipens and antihistamines are available for those with known allergies.
- Satellite communication devices are kept charged and tested for use during solo tasks or emergencies.
- The team follows strict “leave no trace” principles, minimizing habitat disturbance, restoring sampling areas, and removing all debris upon departure.

## 6. Next-Phase Analysis Priorities

The data collected in Piute Canyon provides the foundation for targeted ecological analysis, informing both immediate research efforts and broader conservation strategies. The next-phase objectives include:

- Synthesizing preliminary habitat quality indices and species diversity metrics using cross-team datasets
- Facilitating collaborative discussion to interpret initial findings and identify ecological trends
- Pinpointing data or sampling gaps for targeted follow-up expeditions
- Formulating conservation recommendations based on field observations, with emphasis on sensitive microhabitats and species
- Planning for advanced laboratory analysis, including genetic studies, detailed soil chemistry, and modeling of microclimate parameters

These efforts are designed to maximize both scientific insight and practical conservation impact.

## 7. Collaborative Review and Data Archiving Structure

All field documentation—ranging from raw data files to finalized protocols—is archived on a secure, shared project drive accessible to all core team members. A formal review session will be scheduled, either in-person or via teleconference, within five days of the trip’s conclusion. During this session, each participant will have the opportunity to contribute observations, flag any issues, and refine analytical priorities. Versioned archives will include:

- The final itinerary and activity logs
- All data tables (digital and paper formats)
- Protocol outlines and participant contact lists
- Incident and safety reports

The review process ensures transparency, fosters collaborative analysis, and prepares formal summary documents for submission to research partners and grant funders.
