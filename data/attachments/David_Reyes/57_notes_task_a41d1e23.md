# Field Day Itinerary & Meal Record — Saturday, 2024-08-10

## Overview
I treated Saturday as a full “field-and-admin” day: an early start in the Owen’s River valley for bird photography and note-taking with Miguel, a quick family lunch when Carlos joined us mid-morning, and then a quieter afternoon and evening spent turning field experience into practical outputs—planning a workshop, tightening volunteer logistics, and protecting the day’s images with a clean backup.

---

## 1) Morning Field Itinerary (Narrative + Observed Sequence)

Precise sunrise and twilight timestamps weren’t captured in the record I’m working from, so I’m sticking to the same open-ended time blocks that match what actually happened.

### Pre-dawn — On the road toward the Owen’s River valley
I was up before first light and on the road in my Subaru Outback, headed toward the Owen’s River valley. I had my Canon EOS 90D ready to go, and I refilled my hydration pack before things got busy—simple prep, but it makes the morning smoother once I’m on the trail.

### Just before sunrise — Meeting at the marsh trailhead
Miguel Castillo rolled in just before sunrise and greeted me near the marsh trailhead. He had his familiar desert hat on and a camera backpack ready for the morning. That quick meet-up set the pace: no wasted time, just a shared understanding that the best window was about to open.

### Sunrise / early soft light — First sightings and photography begins
When the valley settled into that soft early light, we almost immediately spotted a pair of Virginia rails darting through the reeds. That kind of early success changes the whole mood of a field session—it’s energizing and immediately focuses your attention, because rails don’t usually stick around in the open for long.

We stayed in the marsh area near the trailhead and kept working the edges of the reeds, with me shooting on the EOS 90D.

### Early morning — Marsh wren + soundscape notes
Not long after the rails, I caught an early-foraging marsh wren and got images I was happy with. The visual work was only half of it, though. I also noticed a subtle shift in bird calls—the soundscape changing as the morning moved forward—and I took the time to catalog that variation in my field journal. It’s the kind of detail that’s easy to ignore when you’re chasing photos, but it’s exactly what helps later when I’m comparing days or building a longer-term archive.

### Breakfast break on the trail — Food and stories
We paused for a trail breakfast: homemade almond-date bars and lemon-mint iced tea. It wasn’t fancy, but it felt perfect for the setting—sweet, steady energy from the bars and something cold and bright to drink.

While we ate, our talk shifted into the usual field-life storytelling. I told Miguel about a recent desert hare encounter, and he countered with a tripod mishap from last week. It was the kind of conversation that keeps the day light even when we’re being serious about the work—sharing the small wins and the funny frustrations that come with field photography.

### Mid-morning — Carlos joins for a low-key family lunch
By mid-morning, Carlos Reyes joined us for a low-key family lunch. The dynamic changed in a good way—less “two-person field push,” more of a relaxed overlap between family time and shared natural history interest.

We swapped bird sightings, and Carlos talked about a rare loggerhead shrike display he’d seen. I also asked him to send me his old burrowing owl photos for my expanding archive. Even a short lunch turned into meaningful forward progress on the documentation side.
