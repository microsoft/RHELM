# Daily Meal Plan and Special Event Menu for Conservation Field Team (May 30)

## Overview

This document outlines a comprehensive daily meal plan and celebratory lunch for David Reyes’ conservation field team, purpose-built for research work in desert ecology. The menu addresses operational needs, inclusivity for a diverse group, and a strong commitment to ecological sustainability. Each component is carefully structured according to current academic guidelines and best practices, supporting logistical planning, nutrition, and sustainable field operations.

---

## 1. Scheduled Field Snacks

### Morning and Midday Energy Support

During a typical field day, reliable, portable snacks are essential for maintaining energy and morale. Given the challenges of desert conditions—heat, dryness, and physical exertion—snacks must deliver stable nutrition, easy access, and robust shelf life. Below is our time-structured snack schedule:

| Time        | Snack                   | Portion           | Key Ingredients                            | Field Benefits                                    | Storage & Distribution       | Energy Value       | Hydration Profile                           |
|-------------|-------------------------|-------------------|--------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------|--------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| 07:30–08:00 | Almond-Date Bars        | 2 × 40g bars      | Almonds, pitted dates, chia seeds          | Dense calories, sustained energy, no refrigeration| Vacuum-sealed, heat stable  | ~200 kcal/bar; 4g protein | Low; prompts thirst, best with water       |
| 10:30–11:00 | Chili Roasted Chickpeas | 50g per person    | Chickpeas, olive oil, chili powder, cumin, salt | Protein, fiber, salt for electrolytes, portable    | Airtight containers         | ~180 kcal/serving; 7g protein | Low; aids with salt balance, drink water    |

#### Selection Rationale

Both snacks are non-perishable and withstand desert heat, reducing spoilage risk. Their packaging—either reusable containers or compostable wraps—keeps waste low and environmental impact minimal. The almond-date bars provide a slow release of energy valuable for endurance, and the chickpeas offer plant protein along with salt to help replenish electrolytes lost during exertion. This approach adheres to field nutrition guidelines for outdoor research teams ([Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics][1]).

## 2. Sunburst Café: Team Celebration Lunch (12:30–14:00)

### Lunch Menu

To acknowledge the team’s achievements and fuel recovery after a demanding morning, lunch is both celebratory and practical. It is designed to ensure maximum nutrition, hydration, and safety in a field environment.

**Featured Dishes:**
- Lemon-Roasted Cauliflower Trays
- Citrus Salad Jars (prepared together with the team)

#### Ingredient Highlights

**Lemon-Roasted Cauliflower**
- Cauliflower florets
- Olive oil
- Fresh lemon juice and zest
- Garlic, parsley, cracked pepper, sea salt

**Citrus Salad Jars**
- Oranges, grapefruit, lime
- Spinach, arugula
- Toasted pumpkin seeds
- Red onion, mint, light vinaigrette (olive oil, lemon)

#### Preparation and Food Safety

Lunch preparation is organized for both efficiency and inclusion. Cauliflower is batch-roasted using portable convection ovens or solar cookers, allowing for scalability. The citrus salad jars are assembled collaboratively: team members take turns slicing, layering greens and fruit, and dressing each jar. This method encourages teamwork and gives everyone input into the final product. To minimize hygiene risks, all produce is pre-washed, handled with sanitized utensils and gloves, and kept chilled in coolers according to USDA and EPA safety advice ([EPA][2], [USDA][3]).

#### Nutritional and Ecological Context

The menu offers a rich source of vitamin C, fiber, and anti-inflammatory nutrients, with plenty of hydrating produce—ideal for field recovery and performance. Every effort is made to source ingredients from local farms, lowering the footprint associated with transportation and supporting community agriculture. All serveware is compostable or reusable, in keeping with EPA event guidelines and the conservation ethos of minimizing single-use materials ([EPA][2]).

During breaks and post-lunch seminars, herbal teas lend both hydration and cognitive support without reliance on caffeine. The following blends are offered:

|-------------------------|------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Desert Peppermint Cooler| Peppermint, lemon balm             | Alertness, cooling effect                 | Local/organic sources                     | Steeped with filtered water, hot or cold|
| Rooibos Recovery        | Rooibos, hibiscus, rosehips        | Antioxidants, hydration                   | Fair-trade, climate-resilient crops       | Brew hot and serve warm or iced         |
| Sage Citrus Infusion    | Sage, dried lemon peel             | Anti-inflammatory, clarity                | Wild-harvested or organic                 | Steep hot or cold, gentle steeping      |

Each tea is served in insulated thermoses and clearly labeled with all ingredients for allergy management. Herbs are ethically sourced and fair-trade where possible. The blends are caffeine-free and avoid added sugars, supporting hydration with natural flavor and avoiding the diuretic effects that caffeinated drinks might cause in a desert context.

## 4. Dietary Inclusivity and Sustainability

### Adaptations for Dietary Needs

Every snack and lunch item is vegetarian and vegan, intentionally designed to avoid all animal products and common allergens, such as dairy, eggs, and gluten. Ingredients are labeled plainly in accordance with best practices for diverse field teams ([Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics][1]).

### Environmental Stewardship

Menus based on plant foods reduce greenhouse gas emissions and water impact compared to animal-based options ([EPA][2]). Local and seasonal sourcing also supports biodiversity and minimizes logistical reliance on distant supply lines—a foundational principle for any conservation operation. Throughout, reusable and compostable serveware are prioritized to keep waste minimal and reinforce project values.

### Alignment with Standards

All food selection, handling, and sustainability efforts are strictly aligned with professional guidelines from the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics ([1]), USDA and EPA ([2], [3]), and recent research on nutrition for fieldwork in arid conditions ([4]).
