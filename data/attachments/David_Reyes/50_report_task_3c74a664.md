# Meeting Minutes  
**Desert Conservation Fall Webinar Planning**

**Date:** June 29, 2024  
**Participants:**  
- David Reyes, Research Ecologist & Conservation Project Lead  
- Dr. Linda Padilla, Conservation Scientist  

---

## Discussion Summary

The planning meeting for the Desert Conservation Fall Webinar focused on finalizing panelist participation, outlining technical logistics for the hybrid event, and establishing clear strategies for engagement and outreach. The committee is committed to delivering an accessible, interactive webinar that brings together leading voices in desert conservation, including field-based practitioners, researchers, and community partners.

---

### Panelist Confirmations and Expertise

Dr. Stefan Haas has been confirmed as the lead panelist, contributing expertise in arid ecosystem restoration and adaptive management practices within desert reserves. The planning committee is also in the process of finalizing additional panelists, including a specialist in indigenous stewardship and a Mojave Desert hydrologist. These selections are intended to ensure a wide-ranging and interdisciplinary dialogue, with each expert offering both recent field experiences and an academic perspective on topics such as community-based conservation, climate adaptation, and water resources management in arid landscapes.

### Venue and Technology Provisions

The webinar will be hosted as a hybrid event from the Desert Research Center, making use of its full audiovisual facilities. To guarantee a smooth experience for both in-person and remote participants, the technical team will set up a minimum of 25 Mbps dedicated connection for upload and download. Recognizing the frequent connectivity challenges typical of the Mojave region, redundant broadband systems will be installed and cellular hotspots will be available as backup. Prior to the event, live tests of the physical venue’s system will be conducted to confirm stability and capacity during peak usage periods.

### Integrating Field Content Virtually

To bring the unique character of the Mojave field environment to a broader audience, the committee has committed to incorporating high-definition (1080p) pre-recorded videos from key research sites. These segments will offer immersive insight into on-the-ground restoration efforts and monitoring activities. Videos will be carefully compressed for optimal streaming even under limited bandwidth conditions, with file sizes kept below 1GB where possible. Live Q&A sessions will follow each field video, and streaming of live segments will be restricted to locations with reliable connectivity to avoid disruptions for remote viewers.

### Addressing Regional Connectivity Challenges

Recent on-site tests indicate that 4G/LTE coverage at the Desert Research Center is limited and may be subject to sudden drops. To mitigate these risks, Wi-Fi mesh repeaters are being deployed throughout the facility, and critical media content will be loaded to a local server as a backup measure. An IT support team will be on standby to rapidly address any issues that arise during the event, ensuring continuity for all attendees.

### Enhancing Audience Engagement

To foster connection between panelists and the broader audience, each speaker will share a 3-5 minute real-world anecdote from their recent fieldwork or conservation collaborations. These stories are selected to align with pressing regional conservation priorities, such as the challenges of restoration following wildfire or success stories in species recovery. Each anecdote will be visually supported by photos or short video clips to deepen engagement.  

Remote attendees will participate through moderated chat, with dedicated time allocated for Q&A. The in-person audience will have the opportunity to engage directly with panelists during open forum sessions. To promote inclusivity, all webinar content will include closed captioning and alternative text descriptions for images, and live polls and infographics will gauge participant feedback in real time.

## Key Decisions

- **Panel Leadership:** Dr. Stefan Haas confirmed as the lead panelist, focusing on restoration and field monitoring strategies in arid systems.
- **Venue:** The Desert Research Center designated as the primary webinar venue, supporting both in-person and virtual components.
- **Connectivity:** Redundant broadband and mobile hotspot systems will be deployed to compensate for potential connectivity fluctuations in the Mojave.
- **Content Delivery:** A mix of pre-recorded and live sessions will be integrated to support both virtual and on-site audiences.

## Outreach and Engagement Strategies

To maximize scientific exchange and connect with key stakeholders, a multi-pronged outreach approach is being implemented:

### Targeted Communication

Personalized invitations will be sent to regional conservation practitioners, university departments, and Mojave land management agencies to ensure participation from those most directly involved in desert stewardship. The event will also be promoted through national ecological societies’ newsletters and online research forums to reach a broad professional audience.

### Academic and Scientific Outreach

The communications team will circulate event details via academic mailing lists—including ecology, environmental science, and anthropology departments—and post announcements on platforms such as ResearchGate and LinkedIn groups. Requests for collaboration and co-sponsorship have gone out to university field stations and local scientific societies to broaden scholarly engagement.

### Regional and Community Partnerships

Direct outreach will target tribal stewardship groups, regional land management agencies, and NGOs working in the Mojave. A press release template has been prepared for local media outlets, helping to publicize the event's commitment to diverse participation and highlighting current field research in desert conservation.

### Multi-modal Engagement

A dedicated event page will be launched, offering live updates, a countdown timer, and technical assistance resources. In advance of the event, a 60-second teaser video featuring fieldwork highlights from each panelist will be shared across social media and organizational web pages to generate excitement. Following the webinar, attendees will receive a feedback survey, and a summary of key takeaways will be distributed to foster ongoing collaboration and knowledge exchange.
