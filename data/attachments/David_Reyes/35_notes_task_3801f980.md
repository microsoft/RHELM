## 1. Meal Overview

This meal log card details my experience preparing and consuming Spicy Veggie Wraps accompanied by herbal tea while conducting ecological fieldwork in a remote, arid environment. The recipe prioritizes convenience, shelf-stability, and nutrition—all essential for extended field deployments where resources are limited and conditions can be challenging. Each ingredient has been vetted for compliance with USDA food standards and desert survival recommendations, ensuring both reliability and suitability in harsh climates.

I designed this meal plan specifically for days when portability, hydration, and sustained energy are crucial. Every component packs easily, withstands heat, and allows for simple assembly without extensive kitchen equipment. I relied on the provided log card structure to track meal effectiveness and environmental adaptability, making incremental changes based on daily feedback.

## 3. Field Preparation Procedure

When preparing the wrap in the field, I focus on simplicity and efficiency:

1. I open the vacuum-sealed wrap and position it on a clean, flat surface—often using wax paper or the sturdy cover of my field notebook.
2. Next, I layer the dried bell pepper strips (or, on lucky days, jarred bell peppers) down the center.
3. I add canned chickpeas, making sure to drain any excess liquid to avoid sogginess. Occasionally, when supplies run low, roasted chickpea snacks fill in nicely.
4. A generous sprinkle of my pre-mixed spice blend brings the whole meal to life—cumin, smoked paprika, cayenne, a hint of black pepper and salt. I adjust the spices based on the day’s heat and my own appetite.
5. For added richness and satiety, I drizzle olive oil from a single-serve packet, though I sometimes skip this if I want a lighter meal or when packing space is tight.
6. Rolling the wrap tightly, I fold in the ends so nothing spills out during hikes. To keep it clean and portable, I secure it with foil or a biodegradable wrap.
7. Depending on my schedule, I either eat right away or stash the wrap in a shaded, insulated pouch for later.
8. After eating, I jot down quick notes about my hydration, energy, and any relevant environmental details—heat, wind, fatigue—in the nutrition/feedback section.
9. I remain vigilant about waste, carefully packing out any wrappers or remnants according to Leave No Trace principles.

The process is streamlined to require only a small knife. When my team joins for a shared meal, we scale up ingredients and pass around the spice packet for everyone to season to taste.

## 4. Herbal Tea: Hydration and Local Adaptation

Herbal tea is both a comfort and a vital hydration strategy. I opt for blends that don’t add to dehydration and fare well under desert heat:

- My go-tos include chamomile, sage, desert mint (Mentha species), and holy basil (Ocimum tenuiflorum). Chamomile and desert mint, in particular, are mild, soothing, and reduce the urge for less hydrating caffeinated drinks.
- On good days, I forage for wild sage or desert mint—common in the arid regions of North America—always double-checking plant identity with a field guide or my botanist colleague before consuming anything new.
- To prepare, I use my compact camp kettle and boil water over a portable stove. When fire regulations allow, I enjoy using a solar kettle or a small campfire.
- I steep a tablespoon of dried herb or a fresh sprig for five to eight minutes, then strain out any solids.
- During strenuous outings, I’ll occasionally add a pinch of salt or a dash of electrolyte powder to the brew. This helps with hydration, especially in the hottest part of the day.

I’m mindful to choose teas with negligible caffeine and monitor my body’s response to both hydration and the heat. Safe foraging is my top priority; mistakes in plant identification are easily avoided with careful recordkeeping and expert consultation.

For more detailed info about safe herb foraging and desert hydration, I refer to the US Army Survival Handbook and the National Park Service’s Desert Plant Guide.

## 6. Comments & Team Observations

**Field Feedback:**  
- **Recipe Adjustments:** Today, I substituted roasted chickpeas for canned due to limited water for rinsing. The roast gave a pleasant crunch, but the wrap felt drier—an extra drizzle of olive oil made up for that.
- **Performance and Hydration:** Compared to my usual trail mix, this wrap (combined with the herbal tea) seemed to boost my endurance during transect walks. I felt less sluggish, and my water intake actually decreased.
- **Packability:** Using jarred bell peppers increases waste and weight, but dried pepper strips are significantly lighter and space-saving. For extended trips, I plan to prepackage spice blends in smaller, refillable pouches.
- **Local Plant Notes:** My team identified a patch of desert mint (Mentha spicata) nearby—perfect for brewing refreshing tea. We documented coordinates and leaf characteristics for reference.
- **Team Input:** My field partner noted that flatbreads hold up better against high heat than soft wraps, so we’ll trial those tomorrow. Suggestions for further waste reduction include switching from foil to wax paper and minimizing single-use packets.
