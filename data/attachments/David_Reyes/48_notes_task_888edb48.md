## Practical Improvements (Suggestions Only — Not Claims About What Happened)
These are straightforward upgrades that keep the routine heat-safe while making future logs more comparable and useful.

### 1) Scheduling and heat safety
- Set a clear session window (for example, **15–30 minutes**) with planned breaks rather than pushing through fatigue.  
- Add a simple “stop check” for heat illness signs (dizziness, confusion, nausea, cramps, unusual fatigue) and end the session immediately if they appear. Ref: CDC heat illness prevention guidance [1].

### 2) Hydration tracking that stays lightweight
- Record water and other drinks in rough household units (cups/oz) and tag them as **before / during / after** instead of relying on exact clock times.
- Include a quick dehydration screen such as **urine color** plus a short note on thirst. If risk looks elevated, prioritize fluids and cooling. Ref: MedlinePlus dehydration overview [2].

### 3) Routine consistency and progression
- Keep a repeatable template (warm-up → 4–6 movements → cool-down) and track just one progression metric, such as **total rounds** or **RPE (rating of perceived exertion)**. Ref: ACSM general exercise guidance [5].
- If multiple people are around, label each person’s involvement as **Full / Modified / Observed** per movement. It keeps the log honest while removing ambiguity.

### 4) A quick end-of-session “heat check”
- Add one line at the end: headache, cramps, lightheadedness, nausea, or unusual fatigue recorded as **yes/no + brief note**. Ref: CDC [1].
