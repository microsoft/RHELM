# Ecological Field Equipment Cleaning and Calibration Review Report  
**Ridgecrest, CA (Mojave Desert) | January 23, 2024**

---

## Overview

This report presents a detailed account of equipment cleaning and calibration procedures conducted on January 23, 2024, for critical ecological field tools used in and around Ridgecrest, California, within the Mojave Desert. Devices assessed include the Garmin Instinct Solar GPS unit, a range of field sampling equipment (nets, traps, soil samplers), and the Canon EOS 90D camera. Maintenance activities were systematically organized into morning and afternoon sessions, strictly following established manufacturer guidelines and standardized field protocols referenced in peer-reviewed literature.

The following sections offer comprehensive tables and step-by-step methodologies for each device, a full inventory of cleaning supplies utilized, up-to-date equipment status notes, identified issues with documented corrective measures, and an assessment of post-maintenance performance. Recommendations are provided at the end, with an emphasis on sustainable preventive maintenance tailored to the demanding, arid conditions of the Mojave Desert and best practices for securing data integrity throughout field operations.

## Device-by-Device Maintenance Procedures and Outcomes

### Garmin Instinct Solar GPS

#### Cleaning and Calibration

- **Surface Cleaning:**  
  Carefully removed accumulated desert dust from the device’s housing and around buttons with an antistatic brush. The screen and casing were sanitized using 70% isopropyl alcohol wipes, with particular attention to crevices. Pressurized air (via blower) was directed at the USB charging port to clear fine grit.
  
- **Ports and Electrical Contacts:**  
  All contacts and ports were visually examined for corrosion and debris. Residual sand was swept away with isopropyl wipes and a follow-up blow from the air tool.

- **System Restart and Software Update:**  
  The device was restarted to clear cache and re-synchronize onboard memory, and the firmware update status was confirmed as current per Garmin documentation [1].

- **Navigation Calibration:**  
  Location calibration was initiated in an open area, using the manufacturer’s prescribed steps. Both compass and barometric altimeter were recalibrated through the device menu using standardized orientation techniques.

- **Battery Health and Charging:**  
  Solar charging function was verified, and battery statistics were recorded for subsequent field days.

#### Issues and Corrective Actions

| Issue Identified            | Response                          |
|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Dust in charging port       | Cleared with air blower; no residual|
| Satellite fix delay         | Performed hard reset and compass calibration|
| Isolated battery warning    | Replaced with new OEM battery     |

#### Post-Maintenance Performance

A controlled test walk was performed over mapped reference points. The GPS achieved a satellite lock within 50 seconds. Position accuracy remained within 2.7 meters—fully aligned with manufacturer and published field expectations. Waypoint management and track logging worked reliably, and compass accuracy was confirmed against celestial and shadow cues.

### Sampling Equipment: Nets, Traps, and Soil Samplers

#### Cleaning and Calibration

- **Nets and Traps:**  
  Mesh elements were thoroughly soaked in dilute detergent and gently scrubbed with nylon brushes to remove persistent soil and organic residues. After a freshwater rinse and mechanical inspection for damage, the mesh was air-dried. Metal frames and handles were disinfected with 70% ethanol wipes, ensuring no chemical residue was left behind.
  
- **Sherman Traps:**  
  Springs and hinges on all traps were checked for functionality. Fine tweezers were used to extract embedded sand and grit, and moving parts were lubricated with a non-petroleum, dust-repellent lubricant.

- **Soil Samplers:**  
  All external and internal surfaces were scrubbed clean, with threads and moving components cleared of sand. Calibration was checked via gravimetric and volumetric standards to ensure accurate sample measurement, referencing USGS and EPA tolerances [4,5].

#### Issues and Corrective Actions

| Equipment           | Observed Problem         | Resolution                                                         |
|---------------------|-------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Butterfly net       | Small mesh tear         | Patched using mesh repair kit and adhesive; retested for tension   |
| Sherman traps       | Slow closure            | Removed grit, applied suitable lubricant, restored full function   |
| Soil sampler        | Sticking thread         | Cleaned and lubricated; recalibration performed; threading now smooth |

#### Post-Maintenance Assessment

Mechanical tests of nets and traps revealed smooth, reliable operation—even on units previously reporting sluggish performance. Inspected nets with repairs maintained proper tension and mesh integrity under moderate load. Soil sampler delivered samples within stated volume accuracy, with measured variance well inside ±2% as required for ecological sampling.

### Canon EOS 90D Camera

#### Cleaning and Calibration

- **Optical and Exterior Cleaning:**  
  All dust was removed from the lens, viewfinder, and body using an air blower, followed by careful wiping of the lens and screen with a microfiber cloth and approved lens cleaner. Attention was paid to both front and rear optical elements.
  
- **Sensor Maintenance:**  
  Enabled the sensor cleaning function before using purpose-specific sensor swabs to gently eliminate a stubborn dust spot.
  
- **Power and Storage:**  
  Inspected battery terminals and SD card contacts with an electronics brush, confirming no corrosion or foreign particles.

- **System and Image Calibration:**  
  In-camera autofocus and white balance were recalibrated using a standardized color chart under natural light. Firmware was checked for updates via Canon’s EOS Utility software, and was confirmed current [2].

| Identified Issue        | Action Taken                              |

| Lens smudge             | Removed with microfiber cloth and lens solution |
| Persistent sensor dust  | Cleared with sensor swab after cleaning cycle    |

#### Functional Verification

A set of high-resolution images captured under daylight conditions confirmed accurate focus, clean imaging, and correct white balance. The sensor showed no remaining blemishes post-cleaning. Image storage and read/write functionality on SD cards fully met field performance expectations.

## Recommendations

### Preventive Equipment Care

- **Routine Dust Removal:**  
  Brush and air-blow all equipment surfaces after every field use to limit abrasive wear from desert particulates.
- **Protective Storage:**  
  Store sensitive electronics in padded, sealed cases. Include silica gel packs to prevent moisture accumulation, even in low-humidity environments like the Mojave.
- **Lubrication Protocol:**  
  Lubricate hinges, springs, and threaded elements on field gear with dust-repelling, non-petroleum products at the end of each collection day.
- **Firmware and Battery Protocols:**  
  Consistently check for software updates before deployment. Charge all batteries overnight and store fully charged spares in moisture-proof containers.
- **Scheduled Calibration:**  
  - **GPS:** Validate positioning accuracy weekly against benchmarked reference points.
  - **Sampling Gear:** Recalibrate soil samplers and repair/inspect nets on a monthly basis or post-intensive collection events.
  - **Camera:** Conduct daily checks of focus calibration, sensor cleanliness, and image test shots logged prior to documentation sessions.

### Data Integrity and Field Readiness

- **Detailed Logging:**  
  Maintain thorough, contemporaneous records of equipment maintenance and calibration—including supply batch numbers, component repairs, and test results—both in hardcopy field logs and digital files.
- **Frequent Data Backup:**  
  Export and duplicate all digital files (GPS data, photographs) at least twice a day to separate storage devices, ensuring redundancy and minimizing data loss risks.
- **Anomaly Tracking and Escalation:**  
  Document any signs of persistent equipment malfunction and flag them for in-depth review if resolved by field protocols.
- **Cross-Validation:**  
  Systematically duplicate at least 10% of voucher specimens and photo records for independent verification, bolstering scientific rigor.

### Adaptive Field Practices

- **Accelerated Maintenance During Extreme Conditions:**  
  Increase cleaning and calibration frequency during and after dust storms or extended multi-day field efforts.
- **Staff Training:**  
  Administrate regular training sessions for staff and volunteers addressing equipment care, Mojave-specific risks, and on-site troubleshooting.

## Broader Significance

Implementing consistent, methodical maintenance routines is vital for sustaining uninterrupted operations in the harsh conditions of the Mojave Desert. These protocols guard against premature equipment failure, prevent data loss or contamination, and assure that records—both digital and physical—remain admissible and credible for ecological research and regulatory compliance.

The maintenance log, incorporating all calibration records and post-maintenance outcomes, creates an unbroken chain of custody for data collected during fieldwork. This level of detail ensures that every datum and image can be traced to well-maintained, accurately calibrated instruments—an essential requirement for transparent, reproducible ecological science.
