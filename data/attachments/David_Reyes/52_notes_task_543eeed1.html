July 2024 Desert Birdwatching & Hiking Itinerary

Collaborative Fieldwork: David Reyes & Maria Lopez

Fieldwork Period: July 3 – July 28, 2024

Overview

This itinerary outlines a collaborative fieldwork plan designed for David Reyes (Research Ecologist & Conservation Project Lead) and Maria Lopez in the arid and semi-arid environments characteristic of the Southwestern United States—specifically the Sonoran, Mojave, and Chihuahuan deserts. Our research focus blends early morning bird surveys, habitat assessments, and hiking protocols, emphasizing both avian conservation priorities and logistical considerations required for safe and effective field work. The schedule allows for flexible adaptation to local conditions, with a particular emphasis on verifying weather forecasts to safeguard data quality and participant wellbeing.

Research Focus: Target Bird Species and Conservation Objectives

Throughout July, our fieldwork will center on several key avian species, chosen for their relevance as ecological indicators, their conservation status, and their representation of distinct desert habitats:

  • LeConte’s Thrasher (Toxostoma lecontei) – A rare species used to assess the integrity of undisturbed desert scrub; primary goal is population mapping.
  • Bendire’s Thrasher (Toxostoma bendirei) – Of conservation concern; work will focus on determining current breeding status.
  • Gilded Flicker (Colaptes chrysoides) – Endemic to the Sonoran desert, monitored for cavity-nesting habitat quality and density.
  • Bell’s Vireo (Vireo bellii) – An important riparian habitat indicator; repeat visits for nest monitoring and population counts.
  • Southwestern Willow Flycatcher (Empidonax traillii extimus) – Endangered; monitoring focuses on breeding sites within willow-dominated riparian corridors.
  • Cactus Wren (Campylorhynchus brunneicapillus) – Key to mapping territory structure in cactus-rich areas and tracking restoration progress.
  • Gray Vireo (Vireo vicinior) – Focus on migration tracking in semi-arid shrublands.
  • Elf Owl (Micrathene whitneyi) – Nocturnal surveys at dusk to monitor population and vocal activity at selected sites.

Across all outings, our objectives include gathering data on population sizes, mapping distributions, tracking breeding and migration patterns, and evaluating habitat attributes. These efforts feed directly into recommendations for site management and inform broader desert conservation strategies.

Field Station Maintenance Protocol

Efficient equipment and station upkeep is crucial for uninterrupted data collection and team safety. The following checklist will be completed during each scheduled station visit (7/6, 7/18, 7/22, 7/25):

  • Equipment Calibration
  • GPS units and data loggers verified and corrected daily

  • Audio recorders and microphones tested for reliable bird call recording

  • Camera Operations

  • Motion-sensor trail cameras installed and adjusted at wildlife corridors

  • Battery and storage capacity checked before redeployment

  • Water Management

  • Drinking and emergency water supplies monitored and topped up regularly

  • Natural water source flow and condition assessed along station perimeters

  • Solar Panel Maintenance

  • Panels dusted and cleaned early in the morning for optimal performance

  • Battery and panel connections inspected for consistent charging

  • General Facility Operations

  • Digital data and handwritten field notebooks backed up daily
  • Perimeter walks to check fence integrity; repair any damage as needed
  • Shade shelters and first aid kits inspected and restocked accordingly

  • Weather station and sensor apparatus cleaned and sanitized

  • Safety Procedures

  • Fire, weather, and emergency protocols reviewed with all participants before fieldwork
  • Satellite communicator carried at all times; local emergency contacts updated

Tasks should be checked and adapted based on real-time equipment logs and immediate field conditions.

July: Research Goals and Program Milestones

Project Objectives

  • Map territories for LeConte’s and Bendire’s Thrasher within Sonoran scrub habitats.
  • Assess breeding status and nesting success rates for Bell’s Vireo and Southwestern Willow Flycatcher, focusing on riparian zone health.
  • Establish cavity nest density data and habitat features for Gilded Flicker, supporting long-term monitoring.
  • Track migration flux and territory shifts for Gray Vireo; conduct dusk vocal surveys for Elf Owl populations.
  • Record population clusters for Cactus Wren; monitor the progress of restoration plantings at station north plots.
  • Ensure rigorous calibration of all field equipment; back up digital records to maintain data integrity.
  • Update the indicator species database, complete breeding status reporting for endangered taxa, and document habitat restoration impacts in riparian and upland zones.

Expected Field Milestones

  • Frequent sightings of Toxostoma lecontei, Campylorhynchus brunneicapillus, Colaptes chrysoides, and Vireo bellii anticipated.
  • Special focus on documentation (visual and audio) for rare or sensitive species, including breeding sites of Empidonax traillii extimus and dusk activity of Elf Owl.
  • GIS map updates, breeding success metrics, and photographic evidence collected for reporting to local conservation partners and advisory boards.
