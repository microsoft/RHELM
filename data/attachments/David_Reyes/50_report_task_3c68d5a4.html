Digital Archive Backup Report

Ridgecrest Wildlife Archive — Photographic Data

Backup Date: June 29, 2024

Prepared by: David Reyes, Research Ecologist & Conservation Project Lead

Operation Summary

On June 29, 2024, the Ridgecrest Wildlife Archive completed its scheduled incremental backup of photographic datasets. This procedure is essential to maintaining comprehensive protection and continuity of our growing collection, in line with DataONE best practices[1] for research data stewardship. The focus of the session was to synchronize recent fieldwork imagery—particularly bobcat and desert quail datasets—from our primary SSD storage device to our encrypted AWS S3 cloud repository.

The backup process was carried out between 02:00 and 03:45 PDT, utilizing ArchivePro v3.2’s automated cloud synchronization capabilities. Prior to transfer, all new and recently modified files were identified, allowing for an efficient, selective backup of 3,417 images. Verification routines incorporated dual-layered SHA256 checksum validation before and after upload, supported by automated redundancy checks for reliability.

During the session, the system proactively flagged and resolved two issues: a file naming conflict in the “Bobcat_Sequoia_2024” folder and a checksum verification failure for a single desert quail image. Both were handled through pre-configured automation—file renaming scripts and adaptive re-upload procedures—without the need for manual intervention. These timely resolutions ensured there was no data loss or prolonged interruption, with all actions meticulously logged to maintain audit trails and ongoing compliance.

SSD to Cloud Synchronization: Process, Verification, and Error Handling

Staging and Transfer Workflow:
All photographic data from the primary field SSD was prepared for transfer according to our established hierarchical folder system (organized by year, species, and event/date). This structure, in addition to supporting efficient review, also optimizes metadata association and future retrieval. The incremental backup algorithm ensured only new or modified files were sent, reducing both bandwidth usage and the risk of transmission errors.

Verification Procedures:
Each file underwent checksum generation and validation on both storage ends, reinforcing data integrity throughout the process. ArchivePro v3.2’s built-in redundancy allowed for immediate detection of inconsistencies; any file failing post-upload verification was flagged for automatic reprocessing. During this session, all files completed verification successfully except one, which failed its initial upload but was seamlessly corrected. These checks are documented as standard protocol to maintain our commitment to best practices.

Management of Errors and Anomalies:
- A file naming conflict in “Bobcat_Sequoia_2024” was detected at 02:28 PDT, resolved via automated renaming scripts. - One desert quail image failed checksum verification following upload but was automatically re-uploaded and verified correctly on the second attempt. - No sustained synchronization disruptions occurred, and the system logs reflect immediate recognition and response to each anomaly.

Adaptive Management Measures:
Automated scripts addressed file standardization requirements and enabled rapid, hands-off resolution of conflicts. Meanwhile, real-time notifications kept all relevant personnel informed, ensuring transparency and swift follow-up as needed.

Folder Structure and Data Organization Updates

Maintaining an intuitive and reliable digital structure is key to supporting both day-to-day research operations and long-term data stewardship. Our archival system employs a hierarchical taxonomy, with folders defined first by year, then by fieldwork period, species, and specific collection events or locations. This model preserves traceability and supports metadata-driven workflows.

Recent Adjustments: - Within “Bobcat_Sequoia_2024,” new subfolders were introduced to reflect expanded camera trap deployments in previously unsampled zones. - In response to increased photographic volume, “Desert_Quail_Ridgecrest_202406” was divided into “Week3” and “Week4” subfolders, maintaining manageability and ensuring no folder exceeds the 500-item threshold. - Several bobcat image sequences were retagged following a GPS metadata audit, improving spatial accuracy and data reliability.

Data Flow from Field to Archive:
Images are captured, then transferred daily from the field cameras to the SSD, accompanied by time-stamped logs. Immediately after local transfer, metadata—including GPS tracks and field researcher annotations—is integrated, providing rich context for each image. On completion of the cloud backup, all files inherit standardized metadata and are ready for subsequent analysis, curation, or sharing in gallery format.

Assessment of the Backup Operation

The June 29, 2024 backup operation met and, in several respects, surpassed core ecological data management standards:

  • Data Integrity: Complete SHA256 checksum verification was achieved for all archived files. Detected anomalies were resolved promptly within the session, ensuring a fully reliable backup.
  • Responsiveness: All error management protocols operated effectively and as designed. Automation facilitated immediate corrections and prevented workflow interruptions.
  • Quality Assurance: Error incidence remained below 0.1%, with every issue addressed and closed. This is consistent with, or exceeds, [DataONE Best Practices][1] for research data management.
  • Organization: Ongoing adjustments to folder structures and metadata systems enable transparent, organized, and accessible archived data.
  • Recordkeeping: Detailed logs of every operation, error, and resolution are maintained according to NOSC standards, ensuring that our archival system is fully auditable for future reference or sharing.

Overall, the archive reflects a rigorous commitment to ecological data stewardship, positioning our datasets for both immediate research requirements and the needs of long-term conservation.
