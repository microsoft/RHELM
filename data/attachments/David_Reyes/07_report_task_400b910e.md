**Observer:** David Reyes

**Start Time:** 06:32 (local)

## 1. Location and Habitat Overview

**Area Surveyed:**  
East-facing foothills of the Mojave Desert, situated near the Kelso Dunes.  
**Central GPS Reference:** 34.8925°N, 115.7013°W  
**Elevation:** ~960 meters (3,150 feet) above sea level

### Habitat Description

The survey area is characterized by rolling alluvial fans and gently sloped terrain broken by rocky outcrops. Open flats dominate the landscape, with intermittent sandy washes channeling through the site. Vegetation predominantly consists of creosote bush (Larrea tridentata) and white bursage (Ambrosia dumosa), punctuated by scattered stands of Joshua trees (Yucca brevifolia), cheesebush (Ambrosia salsola), and occasional desert senna (Senna armata). The soil is primarily loose, well-drained sand, interspersed with gravel and areas of fine silt within the washes. Scattered basalt rock fragments dot the site, and water is notably absent aside from ephemeral run-off depressions observed after recent rains.

Microhabitats within the study area include dense shrub thickets, open expanses of sandy soil, and ephemeral grassy patches. Numerous shallow arroyos bisect the terrain, providing additional structural variety. There are clear signs of an active prey base, evidenced by numerous old rodent burrows and recent digging activity.

## 2. Research Objective

The primary aim of this field outing was to assess the occurrence and habitat use of kit fox (Vulpes macrotis) in these foothill environments. Results from this trip will inform ongoing conservation monitoring, help prioritize critical habitat areas, and support adaptive management efforts within the larger Mojave Desert conservation initiative.

## 4. Kit Fox Observation Summary

### Confirmed Sighting

- **Time:** 06:58  
- **Location:** 34.8931°N, 115.7019°W (sandy arroyo margin)  
- **Habitat:** Edge of a rodent burrow system beneath a dense creosote and cheesebush thicket, located on the leeward side of a rocky outcrop approximately 60 meters from the nearest OHV track  
- **Distance:** Roughly 40 meters from observer  
- **Age Class:** Not determinable (individual partly obscured; general morphology matches adult or subadult)  
- **Description:** The observed fox was slender, with a pale-gray coat and prominent black-tipped bushy tail. It displayed erect ears and a highly alert posture throughout.  
- **Behavioral Notes:**  
    - Emerged from what appeared to be an active den shortly after sunrise — indicative of denning behavior.
    - Paused at the burrow entrance, scanning its surroundings with apparent caution.
    - Engaged in pouncing behavior targeting a presumed kangaroo rat in an adjacent sandy patch, though the prey escape was successful.
    - When wind gusts increased, the fox retreated swiftly under the creosote cover, using noticeable tail flicks as it did so.
    - No vocalizations were detected during the encounter.

**Ecological Interpretation:**  
This direct observation provides clear evidence of kit fox activity within this microhabitat, specifically highlighting den site fidelity in areas combining well-developed shrub cover, sandy substrate, and active rodent populations. The unsuccessful foraging display underscores the importance of open, sandy areas with abundant small mammal prey and thick shrubbery for shelter—a recurring theme in kit fox habitat studies. The absence of recent human activity in the immediate area reinforces the suitability of this site for sustained kit fox occupancy.

## 8. Reflections, Recommendations, and Conservation Implications

### Survey Highlights

The direct detection of a kit fox in this section of the Mojave foothills—through a clear, behaviorally informative sighting—affirms the ongoing presence of the species in a landscape largely free from immediate anthropogenic disturbance. The combination of detailed observational data and photographic evidence strengthens the baseline for future monitoring. High diversity among both prey and competitor species was noted, further underscoring the ecological integrity of these foothill patches.

### Adaptive Recommendations

To complement direct observation efforts:
- I recommend deploying motion-sensor camera traps at active rodent burrow systems to increase detection probability, particularly during nocturnal periods when kit fox activity peaks.
- Repeating transect walks during dawn and dusk, especially on calmer days, will maximize opportunities for both visual contacts and indirect sign documentation.
- Collaborating with regional survey teams can help expand the spatial analysis of kit fox presence across similar microhabitats, particularly in water-limited zones.
- It is also important to consistently record indirect evidence (tracks, scat, den sites) alongside direct sightings to develop a more nuanced understanding of population density and movement within these fragmented habitats.

### Conservation Priorities

Current observations highlight the necessity of preserving intact sandy-shrub mosaics that provide critical denning and foraging habitat. Proactive management of off-highway vehicle (OHV) use and enforcement against illegal trail incursions will help minimize disturbance in these sensitive microhabitats. Both denning and prey capture activity observed today support the growing body of evidence pointing to the role of healthy rodent populations and protective shrub cover in sustaining viable kit fox populations.

Continued reconnaissance and methodical data collection will provide the robust foundation necessary for upcoming regional land management plans and species assessments. These efforts will contribute directly to the long-term survival of kit fox populations in the Mojave and similar arid landscapes.
