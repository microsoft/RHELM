This midday virtual meeting was organized as part of ongoing efforts to integrate Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) into site-specific conservation planning for the Mojave Desert. ## Technical Disruptions: Detailed Account

The meeting was significantly impacted by recurring technical difficulties, largely due to unreliable rural broadband access in both Ridgecrest and Cahuilla territories. These problems hindered effective dialogue and limited the amount of agenda content that could be discussed.

- **Internet Connectivity:** Both parties experienced unstable broadband. At the Elder’s location, upload speeds frequently fell below 1 Mbps, with download speeds under 2 Mbps (as indicated by Zoom’s diagnostics). These intermittent drops led to session interruptions and extended periods of lost connection.
- **Audio Lag and Dropouts:** There were pronounced audio delays (up to 10 seconds) and repeated instances where the audio cut out entirely. The problem worsened with several participants unmuted simultaneously, leading to occasional echo and feedback that further blurred communication.
- **Regional Infrastructure Challenges:** High-speed internet remains inconsistent in much of rural California. Ongoing maintenance in both Ridgecrest and tribal areas exacerbated connection instability, particularly during the midday hours when usage peaks.
- **Zoom Platform Performance:** Zoom attempted to compensate for poor connectivity by lowering video quality and, on multiple occasions, displayed “reconnecting” prompts, especially on the Elder’s end.

Despite multiple efforts to mitigate these disruptions, communication was only intermittently possible.

## Key Agenda Items Not Addressed

Critical discussion areas remained largely untouched or were covered only superficially due to the connectivity issues:

- **Restoration Plot Protocols:** There was insufficient time to establish detailed criteria for new site selection and plant species prioritization, nor to define culturally informed restoration targets.
- **Seasonal TEK Indicators:** The integration of Elder-identified biological indicators into Western monitoring protocols was deferred to a future conversation.
- **Data Governance Agreements:** Plans to finalize arrangements for managing culturally sensitive data and setting up community-led validation were set aside.
- **Joint Fieldwork Coordination:** Logistical planning for safety, transportation, and culturally necessary preparedness could not proceed.

## Proposals and Considerations for Rescheduling

To ensure a more successful follow-up and accommodate Cahuilla community protocols, the following steps are recommended:

- **Consultation on Timing:** Engage directly with the Elder to identify mutually agreeable dates and times, respecting both the tribal calendar and key project timelines, while also steering clear of significant ceremonial days and peak environmental field periods.
- **Hybrid or Alternative Formats:** If persistent internet challenges are likely, consider arranging hybrid meetings. The Ridgecrest team can connect virtually, while the Elder may participate by telephone or, if possible, through scheduled in-person components at the earliest opportunity.
- **Technical Preparation:** Institute a standard technical check 24–48 hours before the meeting, involving all participating team members and tribal partners.
- **Advance Material Sharing:** Distribute concise agenda overviews and supporting visual materials in PDF or printed form ahead of the meeting, giving the Elder the ability to follow along if connection fails.
- **Contingency Planning:** Clearly document backup communication approaches in the meeting invitation—such as fallback telephone calls or asynchronous voice recordings.
- **Liaison Coordination:** Assign primary responsibility to Mr. Vasquez, the tribal liaison, for confirming the Elder’s preferences and well-being with respect to these arrangements.

- Reconvene with a focus on collaborative protocol development and the systematic integration of TEK into ongoing projects.

- Document any additional barriers encountered, so these can inform ongoing improvement of collaborative infrastructure.

### Implications for Project Coordination

Although the isolated delay is manageable, repeated disruption risks cascading effects, especially with respect to fieldwork readiness, ethical review for data sharing, and incorporation of the Elder’s recommendations into seasonal planning. Proactive rescheduling and reliable communication strategies will be essential to keeping both the project and the partnership on track.
