## 6) Outcome assessment (what caused the dryness and unevenness)
The **dry, uneven hand-pies** traced back to one main issue: **they stayed in the heat too long while I had attention in too many places**.

That showed up in three concrete ways:

1. **Late doneness checks**  
   Once hand-pies hit “done,” the remaining time is basically dehydration time—especially at corners and seams.

2. **Uneven heat exposure (missed or delayed rotation)**  
   When the pan doesn’t get rotated on time, one section browns harder and dries out, while other pieces lag behind.

3. **Carryover heat after baking**  
   Even after coming out of the oven, a hot tray keeps pulling moisture out of the crust. If the pies rest too long on the pan, the bottoms and edges keep firming up past the pleasant zone.

The result wasn’t just “a bit dry.” It was **inconsistent**—some bites felt tough and overdone, others just felt like a normal baked pastry. That mismatch is what made the batch feel off.

## 8) What I’m changing next time (practical fixes, not wishful thinking)

### A) One person owns the oven—no exceptions
If hand-pies (or anything timing-sensitive) are on the plan again, I’m assigning a single **oven lead** whose job is only:
- set timers,
- rotate the tray,
- check doneness on schedule,
- call the pull time.

Then I separate the rest:
- **Proposal facilitator/timekeeper** (keeps the meeting from bleeding into baking focus)
