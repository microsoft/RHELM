## 1. Breakfast: Insulated Burrito Variants

Morning nutrition is critical to sustaining concentration, mood, and physical capability during prolonged exposure to heat and exertion in the desert. We prepared two breakfast burrito options, stored in preheated insulated containers to retain warmth and freshness. Both options feature balanced macronutrient profiles, moderate electrolytes, and practical packaging suitable for field conditions.

### 1.1 Spicy Tofu-Chili Thermos Burrito *(Vegan)*

- **Ingredients:**
  - Firm tofu (150g, cubed)
  - Black beans (100g)
  - Diced tomatoes (80g)
  - Bell peppers (60g)
  - Onion (40g)
  - Chili powder (1 tsp)
  - Cumin (0.5 tsp), smoked paprika (0.5 tsp)
  - Olive oil (1 tbsp)
  - Whole-wheat tortilla
  - Salt, to taste

- **Preparation:**
  Sauté tofu and vegetables until golden and aromatic. Mix in beans, tomatoes, and spices, reducing liquid until the filling holds together. Wrap the mixture in a tortilla and transfer immediately into a heated thermos to preserve heat and texture until consumed.

- **Nutritional Focus:**
  Each burrito supplies approximately 18g protein, 45g carbohydrates, and 12g fat. This combination ensures steady energy, provides plant-based protein, and offers necessary sodium and potassium for electrolyte balance—essential during physical work in dry climates. Fiber content supports sustained energy release.

### 1.2 Egg & Potato Thermos Burrito

- **Ingredients:**
  - 2 large eggs (scrambled)
  - Boiled potatoes (100g, cubed)
  - Onion (40g)
  - Bell peppers (60g)
  - Cheddar cheese (30g, optional)
  - Whole-wheat tortilla
  - Butter or olive oil for cooking
  - Salt and pepper

- **Preparation:**
  Sauté potatoes with onions and bell peppers until tender, then add eggs and scramble. Combine ingredients, fold in cheese if included, and roll tightly in a tortilla. Pack into a thermos immediately after cooking.

- **Nutritional Focus:**
  Each serving provides roughly 20g protein, 50g carbohydrates, and 15g fat. Eggs offer complete animal protein and help meet elevated energy needs. Potassium in the potatoes assists with muscle function, and sodium from cheese and seasoning supports hydration.

### 1.3 Field Preparation & Practicality

Insulated food containers provide critical spoilage control and preserve palatability even in temperatures exceeding 30°C. Using burritos as a breakfast solution limits mess and hand contamination, supporting field hygiene. Sodium was included thoughtfully to balance electrolyte loss while avoiding excess intake (see [1][2]).

## 2. Snacks & Hydration During Fieldwork

Continuous nutrition and hydration are vital for sustaining both physical performance and vigilance during survey hours. Our approach involves permissive, regular snacking and planned fluid intake.

### 2.1 Snacks

- **Selections & Portions:**
  - Salted mixed nuts (40g/serving; 2 servings/day)
  - Dried fruit (apricots or figs; 1–2 pieces per snack, twice daily)
  - Granola/oat bars with seeds (1 bar/snack break, 2 breaks total)
  - String cheese (1 stick per break, optional)
  - Whole-grain crackers (~30g per snack)

- **Timing:**
  Snacks are scheduled every 2–3 hours (approximately at 09:30, 12:00, and 14:30).

- **Purpose:**
  Regular intake of carbohydrates and moderate protein maintains blood glucose, supporting sustained energy and mental focus [4]. Portion-controlled snack breaks prevent heat stress tied to heavy meals, while salted items actively replace sodium lost to perspiration [1-3]. All snacks are individually packaged to limit contamination and expedite distribution.

### 2.2 Fluids

- **Fluids Provided:**
  - Water (served in insulated or UV-blocking bottles)
  - Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS: 500ml water + 1/4 tsp salt + 2 tsp sugar)
  - Commercial electrolyte drinks (low sugar)

- **Intake Standards:**
  - Water: Minimum 250–500ml per active hour (targeting at least 2.5L during survey hours)
  - Electrolytes: Additional 250ml ORS required every two hours, especially under high heat

- **Rationale:**
  Consistent alternation between plain water and electrolyte solutions supports total fluid and electrolyte balance, preventing both dehydration and dilutional hyponatremia. Hydration practices follow preemptive drinking strategies recommended for desert environments—never waiting for thirst as an indicator [1-3].

## 3. End-of-Day Hydration Monitoring

End-of-day hydration is tracked, with special focus on David’s intake due to his prior heat sensitivity and muscle cramp history.

### 3.1 Fluid Intake Protocol

Each team member logs total daily fluid intake, aiming for 3–4L daily split between active fieldwork and post-field recovery (CDC/USGS recommendations [1][2]).

### 3.2 David’s Hydration Record

- During fieldwork: 2L water, 1L ORS/electrolyte solution (documented hourly)
- Post-field: 1L water over 90 minutes upon return
- Bottles are filled with filtered water and ORS is prepared prior to departure
- Symptoms such as muscle cramps or headache are logged, with electrolyte amounts adjusted as indicated by feedback

### 3.3 Water vs. Electrolyte Intake Strategy

Water is prioritized during light/moderate exertion, and when snacks containing sodium are consumed regularly. Electrolyte drinks are reserved for heavy sweating, elevated temperatures, or muscular symptoms. We opt for low-sugar solutions to reduce gastrointestinal discomfort. Post-field recovery continues with a mixture of water and mild electrolytes, optimizing rehydration without causing sodium overload.

## 5. Field Logistics and Food Safety

Food safety and freshness are managed throughout the survey day with careful attention given to transport and storage.

- All burritos and perishable snacks are packed in insulated containers.
- Egg and cheese fillings are kept cold with ice packs if the forecast temperature is above 30°C.
- Non-perishable snacks are pre-wrapped and field-durable.
- Food and fluids are stored in shaded backpack compartments, away from direct sunlight.
- Foods are prepared fresh on the morning of the survey to limit spoilage risk.
- Packaging is rugged, resealable, and simple to open, minimizing contamination.
- High-risk items (mayonnaise, soft cheeses) are excluded from all field meals.

## 7. Safety and Comfort Guidelines for Desert Operations

Maintaining hydration and nutrition is paramount to safety and performance during desert fieldwork.

- Team members are reminded to drink at least 250–500ml water every hour and alternate with electrolyte fluids to replace sodium and potassium lost to sweat.
- Monitor for dehydration signs: fatigue, dizziness, dark urine, headache, or cramping.
- Food is freshly prepared and transported in insulated containers; exposure to high temperatures is minimized.
- All packaging is resealable to prevent contamination and spoilage.
- Carbohydrates and protein at breakfast and snack times help sustain energy and prevent mental fatigue.
- Electrolyte and salt intake are monitored closely to match individual sweat rates and prevent both deficiency and overload.
- Field leaders track food and fluid tolerance, note any adverse gastrointestinal symptoms, and record food acceptance for future menu adjustments.
