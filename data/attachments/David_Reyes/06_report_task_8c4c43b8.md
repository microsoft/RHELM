## Executive Summary

Communities in the Ridgecrest area and across the Mojave Desert are experiencing rapidly intensifying climate challenges. Prolonged drought, record-setting heat, and the growing pressure from invasive species are placing immense strain on both the natural ecosystems and the traditional ways of life that have long defined Indigenous presence in this region. These changes threaten the vitality of culturally significant landscapes, native species, and the ability of Indigenous communities to carry on their environmental stewardship traditions.

This proposal lays out a collaborative framework for climate adaptation focused on strong Indigenous leadership. Our strategy combines Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) with up-to-date conservation science to restore and protect desert habitats, support Indigenous-led resilience efforts, and ensure that cultural priorities influence every stage of the project. The core outcomes include: restoration and enhancement of at least 500 acres of priority habitats, capacity-building for Indigenous monitors, integrated monitoring networks, and continuous, culturally informed engagement.

## Conservation and Adaptation Objectives

1. **Protect Key Desert Habitats**
   - **Goal:** Restore and enhance at least 500 acres of priority habitats—emphasizing Joshua Tree woodlands and the ranges of species like the desert tortoise.
   - **Measurement:** Conduct annual population and habitat surveys; track progress relative to 2024 baseline data.

2. **Advance Indigenous-Led Adaptive Management**
   - **Goal:** Train a minimum of 10 Indigenous community members in both TEK and Western ecological monitoring, equipping them as community field monitors.
   - **Measurement:** Record number trained and assess skill development through pre- and post-training evaluations.

3. **Implement Integrated Monitoring and Early Warning Networks**
   - **Goal:** Establish a dual-method monitoring system fusing quantitative field instruments with TEK observation to track changes in water dynamics, fire risk, and invasive species.
   - **Measurement:** Develop and execute data protocols; log field data points; host quarterly review sessions that assess both technical and TEK-based findings.

4. **Facilitate Continuous, Culturally-Informed Engagement**
   - **Goal:** Incorporate Indigenous perspectives throughout the project, securing formal feedback at key milestones.
   - **Measurement:** Count and characterize engagement events; document project plan changes made in response to Indigenous input.

## Project Methodology

### Technical Implementation

Our approach to project implementation blends the strengths of traditional knowledge and modern conservation science.

- **Site Selection:** Sites are identified through a cooperative process bringing together Traditional Use Maps, oral histories, and GIS vulnerability analyses based on current habitat and species data.
- **Baseline Assessment:** A combination of satellite imagery, on-foot ecological surveys, and Indigenous-guided seasonal observations will provide comprehensive ecological baselines.
- **Resilience Interventions:** The project will carry out native plantings adapted to drought and heat, targeted microhabitat restoration, and, where culturally appropriate, the use of prescribed fire practices rooted in tribal traditions.
- **Data Management and Synthesis:** All collected data—quantitative and TEK narrative alike—will be integrated according to agreed-upon open data protocols, managed by a joint Indigenous-scientist committee.

- All protocols for data collection and fieldwork will be shaped and approved in partnership with Indigenous representatives.
- Culturally significant ecological events—such as the flowering of key plants or the arrival of certain animals—will be recorded alongside standard scientific metrics.
- Teams will be comprised of at least 50% Indigenous monitors, ensuring field methods reflect community knowledge and values.

## Rainfall Data Analysis

### Overview

Understanding rainfall patterns is essential for desert ecosystem management. This project focuses on overnight rainfall events of at least 2mm—which play a critical role in soil moisture recharge and can trigger significant biological activity.

- **Monitoring:** Local automated weather stations (NOAA-affiliated) in Ridgecrest record hourly and 6-hour increments. Data from 2021–2023 reveal yearly variation in notable rain events.
- **Findings:** During this period, annual counts of overnight ≥2mm events ranged from 7 to 9. Detailed field notes and TEK input indicate these events are closely linked to ephemeral plant bursts, increased animal activity, and key seasonal markers for local communities.

| Year | Monitoring Station          | Data Resolution | # Overnight 2mm+ Events | Observable Ecological Outcomes         |
|------|----------------------------|-----------------|------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| 2021 | Ridgecrest Mesonet         | Hourly          | 9                      | Emergence of annual plants, increased ant activity |
| 2022 | North Mojave Desert RAWS   | 6-hour          | 7                      | More frequent coyote and mule deer tracks |
| 2023 | Ridgecrest Mesonet         | Hourly          | 8                      | Early blooming of Joshua Trees         |

### Ecological Indicators

- Rapid post-rain germination, visible within days
- Wildlife tracks and movement recorded by cameras
- TEK-driven observations, such as recorded “green-up” periods, guide survey planning to capture ecological transitions

## Community Engagement Plan

### Engagement Phases

1. **Pre-Launch Partnership Building**
   - Begin with tribal council meetings led by Indigenous facilitators, both in-person and virtual, to gather community priorities and affirm the value of historical knowledge.
2. **Co-Design and Training**
   - Facilitate TEK workshops for all project partners and distribute accessible, bilingual informational materials (English and Native languages).
3. **Ongoing Participation**
   - Schedule monthly co-monitoring field outings, rotating among participating communities, to foster collective learning and relationship building.
   - Gather immediate feedback through regular surveys and group dialogues.
4. **Decision and Review Periods**
   - Conduct midpoint roundtables to review project data and adjust methodology as needed—always taking into account cultural and seasonal traditions when planning gatherings.
5. **Final Reflection and Dissemination**
   - Celebrate results through inclusive events (featuring storytelling, art, and traditional foods), radio broadcasts, digital platforms, and print publications.
   - Ensure active contributors are recognized through co-authorship and honoraria.

**Cultural Relevance:**
- Create welcoming environments at all gatherings by incorporating storytelling, art, and traditional meals.
- Offer confidential, non-written channels for feedback, such as oral interviews or testimonies.

**Targeted Feedback Request:**
- Is the plan welcoming and accessible for your community?
- Are there adjustments needed in language, venues, or engagement formats to ensure all voices are heard?
