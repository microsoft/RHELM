# Travel Itinerary & Packing Checklist — Indian Wells Valley Desert Reserve (2024-06-15)

I put this together as a clean record of the day: when we left, who was there, what we did, and what we brought. The core details below stay strictly aligned with the confirmed “Actual Events.” Where exact timing depends on sunrise/sunset or drive time, I’ve kept it clearly labeled as an **estimate**, with the same verification links included at the end.

## Overview (What this day was about)

Today’s trip to the Indian Wells Valley Desert Reserve felt like the best kind of family outing: structured enough to get real hiking and photography done, but relaxed enough to slow down for birds, tracks in the sand, and the conversations that always seem to happen when everyone finally has time to breathe. Carlos’s birthday gave the day a quiet emotional center, and the dusk journal circle—moved into a sheltered boulder alcove when the wind picked up—ended up being the kind of moment that sticks.

## 1) Itinerary (Timeline + Activity Sequence)

**Fixed detail:** We departed at **6:30 AM** in the **silver Subaru Outback**.

**Time-only estimates used to make the timeline readable (verify if needed):**
- Local drive within Ridgecrest/Indian Wells Valley: **~20–35 minutes each way** (estimate)
- Mid-June sunrise in the Ridgecrest-area desert: **~5:35–5:45 AM** (estimate; see sources)
- Mid-June sunset: **~7:55–8:10 PM** (estimate; see sources)

### Timeline

**Early morning (home) — Pre-departure**
- **~5:30–6:15 AM (estimate):** I woke up early and did my usual methodical prep. I hydrated with **herbal tea**, then moved through the gear check in a calm, deliberate way: my **Canon EOS 90D kit**, the **Osprey Talon backpack**, and a quick check that the **fresh lemon bars** were sealed properly in their container so they wouldn’t get crushed in transit.

**Drive + meet-up**
- **6:30 AM (fixed):** We left home in the **silver Subaru Outback**—me, Carlos, and my mom.
- **~6:50–7:05 AM (estimate):** We met up with **Miguel Castillo** near the reserve access point. He was on time, wearing his **desert hat** and carrying his **camera gear**, and we did a quick sync on what kind of shots we were hoping to get.

**Morning on the Mesquite Dunes trail — Hiking + photography**
- **~7:25–9:30 AM (estimate):** We started out on the **Mesquite Dunes trail**. The morning felt genuinely comfortable—**pleasant temperatures**, helped by an **overnight breeze** and **recent rainfall**. Early on, Miguel shared advanced photography tips geared toward early light. Even though our departure was 6:30, the light still had that “fresh” feel once we were out on the trail and settled into shooting.

**Birdwatching segment**
- **~9:30–10:30 AM (estimate):** I led a birdwatching stretch, which was one of my favorite parts of the day. We picked out **horned larks**, and then we got surprised by a flock of **desert thrashers**—the kind of sighting that flips everyone from casual interest to fully alert in an instant.

**Tracks + navigation check**
- **~10:30–11:30 AM (estimate):** In a sandy area where the substrate made tracks easy to read, I spent time photographing and studying **kit fox prints**. I also took the opportunity to test my **recalibrated Garmin GPS** while I was already paying attention to location and ground detail.

**Lunch + stories (and a birthday tone to the day)**
- **~11:45 AM–1:00 PM (estimate):** We stopped for lunch and ate what we’d packed: **homemade vegetarian salad jars**, **lemon-roasted cauliflower**, and the **leftover lemon bars**. The food felt like a small luxury out there—bright, lemony, and satisfying without being heavy.
  
  Conversation-wise, lunch was where the day’s deeper thread showed up. We traded stories from past fieldwork, and Carlos talked about his conservation efforts—especially meaningful because it was his birthday. It gave the whole outing a reflective undertone: not just being outside, but thinking about what it means to protect places like this.

**Afternoon wandering + photos**
- **~1:00–4:30 PM (estimate):** The afternoon stayed intentionally loose—more exploring than “pushing miles.” We kept moving at an easy pace and made space to review photos and take more wildlife shots as opportunities came up. I noticed how much better I felt compared to drier, dustier days; the combination of recent rainfall and staying on top of hydration made a real difference, especially in how my sinuses felt.

**Late day regroup + evening reflection**
- **~4:30–7:30 PM (estimate):** We regrouped and spent time looking back through the day’s images—one of those satisfying end-of-day rituals where the photos help you realize how much you actually saw.
- **~7:30–8:30 PM (estimate; dusk window):** As dusk settled in, a **gentle wind** picked up enough that we moved the **campfire journal circle** into a **sheltered boulder alcove**. That shift made the space feel more intimate and protected, and it fit the tone of what we were talking about—legacy, conservation, and what we want to carry forward.

**Return + upload**
- **~8:30–9:10 PM (estimate):** We drove home.
- **~9:15–10:15 PM (estimate):** Once we were back, I uploaded a vibrant batch of wildlife photos into my archive. Ending the day that way—saving the work, not letting it sit—felt like putting a final period on the trip.
