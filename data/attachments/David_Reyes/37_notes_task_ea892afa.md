### 1A) Lemon Bars — baseline “classic lemon bars” method

**Primary baseline recipe sources (ratios + technique):**
- King Arthur Baking lemon bars [1]
- Serious Eats lemon bars [2]

#### Ingredients (typical for 1 pan of bars)
| Component | Ingredient | Quantity (baseline) | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Shortbread crust | All-purpose flour | 150 g (≈ 1 1/4 cups) | Standard press-in crust ratio |
|  | Granulated sugar | 50 g (≈ 1/4 cup) |  |
|  | Salt | 1/4 tsp |  |
|  | Unsalted butter (melted or softened) | 113 g (1/2 cup; 1 stick) | Melted = fast, easy press-in |
| Lemon filling | Granulated sugar | 300 g (≈ 1 1/2 cups) | Typical sweetness range for lemon bars |
|  | All-purpose flour | 25–35 g (≈ 3–4 tbsp) | Thickens |
|  | Salt | 1/4 tsp |  |
|  | Eggs | 4 large | Common baseline |
|  | Lemon juice | 180 ml (≈ 3/4 cup) | Fresh is standard; exact source unknown today |
|  | Lemon zest | 1–2 tbsp | Optional but common |
| Finish | Powdered sugar | As needed | Added after cooling |

#### Baseline method (with practical defaults)
1. **Set up the pan and oven**
   - Rack: middle (default)
   - Oven: 350°F / 175°C (common baseline)
   - Pan: commonly 9×13-inch; 8×8 or 9×9 also used (exact pan used today not recorded)
   - Line with parchment with an overhang so the bars lift out cleanly.

2. **Crust**
   - Stir flour + sugar + salt, then mix in butter until the dough looks like damp sand.
   - Press firmly and evenly into the pan (a flat-bottomed cup helps).
   - Bake until lightly golden, roughly 15–20 minutes under stable power. (Exact timing wasn’t captured today.)

3. **Filling**
   - Whisk sugar + flour + salt, then whisk in eggs until smooth.
   - Add lemon juice and zest and whisk again.
   - Pour onto the hot crust (standard approach; helps the filling start setting immediately).

4. **Finish baking**
   - Bake until edges are set and the center has a small wobble, often 20–25 minutes.
   - Cool fully, then chill for cleaner cuts.
   - Dust with powdered sugar just before serving.

**What this baseline helps diagnose:** lemon bars are very sensitive to overheating once the filling is set. If the oven stops and restarts (or temperature swings) mid-bake, the total time the crust spends exposed to high heat can creep upward, and the edges/bottom can burn even if the filling looks “done enough.”

### 1B) Spicy Zucchini Muffins — baseline quick-bread muffin method

**Primary baseline recipe sources (structure + handling):**
- King Arthur Baking zucchini muffins [3]
- Serious Eats zucchini bread principles (moisture handling and mixing) [4]

#### Ingredients (baseline for ~12 muffins)
| Category | Ingredient | Quantity (baseline) | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Dry | All-purpose flour | 250 g (≈ 2 cups) |  |
|  | Baking powder | 2 tsp |  |
|  | Baking soda | 1/2 tsp |  |
|  | Salt | 1/2 tsp |  |
| Spices (“spicy”) | Cinnamon | 1 tsp |  |
|  | Ground ginger | 1/2 tsp | Optional |
|  | Cayenne or chipotle powder | 1/8–1/4 tsp | Heat level wasn’t recorded today |
|  | Black pepper | pinch | Optional |
| Sweetener | Brown sugar (packed) | 150 g (≈ 3/4 cup) | Moisture + flavor |
| Wet | Eggs | 2 large |  |
|  | Neutral oil (or melted butter) | 120 ml (≈ 1/2 cup) | Oil keeps muffins tender |
|  | Vanilla extract | 1 tsp | Optional |
| Zucchini | Grated zucchini | 250–300 g (≈ 2–3 cups) | Moisture is the key variable |
| Optional add-ins | Nuts or raisins | ~1/2 cup | Unknown if used today |

#### Baseline method (muffin method, with the critical moisture step)
1. **Prep**
   - Oven: 375°F / 190°C (typical muffin temperature)
   - Rack: middle
   - Muffin tin: 12-cup; line or grease.

2. **Zucchini moisture control**
   - Grate zucchini.
   - If the zucchini is watery, squeeze it firmly in a clean towel. This single step often separates “light and tender” from “heavy and gummy.”
   - Today’s batch ended dense/underdone, and whether I squeezed the zucchini wasn’t documented—so this is a priority to capture next time.

3. **Dry bowl**
   - Whisk flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and spices to distribute leavening evenly.

4. **Wet bowl**
   - Whisk eggs and brown sugar until combined.
   - Whisk in oil (and vanilla if using).
   - Fold in zucchini (and add-ins if using).

5. **Combine**
   - Add dry to wet and fold just until there are no dry streaks. Stop early rather than chasing a perfectly smooth batter; overmixing is a reliable path to dense muffins.

6. **Bake**
   - Fill cups about 3/4 full.
   - Bake roughly 18–24 minutes, then test the center muffins (not the edge ones): a tester should come out clean or with moist crumbs, not wet batter.
   - Cool 5 minutes in the pan, then move to a rack.

**What this baseline helps diagnose:** dense and underbaked muffins usually point to a short bake, a too-wet batter (often unsqueezed zucchini), inaccurate leavening, or a disrupted workflow where steps get skipped or rushed.

## 4) Action Plan (focused fixes for the two failures)

This section is the “do it differently next time” part. The goal is not perfection; it’s resilience—especially when the power is unstable or work interruptions happen.

### 4A) Lemon bars — prevent another burned crust during outage conditions

**Pan, rack, and early browning control**
- Use a **light-colored metal pan** if available and keep the rack in the **middle**. This reduces the edge/bottom scorching that shows up fast when bake time stretches.
- Par-bake the crust only to **pale gold**, not deep color. A crust that looks “perfect” before filling often ends up too dark after the filling bake—especially if the oven cycles unpredictably.

**What I’ll do the moment the power drops**
- Leave the **oven door closed**. Heat retention is the one variable I can control immediately.
- Write down (even roughly) **when the power cut out** and **when it returned**. Those two notes are enough to make the next log genuinely useful.

**What I’ll do when power returns**
- Confirm whether the oven actually resumed heating (some ovens need a manual restart).
- Switch to doneness cues instead of the clock: edges set, center with a small wobble. Once it reaches that point, pull it rather than chasing an additional “recipe minute count.”

**If browning accelerates**
- Shield the edges with thin strips of foil or loosely tent the top. The goal is protection from direct radiant heat without pressing foil into the filling.

### 4B) Spicy zucchini muffins — avoid dense/underdone results when I’m pulled away

The muffins failed in a way that matches distraction: steps get skipped, timing gets fuzzy, and the last doneness check doesn’t happen.

**Interruption-proof setup (do this before I take any calls)**
- Pre-measure dry ingredients into one bowl and wet into another.
- Grate zucchini and decide immediately: squeeze or don’t squeeze. If I squeeze, I’ll note it; if I don’t, I’ll note it.
- Set one primary timer and one backup timer.

**Moisture control (high priority)**
- Default to **squeezing** grated zucchini. It’s the simplest way to keep the batter from turning heavy and slow-baking in the center.

**Mixing rules**
- Fold until combined and stop. If I’m interrupted mid-mix, I’ll finish mixing quickly and gently rather than trying to “fix” the batter by stirring more.

**Doneness is non-negotiable**
- Test the center muffins with a toothpick or skewer. If it comes out with wet batter, they stay in.
- If I’m unsure, I’ll add time in short increments and retest rather than guessing.

**References:** baseline formula and moisture-handling principles are consistent with [3] and [4].

### 4C) Safety and quality during blackouts (practical framing)
For baking, a rolling outage is usually a **quality** problem first—uneven doneness, texture shifts, scorched edges after the heat returns. It becomes a food-safety issue when egg- or dairy-based batters/fillings sit too long in a lukewarm range without finishing the bake. If the timing becomes unclear because of repeated outages, the safest approach is to document what’s unknown and avoid trying to “save” a batch that spent an extended period warm without fully baking.

General guidance for handling food safety during outages is covered by USDA FSIS materials [5].
