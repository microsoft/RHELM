# Field Observation Report  
**Morning Ecological Survey — South-East Ridgecrest**  
*June 21, 2024*

---

## 1. Overview

**Date & Time:**  
June 21, 2024, 06:15–10:00 PDT

**Location:**  
Mojave Desert, south-east foothills of Ridgecrest, CA  
Coordinates: 35.5254°N, 117.5796°W (WGS84), Elevation: ~820 m (2,690 ft) AMSL

**Habitat Description:**  
The survey area is representative of lower Mojave creosote bush scrub, punctuated by scattered Joshua trees and ephemeral drainage channels. Sandy alluvium dominates the terrain, interspersed with volcanic rocks and microtopographic mounds that create a rich mosaic of habitats. This complex substrate supports a variety of desert flora and offers numerous refugia for fauna.

**Weather Context:**  
*In-situ measurements (starting 06:30):*  
- Temperature rose from 21.5°C to 28.4°C by 09:45  
- Relative humidity (RH) dropped from 42% pre-dawn to 28% by late morning  
- Wind was light (3.2–6.1 km/h SSE), with sporadic gusts up to 9.4 km/h as recorded by handheld anemometer

*Long-term climatology for Ridgecrest (NOAA/WRCC):*  
- June average max: 35.8°C, min: 17.5°C, mean RH: ~24%  
This morning’s temperatures were approximately 3°C below the June decadal mean, with early humidity elevated. The lingering moisture resulted from a recent subtropical moisture plume, evidenced by weak nocturnal return flow and residual ground wetness.

**Field Implications:**  
Cooler temperatures and higher humidity favored active plant transpiration and pollinator activity. These conditions also improved detectability of both flora and fauna during the early hours. The elevated moisture profile suggested increased potential for convective precipitation, raising the possibility of tracking rapid vegetation responses and rare ephemeral species in subsequent surveys. Considering these dynamics, flexible and adaptive field monitoring remains important, especially during unseasonal moisture influxes.

**Weather and Synoptic Context:**  
Elevated humidity overnight was associated with a weak mid-level subtropical air mass, with no measurable precipitation recorded in the previous two weeks. Tracking the timing of future surveys to coincide with moisture pulses, particularly after convective storms, will be valuable for capturing episodic community responses.

## 4. Flora Observations

**Native Species Observed:**  
- **Dominant shrub:** Larrea tridentata (Creosote bush)  
- Ericameria nauseosa (Rubber rabbitbrush)  
- Yucca brevifolia (Joshua tree)  
- Sphaeralcea ambigua (Desert mallow), sparse  
- Opuntia basilaris (Beavertail cactus)—focal species

**Beavertail Cactus (Opuntia basilaris) Clusters:**  
A total of four 5 x 5 m quadrats were surveyed. Each contained O. basilaris, with densities ranging from 9 to 24 individuals per plot. Approximately 15% of these individuals were in active bloom, displaying turgid petals and pollen-laden stamens.

- **Flower color:** Deep magenta predominated (92%), with pale mauve variants (8%).  
- **Microhabitat:** Plants were typically found on south-facing talus fringes within 20–50% shrub canopy cover, benefiting from well-drained sandy substrate.  
- **Phenology:** Flowering was more prolonged than usual, seemingly supported by higher humidity.  
- **Stress Indicators:** Mild pad shriveling affected under 20% of individuals, with mite evidence (Tetranychus sp.) noted on 2 plants.

**Pollinator Activity:**  
Between 07:30 and 08:15, I observed active foraging by honey bees (Apis mellifera), native Andrena species, and a single carpenter bee (Xylocopa sp.), with multiple pollinators often visiting the same pad. This robust activity underscores the resilience of O. basilaris populations post a relatively dry winter, supporting its value as a focal indicator for ongoing monitoring.

*Representative photograph available: Opuntia basilaris bloom, Plot 2, 07:42 (35.5258°N, 117.5791°W).*

## 6. Field Challenges and Resolutions

- **GPSMAP 66s Battery:**  
  High-frequency logging depleted batteries after 3.5 hours. Replaced with lithium-ion rechargeables; going forward, a standard protocol for hourly battery status checks is warranted.
- **SensHand SH-200 Probe:**  
  Encountered non-reading error when used in areas with large cobbles. Function restored after reset and slight relocation. Upgrading to a more rugged probe for rocky terrain is advisable.
- **Canon EOS 90D Lens Fogging:**  
  Pre-dawn lens fog cleared after warming with sunrise. Carrying lens wipes and considering a lens heater will prevent delays in future early-morning ops.

All minor incidents were addressed on-site with negligible disruption to the survey schedule.

## 8. Conclusions and Recommendations

The south-east Ridgecrest foothills exhibited cooler and more humid morning conditions compared to seasonal norms, which supported heightened biotic activity—particularly in plant-pollinator interactions and avian foraging. The strength and persistence of Opuntia basilaris blooms, combined with diverse pollinator visitation, point to notable resilience following the prior dry season and reinforce this species’ suitability as an ecological indicator.

Avian and small mammal activity, especially the clear nesting of greater roadrunners and the abundance of white-tailed antelope squirrels, reflect functional trophic dynamics despite ongoing aridity. Absence of LeConte’s thrasher during this event underlines the necessity for continued, systematically timed surveys across different seasons and following moisture events.

**Management and Monitoring Guidance:**  
- Maintain adaptable survey protocols to leverage periods of above-average humidity and track rapid biological responses to episodic precipitation.
- Continue monitoring Opuntia basilaris across varying microhabitats; document floral and pollinator phenology with photographic and data records for longitudinal studies.
- Ensure reliable operation of critical equipment through regular calibration and backup battery protocols, especially during extended or nocturnal surveys.
- Schedule additional surveys immediately following any convective rainfall to document ephemeral plant and animal activity.
- Enhance detection coverage for LeConte’s thrasher through coordinated playback and visual surveys with other research teams.

**Recommended Actions:**  
- Integrate robust field calibration checks and enforce backup battery management.
- Plan concurrent surveys at multiple Mojave Desert sites following updates from NOAA or WRCC indicating approaching summer storms.
- Collaborate with pollinator and mammal specialists in future survey cycles, making the most of this area’s pronounced indicator species richness.

All data and observations from this survey have been archived in the central Mojave monitoring repository, cross-referenced by plot, GPS location, and date for regional meta-analysis and long-term monitoring frameworks.
