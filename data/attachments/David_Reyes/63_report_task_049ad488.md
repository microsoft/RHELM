## 4. Workshop Discussion Summary

### Welcome & Objectives

David Reyes opened the workshop by clarifying its two-fold purpose: First, to boost autumn volunteer engagement, and second, to ensure that all messaging upholds the scientific integrity and values of our adaptive restoration roadmap. David emphasized the need for a timely flier release to coordinate with the peak of September and October field activities.

### Feedback from August Conservation Events

Emily Tran shared observations and survey results from recent events:  
- 43 participants attended across two restoration days.  
- Surveys highlighted strong enthusiasm for hands-on work (62%), with many volunteers interested in returning.  
- However, several participants felt the provided orientation materials were overly technical or did not reflect local experiences or language.  
- Maria Lopez added feedback from the Sagebrush Table Café initiative. Patrons expressed the need for bilingual materials and a stronger presence of authentic community narratives on outreach materials.

The team concluded that to be effective, the flier’s tone should be friendly and plainspoken. Technical language will be minimized, and communications will give greater prominence to personal stories and regional impact.

### Conservation Messaging: Mojave Flora & Fauna

The team reviewed the finalized list of priority species: Mojave fringe-toed lizard, Joshua tree, creosote bush, and representative pollinators. Agreement was reached that every featured species should be linked to an active restoration objective to prevent tokenism and ensure educational value.

Emily proposed incorporating simple “spirit species” icons for each species, each paired with a QR code leading to more in-depth content online. This strategy balances accessibility with substance, allowing the flier to remain visually approachable while offering educational depth for those who seek it. Maria emphasized the importance of referencing Indigenous wisdom and traditional uses, particularly among Cahuilla and Paiute communities, when presenting these species.

David underscored that the conservation narrative must always explain not just what species are present, but why their restoration is crucial for the Mojave’s long-term ecological resilience.

### Volunteer Role Assignments

Roles required for autumn activities were discussed in detail:

- **Fieldwork:** Invasive species tracking, propagation activities, wildlife surveys.  
- **Education:** Delivering school presentations, leading nature walks, engaging with young people and families.  
- **Community Outreach:** Hosting tables at events, distributing materials, and acting as local points of contact.

Emily pointed out an urgent need for volunteers comfortable in public-speaking and environmental education roles, as upcoming school programs remain understaffed. Maria advocated for a new role—“Community Story Ambassador”—to capture and share volunteer experiences in both English and Spanish, ensuring the flier resonates with more diverse audiences.

It was agreed that the volunteer sign-up form will be redesigned so candidates can select training tracks in Field Research, Education Programs, and Community Outreach according to their interests and strengths.

### Integrating Sagebrush Table Café Outreach

Maria summarized survey data (collected August 28) from the Sagebrush Table Café:  
- Respondents requested that recruitment fliers be distributed where community members actually spend time—local grocery stores, libraries, church entryways.  
- Volunteers, including families and seniors, want to see clear, illustrated pathways for getting involved, rather than only contact information.  
- Existing recruitment materials have not adequately represented the intergenerational nature of community volunteers.

David noted that these findings directly support the project’s adaptive objective of expanding volunteer participation both demographically and geographically. The team committed to prioritizing trusted community spaces for flier distribution and scheduling a “sneak peek” demonstration at the Café’s upcoming community event.

### Cultural Q&A: Cahuilla Elder (Zoom Session, 1:20 PM)

During a video consultation, a local Cahuilla elder provided essential guidance. The elder stressed the importance of referencing traditional plant and animal names and sharing the land’s stewardship story authentically. They recommended featuring an acknowledgment statement on the flier: “With gratitude to the first stewards of these lands.” The elder advised that images of animal species should be used in respectful, place-based contexts, not merely as scientific illustrations.

The elder also suggested invitations to participate be worded in a way that recognizes continuity—that new volunteers are joining a long lineage of stewardship, rather than starting something entirely new. The team unanimously agreed to incorporate this language and message of gratitude.

## 6. Cahuilla Elder Cultural Guidance: Summary

Key takeaways from the elder’s consultation included:

- A strong recommendation to honor both Indigenous knowledge and present-day partnerships with Native communities in all outreach.
- Preference for the phrase “invitation to stewardship,” encouraging volunteers to see themselves within an ongoing tradition.
- Visuals should not only feature iconic regional species, but also people across ages and backgrounds working side by side.
- Consensus to include a visible acknowledgment:  
  *“We honor and thank the first stewards of Mojave lands—the Cahuilla, Paiute, and all Indigenous communities.”*

The team agreed that these points should be present in both the visual and written content of the flier.

## 9. Appendix: Flier Messaging Principles

All recruitment materials will be guided by the following communication principles:

- Use clear, accessible language with accurate scientific context.
- Visuals to show real people working in restoration, alongside authentic images of local plant and animal species.
- All materials must be fully bilingual (English/Spanish).
- Explicit recognition of Indigenous stewards and ongoing local partnerships.
- Present simple, actionable next steps (QR code for sign-up, contact info, upcoming events calendar).
