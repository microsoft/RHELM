## Agenda Overview

This meeting was convened to address the ongoing challenges of standardising metadata for oral history collections, with a particular focus on medieval and local Scottish contexts. The session began with a review of the historical motivations behind metadata standardization, especially given the risks facing oral traditions in Scotland. We then examined contemporary best practices, identified essential and supplementary metadata fields tailored to Scottish and medieval oral histories, and set action items and next steps for schema finalisation and peer review.

---

## Key Discussion Points

### Historical Context & Motivation

Dr. McEwan opened the meeting by emphasising the vulnerability of Scottish oral histories, particularly those relating to the medieval period and local communities. Fragmentation, loss of tradition, and inconsistent cataloguing have resulted in significant gaps in the preservation of these intangible cultural heritages. Both participants agreed that robust, standardised metadata is crucial for safeguarding these materials, enriching future research, and facilitating collaboration and resource sharing among archives and researchers. 

Attention was drawn to the unique structural elements that must be recorded in Scottish oral history, such as clan affiliations, parish boundaries, dialect distinctions, and historical events that often lack extensive written documentation. Dr. Sutherland reinforced the importance of capturing these details to ensure the authenticity and richness of historical narratives, particularly in collections that bridge oral and textual evidence.

### Review of Current Standards and Best Practices

The group conducted a comprehensive review of existing metadata standards relevant to oral history. UK guidelines, including ISAD(G) and the Oral History Society's management frameworks, were discussed in detail for their hierarchical structure and clarity. European and international frameworks such as DRI, Dublin Core, and UNESCO's recommendations were evaluated for their adaptability, interoperability, and emphasis on provenance and community participation. 

Both participants noted the widespread adoption of Dublin Core, largely due to its simplicity and flexibility. They also highlighted UNESCO's focus on authenticity and rigorous documentation practices, which are particularly pertinent to oral traditions and medieval contexts. A comparative analysis affirmed the need to supplement core metadata fields with context-specific elements vital to Scottish heritage, such as clan and dialect information.

### Scholarly Rationale for Metadata Selection

The committee recognised the value of a hybrid metadata model: adopting Dublin Core's core fields, while enhancing them with supplementary attributes informed by UK and Scottish museum practices. Dr. Sutherland advocated for finely-grained cultural markers, referencing Museums Galleries Scotland's guidance on intangible heritage. Dr. McEwan stressed the necessity of addressing privacy, especially when cataloguing sensitive or restricted interviews; this was supported through the inclusion of robust rights management fields. 

The National Library of Scotland's metadata exemplars were cited as crucial references for hierarchical structuring and long-term preservation strategies. Both participants expressed concern over repeated gaps in documenting provenance and custodianship within Scottish medieval archives, emphasising that all future cataloguing must rectify these omissions.

## Agreed Metadata Fields

Below is the finalised list of metadata fields, developed for oral histories in Scottish medieval and local contexts. Columns detail definitions, intended uses, examples, and provenance considerations to guide consistent documentation.

| Metadata Field      | Definition                                                                             | Intended Use                                             | Example Entry                         | Provenance Considerations                          |
|---------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| **Title**           | Distinctive name capturing subject, place, and timeframe                               | Indexing, contextual clarity                             | Oral History of Glen Lyon Parish, 1450–1500 | Should link subject and location clearly           |
| **Creator**         | Primary interviewer(s) or responsible historian, with institutional details            | Attribution and responsibility tracking                  | Isobel MacGregor (Interviewer), University of Edinburgh | Record all creators; note institutional roles      |
| **Contributor**     | Interviewees, transcribers, additional participants                                    | Authority, rights, narrative attribution                 | Donald Stewart (Narrator); Mary Campbell (Transcriber) | List contributors and specify roles                |
| **Date**            | Interview date and period covered                                                      | Differentiation of subject and collection period         | Interview: 2023-04-15; Subject: 1450–1500 | Use standard date formats; historic periods in text|
| **Subject**         | Controlled vocabulary and keywords: themes, events, cultures, places                   | Thematic search and retrieval                            | Clan Gregor, Reformation, Gaelic folklore | Employ local/Scottish thesauri                     |
| **Description**     | Concise summary of content, context, and importance                                    | Provides interpretive context                            | Parish reform and folklore in Perthshire | Use authoritative phrasing for scholarly clarity   |
| **Language**        | Language(s) used and translations                                                      | Multilingual access, rights clearance                    | Gaelic, Scots; Translation: English      | Specify dialects where applicable                  |
| **Coverage**        | Geographic and temporal scope, with historic place names                               | Precise search and context analysis                      | Glen Lyon, Perthshire, 1450–1500         | Reference both historic and contemporary names     |
| **Rights**          | Copyright, licensing, restrictions, embargoes                                          | Legal compliance, privacy protection                     | OHS licence; Restricted access until 2030 | Document all access restrictions and discussions   |
| **Provenance**      | Custodianship, collection history, transfer or donation records                        | Authentication, future management                        | Gift of Glen Lyon Historical Society; Recorded Apr 2023 | Detail full transfer and review history            |
| **Clan** *(Supplementary)* | Clan affiliation where relevant                                               | Enhances local specificity and heritage studies           | Clan Gregor                            | Verify via oral tradition and external records     |
| **Parish/Site**     | Administrative or ecclesiastical jurisdiction                                         | Anchors to local social/historical structure             | Glen Lyon Parish                        | Use accurate period boundaries                     |
| **Dialect**         | Specific Scottish/Gaelic dialects spoken                                              | Linguistic analysis, authenticity                        | Highland Gaelic, Perthshire Scots        | Reference historical maps and dialect sources      |
| **Event Reference** | Key historical event featured in narrative                                            | Enables event-based retrieval                            | Scottish Reformation 1560                | Cross-check with primary historical records        |
