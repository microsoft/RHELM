## Contextual Overview and Section Highlights

### Seminar Topic and Historiographical Objectives

The seminar focuses on the theme “Society, Structures, and Sources in Late Medieval England,” chosen to reflect current debates in medieval studies. In recent years, scholars have paid increasing attention to questions of social agency, shifting power dynamics, and the reliability of traditional sources. Recent studies by Carpenter and Wickham-Crowley have notably reshaped how we understand the interplay between feudal order and individual actors. The learning objectives were formulated to encourage active source criticism, engagement with interdisciplinary methods like GIS, and a nuanced appreciation of both micro- and macro-historical perspectives.

### Integration of Materials and Methodology

Seminar materials were selected not only for their historical significance, but also for their relevance to contemporary academic debates. In addition to classic documents (_Domesday Book_, _Magna Carta_), the reading pack features recent articles highlighting advances in digital humanities and medieval urban studies. All resources are properly cited and permissions confirmed. The slide deck is designed to be interactive and visually engaging, incorporating digital maps and newly digitized charters to enhance discussion.

### Logistical Coordination

All room and equipment bookings were handled in advance, with thorough testing of all AV and display technologies carried out on the day prior to the seminar. Arrangements for full access to digitized archival materials were confirmed with The National Archives and support staff. A contingency plan for remote and hybrid delivery is established, guaranteeing accessibility for all participants.

### Seminar Delivery Focus

Discussion points are anchored by detailed analysis of both textual and material sources, supported by well-chosen anecdotes. For instance, the story of Bishop Stapleton, drawn from archival trial records, has been included to illustrate individual agency within broader social structures. The seminar will encourage comparative analysis and microhistorical approaches, enabling participants to practice advanced methodologies as recommended in Bloch’s _The Historian’s Craft_ and current best practices.

### Follow-up and Professional Engagement

A detailed reading list was prepared to facilitate further exploration of key ideas post-seminar, including up-to-date articles and newly digitized historical documents. Collaboration avenues with organizations such as CWAAS have been initiated to foster wider professional exchange. Peer review procedures are clearly outlined, ensuring high-quality feedback and compliance with departmental expectations.
