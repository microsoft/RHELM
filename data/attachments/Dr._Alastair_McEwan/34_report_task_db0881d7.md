## Recommendations

### 1. Exercise and Activity

- **Vary Walking Routes:**  
  To prevent repetitive strain and stimulate different muscle groups, it is advisable to rotate walking routes each week. This approach enhances proprioception, maintains interest, and spreads load across various muscle and joint groups, as recommended by the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy and best practice guidelines for older adults [4].

- **Implement Pre-walk Stretching:**  
  A systematic dynamic stretching routine targeting the quadriceps, hamstrings, calves, and hip flexors should be performed before walking. Research consistently shows this reduces post-activity soreness, alleviates discomfort, and may help maintain optimal knee mobility [5].

- **Continue Resistance Training:**  
  Maintain twice-weekly sessions of light resistance exercises—using resistance bands for lower limb strength. This is particularly helpful in preserving muscle mass and joint stability, both critical for injury prevention and independence [5].

- **Meet Aerobic Activity Targets:**  
  Consistently aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity each week, in line with guidance from NICE and the NHS [6]. Activities such as brisk walking, swimming, or cycling can support both cardiovascular and musculoskeletal health.

### 2. Ongoing Monitoring

- **Maintain Annual Reviews:**  
  Attend annual health screenings and physiotherapy reviews to trace ongoing trends and address any changes proactively. Rapid reporting of new or worsening joint pain, instability, or mobility challenges will facilitate early intervention.

- **Daily Self-Monitoring:**  
  Keep a simple daily log noting any changes in knee pain, swelling, or functional ability. This enables timely identification of issues and allows care to be adjusted as needed.
