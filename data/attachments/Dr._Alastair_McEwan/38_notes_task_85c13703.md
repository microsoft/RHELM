# Evidence-Gathering Strategy and Planning Workflow for the Brittany Research Trip (August 2024)

## Executive Summary

This report details the planning and strategy for the upcoming Brittany research trip, led by Dr. Alastair McEwan and myself, Isobel Grant. Our aim is to ensure a thorough and methodical approach to collecting historical evidence across Brittany's archives in August 2024. The workflow covers every key preparatory and onsite research stage: scheduling, archiving contacts, logistical coordination, documentation practices, and peer collaboration. All steps are designed to accommodate the specific challenges of researching in Brittany’s rural context, drawing upon established historical methodologies and local expertise.

## 3. Archival Access and Preparation

**Securing Access:**

- We are completing all required archival registrations.
- Advance appointments are being coordinated via email and online portals, as most archives require scheduled visits.
- Letters of introduction and detailed research proposals (in French) are being prepared to satisfy institutional requirements.

**On-Site Protocols:**

- We have reviewed handling rules for each institution: pens are prohibited; gloves are mandatory for medieval manuscripts; personal scanning/camera equipment subject to archive approval.
- Camera and scanner usage, where allowed, will be scheduled to avoid disrupting archive workflow.

**Language Considerations:**

- French proficiency is critical, as institutional communications and materials are exclusively French. I am confident reading research documents and translating as needed.
- For more technical or legal terminology, we will have glossaries prepared and may use interpreters for complex archival interviews.

**Correspondence Preparation:**

- All consultation requests and archival queries are drafted in both English and French, ensuring clarity and minimizing communication errors.

---

## 4. Logistics

**Accommodation:**

- We are prioritizing lodgings within walking distance or accessible public transit from major archives, with flexible cancellation in case of rescheduling.
- Rural guesthouses with knowledgeable local hosts are preferred, booked well in advance due to the high season.

**Transportation:**

- Rental cars are reserved to cover archives with no public transport. Routes are mapped daily, including drive times and parking options.
- As a backup, we intend to use bicycles for semi-rural sites and have contacted local universities regarding campus shuttles or ride-sharing partnerships.

**Research Materials:**

- Each day, we carry laptops and tablets with cloud storage and offline note-taking capability, plus archival-compliant notebooks and pencils.
- Our camera (with silent shutter) and portable scanner are packed, ready for use where permitted.
- French-English glossaries, historical maps, and relevant secondary literature are kept on hand for immediate context and translation.

---

## 5. Documentation Workflow

**Field Note Templates:**

We use standardized sheets for every source or collection, noting:
- Date and archival reference numbers
- Source and collection type
- Content summaries
- Relevance to research
- Observations on the physical condition
- Citations for later referencing

Separate analysis forms for each archival visit facilitate structured review and rapid preparation for peer feedback.

**Live Updates and Data Management:**

A shared online spreadsheet enables real-time tracking of archive contacts, access permissions, materials reviewed, and pending tasks. This is synched with our broader project documentation system, ensuring data backup and workflow transparency throughout the trip.

---

## 6. Collaboration and Peer Review

**Connecting with Local Historians:**

- Ahead of the trip, I am corresponding with Breton university historians and community research groups for insight and collaboration.
- At least one in-person meeting with a local historian is scheduled to cross-review field notes and interpretations.
- Throughout the trip, we share findings for external feedback, strengthening contextual accuracy and scholarly rigor.

**Community Engagement:**

Where possible, we meet with local historical societies, archivists, and experts, enriching our workflows with local perspective and advice. We remain diligent in respecting archival ethics, privacy standards, and citation protocols.

---

## 7. Summary and Recommendations

Our research workflow is structured on proven historical methodologies, adapted to Brittany’s archival landscape and rural fieldwork realities. We prioritize direct engagement with primary sources, meticulous documentation, and structured collaboration. Logistics—including travel, accommodation, and data management—receive careful attention to reduce onsite friction and maximize time for research.

Key recommendations:
- Maintain flexibility in scheduling, particularly with archive appointments and transport.
- Ensure all communication and documentation are prepared bilingually to avoid logistical barriers.
- Prioritize daily documentation and post-visit analysis, facilitating stronger evidence collection and peer review.
- Seek frequent local input, both academic and community-based, to contextualize findings.
