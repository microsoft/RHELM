This itinerary outlines my autumn 2024 field investigation of Norse and Medieval sites across Orkney. ## Fieldwork Logistics and Arrangements

### Transport

Reliable mobility is crucial across Orkney’s islands. I’ll use local buses managed by Orkney Islands Council for shorter connections, supplementing with car hire from Kirkwall for flexible site access. For inter-island travel, I’ve reviewed ferry schedules and made advance bookings where tidal conditions influence access (especially for Brough of Birsay).

### Equipment

Fieldwork demands robust recording and documentation tools. My standard kit includes:
- Portable document scanner (for quick archiving in libraries and archives)
- GPS mapping device for precise location data
- DSLR camera with wide and macro lenses; portable tripod for architectural studies
- Photogrammetry tablet for 3D documentation; notes kit for site sketches
- Secure data backups: encrypted USB drives and daily cloud uploads
- Audio recorder for interviews and oral history sessions

I check all equipment before departure and carry spare batteries and protective gear given Orkney’s unpredictable climate.

### Data Management and Communication

At day’s end, I digitize notes and backup files using the hotel’s secure WiFi and cloud storage to prevent data loss. My phone is preloaded with emergency contacts, and I keep hard copies of maps and schedules for reference in the field if devices fail.

### Local Conditions

Autumn in Orkney is often windy and rainy; I pack waterproofs and field-safe footwear. Some historic sites, like Brough of Birsay, are only accessible at low tide. I consult local tide tables daily to plan my travel and field hours.

Proper permissions—secured well in advance—are non-negotiable for all sites, archives, and any earthworks under study.

#### Required Permissions (Advance Arrangements Confirmed)

- **Orkney Library and Archive:** Manuscript and charter research by appointment
- **Brough of Birsay:** Site entry notification through Historic Environment Scotland
- **Chapel ruins (South Ronaldsay, Deerness):** Access via local council archaeologist (two weeks’ notice)
- **Rousay/Midhowe Broch:** Entry coordinated through Rousay Heritage Trust

Careful adherence to these administrative protocols underpins the integrity of the research and respects the custodianship of Orkney’s historical resources.
