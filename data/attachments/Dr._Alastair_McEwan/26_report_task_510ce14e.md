## Detailed Discussion

The meeting opened with a brief review of the historical context of archival digitisation. Dr. McEwan traced the evolution from early microfilm initiatives to the adoption of systematic imaging protocols and metadata standards that now underpin modern digital archives. This introduction provided important perspective on how institutional best practices have matured, especially in response to technological advances and shifting research demands.

Both participants agreed that prioritising collections with high scholarly value and urgent preservation needs is essential. The group referenced landmark projects such as the British Library’s digitisation of its manuscript archives in the 1990s, which demonstrated clear benefits for both preservation and research accessibility. Building on these precedents, the team highlighted evidence from pilot studies in which fragile and highly sought-after materials were digitised first, yielding positive feedback from both academic and community stakeholders.

A substantial portion of the discussion focused on the use of metadata and archival description standards. Dr. McEwan recommended DACS for its depth in describing North American archival materials, while Professor Sinclair advocated mapping to ISAD(G), citing its international acceptance and flexibility. There was consensus that a hybrid approach—developing crosswalks between DACS, ISAD(G), and Dublin Core—will best support collections that serve both local and global audiences. The group also resolved to review guidance from the Digital Preservation Coalition to ensure technical imaging specifications (including scanning resolution—minimum 300 dpi TIFF files—and consistent colour calibration) are fully aligned with current best practices.

The sustainability of the digital assets was recognised as a key ongoing challenge. Both participants acknowledged the practical difficulties of maintaining digital archives, such as continual migration to new formats and evolving access platforms, and agreed that a clear, evidence-based sustainability statement should be included in the grant proposal.

Stakeholder engagement strategies were discussed in depth. The team will hold consultations with faculty, archivists, and interested community groups, ensuring that the digitisation project supports broad research, educational, and public interests. Incorporation into institutional repositories and open-access platforms was confirmed as a central goal, with explicit compliance with the FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable) guiding project development.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

Both participants agreed to proceed with drafting the grant proposal, emphasising conformity with both DACS and ISAD(G) standards and documenting crosswalks for collections with intersecting international and local relevance. The prioritisation of archival materials will be informed by a weighted matrix assessing research value, physical condition, and historical significance, backed by data from pilot projects and peer-reviewed sources.

A follow-up session will be organised with metadata specialists to review schema interoperability and technical integrations. The next virtual meeting is tentatively scheduled for 24 June 2024, where the team will review the draft proposal and finalize strategies for repository integration.

Over the coming weeks, further research will be conducted into recent digitisation initiatives with strong FAIR-compliance and proven sustainability models. These findings will be incorporated into the final narrative to strengthen the project’s justification and methodology.
