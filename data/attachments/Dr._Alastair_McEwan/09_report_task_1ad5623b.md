# Fieldwork Incident Report: Caerlaverock, Solway Firth

**Researcher's Name:** Dr. Alastair McEwan  
**Date:** 9 March 2024  
**Location:** Caerlaverock, Solway Firth  
**Project Title:** Photographic Documentation of Coastal Medieval Ruins and Saltpans

---

## 1. Field Visit Objectives

Caerlaverock, situated on the north shore of the Solway Firth in Dumfries and Galloway, holds exceptional historical and archaeological significance, both as a site of medieval fortification and coastal industry. The area includes Caerlaverock Castle, the remnants of medieval saltpans, and coastal ruins connected to centuries-old salt production and trade. The focus of this fieldwork was to develop a thorough photographic archive of these remaining features, which is crucial for several reasons:

- Systematic documentation of extant medieval structures and saltpan remains for detailed historical analysis.
- Creation of a spatial reference archive to aid in long-term monitoring of erosion, tidal changes, and the effects of human activity.
- Support for ongoing research into medieval coastal industries, settlement patterns, and environmental resilience.
- Supply of high-quality visual resources to strengthen future conservation proposals and interpretive materials for both scholarly and public audiences.

Photographic documentation not only supports direct analysis and comparison with earlier surveys, but also preserves a visual record for times when further deterioration may limit access. These images and associated logs serve as important evidence for publications, site management decisions, and heritage advocacy.

## 2. Incident Narrative

### Weather Conditions and Field Environment

The team began work at 09:30, facing overcast skies and persistent light drizzle, with moderate south-westerly winds averaging 23–28 mph. Visibility was reduced to approximately 800 meters, compounded by ground saturation left behind by prior tidal inundation. This made access to several features particularly challenging. The saltpan sector, which lies in marshy terrain, presented heightened mobility risks, slowing the setup of equipment and necessitating extra caution by all members throughout the morning.

### Injury to Hamish McEwan

At 11:45, while positioning survey equipment near the southern saltpan ruins, Hamish McEwan suffered an inversion injury to his right ankle, having stepped onto unstable ground. All team members were equipped with radios and high-visibility clothing, and standard risk assessment procedures had been completed ahead of the visit, including assigned buddy navigation and designated access routes. Despite these precautions, the unpredictable conditions of the marsh led to the injury. Fieldwork was immediately suspended following the incident. Hamish was stabilized on-site, in line with university health and safety protocols, to prevent further harm.

### Technical Malfunctions

#### Camera Malfunction (Nikon D750)

From 10:05 onward, our Nikon D750 experienced intermittent failures in data writing. The error message “Err” repeatedly appeared, and multiple exposures went unsaved. Attempts to restart the system, swap batteries, and replace the SD card did not resolve the issue, which by 10:50 resulted in complete failure of data recording. On inspection, the SD card did not respond in a laptop reader later, suggesting moisture-induced electronic failure. The camera was promptly bagged and dried, and silica gel pouches were used in a preventive effort.

#### Laptop Failure (Dell XPS 15)

Efforts to back up image data to the field laptop began at 11:00, but the Dell XPS 15 would not complete the boot cycle, repeatedly displaying a "No bootable devices found" error. All standard troubleshooting steps were carried out, including removal of peripherals, external power diagnostics, and multiple restart attempts. The device remained nonfunctional throughout. The surrounding damp conditions appear to have contributed to this hardware fault.

## 4. Immediate Actions Taken

### First Aid Response

Hamish’s ankle was quickly stabilized with a semi-rigid splint and compression wrap, and he was given oral pain relief. He remained stationary, with his leg elevated, while the incident details were logged for institutional records. Communications were maintained with support staff at base to coordinate extraction.

### Managed Withdrawal from Site

A gradual and controlled exit from the marsh was executed, with the team assisting Hamish and maintaining ongoing radio communications. Emergency transport was mobilized from our staging location in Caerlaverock village. All field team members returned safely with no further incident.

### Data Recovery Efforts

After returning to the department, a full suite of recovery procedures were carried out:

- The damaged SD card was dried and cleaned, then scanned using sector-level recovery software (Recuva, PhotoRec).
- The laptop underwent hardware diagnostics, with the failed storage module isolated and scheduled for specialist data extraction.
- All retrievable files were duplicated and stored under version control, with comprehensive error logs appended to the archive.

These procedures resulted in partial recovery of images (see table above). Unfortunately, several key records and logs could not be restored.

## 5. Recommendations for Future Fieldwork

To reduce risks and prevent similar losses in future surveys of coastal medieval sites, the following steps are recommended:

**Safety Protocols**
- Increase the frequency of real-time radio checks and team audits, particularly when navigating wet or unstable terrain.
- Undertake comprehensive pre-field mapping of hazardous areas.
- Schedule fieldwork during windows of dry weather to minimize slip and mobility hazards.

**Equipment Management**
- Conduct waterproofing checks on all electronic devices before deployment.
- Store backup cameras, SD cards, and other vital electronics in sealed, waterproof containers.
- Test all equipment under simulated field conditions prior to departure.

**Contingency and Emergency Planning**
- Complete mandatory dry-run exercises for critical field operations, including mock withdrawals and injury scenarios.
- Implement multi-stage site withdrawal protocols and clearly outline roles in injury management for all team members.

**Data Integrity and Collaboration**
- Assign two independent photographic operators for all targets of primary research significance.
- Hold regular team debriefs at the close of each field session, with immediate image archiving and version control.
- Organize post-field collaborative workshops for image review, annotation, and immediate identification of missing data.
