# Comprehensive Archival and Digitisation Report  
**16th-century Maxwell Volume, 16 November 2024**

## Executive Summary

This report documents the systematic cataloguing and digitisation of the 16th-century Maxwell volume, completed in accordance with international archival standards. It details all metadata fields applied, describes the digitisation protocols observed in the session led by Dr. Sutherland, and offers a comprehensive analysis of previously overlooked marginal notes—including the significant annotation referencing a long-lost Dumfriesshire chapel. ## 1. Archival Catalogue of the Maxwell Volume

### 1.1 Methodological Framework

Throughout the cataloguing process, internationally recognised standards were followed (ISAD(G), Dublin Core, and EAD), ensuring every aspect of descriptive and administrative metadata was recorded. This approach will facilitate interoperability between institutions and ensure the volume remains accessible for future research.

### 1.2 Complete Metadata Record

- **Title:** Maxwell Family Volume (16th Century)
- **Author / Scribe:** Attributed to various Maxwell family members, as confirmed by annotation styles and handwriting comparison.
- **Date of Creation:** Circa 1550–1590
- **Physical Description:**  
  Bound manuscript, calfskin cover, 210 x 150 mm. Comprises 120 folios, with hand-sewn gatherings, written in iron gall ink on laid paper.  
- **Current Condition:**  
  Shows moderate foxing and some warping of vellum; recent conservation work has stabilised the folios.
- **Marginal Notes:**  
  Extensive annotations in margins, including genealogical details, ecclesiastical notes, and records of estate activities. Notably, a new annotation concerning a lost Dumfriesshire chapel appears on folio 47v.
- **Provenance:**  
  Originally held at Terregles estate, transferred to Dumfries Archive in 1903.
- **Acquisition Reference:** Dumfries Archive Accession 1903-27-MXV
- **Access Permissions:** Access restricted to accredited researchers owing to the volume’s fragility and significance.
- **Digitisation Status:** The entire volume has been digitised to preservation standards (see below).

## 2. Digitisation Session and Protocols  
*Led by Dr. Sutherland, 16 November 2024*

### 2.1 Digitisation Protocols

Every step of the digitisation process adhered to best practices as outlined by the Digital Preservation Coalition (2024 standards):

- **Imaging Resolution:**  
  Master files captured in TIFF format at 600 ppi. Preservation copies produced in JPEG2000 for redundancy and broad access.
- **Color Calibration:**  
  Calibrations using an X-Rite ColorChecker chart were conducted at the beginning and end of the session to ensure accurate colour representation.
- **File Naming Convention:**  
  Example: Dumfries_MXV_047_v_2024.tif, providing consistency and ease of retrieval.
- **Preservation and Storage:**  
  Files are stored on a local network-attached storage (NAS) unit with backup on a dedicated dark archive cloud service to guarantee long-term protection.
- **Access Protocols:**  
  High-resolution master images are reserved for qualified researchers; medium-resolution JPEGs are published in the public catalogue to balance scholarly access with conservation needs.
- **Handling Procedures:**  
  All materials were handled using a supportive book cradle and non-contact page turning techniques. Only white cotton gloves were used, and lighting remained non-invasive throughout.

This careful approach has preserved both the material and informational integrity of the Maxwell volume, ensuring the original document is unharmed while maximising digital accessibility for future scholarship.

## 3. Analysis of Marginal Notes: The Dumfriesshire Chapel Annotation

### 3.1 Discovery Details

During cataloguing, a cursive annotation was discovered on folio 47v—written in a Secretary hand typical of the late 16th century. The note references a chapel dedicated to St Blaan near the Kinnel Water, previously unacknowledged in surviving ecclesiastical records.

### 3.2 Tabulated Summary

| Note Content | Folio/Page | Paleographic Observations | Initial Interpretation |
|--------------|------------|--------------------------|-----------------------|
| “In the yair 1574, Jhon Maxwell did gif ane silver chalice to the chapell of Sanct Blaan neir the burne of Kinnel, bot the place now lyeth waisted.” | Folio 47v | Written in cursive Secretary hand from the 1570s, with distinct minims and flourishes typical of the Maxwell family scribes; faded but legible iron gall ink. | Reports a donation of a silver chalice by John Maxwell to St Blaan’s chapel near Kinnel Water, noting the chapel’s abandonment. The site is not listed in known records, pointing to an otherwise undocumented ecclesiastical foundation in Dumfriesshire. |

### 3.3 Paleographic Assessment

The annotation’s script is a classic Secretary hand from the late 16th century. Comparison with other family manuscripts (folios 11r–15v) shows consistent handwriting features: archaic Scottish spellings, long minims, and prominent terminal flourishes. Such characteristics support attribution to a Maxwell family scribe active in the period.

### 3.4 Historical Context

The reference to a “chapell of Sanct Blaan neir the burne of Kinnel” does not appear in established ecclesiastical directories or surveys, although several now-lost chapels are recorded in the area. John Maxwell’s patronage of religious sites is well documented; this note therefore provides new evidence of a site missing from both written and oral ecclesiastical histories, with specific geographic detail supporting local traditions of abandoned worship sites.
