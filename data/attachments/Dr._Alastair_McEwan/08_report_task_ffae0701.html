Restoration Progress Report

16th-Century Latin Treatise Digitization & Conservation Project

University of Glasgow Archives

1 March 2024

Author: Dr. Alastair McEwan, Senior Historian

Executive Summary

This report presents a substantive update on the digitization and conservation of a 16th-century Latin treatise held by the University of Glasgow Archives. Over recent months, the project team has made notable progress in stabilizing the manuscript, producing high-resolution digital images, and advancing English translations. All stages have been conducted in strict alignment with both Scottish archival traditions and internationally recognized standards for digital preservation.

Through targeted conservation treatments, we have addressed physical deterioration, improved legibility of ink and parchment, and established a robust digital archive. These efforts have significantly deepened our understanding of the manuscript's historical function and provenance within the context of Scottish academia. During this period, we have encountered several challenges, including ambiguities in translation, technical setbacks such as the loss of photographic files due to an SD card failure, and unforeseen delays in condition assessment. These issues have introduced the need for additional review cycles and supplementary documentation. In light of these circumstances, an extension of the final submission deadline is requested to safeguard the project's academic rigor and archival standards.

Project Narrative

1. Restoration & Conservation Activities

Technical Conservation Measures

A thorough sequence of conservation treatments was implemented to ensure the stability and longevity of the treatise:

  • Surface Cleaning: All parchment folios were cleaned using aqueous microfiltration gel and vulcanized rubber erasers, following protocols from the British Library and Scottish Archives. The cleaning process was carefully controlled to avoid abrasion and preserve delicate script and pigments.[1][2]
  • Support & Repair: Margins and vulnerable folio edges were reinforced with Kozo Japanese tissue, attached with wheat starch paste. This technique, recognized as best practice, has successfully stabilized areas prone to tearing and loss.[2]
  • Environmental Controls: The manuscript now resides in a climate-controlled storage environment, calibrated at 40±2% relative humidity and 18±1°C. Regular digital hygrometer readings confirm conditions remain optimal for parchment, preventing issues such as gelatinization, ink corrosion, and biological activity.
  • Photodocumentation: Conservation stages and folio conditions were documented with calibrated, high-resolution imaging systems following FADGI guidelines for color accuracy and digital authenticity. Color reference targets were employed to establish reproducibility and a baseline for future analysis.[3][4]

Condition Assessments

Initial and follow-up assessments have established a clear trajectory of improvement:

  • Initial Survey (January 2024): During the first full inspection, abrasion and ink fading were recorded on folios 18-21, alongside localized pigment loss. All damage zones were mapped in accordance with ICOM-CC assessment protocols.
  • Post-Conservation Assessment (February 2024): After cleaning and repair, both folio integrity and legibility improved notably. Recurrent visual and environmental monitoring is scheduled to ensure ongoing preservation. Detailed reports were filed per the University of Glasgow’s condition assessment procedures.

Seamless communication between conservation, archival, and research teams has underpinned efficient progress and quick response to emerging issues.

2. Digitization Protocols & Preservation Ethics

Digitization and digital management procedures were meticulously designed to meet leading standards:

  • Image Capture: Each folio was digitized at 600dpi in TIFF format to produce archival master images. Copies are stored on LTO tape for long-term security, with accessible JPEG2000 surrogates available for researchers, in compliance with JISC and Glasgow digital curation policies.[4]
  • Metadata & Cataloguing: All digital files are catalogued using PREMIS and EAD standards for metadata, supporting transparent management and interoperability within the archival system. Descriptive richness was achieved with ISAD(G) extensions.
  • Integrity and Authenticity: Regular checksum verification ensures digital files remain uncorrupted; audit trails are maintained as evidence of unbroken chain-of-custody and authenticity.
  • Rights Management: Copyright and moral rights over both the original manuscript and its digital forms are explicitly asserted in line with UK archival guidance, preserving both institutional and intellectual property.

This holistic approach secures the manuscript’s accessibility for future scholarship without compromising its integrity.

3. Scottish Medieval Archival Practices

To ensure historical fidelity, the project closely adhered to established practices in Scottish medieval manuscripts:

  • Palaeographical Analysis: Detailed script analysis revealed the scribe’s use of an Insular Minuscule variant, cross-referenced with National Library of Scotland MS Adv.23.1.1. Identification was further verified using the Handwriting Concordance of Scotland, supporting our provenance findings.[5]
  • Folio Arrangement and Annotation: The catchword-based foliation system, observed throughout the treatise, was preserved during digitization, mirroring period Scottish conventions. Annotations by the original binder were transcribed to complete the archival record and provide valuable clues regarding early handling and ownership.

These measures consulted current scholarship and contributed significant new data to institutional palaeographical studies.

4. Translation Progress & Philological Challenges

Methodology

Translation work has advanced steadily, though not without complications typical of early modern Latin texts:

  • Lexical research was based on Lewis & Short’s Latin Dictionary and the Thesaurus Linguae Latinae. Syntactic mapping utilized Corpus Corporum Latinorum, and patristic references were confirmed against the Clavis Patrum Latinorum.[5][6]
  • Segments underwent double-blind review for idiomatic and contextual accuracy, in collaboration with peer experts, ensuring that nuanced meanings and historic usage were faithfully captured.

Issues and Resolutions

  • Semantic Ambiguity: A notable example occurs on folio 13r, where the phrase “Disciplinae iustitiae prolata” resists straightforward interpretation due to regional semantic drift. Comparative analysis with the Aberdeen Registers has provided valuable context, although consensus among translators continues to be sought.
  • Unattested Neologisms: Folio 20v contains “Scrinatio,” a term absent from extant dictionaries. Investigation suggests it represents contemporary technical jargon—likely related to manuscript storage or scrutiny—but full clarification remains under review.
  • Physical Lacunae: Water damage in three folios has resulted in textual gaps; recovery is limited and further interpretation requires cross-referencing with related sources. Additionally, glosses in court-hand script present palaeographical challenges, requiring specialist input.

Despite these obstacles, the translation now covers the majority of the treatise, with annotations documenting issues for future revision.

5. New Research Findings

Emerging data have enriched our understanding of the treatise’s origins and significance:

  • Provenance Confirmation: Watermark analysis links certain parchment sheets to the Glasgow papermaking industry, notably those referenced in James Baird’s Ledger Book (1558). Editorial features match those in the University’s 1586 graduate rolls, offering firm evidence for the treatise’s academic context and dating.[7][8]
  • Historical Function: Content analysis identifies the treatise as instructional material in advanced Latin rhetoric and moral philosophy, corroborated by cross-references with the contemporary Treatise on Scholastic Method (MS Ed-1587-Scot) and period roll-books.

These archival connections further cement the treatise’s value in reconstructing Scottish intellectual history.

Technical Failure Documentation

Unreadable SD Card Incident

During photodocumentation, one SD card was found to be unreadable after a hardware failure. Unfortunately, this resulted in the loss of visual records for key restoration stages on folios 16-21, specifically those following surface cleaning and tissue repair procedures.

Formal Request for Submission Extension

To maintain the high standards of scholarship and archival practice that this project demands, an extension of the final submission deadline is formally requested.

Justification

  • Thorough Translation and Analysis: Outstanding translation ambiguities and complex paleographical matters require additional expert review and collaborative translation cycles to ensure academic integrity.
  • Technical Remediation: The unrecoverable SD card has necessitated partial re-documentation of restoration stages and comprehensive annotation of affected folios to maintain archival reliability.
  • Institutional Compliance: University guidelines provide for extensions in cases where technical setbacks have verifiable impact on documentation and research outcomes.

Requested Action

With this in mind, an extension of four weeks—moving the final deadline to 29 March 2024—will allow for complete visual and scholarly documentation, final peer-checking of translations, and strict adherence to University of Glasgow archival protocols.[2]
