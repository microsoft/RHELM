## Overview of Digitisation Session

Today’s digitisation focused on the comprehensive, archival-standard imaging of a 19th-century sketchbook that references the lost chapel site. The session was held in a carefully controlled environment, prioritizing both preservation and the highest possible image quality. My primary objective was not only to capture each sketch and annotation in detail, but also to document physical traces and marginalia that could shed light on the chapel’s historical and architectural context.

### Equipment and Setup

I used a Canon EOS 5D Mark IV with a macro lens to achieve high-resolution images of the delicate sketches and handwritten annotations. Illumination was provided by adjustable LED panels, and I employed raking light at a 25-degree angle to accentuate paper texture and expose underdrawings invisible in standard lighting. To support the fragile sketchbook, I used acid-free foam to cradle the spine and pages, and handled everything with nitrile gloves to prevent contamination. Where the paper and graphite allowed, I gently flattened pages with a glass sheet—taking care to avoid any risk to the originals.

### Digitisation Procedure

- **Imaging Process:** Each page was photographed under both standard and raking light conditions. The raking light was especially valuable for uncovering faint graphite lines and shallow indentations, which would have otherwise remained unnoticed.
- **Handling and Documentation:** I turned each page using a non-metallic spatula, strictly avoiding direct hand contact. For every image, I recorded comprehensive metadata, including page number, lighting setup, and observational notes in a digitisation worksheet.
- **Verification Measures:** Alongside every annotation, I positioned a calibrated color and scale card to ensure accuracy in spatial dimensions and tonal balance during post-processing.

## Observations on Methodology

### Lighting Strategy

Raking light was integral to revealing much of the sketchbook’s hidden details. The LED panels offered a stable color temperature, which helped keep post-capture processing straightforward. While the angled lighting emphasized faded graphite and paper surface characteristics, I often had to balance increased shadow effects in the digital files to maintain overall legibility. Some highly textured surfaces caused reflection artifacts, which I mitigated by adjusting light angles and retaking those images.

### Image Quality and Handling

With macro imaging, I was able to capture the fine nuance of both the handwriting and the aged paper’s fiber structure. The color reference card streamlined tone and contrast calibration. Through careful use of foam supports and gentle handling, I avoided any damage—a critical factor given the paper’s mid-19th-century fragility.

### Challenges

There were a few physical constraints: annotations located near the gutter were difficult to access, and the tight binding limited image capture in some places. Reflective artifacts from textured paper occasionally reduced annotation clarity, though varied lighting angles improved outcomes. Some marks remain indistinct, highlighting the need for multispectral imaging to differentiate between graphite, charcoal, and historic ink traces.

## Historical Insights and Hypotheses

The digitisation work provides several avenues for deeper historical interpretation of the lost chapel site:

- **Architectural Evolution:** The sketchbook’s record of the nave’s atypical width suggests either an undocumented expansion or architectural adaptation during a transitional period. This deviation from regional norms points to phases not captured in written sources.
  
- **Epidemic Response:** The annotation about a blocked window during the plague period directly aligns with established historical measures undertaken to mitigate disease spread. This supports the idea that the chapel remained an active community site well into major epidemics.

- **Material Repurposing:** Stone removal for farm fencing, specifically referenced in the margin, corroborates existing patterns in rural ecclesiastical sites where building materials were recycled following ruination. This helps explain discontinuities in the chapel’s archaeological record.

- **Subsurface Features:** The mention of a crypt entrance highlights the importance of undertaking ground-penetrating radar surveys to investigate possible burial sites beneath current ground level. This may reveal a significant, undocumented funerary function.

- **Fire Damage:** The observation of soot traces on the south wall adds tangible evidence to local oral accounts of a destructive fire. This supports further investigation into regional parish records and material relics for confirming these events.

Together, these findings suggest a dynamic history of the chapel site, marked by phases of construction, structural adaptation, material repurposing, and the impact of regional crises. The sketchbook’s details tie archival, archaeological, and oral sources together, offering a nuanced narrative of site formation, use, decline, and transformation.
