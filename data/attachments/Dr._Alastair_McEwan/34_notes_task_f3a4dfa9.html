Autumn Research Trip to Brittany: Structured Planning Note

Prepared for Dr. Alastair McEwan and Isobel

1. Trip Overview and Research Objectives

This autumn, Dr. McEwan and I are heading to Brittany to further our ongoing historical research projects and explore several facets of Western European regional history. Our objectives are clear:

  • Thorough Examination of Archives: We plan to delve into both local and regional archives, focusing on primary sources from the early modern and late medieval periods. Established Breton collections hold materials vital for our work on governance, identity, and social change.
  • Field Investigation at Significant Sites: Site visits will allow us to better understand the socio-political dynamics, linguistic developments, and migration patterns that have shaped Brittany. We’ve selected locations that offer rich opportunities for contextual fieldwork.
  • Engagement with Cultural Institutions: Meetings are scheduled with heritage committees, museum curators, and local historians. Our aim is both to expand our network and to open doors for collaborative research.
  • Integration with Committee Projects: The trip is closely aligned with the priorities laid out by our academic committees for 2024—particularly urban-rural transitions, maritime heritage, and the resurgence of the Breton language.
  • Documentation of Emerging Themes: We’ll stay flexible, keen to record new findings and fresh hypotheses—whether they arise during site visits, formal interviews, or casual evening discussions at The Globe Inn.

We are relying exclusively on respected institutional sources and the region’s established archives to ensure our research remains both rigorous and impactful [1][2].

  • Field Notebooks: Hard-bound A4/A5 notebooks with archival-quality paper for robust note-taking.
  • Reference Materials: Key regional histories, facsimiles of Breton charters, and essential dictionaries (French and Breton-English).
  • Documenting Equipment:
  • DSLR camera suited for low-light archive environments
  • Portable audio recorder for interviews and oral histories

  • Membership or reader cards for departmental archives

5. Additional Notes and Ideas: Reflections from Dinner at The Globe Inn

Recent dinners at The Globe Inn have proven to be fertile ground for new ideas and connections. As I listened to stories shared by colleagues, several themes emerged that warrant closer attention during our fieldwork:

  • Oral History Traditions: The importance of capturing oral histories in rural Brittany became unmistakably clear. I’m motivated to arrange interviews with village elders in Quimper and Vannes, where unique local narratives thrive.
  • Maritime Identity in Saint-Malo: Conversation often turned to the privateering legacy and records of emigration from Saint-Malo. I’ve made a note to examine family registries and ship ledgers that could shed light on longstanding migration patterns.
  • Urban-Rural Transitions: There was animated discussion about post-war urban expansion in Rennes. Building on committee suggestions, I plan to review urban planning archives to develop a comparative perspective.
  • Breton Language Revival: Several anecdotes highlighted renewed interest in regional dialects—contacting language activists and clergy in Quimper may deepen our understanding of revival efforts.
  • Research Expansion Possibilities: The Festival Interceltique de Lorient came up frequently, prompting me to consider exploring links between contemporary cultural events and historical precedents—perhaps even fitting a festival day trip into the itinerary if time allows.

I’m leaving plenty of room in my notebook for unexpected encounters and insights, whether they arise on-site or in the course of casual conversation.
