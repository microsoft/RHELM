## 3. Summary of Discussions

### 3.1 Conceptual Framework for a Community-Led Heritage Walk

The session began with a shared vision for an accessible, community-driven heritage walk that celebrates Glasgow’s rich history through a series of landmark locations. The project aims to present an inclusive and well-researched narrative, representing multiple voices and historical perspectives.

Key historical periods for the walk were outlined:
- **Medieval Glasgow:** Focus on the city’s merchant origins and the Cathedral precinct as early urban centers.
- **Enlightenment Era:** Spotlight the University’s relocation and the emergence of intellectual hubs, with attention to distinguished alumni such as Adam Smith.
- **Industrial Revolution:** Examine the evolution of shipbuilding and tenement housing, highlighting economic and social shifts.
- **Contemporary Heritage:** Address Glasgow’s transformation through migration, postwar redevelopment, and ongoing multicultural influences.

The walk will feature significant local figures whose stories embody Glasgow’s spirit:
- *St. Mungo*: Revered as the city’s patron saint and a foundational figure.
- *Adam Smith*: Noted economist and pivotal University alumnus.
- *Mary Barbour*: Celebrated social reformer, famous for organizing the 1915 rent strikes through ‘Barbour’s Army’.

Plans were made to incorporate regional traditions and oral histories, particularly drawing on Fiona MacLeod’s family roots in the Gorbals. Fiona shared stories passed down through generations, illustrating how personal experiences can enrich the narrative beyond academic sources.

Community co-design emerged as a central principle. Engaging local heritage societies, residents’ groups, and other stakeholders will help ensure authenticity and encourage local ownership of the project. The walk route will be open for suggestions and amendments from these groups.

### 3.2 Involvement of Local Schools

Engaging local schools is viewed as essential for fostering connections between young people and the city’s heritage. The initiative is closely aligned with curricular frameworks, especially the Curriculum for Excellence (CfE) Social Studies and Expressive Arts strands [2]. 

Plans include:
- Creating educational materials tailored for pupils at CfE levels 2 and 3, emphasizing place-based learning.
- Collaborating with teachers to design classroom modules and guided tour sessions, backed by preparatory and reflective classroom activities.
- Promoting student involvement in the project through assignments such as oral history interviews with older family members, mapping exercises, and creative writing tasks inspired by Glasgow’s past.
- Offering school pilot visits ahead of the public launch, providing opportunities for students and teachers to test resources and share feedback.

Fiona highlighted the successful collaboration with Hillhead Primary on previous heritage initiatives, noting high levels of student interest and promising outcomes.

### 3.3 Partnership with CWAAS: Potential and Expected Outcomes

The Cumbria and Westmorland Antiquarian and Archaeological Society (CWAAS) was identified as an ideal partner, given their longstanding expertise in community heritage, archival research, and public engagement [3]. The partnership is expected to offer several advantages:

- Access to carefully curated archives and specialist input for historical accuracy.
- Joint organization of training workshops, enabling Glasgow volunteers to develop skills in heritage walk curation and delivery.
- Resource exchange, including maps, historical research papers, and guest speakers for public events.

By collaborating, both organizations aim to strengthen the scholarly dimension of the project and broaden its reach. There is potential for joint publications, public engagement reports, and expanded networking opportunities. Fiona recalled participating in the annual CWAAS Heritage Open Day, emphasizing how joint ventures have historically fostered enthusiasm and cross-border cooperation.

### 3.4 Practical and Logistical Considerations

A detailed timeline was created for the initiative:
- **May–June 2024:** Consultation with local community groups and school pilots.
- **July–August 2024:** Development of educational materials, walk maps, and digital content.
- **September 2024:** Organize and host pilot heritage walk events.
- **October 2024:** Gather and evaluate feedback from participants and stakeholders.

Resource requirements were also discussed:
- **Funding:** Initial grant applications will be made to the University Community Fund, with additional requests for support from CWAAS and Glasgow City Council.
- **Materials:** Production of route maps, educational info packs, digital resources for promotion and school use.
- **Personnel:** Appointment of lead coordinators, volunteer guides, and teaching staff.

For effective stakeholder engagement, the plan includes:
- Early meetings with neighborhood associations, school leadership, and CWAAS representatives.
- Public launch through university communication channels and local press coverage.
- Structured feedback forums integrated into the autumn review phase, ensuring the community’s voice remains central in decision-making.
