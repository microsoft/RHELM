## Research Objectives and Priorities

### Central Themes

My work in Brittany will center on the political transformations and maritime economies of medieval and early modern Brittany, with a focus on local governance structures, trade networks, and rural community development. I plan to compare Anglo-French archival records, seeking new perspectives on connections between Cumberland and Brittany, especially as reflected in parish documents, letters, and regional charters.

Ecclesiastical documents (12th–16th centuries) take priority since they provide unique insights into religious life, clerical power, and the provenance of Brittany’s cultural landscapes. In parallel, I aim to gather oral histories and document material culture suitable for digital humanities projects. Joint archival sessions and interviews with French colleagues (especially Dr. Le Goff) will enrich these narratives and foster new research collaborations.

### Target Archives and Sites – Rationale and Plans

**Archives départementales d’Ille-et-Vilaine (Rennes):**  
The archive provides unmatched access to medieval charters, parish registers, and shipping records. These materials are crucial for contextualizing Brittany’s evolution and facilitating direct comparisons to UK sources.

**Musée de Bretagne (Rennes):**  
My scheduled appointments include time with the museum’s ethnographic collections and exhibition spaces. These resources support visual and material history research and form the backbone for future public engagement and exhibition planning.

**Château de Fougères:**  
Architectural fieldwork here will examine fortification design, preservation approaches, and historic site documentation. I’ve arranged a guided tour to accompany structural mapping and note-taking.

**Bibliothèque Municipale de Rennes:**  
This library’s rare manuscripts and early printed books are indispensable for understanding Brittany’s literary heritage. I plan to review key collections, digitize relevant content, and discuss cataloging standards with onsite staff.

### Anticipated Challenges

Working across French archives requires flexibility. Digitization and cataloging vary widely by institution, which may complicate data search and retrieval. Some sites restrict photographic equipment or require special permits for electronic devices. Language barriers can occasionally slow communication with archival staff or complicate paperwork, though I’ve prepared French-language versions of all critical documentation.

Oral history work demands strict adherence to data protection regulations, especially with personal information and interviews. Limited opening hours, advanced reservation needs, and potential strikes or closures all present risks, which I’ve mitigated through flexible scheduling and identifying backup research sites.

### Relevance to Current Publications and Academic Projects

Findings from this trip are foundational for my upcoming monograph examining Brittany’s identity in relation to the Anglo-French world, a project planned for submission to the CWAAS quarterly. Collaborative research with University of Rennes faculty will underpin joint articles and interdisciplinary presentations, fulfilling the requirements of our mutual research agreement.

Data, interviews, and photographic documentation will also provide material for the Cumberland & Westmorland Antiquarian & Archaeological Society’s newsletter and annual report. This ensures transparency and meets all dissemination standards set by my university and affiliated societies.
