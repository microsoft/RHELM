## Overview

Our program at Caerlaverock Castle on 15 June 2024 is designed to foster meaningful historical engagement for all participants, highlighting the castle’s key medieval phases and traditions. The sessions will center on interpreted experiences, hands-on learning, and open scholarly dialogue. Every activity is rooted in extensive research and supported by primary sources, notably the Caerlaverock Roll and verified castle records. Attention throughout the day will be paid to both historical accuracy and visitor inclusivity.

## Activity Explanations and Research Context

**Arrival & Briefing:**  
First thing in the morning, all participants gather at the Gatehouse for introductions, assignment of roles, and review of practical arrangements. This briefing uses official castle guides and documented protocols ([1], [2]) to ensure everyone is fully oriented and prepared.

**Introduction – The Story of Caerlaverock:**  
The opening talk presents Caerlaverock’s history within its distinctive architectural and social setting. Reference is made both to secondary literature and to primary records like the Caerlaverock Roll, which vividly documents the 1300 siege. These accounts set the tone for our approach to authenticity ([2]).

**Dramatic Vignettes – Siege & Defense:**  
Live reenactments focus on key episodes from the Wars of Scottish Independence. Volunteer actors illustrate the strategies and lived experiences of defenders during the sieges, drawing on both contemporary chronicles and material evidence found in castle archives ([2], [3]).

**Historical Sword Demonstration:**  
This interactive session showcases weaponry and martial techniques from circa 1300. Using safe, historically accurate replicas and guidance from medieval training treatises, I will explain the discipline and context behind each move ([3], [4]).

**Interactive Q&A: Authenticity in Castle Life:**  
Visitors are encouraged to question and investigate our interpretations of medieval domestic life. Using excerpts from the Caerlaverock Roll and household records, we discuss debates over authenticity and invite guests to handle, observe, and reflect on primary source materials ([2], [4]).

**Lunch Break & Networking:**  
A relaxed luncheon in the courtyard and conservatory offers time for informal exchanges between guests, staff, and presenters. This is an ideal opportunity for professional networking and sharing personal impressions.

**Medieval Music Segment:**  
Musicians perform pieces sourced from 13th–14th-century Scottish manuscripts. We engage with questions about reconstruction and invite guests to compare ancient tunes with their modern counterparts ([5]).

**Living History: Roles of Castle Inhabitants:**  
Participants join interpreters to explore the daily routines of Caerlaverock’s residents. Activities include demonstrations on food preparation, administration, crafts, and wardrobe selection, all based on inventories and authentic records from the period ([2], [6]).

**Tea Break & Portraits:**  
During the afternoon break, guests gather for formal group portraits and candid images. The visual documentation will be stored for ongoing research, archival purposes, and publicity.

**Panel: Debates on Historical Interpretation:**  
A public panel discussion, moderated by technical advisors, encourages active peer review of our historical presentations. The session references current academic standards and highlights key controversies in castle history and historical accuracy ([4], [7]).

**Ceilidh: Traditional Scottish Dancing and Closing:**  
To celebrate the end of our program, we host an authentic Scottish ceilidh with musicians and castle staff. All guests are invited to join in, experiencing the music, movement, and communal spirit as described in Scottish folklore and tradition ([5], [8]).

**Event Wrap-Up:**  
At day’s end, we transition to the Great Hall for a brief reflection, collection of documentation, and research summaries. All observations and participant feedback are gathered systematically to inform future events and ongoing studies.

## Checklist for Historical Presentation and Documentation

- Replica sword (late medieval style, blunted for safety)
- Digital camera (body and portrait/landscape lenses, spare batteries)
- Cleaning kit (lens cloth)
- Historically accurate costumes (late 13th-century Scottish noble set, weather-appropriate alternatives)
- Research journal (minute-by-minute notes, feedback, observations)
- Primary sources:
  - Printed Caerlaverock Roll selections
  - Facsimile castle records and inventories
  - Visitor bibliography and explanatory handouts
- Visitor handouts: overview of castle history, Q&A guidelines
- Digital recorder or app for capturing discussions and insights
