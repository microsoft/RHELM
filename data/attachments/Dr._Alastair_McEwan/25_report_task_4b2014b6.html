Incident Report: Failed Digitisation Session at Dumfries University Archives

Date of Session: 2024-05-15
Prepared for: University of Glasgow, Dumfries University Governance, Cumbria and Westmorland Antiquarian & Archaeological Society (CWAAS), and Institutional Research Committees

Executive Summary

On May 15, 2024, a digitisation session was scheduled at Dumfries University Archives as part of an ongoing initiative to preserve parish registers and related documents in digital form for academic research. Central to this effort were the Lochmaben parish registers, which hold substantial value for studies in local history, genealogy, and medieval parish life within Dumfries-shire. These records capture the community’s vital transactions, ecclesiastical practices, and demographic patterns, forming a crucial base for reconstructing historical narratives in southwest Scotland.

The session was intended to add significant content to a collaborative digital archive, expanding access for researchers and key stakeholders, including the CWAAS. However, operational setbacks on the day prevented the planned digitisation work, resulting in project delays, lost opportunities for stakeholder engagement, and interruptions in research workflows. This report provides a detailed account of the incident, analyses underlying causes, assesses impacts, and outlines practical recommendations for future improvement, following established UK archival practices.

Session Objectives

The digitisation session was designed to:

  • Preserve Vulnerable Records: Digitise Lochmaben parish registers and ancillary materials in line with standards set by the University of Glasgow and CWAAS, ensuring long-term, secure digital preservation for critical records.
  • Enable Greater Access: Broaden online accessibility for a diverse community of scholars, local historians, and genealogists, thereby maximizing the relevance and utility of the Dumfries University Archives.
  • Meet Project Milestones: Fulfill scheduled deliverables for the regional historical study, facilitating interdisciplinary analysis of medieval Scottish parish communities.
  • Respond to Stakeholder Needs: Address specific requirements outlined by partner institutions—including Dumfries University and CWAAS—by ensuring digitisation of materials aligned with their research objectives and timelines.

Chronological Timeline of Events

The following timeline reconstructs key moments during the digitisation session, based on archived logs and staff correspondence.

08:30 — Lead Archivist began session setup, including equipment testing and workstation preparation.

08:45 — Archive Assistants A and B initiated document retrieval. Assistant A reported feeling unwell.

09:00 — Lead Archivist requested the Lochmaben parish register (Ref: DUA/PAR/LOCH/001) from Stack 4B, but the item was not at its scheduled location. Immediate concern was raised regarding possible misfiling.

09:20 — Assistant A left the session due to illness, leaving only one assistant available for document retrieval.

09:30 — Attempts to retrieve ancillary documents began, but access was limited by incomplete cataloguing and location discrepancies (details in Table 1).

09:50 — The team consulted the University Archivist to address the missing register and investigate the misfiling.

10:10 — The issue was escalated to the Archive Supervisor and Dumfries University management, with notifications sent to all project stakeholders.

10:30 — The team concluded that digitisation objectives could not be met that day; the session was formally abandoned and incident documentation began.

Issues Encountered

Misfiled Lochmaben Parish Registers

The Lochmaben parish registers (Ref: DUA/PAR/LOCH/001), scheduled for digitisation, were absent from both their official storage (Stack 4B) and the digitisation prep area. Investigation indicated that the registers had been misfiled during a recent reorganisation of archival holdings. This disruption prevented progress on key deliverables and forced the session’s abandonment.

Staff Illness and Reduced Coverage

Archive Assistant A left due to sudden illness early in the session, eliminating the planned dual-staff document handling protocol. With only one assistant available, retrieval processes were overloaded, and best-practice handling procedures could not be fully observed. The reduced staffing not only slowed workflow but also increased the risk of procedural errors.

Incomplete Access to Ancillary Documents

Three major ancillary documents—Kirk Session Minutes, Baptismal Registers, and Local Charters and Deeds—remained partially or fully inaccessible because of catalogue inaccuracies and restricted location permissions. These issues limited the scope of digitisation and forced researchers to defer crucial cross-referential analysis.

Impact Assessment

The disrupted session had immediate and measurable impacts:

  • Project Timeline Delays: Essential milestones were missed, with estimated delays of at least two weeks anticipated before materials and workflows could be reorganised and rescheduled.
  • Impacts on Deliverables: Stakeholder expectations, particularly those of CWAAS and institutional partners, could not be met. The session’s failure threatens funding continuity and diminishes confidence in the project’s management.
  • Research Setbacks: The lack of accessible digitised records stalls ongoing academic and genealogical research, restricts primary source analysis, inhibits the progress of publications, and undermines collaborative work across disciplines.

Immediate Actions Taken

  • Incident Documentation: The session was thoroughly documented in real time, and tracing efforts for misfiled materials began using an updated location audit.
  • Staff Reallocation: Available staff were reassigned as needed to search for missing items and provide supplementary retrieval support.
  • Stakeholder Communication: The issue was escalated promptly to supervisors, management, and external partners, with updates circulated to all affected parties.
  • Policy Review: Dumfries University initiated consultation with the University of Glasgow’s digitisation risk team to review incident protocols and receive guidance on best-practice responses.

Recommendations for Prevention

Enhanced Register Tracking and Audits

Regular misfiling threatens archival integrity and impedes operations. Instituting quarterly audits—both physical and digital—alongside RFID tracking and improved accession logs can substantially reduce misfiling risk, drawing on proven methods such as those implemented by the British Library.

Robust Staffing Contingency Plans

Staff illness or absence exposes workflows to interruptions. Expanding staff pools, cross-training assistants, and maintaining readily available backup personnel are critical measures. Rotational and substitution protocols, as adopted in recent CWAAS digitisation projects, offer effective models for resilience.

Comprehensive Cataloguing Review

Cataloguing deficiencies directly block access and slow research progress. A phased review of catalogued materials, prioritized by research criticality and validated via double-verification, should be pursued. Consulting current literature on metadata standards and incorporating regular recataloguing cycles can further safeguard reliability, as evidenced in recent archival science studies.

Conclusion

The interruptions experienced during the digitisation session at Dumfries University Archives highlight the critical need for meticulous archival management, strategic staff planning, and regular review of cataloguing protocols. By committing to these improvements, the institution can significantly reduce future disruptions, protect irreplaceable historical records such as the Lochmaben parish registers, and support ongoing research endeavours. Prompt action and strengthened workflows ensure that these valuable resources remain accessible for academic and community use well into the future.
