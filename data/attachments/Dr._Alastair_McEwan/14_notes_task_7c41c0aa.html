Reflections and Analysis

Connections to Local Heritage

The stories and memories Hamish shared during our session have direct relevance for research into the cultural heritage of the Solway Coast. His recollections about smuggling and fishing traditions provide tangible links between personal experience and broader historical accounts. These narratives resonate with documented sources and help corroborate the collective memory of the community. By revisiting old festival customs and the beliefs tied to coastal life, Hamish’s testimony sheds light on how environment and tradition shaped daily existence—offering valuable material for reconstructing historic practices and designing heritage programming that can engage present-day audiences.

Approach to Oral History

Conducting the interview outdoors, on the actual coastline where many events took place, added significant authenticity. This setting helped Hamish retrieve details with remarkable clarity and nuance, and encouraged a sense of ease and openness. The structure of our questions—open-ended, but anchored in specific themes—let Hamish naturally connect family stories to wider historical trends. While the equipment captured the audio well, there was some minor interference from the wind, which slightly distorted certain sections. For future sessions, investing in wind-protected microphones would enhance clarity and archival quality.
The session adhered closely to the British Library and CWAAS guidelines for oral history, with detailed metadata logged to support future accessibility and use.

Integration with Ongoing Research

Hamish’s accounts of smuggling and informal maritime economics directly supplement my ongoing research into coastal economies and social practices. His stories strengthen evidence of how informal networks shaped Solway life, and provide personal insights to deepen understanding of these dynamics. The discussion of festivals and local lore also supports the CWAAS research agenda, emphasizing the importance of intangible heritage. Most notably, the session highlighted the significance of intergenerational storytelling—not only for historical preservation, but as a mechanism for transmitting cultural knowledge within coastal communities.

Recommendations for Fieldwork and Documentation

  • Every oral history session should carefully document both environmental and contextual details, as these factors enrich interpretation and support methodological transparency.
  • Maintaining uniform timestamps and file naming structures improves later retrieval, analysis, and archiving.
  • Use high-resolution recording equipment, with wind protection where necessary, to ensure preservation against environmental loss and technological change.
  • Metadata should remain adaptable across formats such as CSV, JSON, and Markdown, to allow institutional integration and future-proofing.
  • Written rights and permissions, verified with physical and digital signatures, are essential to safeguard ethical considerations and future use.
  • Historiographic references and cross-links should be updated regularly to incorporate new research and maintain relevance.
  • Make all outputs available to CWAAS, contributing to broader interdisciplinary projects and maximizing their benefit for researchers and the local public alike.
