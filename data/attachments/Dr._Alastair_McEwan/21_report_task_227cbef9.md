## Discussion and Decisions

### Standardizing Folder-Naming Conventions for Medieval Research Documentation

A key priority for both Dr. McEwan and Ms. Kerr is the creation of a consistent folder-naming protocol for organizing digital research assets. Effective organization is foundational to maintaining the integrity and accessibility of scholarly resources. Drawing on established archival procedures and university guidance, the following principles were agreed upon:

- **Chronological and Thematic Tagging:**  
  Each folder will be clearly labeled with project phase, geographic or topical focus, and creation date. Example: `2024_05_Dumfries_ChroniclingPhase_I`. This approach facilitates easy chronological sorting and thematic retrieval.

- **Descriptive Contextualization:**  
  Archival standards emphasize concise yet descriptive folder titles to clearly indicate content [1]. Names must convey enough detail to be immediately understandable to all collaborators.

- **Version Control:**  
  To track iterative revisions, version numbers such as `v01` will be appended to folder names in accordance with institutional requirements [2]. This safeguards against misfiling and helps document project evolution.

- **Preservation of Provenance:**  
  Maintaining original document identifiers and metadata is crucial for historical traceability, as outlined in archival protocols [3].

After reviewing practices from leading repositories including the British Library’s medieval studies archive, the team committed to implementing these conventions across all project documentation. The new system will be regularly reviewed to ensure continued compliance with best practices.

---

### Systematic Assignment and Tracking of Project Tasks

To facilitate clarity and accountability, the group discussed the adoption of a structured task assignment process commonly used in university research environments:

- **Clear Definition and Description of Tasks:**  
  Every task will be precisely articulated, specifying attached objectives and expected outcomes [2]. This minimizes ambiguities and streamlines collaboration.

- **Individual Responsibility:**  
  Tasks are delegated to specific team members, and individual progress will be tracked through routine updates. This reflects both scholarly and project management best practices.

- **Deadline Setting and Milestone Identification:**  
  Each assignment is linked to a realistic completion date, with larger objectives broken into actionable milestones. The team agreed to adhere to the SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) criteria [4] to reinforce productivity.

- **Transparent Review and Verification:**  
  A regular peer review protocol will be followed for all completed tasks, checking both technical and academic adequacy. The group decided to formalize this process in a collaborative task table to be reviewed weekly.

---

### Improvements to Workflow and Documentation

The team identified several practical enhancements for both day-to-day collaboration and long-term record-keeping:

- **Centralized, Version-Controlled Documentation:**  
  All files—including meeting minutes, drafts, and finalized outputs—will be stored in a shared digital workspace with controlled versioning. This ensures integrity and facilitates peer review [3].

- **Standardized Templates:**  
  Meetings, notes, and data logs will use agreed templates modeled on successful formats from research committees [2]. This promotes consistency, clarity, and ease of reference.

- **Secure and Accessible Communication Platforms:**  
  Access to documentation will be managed through secure digital channels, ensuring confidentiality while supporting effective teamwork.

- **Structured Peer Review:**  
  Before significant deliverables are finalized, independent peer review will be required, following the guidelines recommended by the Royal Historical Society and similar bodies [5].

The group recommends full adoption of template-based documentation and naming conventions, with compulsory peer review prior to submission or public archiving.
