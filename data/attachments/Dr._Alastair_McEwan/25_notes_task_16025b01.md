## Choosing Cullen Skink for Dinner

I chose Cullen Skink as my centerpiece dinner because I've always felt drawn to its history and comforting simplicity. The dish hails from the coastal town of Cullen in Moray, Scotland, and is emblematic of resourceful cooking in Scottish homes—making hearty meals from preserved fish and humble root vegetables.

Cullen Skink traditionally features smoked haddock, which was a mainstay in Scottish fishing villages long before the conveniences of refrigeration. Smoking not only imparted flavor but also allowed fish to travel and store well in the days when cold storage wasn't reliable. Pairing the fish with potatoes and onions reflects the local harvests and agricultural traditions dating back to the 19th century. Recently, recipes often include milk, making the soup even more nourishing and smooth.

Beyond its ingredients, this soup carries the warmth and ingenuity of Scottish families who made the most of what they had. I've come across references to Cullen Skink in old Scottish cookery books, and sometimes on the menus of neighborhood restaurants, always holding its place as a symbol of comforting home cooking. While I didn't have primary historical sources on hand, my choice was informed by my previous readings and local culinary traditions.
