## Executive Summary

On May 20, 2024, I attended the opening session of the Advanced Montessori Certification in Environmental Education. The session laid a strong foundation for understanding how to weave environmental literacy into early childhood Montessori classrooms. Key discussion topics included integrating sustainability practices into daily routines, the power of nature journaling for cultivating observational and sensorial skills, and age-appropriate ways to introduce composting. Throughout, these approaches were consistently linked to core Montessori principles: fostering independence, encouraging hands-on learning, nurturing a sense of responsibility, supporting child-led exploration, and developing respect for the environment.

A significant focus was placed on practical, adaptable strategies that can be customized to fit multi-age groups and diverse local contexts. Given the unique ecological features of the Pacific Northwest, the session emphasized leveraging regional resources and climate-specific adaptations. The overall intent was to ensure that environmental education is authentic, meaningful, and accessible for young learners.

## Personal Reflection

### Participation Approach

Due to health reasons, I kept my camera off and did not participate verbally during this session. However, I remained engaged throughout by contributing in the chat, completing polls, and actively taking notes. I found this method allowed me to digest information deeply and focus on recording actionable ideas I want to implement.

### Planned Classroom Integration

**Sustainability Practices:**  
I am excited to establish a classroom composting system designed for Portland’s climate. I plan to use small, child-friendly bins to make composting accessible, and will assign rotating responsibilities to “compost monitors” so all children can participate. To complement this, I will introduce a water- and energy-conservation project and invite students to measure and track our classroom resource use.

**Nature Journaling:**  
I plan to launch weekly nature journaling sessions. We will start by observing and recording native plants, seasonal changes, and weather patterns right outside our windows. For younger children, journal entries will focus on drawings and simple sensory descriptions; older students will help by supporting their younger peers and adding written reflections.

**Regional Context:**  
I see rich opportunity for place-based learning in our area. I intend to connect with organizations such as Portland Audubon and Metro’s Backyard Habitat Certification Program to invite guest speakers, access teaching materials, and provide authentic local context. Emphasizing Pacific Northwest biodiversity will make our lessons more relevant and vibrant for my students.

**Family Engagement:**  
Recognizing the importance of family support, I plan to develop bilingual communications for families to encourage zero-waste lunches and extend sustainability activities into the home. I will also share documentation and invite family participation in composting and conservation projects.
