# Alberta Co-op Shopping List & Reflections  
### For Elena Morales | April 2024

---

## Overview

As I prepared for my April trip to Alberta Co-op in Portland, I took time to craft a shopping list that would serve both my family routines and my classroom as a Montessori Lead Teacher. Spring always feels like a season of renewal, and with mamá joining me for a weekend brunch, I wanted our meals to emphasize fresh, local flavors and wholesome ingredients. My list is influenced by my commitment to organic foods, community agriculture, and the Montessori principles I practice in both my teaching and daily life. These groceries aren’t just for eating—they’re for sharing lessons about food, nature, and independence with my students and family alike.

## Shopping Choices & Montessori Principles

Shopping at Alberta Co-op always feels more like participating in a community than running errands. When I talk with staff about local, seasonal, or organic products, I find they’re eager to share information about their partners and suggest the freshest options. I often explain to my family and colleagues that our shopping supports not just personal nutrition, but local farms, ecological health, and immersive learning opportunities for kids.

I try to select items that children can wash, cut, and assemble themselves. Montessori practical life lessons come alive when students prepare snacks—peeling bananas, mixing oats, or arranging apples and cheese. Even our gardening projects, with seeds like heirloom zinnias, turn a simple purchase into hands-on lessons about plant life and ecology.

I prefer bulk bins for oats or flour, which reduces packaging and models environmental responsibility—a concept I discuss with the children in small ways each week. If a preferred item isn’t available, I’m always open to alternatives; staff often have helpful tips for regional, equally tasty substitutes.

Meal times—at home or school—often become sensory explorations. I like to invite everyone to taste several apple varieties, compare herbs, or savor cheeses. These moments deepen our connection to food and encourage mindful eating.

## Availability & Local Sourcing

Although I couldn’t check real-time inventory for this trip, Alberta Co-op consistently stocks extensive local and organic produce, dairy, breads, seeds, eggs, and honey throughout the year. The store’s relationships with small Oregon farms mean fresh greens, root vegetables, herbs, apples, and eggs are nearly always available. Most bulk grains are sourced from regional co-op partners, and spring is prime time for heirloom flower seeds from local seed companies.

I always recommend stopping by their produce and bulk sections first—staff are knowledgeable and can point me toward the freshest arrivals and special local items. The Alberta Co-op’s dedication to sustainable agriculture and ecological mindfulness aligns with the educational values I bring into my classroom and home.
