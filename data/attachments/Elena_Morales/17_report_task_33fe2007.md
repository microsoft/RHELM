## Best Practices for Montessori Meeting Minutes

**Child-Centered Documentation**  
Record minutes in a way that highlights children’s autonomy, interests, and decision-making. Capture examples of how staff observe and adapt based on student input, supporting the “follow the child” approach.

**Collaborative Planning**  
Acknowledge contributions from both staff and children. Documentation should reflect the balance between facilitation and direct instruction, spotlighting teamwork and shared outcomes.

**Clear, Practical Formatting**  
Utilize headers, agendas, bullet points, and summary tables for clarity and quick reference. Keep a visible record of unresolved matters to ensure accountability.

---

### Ensuring Continuity During Shortened or Interrupted Meetings

- Flag unfinished topics clearly in minutes, assigning follow-up responsible staff and deadlines.
- When meetings are cut short, suggest asynchronous discussions (by email or shared online documents) or brief interim check-ins.
- Observation journals and feedback forms should always be available for staff to capture real-time insights, particularly during periods between meetings.

---

**Montessori Documentation Examples**

- Record details of children’s activity choices and evolving interests during garden work cycles.
- Engage children in regular planning discussions (“child planning circle”), noting their suggestions for future activities.
- Schedule and document biweekly/monthly collaborative reviews to refine garden plans as interests and the seasons change.

---

### Collaborative Teamwork for Project Progress

- Ensure another staff member takes notes when primary leads are absent.
- Use shared platforms (cloud-based docs or physical binders) for accessible, ongoing record-keeping.
- Regularly ask for staff and student feedback after each garden activity, integrating direct quotes or anecdotes.
- Prominently flag tasks still needing resolution, stating clear ownership and action steps for the subsequent meeting.

---

### Immediate Areas for Further Teamwork

- Finalize additional plant variety choices after children participate in the next morning circle voting.
- Develop the best approach for adaptive planning, integrating perspectives from the entire teaching staff and students during upcoming meetings.
