# Incident Report: Pipe Burst in Montessori Art Room

## Incident Overview

**Date of Incident:** April 22, 2024  
**Time:** 9:15 a.m.  
**Location:** Montessori Program, Art Room (East Wing, Ground Floor)  
**Staff Present:**  
- Ms. Emma Carter, Lead Teacher  
- Mr. Luis Tran, Teaching Assistant  
- Ms. Julia Benson, Teaching Assistant  
**Students:**  
- 12 children (ages 3–6; see summary table for additional support needs)

---

## Incident Description

At approximately 9:15 a.m., the Montessori art room was disrupted by a sudden pipe burst in the east wall. A loud bang was followed almost instantly by a rush of water onto the floor. Within moments, flooding extended across much of the room, reaching several tables, shelves filled with art materials, and workspaces containing children’s completed and ongoing artwork.

The children reacted with alarm; several were startled and expressed distress due to the unexpected noise and rapid movement of water. Critical classroom zones—including the painting and sculpture areas—became inaccessible, and many supplies and student creations suffered water damage.

Responding immediately, teaching staff maintained composure and directed the children away from the flooded portion of the room. Drawing on core Montessori principles, they calmly redirected students to the adjacent reading area, furthest from the water’s spread. Teachers accounted for each child and ensured their safety. While Ms. Carter alerted the Facilities team via intercom, Mr. Tran notified administration and Ms. Benson reassured the children.

---

## Impact Assessment

### Disruption to Learning

The prompt interruption forced suspension of the morning art activity. Children engaged in projects were unable to finish their work, and numerous supplies—including paint blocks, clay, and paper—were exposed to water and subsequently removed to prevent contamination or injury.

Due to the flooding, much of the art room became inaccessible. Teachers quickly assessed which materials could be salvaged and removed any damaged items. Completed artwork and works-in-progress suffered varying degrees of water damage, with some pieces rendered unsalvageable.

### Safety and Wellbeing

Safety risks included possible water exposure, electrical hazards due to proximity of outlets, and heightened emotional distress—especially for the youngest children. Staff performed systematic visual checks for injuries and monitored the children for signs of anxiety or discomfort. The outlets in the affected area were quickly deactivated when Facilities staff arrived.

Teachers prioritized emotional reassurance, using calm tones and age-appropriate explanations of the situation. Group activities such as breathing exercises were implemented to help the children recover from the shock, in line with Montessori social-emotional guidelines. Two children who appeared particularly anxious received extra attention and individual support from Mr. Tran.

### Temporary Relocation

To maintain a sense of normalcy, the class was relocated to the library for the remainder of the morning. This decision allowed for learning activities to continue in a calm, organized environment. Ms. Carter rapidly adjusted the schedule, focusing on activities that fit the available space and integrating collaborative group discussions about the unexpected event—engaging students in conversation about "what happens when things need fixing" to support their understanding and resilience.

---

## Response Actions

- Facilities staff arrived within 12 minutes of notification and stopped water flow at the source.
- Electrical circuits in the flooded area were deactivated immediately for safety.
- Teachers used furniture and guidance tape to cordon off wet zones, preventing any risk of accidental entry.
- A roll call was conducted to ensure all children and staff were accounted for, and emergency evacuation procedures were reviewed.
- Art activities and the core Montessori learning routines were temporarily relocated to the library, minimizing learning disruptions.
- A detailed log of the incident was compiled by the lead teacher, and administration was notified.
- Visual inspection of all artwork and materials was conducted to assess damage and determine what was salvageable.

---

## Recommendations and Next Steps

### Preventative Strategies

To prevent similar incidents in the future, periodic facility inspections should be scheduled—especially for water lines located in spaces accessed regularly by young children. It is advisable to install moisture detectors and ensure emergency shut-off points for water are clearly labeled and accessible in high-risk areas. Ongoing staff training should cover emergency procedures and strategies for calmly addressing disruptions, so that staff can continue to respond effectively.

Development of child-friendly talking points about emergencies will support emotional safety and align with Montessori values by providing opportunities for children to learn from real-life events in an age-appropriate, respectful manner.

### Restoration and Support

The Facilities team should carry out repairs promptly and confirm the room’s safety before resuming normal activities. Professional cleaning and disinfection of the affected areas is essential, as is assessment of all art materials and children’s work. Restoration or replacement of supplies and artwork should be managed through the program’s budget and resources.

Emotional follow-up is recommended for all children, especially those who required extra reassurance during the incident. This may include circle time discussions focusing on classroom safety, repairs, and resilience. Teachers should also monitor students for any lingering anxiety and provide individualized support as needed.

### Communication with Families

Maintaining transparency with families is vital. Timely communication should be sent out, summarizing the incident, explaining the response, and reassuring parents that all children were safe and cared for throughout. Detailed plans for repairs and continued learning should be provided, with an open line for questions or concerns. Families should be encouraged to discuss the incident at home in developmentally appropriate ways, supporting emotional wellbeing and positive coping.
