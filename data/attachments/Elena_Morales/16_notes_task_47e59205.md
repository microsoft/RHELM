# Daily Wellness Log: Elena Morales  
## Montessori Program Lead Teacher  
### May 5th, 2024

---

## Sleep Quality

Last night, I managed to get about six hours of sleep, although it was far from restful. I found it difficult to fall asleep, tossing and turning for quite a while before I finally drifted off. Throughout the night, I woke up several times, mainly because my sore throat and headache kept me uncomfortable. By morning, I felt noticeably unrested and aware that poor sleep was likely to shape the rest of my day.

---

## Physical Symptoms

| Symptom     | Severity      | Time Noted        | Details                                  |
|-------------|--------------|-------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Sore Throat | Moderate     | Morning–Evening   | Persistent, worsened when speaking       |
| Fatigue     | Moderate–Severe | All day        | Needed extra rest, limited activity      |
| Headache    | Moderate     | Morning, Afternoon| Intensified by tiredness and lack of sleep|

The sore throat lingered from the moment I woke up, making it uncomfortable to talk and affecting my ability to communicate with others. Fatigue was a constant; I felt depleted of energy and needed to sit or lie down more often than usual. The headache ebbed and flowed but was worst in the afternoon, seemingly aggravated by both fatigue and disrupted sleep.

---

## Meals & Fluids

Due to feeling so tired and unwell, I skipped breakfast entirely, unable to face eating first thing in the morning. Later in the day, I managed to prepare a light, broth-based soup for lunch—keeping it bland and mild so I wouldn’t irritate my throat further. I didn’t eat any snacks throughout the day, and my appetite stayed low. Nevertheless, I paid close attention to hydration, drinking warm water and herbal tea regularly, which brought some comfort and helped soothe my throat.

---

## Physical Activity

I set aside my usual yoga routine and instead did some gentle stretches mid-morning, focusing on my neck, shoulders, and back—areas that felt tight from the night’s fitful sleep. In the late afternoon, I tried to walk Oliver, my dog, but fatigue crept in quickly. After just a short time outside, I realized I needed to turn back and rest. Other than these light activities, I avoided strenuous exercise, choosing instead to prioritize my recovery.

---

## Mood & Social Interaction

Loneliness and isolation colored much of my day. With illness keeping me at home and away from work, I mostly kept to myself. I did have a brief, ten-minute phone call with my mother, who offered words of support and comfort. That conversation brightened my mood significantly, reminding me how important even a short connection can be. Otherwise, I had minimal social contact—the house was quiet, and I missed interacting with colleagues and students. A mild sense of anxiety persisted, especially as I thought about missing work and the responsibilities I hold as a program lead teacher. At the same time, I felt grateful for my mother’s caring presence, even from afar.

---

## Additional Notes

Despite making time for extra rest and gentle self-care, my symptoms continued throughout the day. I felt uneasy about being away from the classroom, concerned for both my students and colleagues. Yet I reminded myself that prioritizing my health today was the best way to support them in the long run. Small acts, like reaching out to family for support and staying hydrated, helped me cope. I deliberately chose not to check in with work, giving myself permission to fully focus on recovery.

---

## Summary Reflection

Today was a tough day—physically, emotionally, and professionally. The restless sleep set the tone, and my sore throat, headache, and deep fatigue made even routine tasks feel challenging. Preparing a simple soup and drinking warm tea became small acts of self-care, and a short, interrupted walk with Oliver provided a brief change of scenery. More than anything, I felt the weight of isolation and concern about not being present for my class.

The phone call with my mother was especially meaningful; it reminded me that asking for help and accepting support are essential, especially on difficult days. As I reflected this evening, I realized my anxiety about work stemmed from my commitment to my students and team, but resting today was necessary—even if stepping back for a moment felt uncomfortable.

I’ve learned that it’s vital to listen to my body’s signals, accept temporary limits, and cherish simple comforts, whether that’s a gentle stretch, a warm drink, or supportive words from a loved one. Tomorrow, I hope to continue recovering and, with luck, feel strong enough to return to my professional responsibilities, ready to re-engage with my classroom and colleagues.
