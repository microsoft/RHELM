Session Overview

On December 1, 2024, I invited Sofia to join me at my home in Portland for a focused review session of my study notes from Maria Montessori’s The Absorbent Mind. We intentionally chose the quiet environment of my bedroom to facilitate honest reflection and in-depth conversation—a setting that helped us connect our professional experiences directly with foundational Montessori principles.

Our primary goal was to revisit several core concepts from Montessori philosophy, to explore how these theoretical elements can shape daily practice in the classroom, and to nurture an atmosphere of shared learning and mentorship. Drawing on chapters from The Absorbent Mind and materials from the American Montessori Society (AMS), I led the discussion, encouraging Sofia to interject with observations, questions, and potential classroom applications.

Throughout the session, we gravitated toward the child’s innate capacity for learning, the importance of responding to sensitive periods, and the teacher’s evolving role as observer and facilitator. I aimed to model the reflective stance that is integral to Montessori leadership, balancing technical rigor with openness to new ideas. Sofia’s contributions added valuable perspective, bridging my interpretations of the text with practical strategies suited to contemporary classroom challenges.

Detailed Review: Concepts & Observations

We identified and discussed four major concepts, using a structured table to document key insights and classroom strategies. This process helped us clarify how Montessori theory translates into effective teaching methods.

Montessori Concept Reference (The Absorbent Mind) Elena’s Feedback Non-Verbal Communication Sofia’s Observations & Classroom Applications
The Absorbent Mind Ch. 2, pp. 21–30 Children’s minds are remarkably receptive in early childhood. It’s essential to foster an environment grounded in trust and variety. Steady nods, gentle hand gestures I can enrich my lessons by designing classrooms with diverse materials and keenly observing how each child participates.
Sensitive Periods Ch. 3, pp. 36–45 During sensitive periods, spontaneous learning flourishes. Timely, well-planned interventions can encourage, rather than disrupt, this natural process. Leaning in, animated facial expression I find it helpful to keep individual checklists for students, noting observed sensitive periods and using these to inform lesson planning.
Role of Adult/Observer Ch. 7, pp. 85–96 The teacher should be present but unobtrusive, exercising patience and careful observation. This approach allows children to take initiative and develop self-regulation. Thoughtful silence, affirming nods I’m reminded to model patience daily, and to document children’s growth discreetly, letting them lead their learning whenever possible.
Normalization (planned for next review) Ch. 19, pp. 215–226 Normalization involves the child’s development of concentration, inner discipline, and social harmony. Eyebrows raised in thoughtful contemplation For our next session, I plan to examine how normalization contributes to a peaceful classroom. I’ll observe and document changes in class dynamics as students develop these abilities.

As we moved through each concept, we frequently paused to discuss real-life examples from my teaching practice. For instance, when reflecting on sensitive periods, I described a moment earlier in the week when a child suddenly became interested in numbers—prompting me to adjust that day’s materials to support her curiosity. Sofia found this anecdote helpful and noted the importance of remaining flexible in lesson planning.

Recommendations and Next Steps

To ensure our learning deepens beyond today’s discussion, we agreed on several immediate and long-term actions:

Topics to Revisit

  • Dive deeper into the process of normalization, with examples from recent classroom experiences.
  • Develop systematic methods for child observation and documentation to better track individual progress and inform interventions.
  • Refine our approach to preparing classroom environments, tailoring these to emerging group and individual needs.

Actionable Steps

  • Begin weekly reflective journaling after lessons to capture insights, questions, and moments of growth.
  • Register for AMS-sponsored webinars focused on observation techniques and strategies for supporting diverse learners.
  • Form peer-led study groups among colleagues to read and discuss The Absorbent Mind, sharing experiences and new research in Montessori pedagogy.

Reflection on Engagement and Growth

Reflecting on today’s session, I felt a renewed sense of purpose in my work as a Montessori educator. Revisiting Montessori’s original writings was not only intellectually stimulating but also emotionally grounding—reminding me why I chose this profession. Sharing my process with Sofia offered mutual benefit: while I articulated my understanding and creative classroom strategies, Sofia highlighted fresh ways to apply these ideas with her students.

Non-verbal communication played a vital role throughout our discussion. I noticed the value of maintaining eye contact, nodding encouragement, and listening attentively—not just for fostering rapport, but for modeling the calm, receptive posture Montessori educators strive for. These moments helped nurture a collaborative space where both of us felt comfortable questioning, reflecting, and proposing new ideas.

Ultimately, our review emphasized the dynamic nature of Montessori practice. While the principles in The Absorbent Mind provide a strong intellectual foundation, it’s the continual reflection, adaptation, and sharing among educators that sustain meaningful growth in ourselves and our students. I look forward to further collaborative sessions and to integrating these philosophies in everyday teaching.
