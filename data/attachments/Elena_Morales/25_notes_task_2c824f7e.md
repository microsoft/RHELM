## Overview

As a Montessori educator, integrating self-care into my daily routine is essential—not just for personal well-being, but also to model the values I strive to offer in the classroom. This daily log is my way of thoughtfully observing how the day unfolded, with particular attention on child-centered pacing, intentional restorative moments, sensory experiences, and the gentle authority that guides my decisions, both personally and professionally. Each entry captures not only what I did, but how I felt, what I noticed, and how these moments relate to my Montessori practice. Alongside the log, I’ve included a simple mood and symptom tracker, which helped me reflect on my emotional and physical states throughout the day.

## Connection to Montessori Principles

Reflecting on today's experience, several Montessori values stood out in my self-care approach:

- **Child-Centered Pacing:** I made a point to slow down, adjust activities to how I felt, and honor my own readiness—just as I do for children finding their unique learning tempo.
- **Sensory Engagement:** From savoring my breakfast to mindful breathing by the window, I relied on sensory experiences to anchor and uplift myself, recognizing these details as foundational for well-being and learning.
- **Gentle Authority and Choice:** Communicating about my health, planning lessons, and organizing my space required both decisive action and inner kindness. These moments balanced autonomy with professional responsibility.
- **Order and Reflection:** The structure of this log, as well as the prompts, brought intentional order to my day. Reflecting in writing deepened my understanding of how self-observation shapes not only my own resilience but also my ability to support others.

## Self-Observation and Adaptation

Documenting my day in this way helped me notice patterns in mood and well-being, and gave me permission to adjust my routines as needed. These small, attentive actions build emotional endurance, clarity, and keep my professional values alive outside the classroom.
