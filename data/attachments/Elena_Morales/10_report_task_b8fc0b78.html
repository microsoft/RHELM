Morning Session: Peace Education

Facilitator: Dr. Priya Desai

The workshop opened with Dr. Desai guiding us through the fundamental Montessori principles that foster peaceful classroom environments:

  • Respect for the Child: Dr. Desai emphasized the importance of viewing each child as an individual, honoring their choices and voice. She shared practical ways to nurture intrinsic motivation so children naturally develop collaborative and harmonious relationships.
  • Prepared Environment: Creating a classroom setup that allows freedom within boundaries was a focal point. Discussions centered on how a thoughtfully organized space—low shelves, defined activity areas, and accessible materials—enables autonomy and supports children’s social and emotional growth.
  • Grace and Courtesy: Through modeling, role-playing, and daily rituals, we examined how grace and courtesy lessons become the bedrock for developing empathy, mutual respect, and effective conflict resolution.

Conflict Resolution in the Montessori Classroom

Dr. Desai provided hands-on strategies for guiding young children through conflict:

  • Peace Table Rituals: She offered clear protocols for using peace tables—a dedicated space for mediation—where children are encouraged to share feelings using “I” statements and collaboratively seek solutions. We explored various materials to keep at the table, such as feelings cards and a soft timer, and ways to introduce these rituals.
  • Observation Before Intervention: It was recommended to allow children time to self-regulate and negotiate before stepping in, unless safety is a concern. Observing these interactions helps educators support growth in social skills.
  • Communication Techniques: We practiced language that acknowledges emotions (“I notice you’re feeling _____”) and encourages children to express their needs openly while learning to listen to others.
  • Reflective Mediation: Dr. Desai demonstrated how to coach children to articulate their perspectives and emotions, listen to peers, and brainstorm solutions that honor everyone involved.

Takeaways from the Peace Education Session

Several actionable ideas emerged: - Begin each day with peace circles or check-in activities to foster community and a restorative atmosphere. - Intentionally plan grace and courtesy lessons within the daily curriculum. - Keep the peace table well-stocked—with timers, peace objects, and emotion cards—to support conflict resolution. - Use reflective questioning to help children build emotional intelligence and interpersonal skills.

Breakout Groups: Discussion & Collaborative Solutions

Participants:
Elena Morales (Portland), Sarah Johnson (Eugene), Miguel Vargas (Salem), Linda Chen (Springfield)

Our breakout group reflected the diversity of Montessori roles and settings across Oregon.

Key Discussion Topics

  • Supporting Transitions: We talked about techniques for easing morning anxieties and facilitating gentle separation for young children at drop-off. Sharing rituals—like visual schedules, sensory objects, and greeting songs—helped us compare experiences.
  • Implementing Core Montessori Practices: Time constraints and class sizes often challenge uninterrupted work cycles. We discussed creative ways to structure transitions and adapt materials for neurodiverse students.
  • Engaging Parents and Families: Collectively, we recognized the need to communicate Montessori philosophy more effectively to parents, particularly those unfamiliar with child-led approaches.
  • Addressing Challenging Behaviors: Dealing with aggression in mixed-age classrooms was another focus. We shared resources and strategies to promote self-regulation, positive peer interactions, and scaffolded problem-solving.

Successes Shared

Our group reflected on recent accomplishments: - Consistent use of peace table routines has led to more thoughtful and independent conflict resolution among students. - Visual schedules and transitional supports have been particularly beneficial for children with sensory or cognitive differences. - Hosting parent education nights has noticeably improved family understanding and engagement with Montessori methods.

Collaborative Solutions Proposed

  • Create and distribute a clear “Montessori Introduction” handout to help families grasp the foundations of child-centered learning.
  • Rotate calming and sensory-based activities during transition times to support student regulation.
  • Build classroom libraries with books about emotions, problem-solving, and peer mediation, making these resources accessible daily.
  • Arrange peer observation exchanges between local Montessori schools, fostering connections and sharing best practices.

Lunch and Networking

Lunch was held in the school garden, providing both nourishment and inspiration. Surrounded by budding plants and children’s garden projects, we discussed ideas for integrating outdoor experiences into our curriculum. Stories of children planting, harvesting, and preparing snacks sparked lively exchanges about hands-on learning and encouraging responsibility. I connected with Anna Li and Linda Chen, and we agreed to collaborate on garden-based lesson plans and share resources for outdoor education.

Sensory pathways in the garden led our conversation toward increasing outdoor engagement for all learners. We considered how tactile surfaces, aromatic plants, and movement stations might be adapted to indoor settings to support children with different needs.

Afternoon Workshop: Outdoor Engagement & Sensory/Nature Journaling

Facilitator: Anna Li

Anna Li’s session offered practical insight into the power of outdoor learning in Montessori classrooms.

Activities & Learning Experiences

  • Sensory Path Exploration: We toured the new sensory path at Fernwood. Stepping on smooth stones, textured mats, and passing aromatic plants, I noticed how students with varying sensory profiles might benefit from these elements. Anna encouraged us to imagine adaptations for our own sites, including indoor sensory paths for year-round accessibility.
  • Nature Journaling: Each attendee received a simple journal and colored pencils. Anna demonstrated how to scaffold journaling—from guided observations and drawings to labeling objects and short reflections. She modeled ways to link nature journaling to literacy and science concepts, such as identifying leaf shapes and recording bird calls.
  • Outdoor Choice Stations: Throughout the afternoon, we rotated through bug hunts, leaf rubbings, movement games, and quiet reflection spots. Anna highlighted the value of choice and autonomy in outdoor play.

Implementation Strategies Discussed

  • Schedule weekly outdoor journaling sessions, integrating observation, letter-sound work, and basic science inquiry.
  • Provide both indoor and outdoor sensory paths, using ramps, visual cues, and tactile materials to ensure accessibility for every learner.
  • Designate a classroom “nature treasures” table to inspire curiosity and foster storytelling.
  • Scaffold participation for all students: supply alternative communication tools, offer individualized support, and encourage flexible means of expression (drawing, writing, movement).

Reflective Insights

Reflections as a Montessori Educator

Throughout the day, I felt affirmed in my approach as a patient, observant, and nurturing teacher. The workshop reminded me that modeling peace and empathy is as crucial as classroom routines or lesson planning. I left with renewed commitment to foster a harmonious environment—by being intentional in our rituals, available resources, and daily interactions.

The opportunity to network and share resources with colleagues was invaluable. Hearing others’ successes and struggles helped me identify new strategies and opportunities to support both my students and their families. I’m eager to deepen collaboration with Anna and Linda, especially in sharing outdoor curriculum ideas.

Next Steps for My Classroom

  • Build daily peace circles into our morning routine; maintain a well-equipped peace table for conflict resolution.
  • Share the Montessori introduction handout and plan quarterly parent education evenings to engage families.
  • Introduce sensory path experiences for smooth transitions and inclusive participation.
  • Start a nature journaling routine that accommodates diverse communication modes—drawing, recording, movement, or conversation.

Fostering Collaborative, Child-Centered Environments

This workshop encouraged me to invite student input when co-constructing classroom agreements and routines. I plan to expand mixed-age group interactions and establish peer mentoring, nurturing collaboration and leadership. Consistent observation and documentation will help me track growth, tailor support, and reflect meaningfully on our evolving classroom community.
