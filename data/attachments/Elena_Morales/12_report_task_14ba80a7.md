## Discussion & Planning Summary

### 1. Child-Centered Activities

We began by reviewing the structure and content of child-focused gardening stations to align with Montessori principles. Activities will include seedling planting, a hands-on composting demonstration, nature-inspired art, and exploration of a sensory garden. Each station is designed to encourage independence and promote mixed-age interaction, letting children explore at their own pace.

Materials have been carefully selected for accessibility and engagement: child-sized gardening tools, a variety of natural art supplies, and multisensory stations suitable for children with diverse physical and neurodevelopmental needs.

### 2. Collaborative Approach to Event Logistics

The group collaboratively mapped logistical details, focusing on smooth transitions and maximizing opportunity for free exploration. Staff assigned themselves responsibility for key areas—Mr. Kim will prepare plant-related stations, Elena has taken charge of scheduling and overall event oversight, and Mrs. Patel will organize refreshments with attention to dietary considerations.

We agreed that setup and breakdown tasks would be shared, ensuring flexibility around individual availability and encouraging joint ownership of the event process.

### 3. Community Involvement & Family Engagement

Creating a welcoming, participatory environment is central to our vision. We discussed rotating volunteer roles for parents, parent–child team activities, and clear sign-up procedures. Elena will design promotional materials, offering translations in commonly spoken languages to widen reach and ensure families feel included.

Ms. Rogers will recruit volunteers through classroom visits, personalized invitations, and accessible communication. Emphasis will be placed on inclusivity, inviting underrepresented families and making volunteer roles welcoming to all.

### 4. Diversity, Inclusion & Accessibility

Accessibility and inclusion were priorities throughout the planning. Station activities are adapted to accommodate children with neurodiversity and physical disabilities. Refreshments will be strictly nut-free, with vegetarian and vegan options available. All event communication will clearly state accessibility features, and Mr. Acosta will conduct a facilities check to confirm ramps, restroom access, and sheltered outdoor areas are available.

### 5. Timeline, Volunteer Needs, and Contingency Planning

We outlined a detailed timeline to guide preparation from today through the event, setting key milestones for each task. Ms. Rogers will lead volunteer recruitment using a coordinated outreach strategy, including classroom briefings and direct messages.

Weather contingency plans include relocating stations to covered outdoor patios or preparing portable indoor activities if needed. We also identified backup staff in case of unexpected absences, ensuring enough support for all event elements.

---

## Decisions & Outcomes

- The event will prioritize child-led, experiential activities reflecting Montessori values.
- We agreed that the event will begin with a welcoming circle, followed by open, free-flowing access to stations.
- Refreshments will showcase healthy, culturally diverse options and respect all dietary needs, including allergies.
- Volunteer roles were clarified with clear sign-up procedures and role descriptions, supporting accessibility and shared responsibility.
- All correspondence and outreach efforts will explicitly highlight the event’s commitment to accessibility, diversity, and inclusion.
- Planning will be reviewed and updated weekly to incorporate ongoing feedback and respond to new needs.

### Volunteer Recruitment

Ms. Rogers will lead recruitment through classroom talks and sign-up sheets placed in the entryway, while personalized outreach and targeted invitations will be sent to families less often represented. To ensure everyone feels welcomed, a brief orientation will be given for parent volunteers. Older students will be paired with younger peers where appropriate, encouraging mentorship and inclusion.

### Contingency Planning

Given the possibility of changing weather, all stations have designated indoor or covered alternatives. A list of substitute staff will be maintained to address potential absences. Any last-minute changes will be communicated quickly through WhatsApp and text messages, ensuring full team awareness.

### Ongoing Event Alignment

Weekly meetings will ensure our plans remain true to Montessori ideals—cultivating independence, offering choices, and honoring each child’s pace. Staff and families will be encouraged to share feedback before, during, and after the event to keep our approach responsive and adaptive.

---

## Feedback & Reflection

Feedback opportunities will be embedded at every stage. Staff and parent memos next week will include space for comments and ideas. Elena will facilitate a 10-minute post-event roundtable to discuss successes and lessons learned. Children and families will be offered simple feedback cards at activity stations so their voices directly inform future events (“What did you enjoy? What would you change?”).

---

## Ensuring Montessori and Child-Centered Practice

Today's session was structured to model Montessori meeting principles: collaborative decision-making, child-centered focus, and active attention to inclusion and community engagement. Documentation of action items, clear timelines, and contingency planning will support transparency and smooth communication with staff and families.

Regular reflection and ongoing adjustments will ensure every element of the Spring Family Garden Day remains aligned with the values and needs of our diverse school community.
