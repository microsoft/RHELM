For Program Leads: Following March 15, 2024 Session

Maintaining an engaging and safe garden environment is an integral part of Montessori education, where practical life meets a love for nature. After our March 15 garden session, this guide summarizes the current status of our supplies, ongoing recovery actions for damaged seedlings, and strategies to foster both learning and community throughout the process.

Program Recommendations:
- Involve children in routine checks and cleaning at session’s end—this builds stewardship and familiarity with safe tool use.
- Every tool and action should be modeled first by adults, reinforcing clear expectations.
- Use repairs and maintenance activities as teachable moments about responsibility for their environment.

Seedling Damage: Recovery Steps and Learning Integration

During the March 15 session, several seedlings sustained damage. Addressing these incidents not only restores the garden but provides rich, hands-on learning opportunities. Suggested Classroom Integration:
- Embed all recovery activities into daily Practical Life lessons, allowing children to connect their work to broader concepts like stewardship and care.
- Foster open discussion, inviting children to share observations and predictions about plant healing.
- Encourage collaborative journaling and tracking, making recovery a visible, shared classroom experience.

Action Plan: Prioritized Follow-Up Tasks

To ensure the garden’s full recovery and continued educational value, this checklist organizes next steps in an accessible, actionable format for lead educators. Each action encourages child participation, supports curriculum integration, and strengthens community bonds.

  1. Immediate Tool & Supply Maintenance
  2. Guide children in inspecting and cleaning garden tools as an end-of-day routine.
  3. Repair torn aprons and gloves in a supervised Practical Life activity, reinforcing responsibility.

  4. Keep detailed records of items needing attention and promptly submit supply orders for essentials.

  5. Seedling Damage Recovery

  6. Schedule dedicated classroom times for recovery actions, supported by clear instruction and contextual ties to the science curriculum.
  7. Integrate lessons on botanical life cycles, observation, and nurture directly into these gardening tasks.

  8. Photograph and document progress, sharing updates with children, parents, and staff to celebrate collective effort.

  9. Reschedule Planting Sessions

  10. Choose a new date (within the coming week) for planting any deferred species, keeping momentum strong.
  11. Ensure the session connects with current curriculum units (such as “Living Things” and “Stewardship”) for continuity.

  12. Offer flexibility for both outdoor and indoor gardening activities, adjusting for weather and logistics as needed.

  13. Parent and Volunteer Communication

  14. Draft a warm group message (see below) to recognize volunteer contributions, provide status updates, and invite support for upcoming activities.

  15. Clearly outline ways parents can assist—by volunteering, donating supplies, or contributing to classroom discussions.

  16. Newsletter Update: Positive Outreach (for Wi-Fi Restored Newsletter)

  17. Share a concise, uplifting summary of the latest session for families:
