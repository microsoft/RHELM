## Impact on Professional Practice

### Symptom Management and Adaptations

- **Hand Tremor:** My hand tremor affected tasks requiring precision, like writing and classroom demonstrations. Shifting to digital lesson planning and inviting students to lead activities helped keep the classroom running smoothly while reinforcing core Montessori principles of independence and shared responsibility.
- **Sore Throat / Loss of Voice:** The gradual loss of my voice forced me to rely on gestures, written notes, and visual classroom signals. Students responded well to these adaptations, showing increased attentiveness and empathy. Transparent communication with my colleagues opened the door for extra support and allowed us to flexibly adjust responsibilities on short notice.
- **Dizziness and Fatigue:** Episodes of dizziness throughout the day prompted me to adjust my pacing, sit during lessons more often, and allow for extra outdoor or quiet breaks. These measures respected my own limitations and modeled for students the importance of attentive self-care.
- **Cough:** A consistent cough required me to step aside for water and fresh air, switch meetings from phone calls to email, and set clear boundaries about what tasks I could take on. These choices provided frequent opportunities to show the value of self-monitoring and boundary-setting to both colleagues and students.

### Effects on Interactions and Decision-Making

- **With Students:** Adjusting my teaching style invited students to take leadership roles and collaborate more—particularly when assisting with classroom routines and learning activities. Modeling vulnerability and flexibility fostered a sense of trust and community, and gave students permission to practice empathy.
- **With Colleagues:** Honest conversations about my symptoms prompted immediate offers of help and a spirit of teamwork. Allowing Mark to step in during group time or asking for backup with manual tasks strengthened our professional partnership and kept learning on track.
- **Self-Care and Wellness Modeling:** Prioritizing simple self-care rituals—warm drinks, stretching, vocal rest, and digital journaling—not only eased my own discomfort but also modeled practical wellness strategies for everyone in the classroom.

## Overall Reflection and Future Wellness Planning

Tracking symptoms and adapting my teaching approach highlighted the close connections between educator wellness, classroom atmosphere, and student engagement. Today reinforced several core lessons:

- Open, timely communication with colleagues makes it possible to redistribute tasks and keep learning seamless even when challenges arise.
- Modeling adaptive strategies—whether delegating, adjusting my own expectations, or showing honest vulnerability—strengthens the classroom culture and aligns directly with Montessori values.
- Recognizing personal limits and seeking rest is essential for sustaining energy and long-term effectiveness.
- Making the decision to consult a healthcare provider for persistent symptoms is an act of responsibility and care, setting an example for both students and staff.

I plan to continue this reflective daily routine, as it strengthens my awareness of how mind, body, and learning environment are always intertwined. These logs are becoming a vital tool for improving not only my personal wellness but also our collective approach to supporting one another in the Montessori community.

## Closing Reflection

Physical health—whether it’s a sore throat, hand tremor, dizziness, or persistent cough—inevitably shapes my decisions, teaching style, and interactions in the classroom. Capturing a detailed account of each day deepens my self-understanding and allows me to plan for future wellness proactively. Trusting in open communication and responsive support systems keeps our classroom resilient and cohesive, and reminds me of the importance of leading by example when it comes to self-care.
