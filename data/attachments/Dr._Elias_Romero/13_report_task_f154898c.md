## Summary of Research Organization Activities (April 27, 2024)

On April 27, 2024, I undertook a comprehensive review and restructuring of our digital research notes for the Cross-European Nutrition Study. The overarching goal was to enhance data accessibility, ensure traceability, and prepare our files for active collaboration across the research consortium. Throughout the day, I focused on organizing both longstanding and recently acquired materials, with a particular emphasis on integrating findings that have emerged from recent international symposia.

My main activities were as follows:

- **File Categorization and Structuring:** I systematically organized research documents into themed subfolders—specifically dietary patterns, micronutrient status, and policy interventions. Each document followed our standardized naming conventions, and I updated version control across the archive to maintain clarity in document histories.

- **Metadata Tagging:** To improve retrieval, I reviewed and implemented uniform metadata tags. This process was critical for files relevant to specific subpopulations (such as adolescents or older adults) or geographic regions. For greater granularity, I cross-referenced population demographics and intervention specifics wherever possible.

- **Integration of Symposium Insights:** Recent findings from the EUPHA Nutrition Section 2024 and the European Micronutrient Conference were synthesized and directly linked to relevant project folders. Detailed annotations now accompany these files, flagging key actionable points for subsequent analysis as we draft upcoming summary papers.

- **Data Cleaning and Backup Procedures:** I identified and eliminated duplicate files, retaining only those with full annotations, and ensured that datasets were securely backed up. All reorganized datasets were saved to our institutional cloud and encrypted external drives, in line with EFSA data management guidelines.

- **Documentation of Actions and Collaboration Readiness:** Each stage of the reorganization was fully documented. This transparent record supports collaboration among our project partners and guarantees compliance with audit requirements, a critical consideration for multicenter studies spanning several European countries [1,2,3].

## Key Insights and Priorities

**Data Gaps Identified**

During the review, several areas requiring further attention became clear:
- Current research notes do not provide sufficient detail on micronutrient intake trends among adolescents in the Baltic countries. This gap restricts our capacity for comparative regional analyses and weakens the overall depth of our transnational assessments.
- Although documentation of policy interventions is robust for Western Europe, there is limited information on the implementation and outcomes of similar policies in Southern and Eastern member states. The lack of outcome data from these regions hinders a comprehensive understanding of policy effectiveness.
- Methodological inconsistency remains an issue across network partners, especially regarding approaches to dietary intake assessment. This heterogeneity complicates efforts to harmonize and synthesize findings across the consortium.

**Literature Synthesis Priorities**

To address these deficiencies, I have identified the following immediate priorities:
- A targeted systematic review of school meal policies, with special emphasis on underrepresented Eastern and Southern European populations. By collating and evaluating both outcome and process data from these regions, we can strengthen our overall analysis and fill an important knowledge gap.
- The synthesis matrix for micronutrient status, particularly for iron and vitamin D in younger populations, needs an update. This task will involve standardizing indicators according to recent recommendations from both the EUPHA and European Micronutrient Conference.

**Organizational and Methodological Improvements**

Ensuring methodological rigor in data management is essential for the longevity and reliability of this multicenter project. To that end:
- I will advocate for the adoption of EFSA-recommended data dictionaries and variable name conventions across all partner sites to create a shared metadata structure [1].
- To further safeguard data integrity, I am instituting weekly automated backups and will set up a semi-annual audit trail schedule. This dual approach should improve both security and retrieval efficiency.
- Recognizing the importance of consistency and transparency, I am drafting a comprehensive, shared organizational manual. This living document will outline protocols for file naming, metadata tagging, and classification standards, serving as both a quality assurance mechanism and an onboarding resource for new collaborators [2,5].
