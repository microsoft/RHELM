## 2. Transcription of the Original Recipe with Context

> “Para la merienda o para acompañar el café.  
>  *Biscotes de higos y almendras*  
>  — 2 tazas colmadas de harina blanca (mejor si es de la fina, del molino)  
>  — ¼ taza de azúcar  
>  — 1 puñado de almendras, tostadas y partidas a lo largo  
>  — 2 puñados de higos secos, cortados en trocitos  
>  — 2 huevos  
>  — Ralladura de 1 limón, un chorrito de anís  
>  — 1 cucharadita de polvo de hornear  
>  Se mezcla la harina con el azúcar y el polvo de hornear, luego se hace un hueco y se ponen los huevos batidos con la ralladura y el anís. Al final, se añade la fruta y las almendras. Se hacen dos panes alargados y se hornean hasta que estén dorados, luego se cortan en rebanadas y se vuelven a dorar.  
>  Salen ligeros y crujientes, para guardar en caja de lata.  
>  — “Como los hacía la abuela en las fiestas de San Miguel”."

This recipe preserves the linguistic features of its time, including informal measurements such as “puñados” (handfuls) and experiential instructions highlighting sensory cues like “dorados” (golden), “ligeros y crujientes” (light and crisp), and references to ingredient quality (“harina fina, del molino” — fine, mill-ground flour). The text also provides a vivid glimpse into the family’s tradition of enjoying these biscotti during afternoon coffee or festive gatherings, particularly the San Miguel celebrations.

## 3. Annotations and Evidence-Based Modifications

### 3.1. Methodological Approach

Recipe digitization was guided by established digital humanities standards [1], current protocols in nutritional epidemiology, and Mediterranean dietary recommendations [2][3]. Every modification has been transparently documented and justified, integrating both scientific and cultural perspectives.

### 3.2. Modifications and Rationale

**Refined Wheat Flour → Half Whole Wheat, Half White Flour:**  
Switching to a blend of whole wheat and white flour raises fiber and micronutrient content while maintaining a familiar biscotti texture, as recommended for Mediterranean-style baking [2][3].

**Sugar Reduced by 20%:**  
Decreasing the sugar aligns with World Health Organization guidance on reducing added sugars for the prevention of chronic disease [3], yet preserves pleasant sweetness from the figs.

**Increased Almonds:**  
Almond quantities were slightly increased to provide higher levels of monounsaturated fats, vitamin E, and magnesium. This not only supports cardiovascular health, as confirmed by Mediterranean diet studies, but also matches daily nut intake guidelines [2].

**Eggs Maintained (Vegan Substitute Provided):**  
Eggs remain for structure and protein; however, for plant-based needs, they can be replaced with 2 tablespoons of ground flaxseed mixed with 6 tablespoons of water, in line with current baking literature [4].

**Anís (Anise Liqueur) Optional:**  
Anise liqueur remains to preserve the original flavor profile but can be omitted for those wishing to avoid alcohol. Orange blossom water is suggested as a suitable alternative, offering floral fragrance and maintaining a Mediterranean flavor [5].

**Baking Powder Unchanged:**  
No modification is necessary, as this leavening agent does not impact sodium levels significantly and is key to the biscotti’s lightness.

**Ingredient Sourcing and Seasonality:**  
Lucía’s original preference for local, sun-dried figs and almonds is retained to support both flavor and sustainable ingredient use [3][6]. Sourcing these ingredients in late summer and autumn reflects their traditional harvest times and sustains the cultural context.

**Allergen Considerations:**  
The primary allergens in this recipe are wheat (gluten), almonds (tree nuts), and eggs. Plant-based substitutions and nut alternatives are detailed in the ingredient table.

## 5. Pairing Recommendations: Tradition Meets Modern Nutrition

### Beverage Pairings

Classic *café solo*—a small cup of pure, strong Mediterranean-style coffee—echoes the original family tradition. Lighter Ethiopian or Colombian Arabica coffees enhance the toasty, nutty, and fruity notes in the biscotti. Pairing biscotti with coffee not only elevates the sensory experience but, as research suggests, can even offer synergistic polyphenol and cardioprotective benefits [2][7].

For those favoring a gentler flavor or an evening snack, green tea works beautifully. Its mild bitterness offsets the biscotti’s sweetness, and its antioxidants contribute to overall dietary balance [7]. Herbal infusions such as lemon verbena, thyme, or fennel are traditional in Mediterranean homes, ideal for after dinner and supportive of digestion.

### Fruit Pairings

For a vibrant, seasonal touch, serve biscotti alongside slices of fresh figs, orange segments, or scattered pomegranate arils. These fruits not only bring color and acidity to the plate, but add vitamin C and complement the rustic flavors of the recipe, maintaining ties to Mediterranean dietary patterns.

### Serving Recommendations

A serving of one or two biscotti is recommended, particularly as part of a merienda (afternoon snack). Pairing with unsweetened coffee or tea and fresh fruit honors both tradition and contemporary portion guidance. This approach supports mindful enjoyment and aligns with modern nutrition standards for whole grains and limited added sugars [2][3].
