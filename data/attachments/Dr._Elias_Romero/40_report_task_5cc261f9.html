Personal Research Timeline Update – September 1, 2024

Introduction: Impact of Grant Rejection on Project Trajectory

The recent rejection of our cross-European grant application has necessitated an extensive reassessment of my food environment study’s scope, scheduling, and use of resources. Our original funding bid envisioned a broad, multinational dataset and robust stakeholder engagement—both of which are critical for tackling the urgent public health nutrition challenges across Europe. Losing this central funding source has directly delayed project initiation, forced a reconsideration of collaborative structures, and made it essential to reassign personnel and infrastructure in the interim. These disruptions present challenges in maintaining the momentum that had begun at the country level, meeting regulatory standards in each jurisdiction, and ensuring uniform data collection approaches. It also brings our methodological planning back under review. Given that public health policy in Europe increasingly depends on high-quality, harmonized data from multi-country research, this setback underscores the urgency of securing alternative resources to minimize negative effects on our planned outputs, impact targets, and responsibilities to both our home institutions and external collaborators.

Next Steps and Recovery Plan

Alternative Funding Strategy

I am now focusing on several avenues to secure alternative or interim funding, thereby enabling the project to retain momentum during this critical period:

  • Horizon Europe Calls: A targeted review is underway of currently open and upcoming Horizon Europe funding calls relevant to food systems, nutrition, and public health. These calls represent the most substantial opportunity for regaining lost ground on multinational ambitions.
  • National and Philanthropic Options: Alongside European-level applications, I am mapping out relevant opportunities from national research councils and philanthropic organizations that could provide either bridging funds or partial co-funding.
  • Internal Support: Within my home institution, I am exploring access to seed or bridge funding mechanisms that could help maintain critical project activities until larger grants are secured.
  • Coordinated Partner Funding: I am working closely with the core partners to determine shared resource mobilization strategies, ensuring that every institution involved advocates for and supports the resubmission effort from both technical and administrative perspectives.

Immediate Action Plan

To ensure project continuity and maintain consortium cohesion, I have initiated the following steps:

1. Horizon Europe Application Preparation

  • Conducting a systematic review of relevant Horizon Europe calls, ranking them based on thematic fit, funding volume, and submission timeline.
  • Informing all consortium partners early of the intention to revise and resubmit, making the rationale behind adjusted timelines and methodologies transparent.
  • Assigning writing teams and clarifying which partners will lead specific proposal sections—including methodological design, impact, and regulatory compliance.
  • Assembling required compliance documentation (e.g., refreshed letters of support, research integrity statements) in parallel with technical writing.

2. Consortium Coordination

  • Calling an urgent virtual meeting with work package leads to recalibrate expectations, agree on revised deliverables, and establish a framework for ongoing digital collaboration.
  • Setting up monthly online check-ins to document partner engagement, track regulatory progress, and anticipate emerging risks.
  • Developing draft protocols for data collection in various scenarios, outlining how the study would proceed at reduced scale if funding uncertainties persist.

3. Communications and Stakeholder Engagement

  • Preparing detailed update briefings for university leadership, relevant public health authorities, and potential future funders, outlining the project’s current status and next steps.
  • Rolling out a communications plan for international collaborators that reassures them of the team’s continued commitment to high scientific and policy standards, even as timelines are revised.
  • Proactively updating partner country contacts about changes and inviting input on locally specific challenges.

4. Regulatory and Ethical Compliance

  • Initiating a rolling review of changes in national- and EU-level regulations and ethical processes that could affect re-submission and implementation.
  • Setting up a compliance task force tasked with preparing addenda and documentation to accelerate harmonized submission as soon as new funding is secured.

Consortium and Collaboration Sustainability

  • Continuing to use digital platforms for transparent decision-making and tracking: this includes version-controlled documents for protocol drafts and a centralized log of partner feedback.
  • Encouraging national partner leads to co-design and co-sign all major application and protocol documents, reinforcing the group’s resilience and unity to external reviewers and funders.
  • Exploring reciprocal resource-sharing with partners—such as temporary staff secondments, sharing pilot materials, or pooling administrative support—to smooth disruptions for individual teams and keep project capacity as robust as possible.

Broader Public Health and Policy Considerations

The project’s delay is particularly concerning given its potential to inform pressing policy debates and imminent regulatory cycles throughout Europe. Our revised schedule risks missing strategically important windows for influencing new policy or regulatory frameworks. Furthermore, adapting to the rescheduled timeline requires close monitoring to ensure compliance with data privacy rules (notably GDPR) and to integrate any new public health emergency regulations introduced in participating countries over the past year.

Thus, efficiency, methodological rigor, and transparent communication with stakeholders become critically important to ensure that despite a squeezed timeline and lower-than-planned initial funding, our outputs will still be sufficiently robust, comparable, and useful for both scientific and policy audiences.
