Executive Summary

This interim progress report summarizes key developments, achievements, and ongoing challenges within the cross-European Nutritional Epidemiology Cohort Consortium as of September 30, 2024. Since project launch, we have initiated participant recruitment and data collection across all seven participating countries, successfully onboarded the Danish national cohort, and established centralized harmonization processes for data integration and quality assurance. While data completeness and recruitment are high in some countries, others face persistent difficulties, particularly regarding the harmonization of dietary assessment methods and achieving uniform data completeness. These collaborative efforts are critical for generating robust, comparable findings to inform nutrition science and public health policy across Europe.

Methods

Cohort Structure and Recruitment

Our consortium comprises research teams from Spain, France, Italy, Germany, Denmark, Sweden, and the Netherlands. Each site adheres to a centrally coordinated protocol, rigorously adapted to suit national and local practice, with oversight from the harmonization committee. The target is to enroll 10,000 adults per country, aged 18–75 years, ensuring representative national sampling frames through population registries, community outreach, or clinical networks, as permitted by local infrastructure and regulations.

Data Collection Instruments

All participating centers have implemented a core set of standardized tools and procedures for data collection: - Food Frequency Questionnaires (FFQs): Each country uses a version adapted and validated to capture local dietary patterns, ensuring cultural sensitivity while maintaining methodological comparability. - 24-hour Dietary Recalls: The GloboDiet platform was adopted consortium-wide to enable uniform structure and digital integration for dietary recall data, providing international compatibility. - Anthropometric Measurements: Height, weight, and waist/hip circumferences are measured using harmonized protocols to minimize inter-center variability and ensure data reliability. - Sociodemographic, Lifestyle, and Health Outcome Surveys: Uniform questionnaires collect essential variables in a standardized manner, allowing direct comparability across sites.

Data Harmonization Strategies

Robust harmonization is central to this consortium, governed by strict adherence to established standards: - Variable Mapping: Each country’s data dictionary is systematically aligned with a central harmonization grid, grounded in EFSA’s methodological recommendations. - Food Item Coding: Dietary data are coded using the FoodEx2 system, facilitating fine-grained, pan-European food group mapping. - Quality Control: Centralized quality control includes double data entry for principal variables, automated logic checks, and regular data freezes with audit trails for version control. - Metadata Documentation: All datasets are described using the DDI standard, ensuring transparent, traceable, and interoperable metadata for future analyses. - Transparency and Reproducibility: Pre-specified statistical analysis plans, open harmonization scripts (in R), and regular updates on data cleaning and harmonization progress are standard practice within the consortium.

Ethical Considerations and Data Security

All participants provide informed consent, and each country complies with relevant national and European data protection laws, including full observance of GDPR. Secure data transfer and storage are assured via encrypted servers and restricted-access systems, ensuring both data confidentiality and research integrity.

Discussion

Progress and Country-Specific Updates

  • Spain, France, Sweden, and The Netherlands have consistently shown high recruitment rates and exceptional data completion. Their well-established infrastructure and strong public health partnerships have been vital. Minor delays, such as those in Spain and France, were predominantly linked to localized COVID-19 surges in early 2024, but these disruptions were managed effectively.
  • Germany has maintained steady enrollment but faces challenges with completeness of the 24-hour dietary recall and anthropometry modules. These issues stem from uneven availability of trained staff across regions, underscoring the need for resource redistribution and targeted staff support.
  • Italy is below the 80% enrollment target, due in part to regional administrative burdens and slower engagement through clinical networks. Targeted recruitment strategies, including expanded outreach and dedicated enrollment campaigns, will be rolled out in Q4 2024.
  • Denmark faces the most significant lag; integration as of Q2 2024 required additional time for ethical approvals and for transitioning to consortium-wide digital platforms. Achieving 6,100 recruits (61%) is commendable given these circumstances. Data completeness, especially for the 24-hour recall (85%) and anthropometry (90%), remains below the consortium average, reflecting the need for additional staff training and participant support. In response, supplementary training sessions and a harmonization “boot camp” are scheduled for October 2024.

Data Harmonization Challenges

  • Dietary Intake Data: Achieving uniform classification and quantification across national dietary assessment tools remains a core challenge. Each country brings unique culinary traditions, food items, and consumption habits, often difficult to directly map within FoodEx2, particularly for local or region-specific foods. Portion size estimation methods are not fully harmonized, despite ongoing calibration exercises.
  • Sociodemographic and Health Variables: Inconsistencies persist, especially relating to education and occupation coding. We are actively working to standardize these using ESCO and ISCED frameworks, with ongoing coordination between national data managers and the harmonization committee.
  • Data Quality Assurance: Centralized double entry and audit procedures have generally improved data coherence. Nevertheless, initial discrepancies were observed in Denmark and Italy, particularly missing or invalid anthropometric data, now being addressed through enhanced field validation and real-time data entry checks.

Implications for Public Health Nutrition

The harmonization of large-scale nutritional data across European cohorts provides a solid foundation for rigorous, comparative analyses of diet and health outcomes. By standardizing data collection and processing, the consortium is set to deliver evidence with direct relevance to European public health policy, spanning chronic disease prevention, food system interventions, and health equity. The recent onboarding of Denmark demonstrates the adaptability and scalability of our harmonization strategies, providing a template for future expansion of pan-European data infrastructures.

Concluding Remarks

The cross-European Nutritional Epidemiology Cohort Consortium has demonstrated strong progress towards establishing one of the most harmonized multicountry nutritional datasets in Europe. Most countries are nearing enrollment goals and achieving high data completeness, despite disparities that reflect unique national challenges, particularly in Denmark and Italy. The systematic, transparent approach to data harmonization and quality assurance has enhanced both the reliability and comparability of collected data. Addressing gaps in dietary recall completion, ensuring equitable progress among all cohorts, and refining harmonization strategies will remain focus areas in the coming months. Through ongoing commitment to methodological rigor and collaboration, the consortium is positioned to generate impactful evidence for nutrition policy and research, benefiting public health across Europe.
