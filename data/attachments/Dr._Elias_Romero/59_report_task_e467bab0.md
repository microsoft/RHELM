## Session Overview and Objectives

This session brought together university researchers and community health organizers to shape a collaborative agenda aimed at improving public health nutrition in Barcelona. The focus was on building practical bridges between current academic curriculum and community realities, emphasizing locally driven interventions and participation. The session’s key objectives were as follows:

- Identify significant local public health nutrition challenges and set clear priorities for action.
- Co-develop workshop modules that feature local produce and traditional food wisdom.
- Establish processes for active community contribution and continuous curriculum refinement.

Discussion opened with a review of recent data on nutrition in Barcelona’s neighborhoods, highlighting persistent inequalities in access, particularly in underserved areas. Participants noted that curriculum improvement efforts must address these inequities meaningfully and inclusively.

### Agreed Priorities

The group identified several central priorities to guide further work:

- **Enhancing nutritional literacy:** Committing to curriculum materials that reflect the cultural backgrounds and lived experiences of community members.
- **Tackling diet-related non-communicable diseases:** Designing interventions with an explicit focus on equity, particularly for marginalized populations.
- **Supporting resilient local food systems:** Encouraging the use of regionally grown produce in both education and practice.
- **Advancing participatory interventions:** Embedding community voices into every step of design and delivery.

## Integrating Local Produce and Collaborative Recipe Swaps

A key strategy discussed was the direct incorporation of local produce—such as sunchokes, purple carrots, and quince—into both the curriculum and practical community activities. This integration serves several nutritional and cultural goals:

### Anticipated Nutritional Outcomes

- **Boosting dietary diversity:** Using local fiber- and phytonutrient-rich foods addresses nutritional gaps and supports overall gut and cardiovascular health [5].
- **Promoting increased fruit and vegetable consumption:** By making locally relevant options prominent in programming, the team aims to support chronic disease prevention efforts recommended by the WHO and FAO [1].
- **Sustaining traditional foodways:** Preserving and adapting regional culinary traditions helps strengthen community ties, sustains interest in nutrition programs, and fosters long-term changes in eating patterns [6].

### Community Engagement Tactics

Several methods were outlined to ensure meaningful community engagement throughout:

- **Recipe Swap Forums:** Regular events where participants exchange traditional and contemporary recipes, using local produce as a focal point. These gatherings serve to bridge generations and cultures, deepening community ownership of program goals.
- **Cooking Demonstrations:** Collaborative sessions led by community members and trained nutrition professionals. These demonstrations not only teach cooking skills but also generate practical ideas for healthy, affordable meals.
- **Storytelling Circles:** Facilitated opportunities for community members to share personal food memories and family recipes, anchoring nutrition education in cultural experience.
- **Crowd-sourced Content Development:** Gathering feedback and new ideas from participants during and after sessions to continually refine workshop activities and curriculum materials.

These approaches are strongly supported by the literature, which points to increased program effectiveness, sustainability, and community impact when participatory and culturally tailored methods are employed ([2], [3], [4], [5], [6]).

## Evaluation and Feedback Processes

Sustained progress requires rigorous and inclusive evaluation. The group agreed on the following approach:

**Formative Evaluation:** Curricular materials and workshop layouts will undergo early piloting, with structured participant feedback surveys and group interviews assessing their relevance, accessibility, and educational value.

**Summative Evaluation:** At program milestones (March and June 2025), the team will compare pre- and post-program survey data, analyze recipe adoption rates, and conduct in-depth interviews to document changes in dietary behavior and knowledge.

**Data Sharing:** A secure, shared data dashboard will be set up to store anonymized assessment results, ensuring transparency and supporting continuous improvement among all stakeholders.

**Ongoing Communication and Review:**

- **Monthly Stakeholder Huddles:** Regular virtual or in-person meetings to assess progress, review challenges, and adjust course as needed.
- **Community Advisory Board:** Continued partnership with recognized neighborhood leaders to maximize local relevance and responsiveness.
- **Open Communication Channels:** Dedicated WhatsApp and email groups will streamline updates and quick troubleshooting.
