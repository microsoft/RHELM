2. Agenda Overview

The primary focus of this meeting was a thorough review and collaborative revision of the manuscript, “Dietary Patterns and Cardiometabolic Risk in European Adolescents: A Multi-Center Epidemiologic Study,” intended for submission to the European Journal of Clinical Nutrition. Discussion items included a critical evaluation of the latest manuscript draft (Version 3), addressing reviewer feedback, strengthening both statistical methodology and reference support, and clarifying responsibilities for each section’s remaining edits. The discussion was organized to ensure alignment with project milestones, particularly those laid out in Work Package 2: Data Analysis & Dissemination. This structured approach aims to maintain transparency and scientific integrity in line with institutional guidelines [1].

3. Discussion Points

Manuscript Review and Collaborative Input

Introduction

Dr. Alvarez initiated the review by emphasizing the importance of incorporating the most recent WHO data on adolescent obesity in Europe (WHO Europe, 2023) to demonstrate the current public health burden. The team agreed that contextualizing the study with updated epidemiological statistics will better highlight its relevance. Additionally, participants reaffirmed the need to clarify why the analytical approach prioritizes dietary pattern assessment over single nutrient analysis. The group concurred to incorporate rationale and key references, including Hu (2002) [2], to underline the strengths of dietary pattern analysis.

Methods

Dr. Dubois recommended expanding the section on dietary assessment instruments, particularly validating the use of the Food Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ). She pointed out that referencing the validation literature (Cade et al., 2002) [3] would strengthen credibility. The selection of confounders was discussed in depth, reflecting a consensus to include variables such as parental education and urban versus rural residence in accordance with EURRECA consortium standards. The statistical analysis plan will be enhanced to describe the process for handling missing data in greater detail, specifically by justifying the choice of multiple imputation techniques. The team also agreed that sensitivity analyses detailing the impact of missing data treatments should be made available in supplementary materials.

Results

The team reviewed the current results section and identified the need for a clearer description of the main dietary patterns emerging from the data. To that end, a new figure will be added to display factor loadings, enabling readers to better interpret the composition and characterization of each dietary pattern. Dr. Romero proposed stratification of results by sex, following recent recommendations from the STROBE-nut guidelines [4], to address possible effect modification and further contextualize the findings. During their review, Dr. Dubois noted the presence of an outlier cluster in one cohort and will conduct a detailed analysis to determine how those data impact the study’s results.

Discussion

Building on the strengths of the study, the team agreed to further contextualize the findings by referencing recent systematic reviews and meta-analyses (Schwingshackl et al., 2021) [5], ensuring a comprehensive discussion of how the present findings fit within the broader literature. Further, the team recognized the importance of elaborating on the public health implications of their results, with particular focus on actionable interventions and policy recommendations relevant to European adolescent nutrition.

References

Dr. Alvarez will carry out a final audit of the references section, ensuring consistent formatting, inclusion of all datasets, and full compliance with the requirements of the target journal and the STROBE-nut checklist.

Specific Methodological Decisions

The group decided to conduct additional analyses comparing multiple imputation to single imputation for missing dietary data, to ensure the robustness of the results. It was clarified in discussion that the interpretation of sex-stratified findings must distinguish between confounding and effect modification, following established epidemiological principles.

Agreed Action Items

  • Introduction: Dr. Alvarez will update the introduction to reflect current prevalence estimates and strengthen the rationale for the analytic approach.
  • Methods: Dr. Dubois will provide detailed information on the FFQ validation process, elaborate on confounder selection, and clearly outline the chosen imputation method, including the rationale behind these decisions.
  • Results: Dr. Romero and Dr. Dubois will collaborate to produce the sex-stratified results and develop a clear, informative figure highlighting factor loadings for the identified dietary patterns. Dr. Dubois will also complete the analysis of outliers.
  • Discussion/Conclusion: Dr. Romero will revise the discussion section, drawing connections to recent systematic reviews and emphasizing practical implications for public health intervention and policy.
  • References: Dr. Alvarez will ensure all sources are properly cited, and formatting is consistent and compliant.
  • Supplementary Materials: Dr. Dubois will compile additional analyses, including sensitivity analyses and expanded methodological descriptions.
