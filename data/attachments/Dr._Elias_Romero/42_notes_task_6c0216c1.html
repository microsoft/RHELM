1. Detailed Evaluation of Coffee Samples

This tasting session at Cafetería El Magnífico was organized to identify distinctive single-origin coffees suitable for public engagement events, emphasizing both sensory quality and alignment with nutrition and health considerations. We evaluated three coffees—Ethiopian Yirgacheffe, Guatemalan Antigua, and Colombian Huila—using Specialty Coffee Association (SCA) protocols for consistency and comparability.

Ethiopian Coffee

Attribute Details
Origin Yirgacheffe, Ethiopia; Smallholder farms; Altitude: 1,800–2,100 meters
Roast Profile Light roast (Agtron 65), development time: 20% (first crack + 60 seconds), emphasizing acidity/clarity
Aroma Pronounced floral bouquet—jasmine and bergamot—accented by citrus zest
Flavor Layers of lemongrass, meyer lemon, peach, with green tea undertones
Acidity Exceptionally bright, lively citric acidity with moderate malic depth
Body Elegant, silky, lightly structured—reminiscent of fine tea
Aftertaste Long, clean floral finish with a gentle touch of honey sweetness
SCA Scores Aroma: 8.5

The Ethiopian Yirgacheffe exemplified the clarity and complexity characteristic of high-altitude washed coffees from this famed region. Its vibrant aromatics and lively acidity made a strong impression from the outset.

Guatemalan Coffee

Attribute Details
Origin Antigua, Guatemala; Finca La Pastoria; Altitude: 1,550–1,800 meters
Roast Profile Medium roast (Agtron 55), development time: 22% (first crack + 80 seconds), focused on sweetness/complexity
Aroma Cocoa, toasted almonds, caramel, subtle floral hints
Flavor Rich notes of baker’s chocolate and brown sugar, ripe plum, and touch of cinnamon
Acidity Balanced, medium-bright, predominantly tartaric in nature
Body Creamy, rounded, medium weight
Aftertaste Sweet chocolate lingering finish, gentle almond
SCA Scores Aroma: 8.0

The Guatemalan selection offered crowd-pleasing balance, with a harmonious interplay of chocolate, almond, and caramel. It was exceptionally easy to drink and felt universally accessible.

Colombian Coffee

Attribute Details
Origin Huila, Colombia; Cooperative lot, smallholder farms; Altitude: 1,600–1,900 meters
Roast Profile Medium-dark roast (Agtron 48), development time: 25% (first crack + 120 seconds), yielding deeper caramelization
Aroma Roasted nuts, gentle molasses, red apple
Flavor Dark chocolate, panela, hints of red apple, subtle spice
Acidity Mellow, rounded, primarily malic in character
Body Syrupy, pronounced richness
Aftertaste Long notes of cocoa, sustained sweetness, mild dry finish
SCA Scores Aroma: 7.8

The Colombian coffee brought forward a robust, heavier body and darker flavor spectrum. While it had comforting depth, it didn’t stand out in terms of aromatics or dynamic acidity compared to the other two.

2. Comparative Analysis and Rationale for Selection

Sensory Distinctions and SCA Insights

During the tasting, the Ethiopian Yirgacheffe emerged as the clear leader. Its layered floral and fruit notes, tactile lightness, and sparkling acidity set it apart—appealing to those who value originality and nuance in specialty coffee. The SCA score of 87 reflects its excellence and suitability for education-oriented tastings that aim to showcase the full sensory range specialty coffee can offer [1].

Guatemalan Antigua’s impressive balance, chocolatey comfort, and broad appeal (SCA: 84) made it an excellent option when seeking a more approachable cup. Its sweet, nutty flavors exemplify why Antigua remains a go-to for those who prefer traditional profiles with a touch of complexity.

On the other hand, the Colombian Huila, while rich and satisfying for fans of fuller-bodied coffees, felt less distinctive. The more muted acidity and familiar flavor notes, reflected in the overall score of 82, suggested it would not best showcase the concept of terroir to a diverse audience.

Criteria for Final Coffee Choices

Primary Selection: Ethiopian Yirgacheffe
I chose this coffee for its complexity, transparency of origin, and high sensory quality. Its expressive nature sparks conversation, making it especially valuable for engaging event participants and illustrating the diversity found in specialty coffee. This choice is supported by current best practices in coffee education, which favor highly individual, origin-driven profiles for public tastings [1].

Backup Selection: Guatemalan Antigua
To complement the Ethiopian, I selected the Guatemalan as a reliable, versatile “backup”. Its classic chocolate and nutty notes are comforting and easily appreciated, particularly by those less familiar with more acidic or floral coffees. This broader accessibility ensures that all participants, regardless of prior coffee experience, can find something enjoyable [2].

Suitability for Different Audiences

By offering both the Ethiopian and the Guatemalan, I created a tasting lineup suited to a mixed public—enthusiasts seeking complexity will appreciate Yirgacheffe’s clarity and vibrancy, while those looking for comfort and familiarity can gravitate toward the Antigua. Emerging research also points to the potential for higher-perceived acidity and aroma complexity to support mindful, moderate consumption—making the Ethiopian an interesting option for discussions around nutrition and holistic eating habits [3].

Professional Frameworks and Literature Alignment

Throughout our analysis, we followed SCA cupping protocols, ensuring objectivity and replicable results. Each coffee was scored across all major SCA categories, and all assessments were made in alignment with the latest research on sensory characteristics influenced by origin, altitude, and roasting [1][4].

3. Food Pairing: Development and Justification

Selected Pairing: Fig and Almond Biscotti

To complement both coffees, I developed a homemade fig and almond biscotti. This recipe was inspired by family baking traditions, with several iterations to achieve the right balance of flavor and nutrition.

Recipe Development Process

I wanted a pairing that would both complement the coffees and align with public health principles. I minimized added sugars and used whole grain flour, dried figs, and whole almonds—making the biscotti a source of fiber, protein, and beneficial fats. The moderate sweetness and toasty almond aroma harmonized beautifully with both coffees: the honeyed notes of the Ethiopian and the nutty-chocolatey profile of the Guatemalan.

Sensory, Nutritional, and Cultural Fit

Sensory science shows that shared aromatic compounds enhance food-beverage pairings [5]. The figs and almonds created a bridge between the coffees' flavor notes, while textural contrast and restrained sweetness helped balance acidity. The biscotti’s Mediterranean roots also felt contextually fitting, serving as an homage to café culture and the dietary heritage I seek to promote. Adapting the traditional recipe also allowed me to tie in Mediterranean diet principles, which support public health [6].

4. Recommendations for Future Coffee Tastings

To further strengthen public tastings and ensure research integrity, I propose several refinements:

  • Sample Documentation: Ensure complete traceability and documentation for each coffee (farm, altitude, processing), preserving transparency and reproducibility.
  • Roast Metrics: Record and report objective roast parameters, such as Agtron values and development ratios, to facilitate accurate comparison between lots.
  • Blind Tasting Procedures: Incorporate blind or semi-blind tastings to reduce bias and validate findings.
  • Panel Calibration: Calibrate all tasters with SCA reference standards to ensure reliable, cohesive scoring.
  • Comprehensive Records: Retain detailed SCA scoring sheets and digital records to enable future audit or review.

In addition, integrating audience demographic data and preferences can further tailor food and beverage choices. I recommend developing educational materials for participants, including background on coffee origins, tasting methods, and insights on how pairing choices support nutrition and health.

5. Conclusion

This tasting session at Cafetería El Magnífico fulfilled its dual goals: selecting exceptional coffees to engage and educate, and constructing a food pairing experience grounded in both culinary tradition and sound nutrition. By adhering to SCA protocols and drawing on the latest sensory science and public health literature, I created a tasting lineup that is both distinctive and widely accessible. Going forward, this framework will guide further events that blend sensory education with health-positive food practices.
