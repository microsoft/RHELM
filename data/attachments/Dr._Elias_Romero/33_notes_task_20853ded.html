Additional Data Collection Prompts

During the visit, I will pay special attention to the following:

  • Product Variants: I will record and photograph all available varieties or cultivars for each main item, and note any differences in branding or packaging.
  • Availability & Price: Presence or absence of specific items will be documented, along with price per kilogram/unit, to support future assessments of food access and affordability.
  • Vendor Interactions: Brief descriptions of each interaction with vendors—including their engagement, product knowledge, and transparency regarding sourcing—will be noted. I will also observe and record shopper behaviors, such as purchase patterns or questions asked.
  • Market Environment: General conditions, such as stall density, product range, crowding, visibility of food marketing, and public health infrastructure (e.g., handwashing points), will be described to contextualize findings.
  • Unavailable Items: Any planned items not observed or procured will be listed, along with possible reasons, such as seasonal gaps or supply issues.

Rationale and Structure

Justification for Categorization

Organizing items by food group enables targeted assessment of dietary diversity and supports robust, standardized data collection. By recording specific varieties and sources, I can analyze the diversity in food supply—a key need in nutritional epidemiology.[1][2] Detailed fields for quantity and intended use create a record that is directly linkable to recipe testing and dietary intake models. Prompting for observational notes enriches qualitative analysis of consumer choice and market dynamics.[3][4]

Data Integration and Adaptability

By maintaining consistent terminology and standardized units, all collected data remain compatible with international research protocols and can be readily integrated into food composition or survey databases. The annotation-focused format allows me to efficiently capture nuanced field notes, and supports the subsequent coding and extraction of both quantitative and qualitative insights.

Alignment with Broader Research Objectives

This approach allows for:

  • Mapping of the seasonal and cultural diversity of local food environments
  • Direct observation of factors influencing consumer choice, including pricing and presentation
  • Identification of practical or structural barriers to accessing healthy foods
  • Baseline data collection for potential future nutritional interventions or trials centered on food environment modification
