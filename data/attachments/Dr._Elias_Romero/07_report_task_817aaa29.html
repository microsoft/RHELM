1. Meeting Purpose and Agenda Overview

The purpose of today’s meeting was to review the current status of the Nutrition Labeling Study and align on next steps for ongoing research efforts. Key agenda items included reviewing the project objectives and progress, discussing updates to study methodology, refining the data analysis plan, addressing issues encountered in preliminary data, revising the project timeline, and organizing coordination with external partners. Open questions and action items were also discussed, with a focus on ensuring clarity of responsibilities and upcoming deliverables.

2. Key Topics and Decisions

Review of Study Objectives

We reaffirmed that the central aim of the study remains the evaluation of the impact of front-of-pack (FOP) nutrition labeling on consumer purchasing decisions. This objective continues to hold significant relevance in light of current European Union and World Health Organization policy discussions on food labeling. Dr. Romero referenced recent EFSA and WHO communications, which emphasize the policy importance of FOP labeling and support the continued priority of our research. Dr. Romero will circulate a concise summary of these objectives and a literature synthesis for the team.

Methodological Updates

Progress on data collection was reviewed. Thus far, the supermarket audit and consumer survey have both commenced, but the pace of participant recruitment for the survey has been somewhat slower than projected. A balanced sampling strategy targeting various age groups and socioeconomic strata has been implemented, and continues to be a priority for representativeness. To address the lag in responses, we agreed to extend the survey period by one week and introduce a structured reminder protocol, as consistent with literature showing modest increases in response rates with follow-up reminders. Marta will lead adjustments to the survey schedule and coordinate reminder outreach.

Analysis Plan and Data Validation

The data cleaning process is proceeding on schedule. However, preliminary review revealed that approximately 10% of responses are missing demographic details. After considering recent meta-analyses on handling missing data, the team agreed on conducting a comprehensive sensitivity analysis to determine if these missing values could bias results. Dr. Romero will draft this expanded sensitivity analysis plan, and Marta will work to identify underlying sources of missing data.

Issues in Preliminary Data

Initial examination of frequency data identified several outlier responses, suggesting some respondents may have misunderstood survey question Q5. Literature in survey methodology indicates this is a common issue and highlights the importance of precise question wording. It was agreed that Q5 will be rephrased in subsequent survey waves to enhance clarity, and additional robustness checks will be introduced in the analysis script to assess the impact of these anomalies. Marta will prepare the revised question wording, and Dr. Romero will integrate these checks.

Timeline Revisions and Upcoming Deliverables

Due to slower-than-anticipated recruitment, the project timeline has been adjusted. The interim report, originally planned for earlier in March, will now be due by March 22, 2024. All subsequent deadlines will be updated accordingly. Dr. Romero will recirculate the revised Gantt chart, and Marta will notify all partners of the timeline changes.

Coordination with Dr. Sophie Dubois (Data Harmonization)

Ongoing collaboration with Dr. Dubois regarding harmonization of European labeling datasets was discussed. Dr. Dubois has offered to share the structure of her dataset to facilitate this process. A dedicated meeting, tentatively set for March 8, 2024, will focus on the technical aspects of dataset integration and alignment. Marta will confirm the date with Dr. Dubois and send a calendar invitation to attendees.

Open Questions and Next Steps

The team recognized the necessity of cross-validating data from French and Spanish sub-cohorts but noted that access to some external datasets remains pending. Additionally, given the cross-national nature of the project, there is a need for explicit guidance from the ethics board regarding protocols for international data sharing. Dr. Romero will contact the university’s ethics board for advice, and Marta will follow up with the French data custodian to clarify access status.
