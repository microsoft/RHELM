2. Key Discussion Points

2.1 Grant Application Outcome and Initial Feedback

The first round submission for the cross-European food environment study grant was not successful. While the reviewers acknowledged the relevance and potential impact of the study, the main concerns centered on the harmonization of methodologies across countries and the clarity of data collection procedures. The funding body emphasized their interest in the research aims and explicitly encouraged a revised submission, inviting improvements in specific areas.

2.2 Reviewer Feedback: Themes and Implications

a. Methodological Consistency
Reviewers highlighted the necessity for a detailed, standardized approach to data collection and analysis that would be applied uniformly by all project partners across different countries. Without this, replicability and comparability of results would be compromised. To address these points, we agreed on the importance of jointly developing a comprehensive Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) manual and creating multilingual data collection toolkits.

b. Feasibility and Coordination
Reviewers questioned the proposed coordination framework, noting the complexity of synchronizing activities among international teams. Specific concerns included management of language differences, maintaining data quality, and ensuring regular, effective communication. The need for visible mechanisms around quality control and clarity in task delegation was also stressed.

We discussed appointing dedicated country coordinators, each tasked with protocol adherence, local oversight, and structured reporting responsibilities, to foster accountability and consistent communication.

c. Impact and Innovation
The feedback called for a clearer articulation of the project’s unique contribution to the field and a well-defined strategy for translating findings into actionable policy recommendations. There was also an expectation for strong engagement with policy-makers and stakeholders, and evidence that the project’s design would enable real impact at the European level.

To meet these expectations, we decided to strengthen the narrative around policy relevance, concrete implementation pathways, and to provide examples of intended stakeholder activities. Planning an initial inter-country pilot phase will serve to validate feasibility and build credibility with reviewers.

2.3 Research Coordination Challenges

We acknowledged several complex coordination challenges inherent to cross-national research:

  • Regulatory Variations: Different interpretations of GDPR and local research governance necessitate clear guidelines for compliance.
  • Logistical Barriers: Diverse training backgrounds and data collection practices could introduce inconsistency; standardized materials and training modules will be essential.
  • Communication: Ensuring regular, structured interactions between country teams is critical. We committed to establishing both a shared digital workspace and a project management dashboard to consolidate progress tracking and facilitate collaboration.

2.4 Strategic Adjustments for Resubmission

In response to feedback and discussion, we agreed upon several key strategic enhancements to the revised application:

  • Unified SOP Manual and Multilingual Toolkits: To be developed and circulated for comment and adoption by all participating teams.
  • Appointment of Country Coordinators: Each country to have a lead contact responsible for adherence to protocols, reporting, and quality assurance.
  • Refined Work Package Descriptions: Detailed definitions of roles, deliverables, and timeframes for each work package to add specificity for reviewers.
  • Inclusion of an Inter-Country Pilot Phase: This pilot will be used to fine-tune harmonized protocols and demonstrate feasibility and adaptability across different settings.

2.5 Detailed Next Step Planning

To maintain momentum, we agreed on a clear sequence of priority actions:

  • Establishment of a real-time project management system to track deliverables and document progress.
  • Sequential drafting of structured responses to the main points raised by reviewers, with iterative review by both principal investigators ahead of the resubmission.
  • Rewriting and strengthening of impact narratives, emphasizing the project’s relevance to European public health and policy.

5. Summary and Outlook

This meeting provided a structured and productive forum for analyzing our grant application outcome, clarifying key areas highlighted by reviewers, and collectively defining strategies to strengthen both the application and project coordination. By implementing harmonized protocols, strengthening team structures, and adopting robust project management tools, we are well positioned to address the funding body’s concerns and demonstrate greater research integration and impact in our resubmission. Aligning our approach with best practices in international research collaboration will further enhance our competitiveness and capacity for meaningful cross-European health research.
