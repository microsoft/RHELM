## Executive Summary

The cross-European public health nutrition research initiative has now entered a crucial phase, marked by strong collaboration across multidisciplinary teams and partner institutions throughout the continent. Over recent months, the project has advanced in several key areas: the systematic digitization of research notes, the adoption of unified data quality protocols, and the integration of new epidemiological data from a broad array of European population cohorts. Notably, we have achieved consensus on harmonized data capture methods, developed interoperable data-sharing platforms, and begun sharing initial insights regarding nutritional disparities between regions.

Despite these advances, the project continues to experience ongoing challenges. These are largely associated with adjusting timelines due to the remote working arrangements, occasional issues with data integrity resulting from the digitization process, and the need to maintain consistent momentum within geographically distributed teams. To address these obstacles, we have refined our communication strategies, invested in more robust quality assurance protocols, and increased training for team members involved in data curation. Sustained attention to agile management frameworks and enhanced coordination between sites will remain essential to keeping the project on track as we enter the forthcoming phases.

## Recent Activities

### Digitization of Research Notes

Over the past quarter, efforts to digitize historical research notes have intensified. We implemented a hybrid workflow, leveraging **ABBYY FineReader** for reliable OCR scanning, followed by structured entry into the **REDCap electronic data capture system**. Teams collaborated using **Microsoft Teams** for synchronous discussions and document annotation, **SharePoint** for secure document storage and version control, and **Jupyter Notebooks** for standardized analysis procedures.

To ensure data integrity throughout the digitization process, we introduced several overlapping quality control steps:

- **Dual Data Entry:** Two independent team members entered each record, enabling cross-verification and minimizing entry errors.
- **Data Validation in REDCap:** Built-in rules automatically flagged inconsistencies or missing values.
- **Systematic Audits:** Data management leads conducted weekly audits examining 10% of recently digitized records, with immediate follow-up on any discrepancies or anomalies detected.

During this process, several issues emerged. Approximately 4% of handwritten notes could not be accurately read by OCR due to language variations and inconsistent note formats, necessitating manual review and data entry. On one occasion, unsynchronized data resulted in the temporary loss of some annotations—a problem that led us to strengthen our backup and versioning systems. Additionally, compatibility issues between legacy datasets and updated schema formats required close manual mapping, especially for records collected prior to the adoption of standard REDCap conventions.

### Project Timeline and Workflow Management

The transition to remote work, and subsequent increases in work-from-home disruptions, was documented among 38% of our team members and led to delays in both digitization and data harmonization. In response, we introduced several modifications to our workflow:

- **Flexible Scheduling:** We established flexible working hours and staggered team sprints, allowing teams to better accommodate personal circumstances and maintain productivity.
- **Routine Communications:** Biweekly stand-up meetings were introduced to quickly identify and resolve bottlenecks.
- **Regional Support Clusters:** Teams of technical and research leads at each participating site acted as first responders to technical and logistical issues.

These adjustments have proved effective; despite initial setbacks, project progress is now nearly back on track, lagging only two weeks behind the original schedule.

## Key Findings and Insights

### Major Epidemiological Insights

As data integration has proceeded, several important patterns are now evident:

- **Regional Dietary Inequalities:** Analysis reveals notable differences in fruit and vegetable intake between Western and Eastern European cohorts. Western cohorts report higher median intake, with socioeconomic status strongly influencing these patterns (p < 0.01, multilevel logistic regression). These disparities highlight the persistent nutritional gap associated with geography and income.
  
- **Micronutrient Deficiencies among Youth:** Vitamin D and iron deficiencies remain prevalent among adolescents in Northern and Eastern Europe. Despite access to supplements, 18–24% of participants in these regions showed deficiencies, pointing to ongoing challenges in addressing micronutrient shortfalls through supplementation alone.
  
- **Growing Processed Food Consumption:** There is a clear upward trend in consumption of ultra-processed foods among urban adults aged 18–35. This dietary shift is associated with higher BMI and increased risk markers for metabolic syndrome (OR=1.23, 95% CI: 1.07–1.41), signaling an emerging area of concern for public health interventions.
  
- **Lasting Impact of COVID-19:** Dietary data collected post-pandemic indicate an increase in consumption of unhealthy snacks and sugar-sweetened beverages, particularly during lockdown periods. These habits have not entirely reverted, especially within lower-income groups, emphasizing the need for targeted recovery strategies.
  
- **Policy and Local Interventions:** Early evidence suggests that regions implementing school-based nutrition programs, especially in Scandinavia, have seen positive effects on reducing micronutrient deficiencies among children. These findings support the value of local policy measures in improving population nutrition.

### Enhancements in Data Quality and Methodology

Efforts to improve data collection and workflow standardization have resulted in measurable gains:

- **Comprehensiveness of Dietary Recall:** The use of remote interview validation has increased both the completeness and accuracy of dietary recall data, reducing underreporting and recall bias.
- **Participant Follow-Up:** New digital follow-up protocols have improved the retention and tracking of high-mobility participants, helping to minimize data loss over time.
- **Error Rate Reduction:** The introduction of dual data entry has significantly decreased data entry errors, enhancing overall dataset reliability and supporting more robust analyses.
