## 3. Summary of Discussions

### Introduction and Purpose

The meeting aimed to further collaborative efforts between academic researchers and community organizations working to improve public health nutrition throughout Barcelona’s neighborhoods. Participants focused on evaluating the current state of interventions, tackling obstacles limiting community impact, and planning next steps for greater inclusion and effectiveness.

### Updates on Nutrition Initiatives

Ana Gómez provided a comprehensive update on ongoing public health nutrition programs, with a particular focus on school-based meal initiatives targeting low-income families. She noted positive developments such as higher participation rates in healthy meal programs and notable improvements in parent involvement. However, Ana emphasized persistent challenges: some meal offerings failed to reflect the cultural backgrounds of participating students, reducing program resonance among certain communities. Ana advocated for more tailored menu planning and engaged parental feedback to better address these issues.

### Emerging Challenges and Community Engagement

The group discussed several pressing challenges. Language barriers continued to hinder participation in nutrition programs among non-native Spanish and Catalan speakers. The limited availability of affordable fresh foods, especially in rapidly changing neighborhoods, was highlighted as a structural barrier complicating nutrition outreach. Additionally, a notable rise in the consumption of ultra-processed foods among youth, particularly in marginalized districts, was seen as an urgent area for intervention. Sergio Torres stressed that current outreach strategies often missed minority groups, underscoring the need for more inclusive recruitment and representation.

### Collaborative Strategies and Best Practices

Dr. Romero reviewed current international research demonstrating the benefits of participatory community engagement, citing both systematic reviews and relevant pilot studies. The group explored the potential of a community health ambassador model, enlisting respected neighborhood residents to act as liaisons and program advocates. Participants agreed that leveraging local leadership could help overcome cultural and linguistic divides, facilitate trust, and tailor communication effectively. The team decided to move forward with a pilot project, committing to the rapid co-development of culturally relevant training modules for these ambassadors.

### Evidence Review and Data Sharing

Carlos Mendez presented preliminary outcome data from education-based nutrition campaigns. Results revealed some positive impacts—such as increased awareness about healthy eating—but progress remained limited by low response rates and small sample sizes in key districts. There was consensus on the need to improve dietary data collection tools, especially to ensure questionnaires were available in multiple languages. Carlos proposed updating and piloting new survey instruments to more accurately capture dietary intake across Barcelona’s diverse population. Group members agreed, noting that better data would directly inform intervention refinement.

### Technical Issues and Communication

Several technical obstacles affected the meeting. Approximately 15 minutes were lost to internet disruptions and background noise, with Ana Gómez's presentation and open discussion particularly affected. Participants attempted to resolve the problems by switching devices and improving microphone etiquette, which partially mitigated issues for the remainder of the session.

Documentation and real-time communication also posed challenges. The absence of a shared online note-taking tool led to some confusion during assignment of action items, evidenced by duplicated suggestions and delays in confirming responsibilities. In addition, integrating datasets from university and community partners presented ongoing technical difficulties. Incompatible file formats and delayed uploads complicated efforts to build a unified database for program evaluation.

### Decisions and Immediate Actions

- **Community Health Ambassador Pilot:** The team unanimously agreed to launch a pilot ambassador model in selected neighborhoods. Development of relevant training materials was prioritized, with the decision contingent on compiling feedback and outcome data after initial implementation.
- **Revision of Dietary Assessment Methods:** A proposal to update dietary assessment protocols has been temporarily deferred, pending the localization and translation of survey instruments and additional consultation with local school partners.

## 5. Outstanding Issues and Next Steps

### Unresolved Questions

- Is the community health ambassador model effective in reaching marginalized groups and improving nutrition outcomes? Outcome data from the pilot will be assessed.
- Which updated dietary assessment tools provide the most reliable and culturally relevant information for our target populations, and are they scalable across all participating organizations?
- What technical or resource investments are required to achieve seamless, sustainable integration of university and community data systems?
- How can future strategies maximize genuine leadership and input from underrepresented groups to ensure long-term impact?

### Immediate Priorities

- Collect and analyze data from the new ambassador pilot and integrate minority group feedback into the next round of interventions.
- Continue refining communication protocols and technical processes, ensuring all partners have access to reliable collaboration tools and shared documentation platforms.
- Engage IT and data management expertise, with an emphasis on developing a standardized approach to data-sharing, privacy, and reporting.
- Prepare an interim progress and challenges report for submission to funding agencies, ensuring that ongoing innovation and areas of need are clearly documented.
