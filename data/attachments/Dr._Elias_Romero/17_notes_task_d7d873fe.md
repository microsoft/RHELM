## Introduction: Integrating Meal Prep into Everyday Public Health Practice

For me, weekly meal preparation isn’t just a domestic routine—it’s a living example of my belief in public health nutrition principles: eating sustainably, enjoying a varied diet, and making the most of my time. As a researcher with an unpredictable schedule, planning meals in advance has become essential. It relieves daily stress around food choices, ensures that I’m eating well through busy weeks, and aligns directly with my professional values.

When I batch cook, I’m intentional about every step. I gravitate towards fresh, plant-based foods—always looking for what’s local and in season. Over the years, I’ve learned that these choices not only reduce my household’s ecological footprint, but also bring more flavor and diversity to my meals. Sharing lunches with Carla twice a week, I’m reminded of the importance of food as a connector. These shared moments give me a sense of community and balance—reminding me that nutrition is as much about social health as biochemistry.

Preparing the week's meals in advance lets me focus on my research, teaching, and family with one less worry. I always look for ways to streamline the kitchen workflow, to make sure the food I prepare stays safe, fresh, and nutritionally robust right through to the end of the week.

## Prepared Dishes: Storage, Safety, and Nutrition at Home

To keep things simple yet balanced, I plan around three main dishes for the week:
- **Chickpea Stew**  
- **Roasted Mediterranean Vegetables**  
- **Quinoa Salad**

Each recipe yields enough portions for the week (and a few to share), fitting neatly into my schedule and fridge. Here’s a practical overview of how I manage storage and maintain nutritional quality:

| Dish                        | Portions | Storage Guidance                                                    | Tips for Best Quality                                                                  |
|-----------------------------|----------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Chickpea Stew**           | 6        | Fridge: glass airtight containers at 4°C, up to 4 days; <br>Freezer: glass/freezer-safe containers, up to 3 months | Let stew cool completely before sealing. Avoid overfilling containers for even cooling. When reheating, I always use a thermometer to reach 74°C—safeguarding flavor and preserving heat-sensitive nutrients like B vitamins.[4] |
| **Roasted Mediterranean Vegetables** | 6 | Fridge: glass airtight container at 4°C, up to 3 days. Freezing not recommended (texture loss) | Roasting actually protects many antioxidants in vegetables. I store the roasted veg in glass for best taste, and often drizzle my olive oil on fresh after reheating, which helps absorb phytonutrients.[5] |
| **Quinoa Salad**            | 6        | Fridge: glass airtight containers at 4°C, up to 4 days.<br>I keep the dressing aside until serving | To avoid a soggy salad, I add vinaigrette right before eating. Glass containers especially help retain flavors and vitamins, which can otherwise degrade with acidic dressings.[6] |
| **Shared Portions for Carla** | 2 per main dish | Small separate glass containers, packed fresh after cooking | I always portion Carla’s meals before full cooling. It’s a small extra step but keeps everything safe and prevents cross-contamination from the start. |

*On containers*: I’ve moved fully to glass for meal storage, after years of reading about nutrient loss and concerns over plastics. Especially with acidic foods and regular reheating, glass keeps flavors and nutrients intact, and is much more durable.[7]

## Batch Cooking: My Approach to Efficiency and Enjoyment

Sunday is my main batch cooking day—a few focused hours transform my kitchen and my week.

Here’s how I keep things organized, efficient, and enjoyable:

**My Own Kitchen Workflow**
- *Evening Before*: I start by soaking chickpeas the night before—this simple act already puts me in the mindset for the week ahead.
- *Chopping*: I chop all the vegetables at once, setting out my ingredients in a “mise en place”—a lesson from my research days that always pays off in time saved and less stress.
- *Roasting*: While the oven works its magic on the vegetables (always on a single baking tray to save energy), I simmer the chickpea stew and cook quinoa on the stovetop.
- *Multi-tasking*: I take advantage of idle time (while things roast or simmer) to prep the salad dressing, label containers, and tidy up.
- *Portioning*: As soon as the hot dishes come off the heat, I allocate Carla’s and my packed lunches directly into their containers, sealing them before they cool to limit contamination.
- *Cleanup*: I make it a point to clean as I go; it feels more manageable and keeps the kitchen inviting.

**Essential Tools I Rely On**
- A large, heavy-bottomed pot (for hearty stews)
- Generous-sized baking tray for vegetables
- Fine mesh colander (rinsing is crucial for quinoa)
- A well-sharpened chef’s knife and sturdy cutting board
- Multiple glass containers with snug lids
- Digital thermometer (for safe reheating—no compromises)
- Kitchen scale (helpful for dividing portions for busy days)
- Salad spinner, especially during the leafy green-heavy months

**Adapting to Real Life**
Life is rarely without interruptions. If I get pulled away mid-prep (say, by Nina needing help with homework), I pause the process at safe points—after chopping or before sautéing—using written checklists or timer alarms so I can step back in easily. This mirrors the batch processing style we use in the lab: label everything, track storage times, and keep a tidy, organized workstation.

## Principles in Practice: Sustainability, Diversity, and Social Well-being

Reflecting on my routines, I recognize how they embody key public health nutrition concepts:

- **Sustainability**: Nearly everything I cook is plant-based, sourced locally or regionally, and chosen with planetary health in mind. These choices directly support resilient food systems.[1]
- **Diversity**: I craft recipes that rotate grains, legumes, and vegetables across the week. This helps to cover a broad array of nutrients that support energy, satiety, and overall health.[2]
- **Safe Storage**: Meticulous about time and temperature, I only prepare what can be safely stored, always label containers, and monitor fridge temps. Evidence-based home storage keeps my meals both safe and flavorful.[4][5][6][7]
- **Social Connection**: Setting aside portions for Carla creates genuine moments of sharing in otherwise hectic days. Social meals are a cornerstone of personal and population-level well-being.[3]
- **Routine as Self-care**: I apply research-minded habits to home life—routine, checklists, and resource planning—because they nurture both my work and my health. This blend of professional and personal keeps me anchored during demanding weeks.[8]
