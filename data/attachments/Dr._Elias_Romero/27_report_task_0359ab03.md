## 3. Summary of Major Changes

**Clarification of Definitions and Population Criteria**:  
To ensure consistency and replicability, the term “urban adults” is now precisely defined according to the United Nations’ urbanization criteria. Eligibility is based on both residency duration and adherence to administrative and infrastructure benchmarks.

**Expanded Methodological Transparency**:  
The choice of dietary assessment instrument is now clearly justified with reference to its prior validation and suitability for the population under study. Confounder selection is described in detail, and the manuscript lays out model-building strategies, adjustments, and diagnostics according to current epidemiological standards.

**Enhanced Reporting of Results**:  
Main results tables and figures now include fully adjusted effect estimates, 95% confidence intervals, and exact p-values, improving the interpretability and transparency of findings.

**Comprehensive Discussion of Biases and Limitations**:  
The manuscript gives a nuanced account of measurement error sources—including potential recall and social desirability biases—and describes how these might have affected observed associations. The cross-sectional design’s inherent limitations are addressed, particularly concerning temporality and possible residual confounding.

**Contextualization of Findings**:  
A dedicated section now compares this study’s findings with those of leading multi-country cohorts and meta-analyses, explicitly noting variations and consistencies that inform the broader evidence base on dietary patterns and chronic disease risk.

**Supplements and Data Transparency**:  
To promote reproducibility, the complete FFQ used in data collection is now provided as a supplementary appendix. Procedures for handling missing data have been clarified, and all tables and figures offer detailed, clear legends.

**Strengthened External Validity**:  
The revisions clarify the sampling approach and benchmark study participants against broader population statistics, allowing for a more accurate assessment of the study's generalizability.

Collectively, these changes substantially improve the manuscript’s scientific rigor, clarity, and value to readers in public health nutrition.
