## Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive account of the revisions made to the EUPHA symposium response paper focusing on public health nutrition and nutritional epidemiology across Europe. The revision process was guided by thoughtful and detailed feedback from Dr. Ingrid Keller, whose expertise contributed significantly to strengthening the submission. I addressed several core themes: modernizing dietary assessment approaches, improving statistical and methodological rigor, expanding relevance to current European challenges, and aligning every section with the most up-to-date standards.

Key improvements include:

- Incorporation of advanced dietary assessment methodologies, including digital tools and biomarker validation, within the context of large European cohorts.
- A more robust and transparent approach to statistical analyses, with comprehensive strategies for managing confounders and correcting for misreporting.
- Richer contextualization reflecting the evolving food system, including regional dietary transitions, post-pandemic concerns, and policy implications for diverse European populations.
- Expanded and updated evidence base, fully synchronized with the latest peer-reviewed literature and current reporting standards (STROBE-nut, EFSA).
- Enhanced mapping of reviewer feedback to concrete changes, supported by clear justifications and full transparency via a detailed change log.

Below, I provide a section-wise breakdown of all major modifications to the submission, accompanied by an evidence mapping table and an appended, timestamped record of tracked changes.

## Section-wise Breakdown of Revisions

### 1. Introduction

The revised introduction now situates the research firmly within the current European context, highlighting new public health nutrition challenges that have emerged or intensified in the aftermath of COVID-19. I addressed the increasing relevance of food security, pervasive social disparities, and profound shifts in dietary patterns across both Western and Eastern regions of Europe. This section incorporates up-to-date findings from EUPHA and WHO/Europe reports (2022–2024) and details the urgent need for harmonized dietary surveillance, especially considering the double burden of malnutrition and the rising tide of chronic diseases such as obesity and type 2 diabetes. By setting out these challenges at the outset, I aimed to underline the critical public health relevance of improved nutritional epidemiology in current European settings [1,2].

**Transition:**  
These developments in the nutrition landscape provided a clear rationale for strengthening methodological and analytical approaches in the remainder of the study.

---

### 2. Methodology

Responding to concerns regarding outdated assessment techniques, I introduced a more comprehensive, multi-modal dietary assessment strategy. This now includes structured 24-hour recalls, validated digital dietary tools (such as mobile food diary apps), and conventional food diaries, all complemented by objective biomarkers for greater validity. For instance, urinary nitrogen was used to verify protein intake, and doubly labeled water techniques provided robust benchmarks for energy intake.

To improve transparency, I detailed all procedures in full accordance with the latest STROBE-nut guidelines. To minimize bias from misreporting, the analysis employed energy intake:basal metabolic rate (EI:BMR) ratios, with specific exclusion criteria for implausible reporters. The revised analytical plan now delineates clear strategies for adjustment for key confounders, including socioeconomic status and urban–rural stratification. Sensitivity analyses were pre-specified and applied to further test the robustness of findings.

To provide clarity in participant flow, I included a flowchart detailing every stage of recruitment, retention, and data completion. These enhancements not only address all reviewer concerns about data quality and bias but also reinforce the reliability of study findings through robust, transparent procedures [3,4,5].

---

### 3. Results

The results section underwent substantial revision to improve granularity and allow for more meaningful interpretation. I reorganized tables to present stratified data across age groups, sexes, socioeconomic status, and migrant populations. Where previous analyses simply aggregated data, this approach now brings forward differences and trends relevant to health equity. I also added specific sub-analyses focusing on vulnerable groups, such as migrants and residents of lower-income regions, which are often overlooked.

All main results were tested with sensitivity analyses—excluding suspected under- or over-reporters—allowing readers to see how findings hold up under various assumptions. I further enhanced the visualization of key trends by including up-to-date figures comparing guide adherence rates to the latest WHO-European dietary recommendations from 2023. These additions make the results both more accessible and more relevant for policymakers planning targeted interventions [2,6].

**Transition:**  
A richer results section naturally leads into a more nuanced discussion of the implications for practice and policy.

---

### 4. Discussion

The discussion now offers a comprehensive synthesis, contextualizing findings within ongoing transitions in European food systems and dietary habits. In this revision, I examined the interplay between healthy diet promotion and sustainable food system reform in the region, and drew clear lines to current health and policy initiatives.

I provided a thorough review of methodological strengths and limitations, including the cross-sectional framework, potential recall bias, and constraints linked to the availability and cost of biomarker measurements. Policy recommendations have been deepened to reflect recent EUPHA and WHO-Europe guidance documents, focusing on priorities for surveillance, digital tool implementation, and harmonized approaches. This section also explores the scalability of digital dietary assessment platforms and their potential for integration across national borders.

Throughout, I emphasized actionable insights for policy and practice, supported by evidence and practical considerations for diverse European contexts. The discussion concludes by recognizing remaining research gaps and outlining the next steps for advancing the field, from standardizing protocols to fostering greater collaboration [7,8].

---

### 5. Conclusion

In rewriting the conclusion, I aimed for clarity, directness, and actionable emphasis. I distilled the study’s implications into clear recommendations for integrating modernized dietary assessments into routine research and surveillance frameworks across Europe. The conclusion calls for strengthened collaboration among countries, harmonized research tools, and greater data-sharing to accelerate progress in addressing regional disparities and promoting health equity. In doing so, I sought to underscore the value and urgency of these advances for both researchers and policy-makers.
