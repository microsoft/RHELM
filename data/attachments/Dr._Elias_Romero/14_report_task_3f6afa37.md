## Executive Summary

The Cross-European Food Environment Study is a collaborative effort to better understand how the characteristics of local food environments across Europe affect dietary habits, health outcomes, and social inequalities. By integrating harmonized data collection and analysis across diverse European countries, this project will examine the relationships between the built and commercial food environment, individual dietary behaviors, and the development of non-communicable diseases (NCDs). These insights respond to urgent policy priorities cited in the European Green Deal and the Farm to Fork Strategy, supporting action on food system transformation at local, national, and European levels.

### Project Aims

- **Characterize and compare** food environments in both urban and rural areas across participating countries, using standardized spatial mapping and audit tools to ensure comparability.
- **Quantify associations** between environmental exposures, dietary intakes (from harmonized cross-sectional nutritional surveys), and key risk factors for NCDs at the population level.
- **Develop and recommend** evidence-based policy interventions to address disparities in healthy food access, inform food system reforms, and help reduce dietary inequalities.

### Scientific Rationale

Despite increasing awareness of the role food environments play in individual and public health, there is still a substantial gap in research that combines rigorous environmental mapping with individual-level dietary and health data across Europe (Núñez-Córdoba et al., 2023). This project directly addresses that gap, building on the recommendations of the Joint Action on Nutrition and Physical Activity (JANPA) and aligning with internationally recognized methods—such as STROBE-nut reporting guidelines and GDPR standards for data handling. Through multicountry collaboration, the study is poised to deliver new insights into both local and common influences on dietary patterns and NCD prevalence, contributing to a broader understanding needed for effective public health policies.

### Methodological Approach

The research consortium will employ a multicenter, cross-sectional observational design. Teams will conduct GIS-based spatial analyses of food retail and service outlets, complemented by in-depth field audits using validated tools (Gloppaz et al., 2021) to assess accessibility and quality of the food environment. Simultaneously, harmonized dietary intake data will be collected through standardized nutritional surveys adhering to STROBE-nut. 

To enable robust analyses, advanced statistical techniques—such as multilevel regression models and meta-analytic methods—will allow for both within-country and cross-country comparisons, accounting for hierarchical data structure (e.g., individuals nested within neighborhoods and cities). Matching algorithms will be used to control for confounding factors like socio-economic status or urban/rural classification, ensuring comparability. All study protocols are harmonized across countries following EATWELL and Food-EPI frameworks, and quality assurance is maintained through regular workshops and joint review sessions.

Data management plans meet the strictest requirements under Horizon Europe guidelines and national legislation. Comprehensive GDPR-compliant protocols govern data collection, pseudonymization, secure storage, and access. Only authorized study personnel will be permitted to access sensitive information, and all procedures have been reviewed by relevant legal and ethics offices.

### Anticipated Impact and Significance for Public Health Policy

This project will generate the first comprehensive, multicountry evidence base mapping disparities in food environment access and their links to population health risks across Europe. The findings will:

- Provide policymakers with objective measures of how food environments contribute to dietary inequalities and NCD risk.
- Strengthen the foundation for targeted policy interventions that improve access to nutritious foods in underserved communities.
- Directly support European Green Deal and Farm to Fork policy objectives, and contribute to progress on the Sustainable Development Goals related to health, nutrition, and equity.
- Deliver practical guidance on effective local, national, and pan-European strategies for food system transformation.

By translating scientific findings into actionable recommendations, the study aims to support reductions in health disparities, promote equitable access to healthy foods, and inform ongoing reforms in the European food system.

## Reflective Section: Collaboration, Lessons, and Next Steps

Throughout the development of this grant, the consortium experienced the value of clear, structured communication across diverse national teams and institutional cultures. Regular online workshops and transparent document sharing were essential tools for aligning methodologies and establishing shared expectations around data management and legal standards from the outset. Early and continuous engagement with data governance and legal experts not only streamlined compliance with ethical and regulatory guidelines but also helped anticipate and address project risks. 

The collaborative process fostered mutual trust and shared accountability, allowing partners to draw on each other's strengths. One important lesson has been the benefit of cross-disciplinary feedback, which consistently improved our research design and analytic rigor. For future steps, the project team will engage proactively with local and EC ethics committees and formalize a schedule for recurring partner meetings and joint progress reviews. Preparing organized strategies for dissemination will be a key priority, ensuring that research findings are communicated effectively to policymakers, stakeholders, and the wider public as soon as results become available.
