## Introduction

As part of my ongoing research into urban nutrition and food environments, I have developed a comprehensive system to document my daily meals and the sources of ingredients in Barcelona. This template allows me to capture all relevant details about what I eat and how I obtain my food, enabling a nuanced analysis of dietary intake, procurement behaviors, and their interaction with public health dynamics in the city. By applying rigorous standards around record-keeping and traceability, I ensure that my data is both scientifically robust and personally meaningful.

---

## Meal Log: Daily Intake Documentation

Documenting my meals with precision is central to understanding my nutritional patterns and the influence of food sourcing. Each day, I record:

- **Date and Meal Occasion:**  
  I note the date, time, and whether it's breakfast, lunch, snack, or dinner. This provides temporal context to my eating habits: for example, lunch at 13:30 after a morning spent at Mercat de l’Abaceria.

- **Ingredients List:**  
  For every meal, I carefully list the ingredients, specifying their weight in grams and whether they were raw or cooked. I always record the source—whether from a market, foraged on my urban walks, or purchased elsewhere. Market-sourced items include the brand, and I distinguish foraged foods (like wild fennel picked in Gràcia) from those bought in established venues.

- **Culinary Technique:**  
  I describe the cooking process, as it impacts nutrient content. For instance, yesterday I sautéed chickpeas with local tomatoes and foraged greens, which preserves more vitamin C than boiling.

- **Nutritional Content:**  
  For each ingredient and meal, I calculate and record the grams of carbohydrates, proteins, fats, and fiber, as well as key vitamins (C, D, B12) and minerals (iron, calcium, zinc). My nutrient data comes from reliable sources—mostly the Spanish Food Composition Database (Bedca) and sometimes USDA FoodData Central—citing the food code and database version each time for full traceability.

- **Traceability Details:**  
  Every packaged product includes its lot number and barcode. For foraged ingredients, I specify GPS coordinates or the exact neighborhood. Market foods are always annotated with the stall’s name, and I occasionally attach photos of product labels for better documentation.

Through this level of detail, I can systematically analyze my dietary intake: How nutrient distributions shift with different meal choices, how preparation methods affect my nutrient consumption, and how the origins of my food shape my overall diet.

## Photo Documentation

Capturing images of my meals and notable ingredients adds an important layer to my records—a visual sense of my food journey each day.

- **Meal Photos:**  
  Each meal is photographed, captioned, and stored with a clear filename. For example, “Lunch: Chickpea Stew with Market Tomatoes and Foraged Wild Fennel” became:  
  `ERomero_20240226_Lunch_ChickpeaStew_MercatAbaceria_FennelGracia.jpg`

- **Systematic Tagging:**  
  I use a standard schema for all filenames: `[ERomero]_[20240226]_[Lunch]_[KeyIngredient]_[Source]`. This keeps my archive organized and connects images directly to my written meal logs.

- **Integrating Images in Logs:**  
  Every photo reference appears right within the meal entry, so I can share documentation effortlessly with colleagues or review the meal’s context during later group discussions.

These meal images often spark conversations in our WhatsApp group and help us compare preparations and ingredient sourcing across different neighborhoods.

## Shopping & Sourcing Log

Understanding how I access food is just as critical as documenting what I eat. Every day, I track:

- **List of Acquired Ingredients and Quantities:**  
  As I walk through Barcelona’s markets or explore local parks, I write down every item obtained—quantities (grams, kilograms, pieces), and whether they were foraged, bought at a market, or found elsewhere.

- **Source Details:**  
  For each market item, I specify the stall or market name (like “Mercat de l’Abaceria, Stall 14”) and annotate organic or fair-trade certifications if present. For foraged items, I note the location and provide a brief description (e.g., “Wild fennel, collected at Carrer de Verdi park, GPS: 41.4065, 2.1568”).

- **Seasonality and Provenance:**  
  I pay close attention to where my food comes from—highlighting those truly local and in season (“Strawberries, Catalonia, late winter harvest”) and marking imports (“Avocado, imported from Murcia”).

- **Traceability Protocol:**  
  For packaged market items, the audit trail includes lot numbers, expiry dates, and photographs of any relevant certification labels. For foraged foods, I include a description of how I identified the plant (botanical key, personal knowledge, and photo evidence) and the environmental setting (urban park, roadside, etc.)

Foraging is an increasingly meaningful part of my routine, deepening my connection with the city and expanding the diversity of my diet. Thorough documentation lets me track the nutritional role of these wild foods and compare them with market produce.

## Qualitative Reflective Notes  

Numbers alone never tell the full story. I include daily qualitative reflections to document my motivations, experiences, and the realities of food access in Barcelona.

- **Culinary Motivation:**  
  Some meals are inspired by nostalgia (recreating my grandmother’s Catalan stew), others by curiosity—like experimenting with wild greens or unknown grains I encounter at the market.

- **Ingredient Selection Rationale:**  
  My choices always balance nutrition, sustainability, affordability, and freshness. Yesterday, I skipped imported berries in favor of seasonal citrus; I felt the local produce better supported both my nutritional goals and the city’s agricultural economy.

- **Discovery Notes:**  
  Sometimes, I come across “forgotten” foods—like purslane growing in urban cracks—which prompt research into their historical place in local diets and their nutritional profiles. These discoveries enrich my appreciation of Barcelona’s food landscape.

- **Public Health Observations:**  
  Market visits reveal differences in food access between neighborhoods—some stalls brimming with affordable fresh produce, others almost empty or selling mostly packaged goods. I notice social and infrastructural factors influencing food choices, from market hours to local policies limiting pesticide use.

Combining these personal reflections with my quantitative data gives my research—and my everyday life—depth and context. These stories help illuminate the complexities behind food choices and nutrition in a dynamic urban setting.

## Organization and Research Compliance

Maintaining consistency and data integrity is paramount:

- **Standardization:**  
  I use precise units and standardized terminology, maintaining a codebook of variables to ensure clarity as my dataset grows.

- **Audit Trail and Versioning:**  
  Every record is versioned, with edits tracked for transparency. This is essential for later data analysis and peer review.

- **Ethics and Data Protection:**  
  All records comply with research ethics; sensitive information is de-identified or securely stored. Privacy and confidentiality are always respected.

- **Database Integration:**  
  Templates fit neatly into Excel and REDCap, allowing seamless analysis and statistical review when compiling or sharing results.

By following these procedures, I safeguard the scientific quality and privacy of my study, making my daily logs ready for publication or external review whenever necessary.
