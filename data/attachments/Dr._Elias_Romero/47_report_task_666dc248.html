2. Overview of Scheduled Research Activities

The research session scheduled for 09:00 to 13:00 CEST aimed to advance key aspects of the department's ongoing qualitative studies in public health nutrition. Our main goals included:

  • Systematic Review of Digital Research Notes:
    We planned to thoroughly analyze participatory observation data collected from 3rd to 10th October, with particular attention to dietary behavior patterns. This analysis was to be conducted using NVivo software, with all relevant files stored on the university’s secure cloud infrastructure to allow seamless collaboration.

  • Collaborative Analysis of Feedback Forms:
    Another focus was the thematic coding and synthesis of digital feedback forms from the recent Coffee Tasting Event held on 11th October. The aim was to integrate new data within our existing codebook and established qualitative frameworks, using up-to-date files maintained on SharePoint.

  • Data Integration and Documentation:
    We intended to keep real-time records of all analytical decisions and synchronize finalized notes, codebook updates, and memos with UB OneDrive/SharePoint for team continuity and future auditability.

All these tasks depended on reliable access to university network infrastructure and central cloud resources, in line with our data governance standards for collaborative research.

4. Impact Assessment

The unexpected network outage had a tangible effect on the day’s planned research workflow:

  • Schedule Disruption:
    The session was delayed for a total of 89 minutes (09:13–10:42), accounting for nearly a third of the scheduled four-hour collaborative slot. During the outage, core research tasks that were dependent on network and cloud access came to a halt.

  • Resources Temporarily Lost/Inaccessible:
    We could not access the digital feedback forms from the Coffee Tasting Event (11 October 2024), the most recently updated NVivo master project file (last cloud sync: 12 October 2024, 17:34 CEST), or the updated analytic codebook hosted on SharePoint. These resources were essential for data integration and advanced thematic analysis.

  • Task-Level Impact:
    With network-dependent files unavailable, we could only make limited progress using printed documentation and incomplete local files. Advanced tasks such as collaborative coding in NVivo and full data synthesis could not proceed until the system was back online. Some time was used effectively to review printed notes, but significant portions of the agenda (thematic coding, codebook updates, and synthesis) were postponed.

  • Research Continuity and Data Security:
    Upon network restoration, we re-checked all critical files. All essential data was intact, and there was no evidence of file corruption or unauthorized access. Risk of data loss was minimal due to frequent automatic cloud backups and systematic manual backups. However, minor continuity risk persists for edits made after the last completed sync but before the outage. IT Services confirmed compliance with the university’s research data governance policies throughout the event.

6. Actions Taken and Recommendations

Immediate Actions

  • Resumption of Work:
    All cloud-dependent analysis activities have been resumed. Both Elias Romero and Carla Ruiz have completed an integrity check of key project files to verify their currency and completeness.

  • Reporting:
    This interruption log, including a brief impact statement, has been formally submitted to the Department Chair and University IT Services for audit and documentation purposes.

Near-Term Next Steps

  • Rescheduling and Planning:
    A follow-up session will be scheduled for October 17 or 18 to recover lost analytical time and complete postponed tasks.

  • IT Collaboration:
    Request a post-incident report from IT Services to determine the root cause of the network switch failure and identify additional safeguards to reduce future risk. All authentication logs, network activity, and file access patterns during the outage are being preserved for compliance and audit.

  • Data Validation:
    Integrity checks (e.g., hash verification) are underway for high-value research files, confirming that restored documents are uncompromised.

Long-Term and Preventative Recommendations

  • Enhanced Continuity Planning:
    The department will update its protocol for offline work during outages, standardize frequent local backups, and implement procedures to ensure researchers have access to recent local copies of critical files at all times.

  • Training and Preparedness:
    Training sessions will be organized to build team competence in quickly adapting to offline workflows and using secure mobile backups when needed.

  • Infrastructure Improvements:
    In coordination with the IT department, the feasibility of redundant network hardware or alternate connectivity options will be explored for research areas. Regular network reliability tests and simulation drills for failover events will be incorporated into departmental practice.
