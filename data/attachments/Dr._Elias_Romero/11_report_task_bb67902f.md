## 3. Discussion Details

### 3.1 Data Collection Protocols

The consortium emphasized the importance of adopting harmonized instruments and procedures to ensure that data collected across all sites are directly comparable. In line with established international practices, all partners committed to using a standardized survey with core modules addressing:
- Food availability and accessibility in local environments
- Food pricing and economic influences
- Marketing exposure, including digital and point-of-sale advertising
- Self-reported dietary behaviours, focusing on frequency and context of consumption

To uphold the scientific validity of the instrument across diverse settings, the team agreed to employ both forward and backward translation procedures, followed by lateral checks for cultural adaptation. Dr. Ingrid Keller noted that these processes were key to overcoming semantic discrepancies and preserving question intent.

Members reaffirmed the necessity of securing ethical approval from local institutional review boards before any pilot activities are initiated. This applies to all data collection, ensuring compliance with not only institutional policy but also European regulatory frameworks.

### 3.2 Methodological Clarifications

The group discussed operational definitions for key constructs such as “food environment,” “accessibility,” and “affordability,” referencing frameworks like INFORMAS and recent guidance from the WHO. Consistency in these definitions was recognized as fundamental for subsequent data synthesis and interpretation.

Sampling strategies received particular attention. Partners agreed to prioritize demographic and geographic representativeness—paying attention to variations in socioeconomic status, age, and the urban-rural distribution within each country. As Dr. Nowak highlighted, harmonization at this stage will facilitate meaningful cross-country and sub-national comparisons.

Consortium members reviewed data quality assurance measures, including double data entry, the use of standard operating procedures (SOPs), and dedicated training sessions for in-country data collectors. Standardization here aims to minimize inter-rater differences and enhance data reliability.

### 3.3 Cross-Country Comparability

Robust comparability is integral to this initiative’s objectives. All partners will undertake structured test-retest reliability checks and convergent validity assessments for the core survey modules before main data collection commences. The team agreed on a validation protocol that incorporates pilot cognitive interviews at each site to confirm comprehension and cultural relevance.

On the statistical side, Dr. Nowak outlined initial modeling strategies, proposing multi-level analyses that account for both national and regional variance. This approach will help control for contextual variables and strengthen conclusions.

Translation was discussed in detail, with consensus on comprehensive forward and backward translation for all survey materials. In addition, each site will pilot translated surveys through cognitive interviewing, ensuring both linguistic and cultural appropriateness.

### 3.4 Outstanding Questions and Methodological Challenges

A final decision on the selection of three pilot cities was deferred, pending the collection of further demographic and logistical data from all study partners. The team aims to ensure that chosen sites are demographically diverse and geographically varied to maximize learning from the pilot.

Further discussion is needed to finalize informed consent documentation and confirm alignment with GDPR standards, particularly regarding data storage, transfer, and participant confidentiality. There remains a challenge regarding the harmonization of socioeconomic status measurement, given the variation in classifications and household income thresholds across different countries.

---

## 4. Decisions and Agreements

- **Survey Piloting:** The new survey instrument will be piloted in three European cities selected for their demographic diversity and logistical feasibility. Final sites will be confirmed following partner consultation and review of local conditions.
- **Validation Protocols:** Uniform procedures for instrument translation, back-translation, and validation will be followed at each site as detailed in the SOPs.
- **Timeline Coordination:** While exact start dates depend on final pilot city selection, the consortium tentatively aims to launch the pilot phase in early summer 2024, with expansion contingent upon successful pilot outcomes.
