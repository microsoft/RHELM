## 3. Food Storage and Safety: Practice and Precautions

Maintaining food safety is a professional and personal priority. All prepared dishes are stored in airtight, labeled containers at or below 4°C in my refrigerator. I follow the recommendations from AESAN and the European Commission closely, making sure that all cooked legumes, grains, and stews are consumed within 3–5 days. For items such as leafy greens, I add a paper towel to the storage container to prevent sogginess and spoilage, consuming them within the week.

When I have extra portions at the end of a cooking session, I immediately transfer single servings to the freezer, making sure every container is labeled with a prep date. I avoid freezing delicate, high-water-content foods like fresh salads or raw vegetable sticks, as their texture does not survive thawing.

Strict separation of raw and cooked foods is maintained in my refrigerator, and I never reuse utensils after handling raw eggs or unwashed produce. For reheating, I use a thermometer to confirm that stews and grains reach at least 75°C before serving. Hard-boiled eggs are cooled, peeled, and stored in their own sealed container; I make it a habit to eat them within a week.

Before using any prepped ingredient, I always inspect for signs of spoilage and avoid leaving prepared foods at room temperature for more than two hours—especially on warmer days.

## 5. Ingredient Sourcing, Provenance, and Sustainability

Freshness and sustainability begin the moment I select ingredients. On Sunday mornings, I frequent Mercat de l’Abaceria for produce and grains, favoring growers and cooperatives who demonstrate a commitment to ecological practices and fair labor. The majority of my vegetables—artichokes, carrots, eggplants, chard, and tomatoes—are sourced locally, ensuring that I cook with ingredients at the height of their flavor and nutritional value.

I purchase legumes in bulk from Spanish producers known for supporting both environmental and social stewardship. Oils and tahini are sourced through certified DO (Denominación de Origen) suppliers, and I purchase eggs from free-range Catalan farms whose sustainability record I have personally verified.

Designing weekly menus around what is seasonally and regionally available has proved to be both a pleasure and a benefit, reducing food miles and reinforcing my connection to local food traditions. By planning meals that use overlapping ingredients and rely on batch-cooking, I make the most of resources, reduce kitchen energy usage, and cut down on food waste. Every week, I look forward to discovering new varieties at the market, a routine that keeps meal preparation joyful and rooted in my local culture.

## 6. Reflections on Public Health Nutrition Strategies

A plant-forward, Mediterranean-style diet is a cornerstone of both my research and my daily life. Each week, I strive for diversity in plant foods—whole grains, legumes, leafy greens, cruciferous vegetables, fruit, and fresh herbs—ensuring a broad spectrum of fiber, phytonutrients, and vitamins. This approach consistently demonstrates a lower risk of non-communicable diseases, a finding echoed in both the literature and client outcomes [5].

The Mediterranean principles not only shape my culinary preferences, but also guide my decisions in sourcing (local, seasonal, minimally processed foods), meal structure (olive oil as primary fat, moderate amounts of eggs, no red or processed meats), and organization (batch-prepared meals for efficiency and minimal waste) [6]. Through portioning and scheduling, I control leftovers and promote timely consumption, which prevents both food spoilage and unnecessary purchases—directly supporting FAO and EU food loss reduction targets [7].

Sourcing food from local markets and cooperatives not only offers environmental benefits by shortening supply chains and reducing greenhouse gas emissions, but it keeps me engaged with the local food economy. I am continually reminded that my daily choices—each market trip, each repurposed meal—contribute to the broader goal of building sustainable food systems [8].

All food safety and nutrition guidelines I follow are drawn from established regulatory bodies and current academic recommendations, supporting both my well-being and the credibility of my work.
