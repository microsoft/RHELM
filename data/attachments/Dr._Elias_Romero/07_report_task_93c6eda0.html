Adolescent Nutrition Project

Comprehensive Analysis Summary — Advances in Dietary Recall and Emerging Trends

Date: February 28, 2024
Author: Dr. Elias Romero, Senior Researcher, Department of Public Health Nutrition, University of Barcelona

1. Executive Summary

Analysis of the most recent dataset from the Adolescent Nutrition Project demonstrates a steady and meaningful increase in reported fruit and vegetable intake among adolescents aged 12 to 18 years in Barcelona. This upward trajectory represents a reversal of the declining trends observed between 2018 and 2022 and is accompanied by a stabilization in total daily sugar consumption. These changes coincide with the introduction of updated dietary recall protocols and digital self-reporting tools, which have also led to notable improvements in data reliability—both between and within raters.

The public health significance of these findings is substantial. A diet rich in fruits and vegetables during adolescence has consistently been linked to a lower risk of developing non-communicable diseases later in life, such as type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disorders [1,2]. Meanwhile, stable sugar consumption is associated with better weight management and may help curb rising obesity rates, a pressing concern among European youth. As these results will form the basis of an upcoming manuscript, they provide timely support for consolidating and expanding successful nutrition education and environmental strategies in schools and communities.

2. Data Analysis Methods

2.1 Sampling and Study Population

This wave of data collection recruited adolescents aged 12 to 18 from a stratified sample of urban and peri-urban secondary schools throughout Barcelona. Stratification accounted for age, gender, and socioeconomic status to ensure representativeness. Out of 960 students invited to participate, 825 completed at least two valid 24-hour dietary recall interviews, resulting in an 86% participation rate. This high response bolsters confidence in the generalizability of the findings.

2.2 Dietary Recall and Data Quality

Dietary intake was assessed using a multi-pass 24-hour recall methodology, supported by validated self-administered online modules. This approach aligns with best practices established in the HELENA study [3], maximizing comprehensiveness and minimizing under-reporting. To further ensure data integrity: - All dietary recalls were double-coded. - A random 10% subsample was re-interviewed within a week to assess repeatability. - Discrepancies were flagged and resolved through consultation between coders.

2.3 Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using R (version 4.3.1) with dedicated packages (survey, lme4, tidyverse) and Stata 18 for supplementary validation. - Generalized linear mixed models (GLMMs) were used to evaluate longitudinal changes (2018–2024) in nutrient intakes, adjusting for clustering by school and repeated measures at the individual level. - Bootstrapping with 1,000 replicates provided robust 95% confidence intervals for key estimates. - Sensitivity analyses excluded participants identified as likely under-reporters based on energy intake-to-baseline metabolic rate ratios.

2.4 Reproducibility and Quality Control

All coding scripts, anonymized datasets, and analytic outputs have been archived and version-controlled on our internal GitHub repository. Standard operating procedures for data handling were documented and independently audited by two analysts, supporting transparency and reproducibility standards.

4. Critical Discussion and Epidemiological Context

The steady rise in average daily fruit and vegetable intake, now approaching the WHO-recommended threshold, mirrors positive trends seen in pan-European initiatives such as the FRESH Teens Initiative and the 2022 Health Behaviour in School-Aged Children (HBSC) international survey [4]. While the mean intake remains just below the recommended 400 grams per day, the proportion of adolescents meeting this benchmark has more than doubled since 2018.

Evidence shows that even incremental increases in fruit and vegetable consumption during adolescence offer protective benefits against future obesity, hypertension, and metabolic syndrome [1,2]. These patterns suggest that the educational and environmental interventions rolled out since 2019—ranging from enhanced school canteen options to digital nutrition literacy campaigns—are not only reaching a broad cross-section of students but are having a real impact on daily dietary behaviors.

Additionally, sugar intake among this cohort has stabilized, a trend that is likely contributing to improved energy balance and a potential moderation of obesity rates. This is particularly notable given the tendency in previous years toward rising sugar consumption, paralleling trends observed across several European countries.

Improvements in dietary recall reliability, with most nutrients showing ICC values above 0.80, place this dataset among the more robust adolescent nutrition cohorts internationally. This enhanced reliability addresses concerns that have often limited the utility of adolescent self-reported dietary data [5,6] and strengthens the validity of these trend analyses.

From a policy perspective, these developments offer strong empirical support for continuing and scaling up interventions that promote healthy eating in schools and communities. The results also highlight the need for targeted strategies to help the remaining adolescents not yet meeting dietary guidelines, with forthcoming subgroup analyses expected to clarify where additional support is most urgently needed.

5. Action Plan: Next Steps and Methodological Enhancements

  1. Subgroup Analyses:
    The next phase will involve a detailed breakdown of dietary patterns by gender, socioeconomic status, and type of school attended. This will help identify potential disparities in nutrition outcomes and prioritize high-need groups for bespoke interventions.

  2. Dietary Pattern Modeling:
    With improved recall reliability, we can now employ advanced statistical approaches—such as principal components analysis (PCA) and latent class analysis—to identify dominant eating patterns within the cohort and assess their association with health outcomes.

  3. Strengthen Data Quality Controls:
    Plans are in place to intensify training for the coding team in digital dietary recall methods. Additionally, we will engage an external review team to audit our data management and reproducibility pipeline, ensuring our findings meet the highest standards of scientific rigor.

  4. Operational Decisions:
    The research team will finalize definitions of "adherence" to recommended intake thresholds, and consider expanding analyses to include micronutrients of high public health relevance, such as vitamin D and iron. The team will also decide on protocols for data sharing with the European Adolescent Nutrition Surveillance Consortium, facilitating cross-country comparisons.

  5. Manuscript Preparation:
    Drafting of the manuscript for submission to Public Health Nutrition will begin imminently. This paper will focus on the significant trend reversals and methodological advancements of the project, placing them in the broader context of adolescent public health in Europe.
