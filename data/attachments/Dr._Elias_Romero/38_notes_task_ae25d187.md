## Research Priorities & Approaches to Public Health Communication

Barcelona continues to grapple with a range of nutrition and urban health challenges. In recent years, both adults and children, particularly in neighborhoods with lower socio-economic status, have experienced rising rates of overweight and obesity [[1], [2]]. These health concerns are compounded by clear disparities in access to healthy food across different districts, as well as the widespread availability of ultra-processed foods in the urban diet. In response, the Barcelona City Council has prioritized initiatives to promote fruit and vegetable consumption, improve school menus, and foster more sustainable local food environments [[1]]. Urban green spaces like Parc de la Ciutadella play an essential role in these efforts—not only as sites for physical activity but also as venues for community engagement and healthy lifestyle promotion.

The city’s established culture of public art and murals creates opportunities for visual advocacy around public health. Artistic interventions provide a dynamic way to reach both residents and visitors, transcending language barriers and connecting with Barcelona’s multicultural, multilingual audience.

**Key Areas of Focus Include:**

- Addressing nutritional inequities and promoting access to healthy foods citywide.
- Utilizing public art to communicate evidence-informed nutrition messages.
- Creating inclusive campaigns that resonate with local, migrant, and international communities.
- Leveraging urban parks as platforms for innovative health promotion.

## Advancing Public Health Through Innovative Communication

To strengthen existing efforts and introduce fresh approaches, several creative public health communication strategies are under consideration:

**1. “Healthy Plate” Urban Art Trail**

I propose designing a mapped route showcasing large-scale murals inspired by Spain’s “Plato Saludable” (Healthy Plate) concept. Each mural would creatively incorporate local produce and Catalan cultural elements. Integrating QR codes, visitors could easily access nutritional information, healthy recipes, and public health resources in Catalan, Spanish, and English—bridging information gaps and making practical guidance accessible to various audiences.

**2. Nutritional Storytelling Through Art**

Partnering with local artists, neighborhood associations, and migrant groups, we can develop murals that narrate the food stories of Barcelona’s communities. These artworks would illustrate the shared evolution of traditional, migrant, and modern diets, reflecting the city’s diversity and ongoing transformation toward healthier eating.

**3. Sensory Walk Challenges**

Urban parks like Ciutadella offer unique settings for encouraging mindful eating and sensory awareness. Visual prompts within murals can invite visitors to pause and reflect on the sensory experiences of taste, smell, and the social nature of mealtimes, tying the act of nourishment to joy and community.

**4. Public Engagement Workshops**

I see great potential in hosting intergenerational mural workshops directly within the park. Inviting school groups, older adults, and culinary professionals to co-create public artworks not only fosters local ownership but also strengthens advocacy around healthy eating habits across age groups.

**5. Promotion of Sustainable, Plant-Forward Diets**

Integrating motifs that represent sustainability—such as depictions of water, local plant life, and bustling market scenes—can help normalize plant-forward diets, emphasizing benefits to both personal and planetary health.

## Immersive Observations: Parc de la Ciutadella, 06:37–07:35

This morning, the atmosphere in Parc de la Ciutadella felt both invigorating and contemplative. As dawn broke, rays of sunlight filtered through trees, highlighting the dewy grass and ushering in joggers, tai chi practitioners, and dog walkers. The historic monuments—especially the dramatic Cascada Monumental—and various public sculptures offered rich visual stimulation, naturally prompting me to imagine how art and public health advocacy could blend seamlessly within these communal spaces.

The soundscape was alive: birds calling from treetops, the rhythmic sweep of city cleaning crews, the soft crunch of gravel under runners’ shoes, clusters of teenagers laughing, and everyday conversations flowing in Catalan and Spanish. These layers of sound reflected the park’s unique social fabric, a meeting point for people of all ages and backgrounds.

I found the scents equally evocative. Freshly mown grass mingled with the sweet aroma of blooming jacarandas, while the smell of bread—just out of the oven from a nearby bakery—almost pulled me toward a small café outside the park gate. The moist earth after park irrigation completed the tapestry, triggering childhood food memories and a renewed appreciation for how much environment shapes appetite and eating meaning.

The overall atmosphere achieved a delicate balance between tranquility and activity—a steady pulse of city life, yet with pockets of reflective quiet. Observing this interplay, I felt inspired by the park’s capacity to bridge tradition and innovation, individual and community, the mundane and the creative.

These sensory impressions deepened my conviction that effective public health messaging in spaces like Ciutadella should aim for multisensory engagement. Art that not only catches the eye but also triggers reflection, emotion, memory, and discussion has the potential to move beyond information-sharing toward true cultural change.

## Translating Observations into Community Engagement Strategies

Drawing on both the empirical data and my experience in the park, I see several practical pathways for advancing public health nutrition advocacy in Barcelona’s urban environments:

- **Situated Storytelling:** Embedding health messages within the daily flow of city life—at parks, markets, and transit hubs—tailors advocacy to local realities, making it both visible and relevant.
- **Cultural Resonance:** Meaningful nutrition messages respect Barcelona’s layered linguistic and cultural identity. Using familiar symbols, neighborhood stories, and multiple languages strengthens trust and community ownership [[4]].
- **Participatory Co-Creation:** Involving residents in every phase of the advocacy process—from conception and design to painting murals—ensures that campaigns reflect evolving needs, sustain conversation, and build lasting connection.
- **Sensory and Emotional Appeal:** Harnessing the multisensory energy of public spaces heightens emotional engagement. Advocacy that feels, looks, and sounds relevant is more likely to be held in memory and discussed among peers and families.
- **Integration with Policy and City Health Goals:** Aligning mural themes and engagement activities with city health strategies—such as those outlined by the Ajuntament de Barcelona and regional diet and sustainability plans—amplifies impact and fosters institutional support [[1], [2], [3]].
- **Continuous Feedback and Evaluation:** Collecting regular feedback and monitoring behavior change in communities supports ongoing adaptation, ensuring advocacy efforts remain relevant and effective.
