Combined Shopping List and Meal Preparation Log – Mercat de l’Abaceria, Barcelona (14 September 2024)

Introduction

On September 14, 2024, I visited Mercat de l’Abaceria in Barcelona to shop for a week’s worth of meals, focusing on fresh, local, and seasonal produce. I set out with a clear intention to support sustainable food practices, prioritize nutritious ingredients, and streamline my meal preparation for the week ahead. Throughout this process, I paid careful attention to the provenance of my food, its nutritional content, and the environmental impact of my choices. In this report, I have documented my shopping list and batch-cooking workflow, included provenance and nutritional analysis, evaluated environmental impact, and detailed ingredient substitutions to ensure the process remains reproducible and transparent for future reference.

2. Sustainable Shopping: Minimizing Waste and Environmental Impact

I approached shopping this week as a small but meaningful way to reduce my environmental footprint. Determined to avoid single-use plastics, I packed two sturdy cotton totes and a handful of mesh produce bags. Barley and other grains went straight into a glass jar I brought from home; vendors were supportive and happy to see customers skipping plastic. Mushrooms were handed to me in a recyclable paper bag, and I politely declined any plastic wrappings at each stall.

Plastic Waste Avoided

By the end of my shop, I had successfully eliminated the following from my waste stream:

  • 6 single-use plastic produce bags (~5g each): 30g
  • 2 plastic pouches for bulk grains (~15g each): 30g
  • 1 pre-packed salad clamshell (~20g): 20g
    Total: 80g of plastic waste avoided this shop

Reflections on Environmental Impact

It's easy to overlook just how much plastic comes home from a typical market trip. Replacing single-use bags with reusables is a small switch that becomes second nature and, if adopted widely, would dramatically reduce waste across the city. Shopping seasonal, local produce has the added advantage of slashing the “food miles” each meal travels—supporting both the climate and my local economy. Bringing my own containers started friendly conversations with both vendors and other shoppers, and I felt a deeper connection to my community’s food systems.

3. Batch Cooking: Preparation and Nutritional Value

Planning my meals ahead saves me time during the busy week and gives me control over ingredients and portion sizes. This week, I decided to prepare two main batch-cooked dishes, both inspired by Mediterranean traditions and using everything from my seasonal market haul.

Dish 1: Barley-Mushroom Stew

Ingredients (Makes 4 Servings)

  • 200g barley (dry)
  • 300g wild mushrooms
  • 1 red onion (about 150g)
  • 25ml olive oil
  • 15g fresh parsley
  • 800ml vegetable stock
  • Salt and black pepper to taste

Cooking Steps

  1. I rinse and soak the barley for a couple of hours before cooking (this cuts down the final cooking time).
  2. In a large pan, I heat the olive oil and sauté the chopped onion for a few minutes, just until translucent.
  3. I add the sliced mushrooms, cooking them down until they’re soft and aromatic.
  4. Next, barley goes in, stirred until each grain is glossy and mixed with the aromatics.
  5. I pour in the stock, bring everything to a gentle boil, then let it simmer over low heat for about 35 minutes, checking occasionally so nothing sticks.
  6. Once the barley is tender, I season with salt and fold in a handful of freshly chopped parsley right at the end.
  7. I enjoy this stew steaming hot, with a little extra parsley sprinkled over each bowl.

Yield and Nutrition

  • Around 1.4 kg total (4 servings of ~350g)
  • Per serving: roughly 315 kcal; 9g protein; 47g carbs (8g fiber); 8g fat
  • Nutritious balance of complex carbohydrates, plant protein, and soluble fiber
  • Rich in key micronutrients from both barley and mushrooms, including vitamin D, potassium, iron, and magnesium

Besides being incredibly filling and soothing as weather turns cooler, this dish fits right into Mediterranean dietary advice and is easy to portion out for quick lunches or dinners.

Dish 2: Roasted Butternut Squash Salad

Ingredients (Makes 4 Servings)

  • 800g butternut squash (peeled)
  • 150g fresh spinach
  • 1 red onion (about 150g)
  • 20ml olive oil
  • 10g fresh parsley
  • 2 persimmons (about 350g)
  • Salt and black pepper

Cooking Steps

  1. I preheat the oven to 200°C and chop the squash into bite-sized cubes. Tossed with olive oil, salt, and pepper, the squash roasts on a tray for half an hour until golden and soft.
  2. While the squash cooks, I slice the onion into thin rings and give the spinach a quick rinse.
  3. I slice each persimmon into thin wedges, their sweetness perfect alongside the squash.
  4. On a serving platter, I spread out the fresh spinach, followed by roasted squash and red onions. Persimmon wedges go on top, along with a drizzle of the remaining olive oil and a sprinkle of parsley.
  5. This salad comes together quickly and can be served slightly warm or at room temperature—a perfect vibrant lunch as the days begin to shorten.

Yield and Nutrition

  • Total yield: about 1.1 kg (4 servings of ~275g)
  • Per serving: ~190 kcal; 2.6g protein; 33g carbs (5g fiber); 5g fat
  • High in beta-carotene (vitamin A), vitamin C, vitamin K, potassium, and healthy polyphenols

With bold colors and fresh flavors, this salad keeps well and boosts my weekly intake of vitamins and antioxidants. The sweetness of the persimmon lifts the dish and adds seasonal variety.

5. Reflections: Urban Food Choices for Sustainability and Public Health

Shopping at Mercat de l’Abaceria not only reconnected me with the rhythms of the growing season but also gave me a sense of agency in supporting local agriculture and reducing environmental waste. By relying on what’s fresh and abundant, I reduced the need for cold storage and avoided long-haul transport—helpful steps for both personal health and the environment.

Choosing hearty plant-based meals this week aligns with Mediterranean diet guidelines and public health recommendations in Barcelona. My recipes naturally emphasize plant-based fiber, antioxidants, and balanced macronutrients while making the most of local traditions and flavors. I’ve found that integrating small changes—such as using reusable bags and bulk purchases, or getting to know the regional sources of my food—offers tangible benefits to both health and sustainability.

The combination of careful ingredient tracking, batch cooking, and transparent substitution documentation also makes it easy to reproduce this template for healthy, low-waste eating in other urban environments.
